# build-subject

The goal is **pragmatic knowledge**: after a subject, the learner can do the thing in real life, in the moment they
meet it, with nothing in front of them (place the country, judge the label on the post, hang up on the scam call).
Knowing about it does not count.

The owner should not have to do this work or catch its mistakes. The skill does the reading, the reasoning and the
checking; the owner sees the result.

## 1. What the owner wants

Read it yourself; never ask the owner to restate it.
- Their messages about the subject, their own course documents and the old lessons they used. List every topic they
  asked for, in their words, in the plan (below). Nothing on that list is dropped without their word.
- How their own material taught it. If their course opened with a way of thinking (Ideology's deciding questions: who
  owns the means of production, how is the society divided), that is the spine of the subject, not one topic among many.

## 2. What the research says (docs/learning-science.md, docs/research/)

These hold for every subject. A subject note can add to them, never overturn them on weaker evidence.
1. **Method first, then a worked case, then practice.** Beginners learn far more when they are taught the way to decide
   and shown it applied step by step than when they work it out from examples (Alfieri et al. 2011, 164 studies;
   Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006; worked examples, Barbieri et al. 2023). Never "work out the rule from these cases".
2. **Teach why each step or question is asked** (P9): the reason is what carries to new cases. Never shrink it to a
   label or a phrase.
3. **Practise from memory, with an explanation after every answer** (P16, P19, R1, R7): recall beats rereading, and
   feedback names the step that decided.
4. **Practise in the form of real use** (D6): the real kind of case, answered with the real action.
5. **Contrast and wrong ideas** (P12, P29): look-alikes side by side with what to compare; the common wrong idea named
   and corrected.
6. **Mix, then space** (P22, P24, R2, R4, R9): once several outcomes are known, mix them; misses come back; everything
   returns on later days until it holds.
7. **Feeling is not evidence** (D4): a lesson that feels smooth can teach less; judge by the check.

A decision that rests on a finding rests on the paper, not on a search summary. If a subject seems to need something
these don't cover (a body skill like singing), research it first and say what it adds.

## 3. The plan (docs/subjects/<id>/plan.md)

One page, written before any lesson:
- **What you'll be able to do, and when**: one sentence ("After this you can ___ when ___"), and the real-world test:
  new cases in the real form, with what passing is.
- **The way of thinking**: the method every case is decided by (the deciding questions, the steps, or how a fact is
  found: Geography's "a place is found from the bigger area it sits in, then what touches it and what water it is on").
- **What it teaches**: every topic, covering every ask from step 1.
- **The lessons**, in order: what each teaches and its check (new cases, real form, a pass mark). Each lesson builds on
  the ones before; the first lessons teach the way of thinking.

Show the owner the first two bullets in a few lines and go on unless they object. Only dropping an ask waits for them.

## 4. Each lesson

1. **The way of thinking** this lesson uses, stated plainly, with why each question or step is asked.
2. **One worked case**, every step shown in the method's own words.
3. **Practice**: real-looking cases from memory, guided first, then alone; feedback after each answer that names the
   step that decided; look-alikes in pairs; the common wrong idea named.
4. **Mixed practice** with earlier lessons' material.
5. **The check**: new cases in the real form, scored.
Reading is what the method needs and no more; a reading limit never cuts the method or the worked case. The project's
lesson standard (`docs/lesson-standard.md`) and validator say how a lesson is built in this app.

## 5. Checking a lesson before it ships

Two fresh agents that did not write it, each with a short brief:
- **The learner** (`review.md`): goes through it cold as a beginner, from the learner view only, writes down the rule
  before the first case, does the check, and lists every place it got stuck.
- **The research check**: reads the lesson against the seven points of section 2 and lists every one it breaks.

Fix everything they find and check again with fresh agents, until both pass. The owner's own try is welcome and
outranks a review, but is never required.

## 6. When the owner corrects something

Treat it as criticism of the design, not as a new rule. Find the root cause, fix the design there, and check whether
the same mistake is elsewhere. Do not add a rule, a gate or a limit to this skill unless the root cause is this skill
itself, and then rewrite the part that was wrong instead of adding to it. This skill stays one short page.
