# Geography: Mexico and Central America

Revision 1, live. Part: Mexico and Central America. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A storm builds in the Caribbean, or the news says migrants are crossing Mexico toward the United States, or a ship waits at the Panama Canal. If the land between the United States and South America is a blank to you, you cannot picture any of it. In about fourteen minutes you will place Mexico, the seven countries of Central America, the Panama Canal and the two seas beside them on a blank map, by finding the area each sits in first, then name the four most often mixed up and say what touches each.

## Step 1: How to find a country

One thing first about the name Central America. The world map you met first (The parts of the Americas, Europe and Africa) counts Mexico in Central America, from Mexico down to Panama. Here Mexico is taught on its own, and Central America means the seven countries of the isthmus below it, from Guatemala to Panama. The same three questions as for any place, each asked for a reason. What bigger area is it in? That tells you which part of the map to look in, so you search a part, not the whole. What touches it? Its neighbors pin the exact spot, and a border is what the news is about. What water is it on? Coast or inland, and which sea, often is the story. Here there are three areas. Mexico is the one big country, south of the United States. Below it the land narrows into the isthmus, a thin land bridge: the northern isthmus holds Guatemala, Belize, El Salvador and Honduras; the southern isthmus holds Nicaragua, Costa Rica and Panama, where South America begins. The Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of Mexico lie on one side and the Pacific on the other, and the Panama Canal joins them. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water.

## Step 2: Mexico and its seas

Look for about a minute at each of three views. Say each name as you find it. None of it stays up: next you find places on a map with no names. The whole map first. Mexico is the one big country, south of the United States. It has the Pacific and the Gulf of California on its west and the Gulf of Mexico on its east; around the bulge of its south-east corner it touches the Caribbean Sea too, which lies south of Cuba.

## Step 4: The southern isthmus

Nicaragua is the biggest, Costa Rica the narrow one, Panama the last before South America. The Panama Canal cuts across Panama from the Caribbean Sea to the Pacific.

## Step 5: A worked example

Guatemala


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of Mexico and Central America with Guatemala lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? Guatemala is the first country of the isthmus, the narrow land south of Mexico. Start from Mexico, the big country named on the first screen.
1. Touches: what is next to it? Mexico is on its north and west. Belize and Honduras are on its east side, and El Salvador is on its south.
1. Water: what water is it on? It has a long coast on the Pacific, on its southwest side, and a short one on the Caribbean Sea, on its east. Tap inside the country.

Then the answer: Guatemala. Tell it from Belize and El Salvador: Guatemala is the big one that touches Mexico; Belize is the small one east of it and El Salvador the tiny one on the Pacific.
