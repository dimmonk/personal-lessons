# Basic Math: A missing number

Revision 1, live. Part: A missing number. Where the subject ends up: Work out the number a situation needs (a total, a price per one, a scaled amount, a percent or the price before one, how much paint or soil to buy, how high a ladder reaches, what savings grow to or a loan costs) and tell whether it is sensible, when a receipt, a recipe, a sale tag, a room or an offer hands you some numbers and a question and nobody names the method.

## The why

A formula, a price list or a plan of a yard often gives you the result and hides the number you need: the temperature in °C, the visits when a pass pays off, the side of a bed.
Here you work back to that number.

## Step 1: Work back to the missing number

A formula says how to go one way: °F = 1.8 × °C + 32 means times 1.8, then add 32. To go back, undo the steps in the opposite order: take away 32 first, then divide by 1.8. 77°F: on the calculator 77 − 32 =, then ÷ 1.8 = 25°C. (Press = after each step: some phone calculators would divide first.)
When does a pass cost less? Pay $9 a swim, or a pass at $40 a month plus $4 each swim. Each swim, the pass saves $9 − $4 = $5, so the $40 is paid back after 40 ÷ 5 = 8 swims, where both cost $72. From 9 swims a month on, the pass costs less; below 8, paying each time does.
A square from its area: a square bed with a path 2 ft wide along two sides, the corner between them left out, covers 140 square feet. Fill in the missing corner, 2 × 2 = 4: the whole is a square of 144 square feet, its side √144 = 12 ft, and the bed is 12 − 2 = 10 ft.

A plan drawn to one scale, each side written in ft: 10 ft by 10 ft (Bed); 10 ft by 2 ft (Path); 2 ft by 10 ft (Path); 2 ft by 2 ft, cut out.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: “Undo it in the same order: divide by 1.8, then take away 32.”
The right line: The last thing done is the first undone: take away 32 first.

## Step 2: A worked example

A plain table, "Weather for Saturday":

> High  |  77°F
> Low  |  65°F
> Wind  |  8 mph
> (°F = 1.8 × °C + 32)
> (Your cousin in Canada wants the high in °C.)

The working, one step at a time:
1. Say what is asked and what is given. Asked: the high, 77°F, in °C. Given: the formula from °C to °F. The low and the wind do not matter.
1. Make a rough answer in your head. Take off 30 and halve: 77 − 30 = 47, half is 23.5. 1.8 is a tenth less than 2, so add about a tenth: about 25.5°C.
1. Name the kind of problem and draw it. The formula goes from °C to °F: times 1.8, then add 32. Backward, undo the last step first: take away 32, then divide by 1.8.
1. Work it out exactly on the calculator, then look back. On the calculator: 77 − 32 = ÷ 1.8 = shows 25°C. Look back: 1.8 × 25 + 32 = 77, close to the 77°F you started from.

Then the answer: 25.5 °C / 25.0 °C. Backward: 77 − 32 = 45, ÷ 1.8 = 25°C.
