# Wealth Preservation: What it costs a year, in dollars

Revision 1, draft (the screen carries the draft line). Part: What it costs a year, in dollars. Where the subject ends up: Run the yearly check on your own US accounts: work out what each costs you a year in dollars, your biggest single bet, how far your mix has drifted, whether you take the full employer match, which foreign holdings need a US filing, and who inherits each account; then make the changes it calls for and leave alone what it clears.

## The why

A fund’s cost is a small-looking percent. In dollars it is not small: 0.95% of $38,400 is $364.80 every year, whether the fund did well or badly.
Here you read a fee sheet, turn the percents into dollars, and decide: change a fund, or leave it alone. Then you do the same on your own accounts.

## Step 1: The rule

Every fund takes a yearly cost, written as a percent of what you hold. To get dollars: what you hold × the percent ÷ 100. Then add any fee the plan takes on top.
Change a fund only when another fund of the same kind, on the same list, costs less. Leave it alone when nothing of its kind costs less: switching only costs you time.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: “Pick the cheapest fund on the list.”
The right line: Pick the cheapest of the same kind. A bond fund costs less than a stock fund, but it holds something else, so it is not a swap.

## Step 2: A worked example

A statement, "Lakeside Manufacturing 401(k)" (a tab named "Statement", one of a household's papers: one open at a time, the first at the start, and the one with a marked line after the answer):

> Your funds  |  Balance  (the column heads)
> Summit Growth Fund  |  $38,400
> Total Bond Index Fund  |  $12,600
> Total  |  $51,000
> (Balances on the last day of the quarter.)

A fee sheet, "Lakeside Manufacturing: what the funds cost" (a tab named "Fee sheet", one of a household's papers: one open at a time, the first at the start, and the one with a marked line after the answer):

> Fund  |  Kind  |  Yearly cost  (the column heads)
> Summit Growth Fund  |  Large US companies  |  0.95%
> Index 500 Fund  |  Large US companies  |  0.04%
> Summit Select Growth Fund  |  Large US companies  |  1.20%
> Harbor Bond Fund  |  Bonds  |  0.60%
> Total Bond Index Fund  |  Bonds  |  0.07%
> Plan fee  |  Taken from your balance  |  $96 a year
> (A yearly cost is a percent of what you hold in the fund. It is taken out of the fund every year.)

The working, one step at a time:
1. Find each fund’s yearly cost on the fee sheet. Summit Growth Fund costs 0.95%. Total Bond Index Fund costs 0.07%.
1. Turn each cost into dollars, and add the plan fee. Summit Growth Fund: $38,400 × 0.95% = $364.80. Total Bond Index Fund: $12,600 × 0.07% = $8.82. Plan fee $96. Together $469.62 a year.
1. Look for a fund of the same kind that costs less. Summit Growth Fund holds large US companies and costs 0.95%. Index 500 Fund holds the same and costs 0.04%.
1. Name the fund to switch to. Index 500 Fund: the same kind, at 0.04%. Total Bond Index Fund costs 0.07% but it holds bonds, so it is not a swap.

Then the answer: Summit Growth Fund: 0.95%; Total Bond Index Fund: 0.07% / $469.62 a year / Change it / Index 500 Fund. Your two funds and the plan fee cost $469.62 a year. Summit Growth Fund costs 0.95%, and Index 500 Fund holds the same kind of companies for 0.04%, so switching saves about $349.44 a year.

**The sheet, when it is empty.** "Your accounts": Type what your statements and your plan’s fee sheet say: the kind of account, its balance, each fund in it, and what each fund costs a year in percent. - Kind of account (choose one)
- A name you will know it by (optional) (words)

- Fees the plan takes from it (optional) (a number, in dollars a year)
