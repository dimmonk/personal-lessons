# Wealth Preservation: What it costs a year, in dollars

Revision 2, draft (the screen carries the draft line). Part: What it costs a year, in dollars. Where the subject ends up: Run the yearly check on your own US accounts: work out what each costs you a year in dollars, your biggest single bet, how far your mix has drifted, whether you take the full employer match, which foreign holdings need a US filing, and who inherits each account; then make the changes it calls for and leave alone what it clears.

## The why

A fund’s cost is a small-looking percent. In dollars it is not small: 0.95% of $38,400 is $364.80 every year, whether the fund did well or badly.
Here you read a plan’s papers, turn the percents into dollars, and decide: change a fund, or leave it alone. Then you do the same on your own accounts.

## Step 1: The rule

Every fund takes a yearly cost, its expense ratio, a percent of what you hold. Use the net ratio: it is what you pay now. Dollars: what you hold × the percent ÷ 100.
Add the account’s fee. A fee charged each quarter is four a year. A fee that is a percent of the account, such as an adviser’s, is that percent × the whole balance.
The same kind means the same category on the papers: Large Blend, Large Growth, Target Date 2055. Large Growth is not a swap for Large Blend.
Change a fund only when another in its category costs less. Leave it when it is the cheapest in its category, whatever its name or price. A target-date fund mixes stocks and bonds for you: leave it unless another with its date costs less.
A switch costs no tax inside a 401(k), an IRA or an HSA. In a taxable account, selling can owe tax on gains: check first.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: “Pick the cheapest fund on the list.”
The right line: Pick the cheapest of the same category. A bond fund costs less than a stock fund, but it holds something else, so it is not a swap.

The working, one step at a time:
1. Find the net expense ratio of each fund you hold. Summit Growth Fund costs 0.95%. The gross ratio, 1.10%, is before a waiver. Harbor Bond Fund costs 0.07%.
1. Turn each cost into dollars, and add the account fee. Summit Growth Fund: $38,400 × 0.95% = $364.80. Harbor Bond Fund: $12,600 × 0.07% = $8.82. Recordkeeping fee $24 × 4 quarters = $96 a year. Together $469.62 a year.
1. Look in the same category for a fund that costs less. Summit Growth Fund is in Large Growth and costs 0.95%. Growth 100 Fund is in the same category and costs 0.04%.
1. Name the fund to switch to. Growth 100 Fund: the same category, at 0.04%. Northfield 500 Fund costs 0.12%, but it is in Large Blend, so it is not a swap.

Then the answer: Summit Growth Fund: 0.95%; Harbor Bond Fund: 0.07% / $469.62 a year / Change it / Growth 100 Fund. Your two funds and the account fee cost $469.62 a year. Summit Growth Fund costs 0.95%, and Growth 100 Fund, in the same category, costs 0.04%, so switching saves about $349.44 a year.
