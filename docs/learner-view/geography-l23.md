# Geography: The Sahel and Central Africa

Revision 1, live. Part: The Sahel and Central Africa. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A headline says "Congo", and there are two of them. Another says a border has closed between Chad and Cameroon. If the middle of Africa is one blank to you, neither tells you much. In about fifteen minutes you will place the twelve countries from Mauritania to the two Congos on a blank map, by finding the area each sits in first, then name the four most often mixed up and say what touches each.

## Step 1: How to find a country

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? That tells you where on the map to look. What touches it? Its neighbors pin the exact spot, and they are what the news names. What water is it on? Coast or inland is often the point of the story. Here there are two areas. The Sahel is the dry belt across Africa just south of the Sahara: Mauritania, Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger and Chad, west to east. Central Africa is the middle of the continent: Cameroon, at the corner where the coast turns south, then small Equatorial Guinea and Gabon on the coast, the Central African Republic inland, and the two Congos around the Congo River. Two small islands, São Tomé and Príncipe, lie out in the Gulf of Guinea. The Sahel is a belt that runs across two of the world map's parts from part 1b: Mauritania, Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger are West Africa there, and Chad is Central Africa. Both are true, and the feedback says so. Water here means a sea, an ocean or a big lake (Victoria, Tanganyika, Malawi), so a river or a small lake does not count: Lake Chad has shrunk too small to count, so Chad is landlocked. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water.

## Step 2: The Sahel, west to east

Look for about a minute at each of three views. Say each name as you find it. None of it stays up: next you find places on a map with no names. From the Atlantic going east: Mauritania, Mali and Niger, with Burkina Faso, the small one, under Mali and Niger, and Chad at the east end. The belt goes on east, past Chad, through Sudan.

## Step 4: Chad, the Central African Republic and the two Congos

The Central African Republic is in the middle of the continent, under Chad. Below it are the two Congos, on either side of the Congo River: the Republic of the Congo, the smaller one by Gabon, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the huge one, which also reaches Lake Tanganyika on its east.
