# Geography: The world's water, mountains and deserts

Revision 1, live. Part: The world's water, mountains and deserts. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A news story says a ship has gone aground in the English Channel, or a rescue is under way in the Alps. If seas, straits, mountain ranges and deserts are one blur to you, the story tells you little. In about sixteen minutes you will tap thirteen of the world's seas, straits, ranges, deserts and rivers on a blank world map, by finding the continent or ocean each is in or beside first, then what it lies between, and name the five most often mixed up, two ranges and three deserts.

## Step 1: How to find a sea, a range or a desert

A sea, a strait, a range, a desert and a river are found like any place, by three questions. Which continent or ocean is it in or beside? A blank world map shows only the outlines of the continents, so this tells you where to look. What does it lie between? A strait lies between two lands, a range between lowlands or seas, a river between its source and the sea it ends in; the lands and seas on either side pin the exact spot, and a news story names them. What water is it on? A sea or strait joins oceans, a river ends in a sea, and a range or desert far inland touches none, which is itself a clue. Here are thirteen places: the Mediterranean, the English Channel and the Bering Sea; the Straits of Gibraltar and Bering; the Mississippi; the Rockies, the Urals, the Alps and the Atlas; and the Gobi, the Arabian Desert and the Outback. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Between, Water.
