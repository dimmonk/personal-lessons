### 2. The great arrivals, in three years

*Unit Ten · rev 5 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 2 · Card 2 of 65*

[reviewers only: card kind `concept`, id `con-wave`]

Three events from the years when the country filled with factories and new arrivals.

*Noor’s family card*

> Noor’s grandmother leaves her a small card, brown at the edges. It says her great-grandfather came by steamship to New York and was checked at Ellis Island in 1905. Noor looks it up and finds that Ellis Island is in New York Harbor, close to the big copper Statue of Liberty. Which one came first? And what else was happening in those years?

From 1877 to 1914, railways crossed the country, factories and cities grew, and millions of immigrants, mostly from Europe, came to work in them. Three events from those years each have a year to learn.

1882: Congress passed the Chinese Exclusion Act. It was the first major law that kept a group of people out of the country because of where they came from.

1886: the Statue of Liberty, a gift from France, was dedicated in New York Harbor. It became a symbol of welcome.

1892: Ellis Island opened in the same harbor. It was the federal station where arrivals were checked, and about twelve million people passed through it.

So for Noor, the statue came first, six years before Ellis Island. This course holds only these three dates from 1877 to 1900.

### 15. Hard times, and what was done about them

*Unit Ten · rev 5 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 2 · Card 15 of 65*

[reviewers only: card kind `concept`, id `con-hard`]

What the Depression was, and what the President did about it.

*Ines’s grandmother’s card*

> Ines is clearing out her grandmother’s desk. In a drawer she finds a small card with a number on it and the words ‘Social Security’. Her grandmother always said that card came out of the Depression. Ines knows the Depression was a hard time, but not what it was, what the government did about it, or who led that.

In 1929 the Great Depression began. Banks failed, and about one worker in four lost their job.

The President’s answer was a set of new programs called the New Deal, led by Franklin D. Roosevelt. Social Security, the program on Ines’s grandmother’s card, is the one to remember.

New programs needed new federal offices to run them, and an office of that kind is called an *agency*. What these offices did is **Carrying out the law**: putting laws into practice, at a scale the founders never saw.

### 26. The civil rights movement: a court, a law and many people

*Unit Ten · rev 5 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 2 · Card 26 of 65*

[reviewers only: card kind `concept`, id `con-civil`]

The movement, from 1954 to 1965, to end segregation.

*Amir’s daughter’s project*

> Amir’s daughter has a school project. Her teacher gave her a sentence to start from: ‘Once, Black and white children were kept in separate public schools. That ended, and it took a court, a law and the work of many people.’ She asks her father three things: which court, which law, and who the people were. Amir knows the answer is somewhere in the 1950s and 1960s, but he can’t name any of the three.

Amir’s daughter already has the three parts of the answer in her question: a court, a law and people. They are the three big parts of the civil rights movement. It pushed to end segregation (keeping people of different races apart, as in separate public schools for Black and white children) and discrimination (treating people worse because of their race).

The court: in 1954 the Supreme Court ruled, in Brown v. Board of Education, that separate public schools for Black and white children are unequal. A court checking a law against the Constitution is **Judicial review**.

The people: Martin Luther King Jr. and thousands of others led marches and boycotts. A boycott is when a group refuses to use or buy something, to press for a change.

The law: the Civil Rights Act of 1964, passed by Congress, outlaws segregation and discrimination.
