# Political Ideologies: The socialist family

Revision 1, draft (the screen carries the draft line). Part: The socialist family. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can read a post, a speech, a platform or a quote and name the ideology it speaks for, or say there is too little to tell, by answering the deciding questions, when someone argues a position or throws a label like "socialist" or "fascist".

## The why

The word "socialist" gets thrown at almost any plan with taxes in it. Most such plans are not.
In about fourteen minutes you will learn one test, who owns the businesses, and one more question, about elections. Together they tell six branches apart.

## Step 1: Socialism means who owns, not who taxes

Socialism means the community owns the businesses and farms, as the state or as the workers. If the owners stay private and the government taxes them to pay for services, that is social democracy, a welfare state, not socialism. "The government does a thing, so it is socialist" is the common mistake: every state builds roads. Check Question 1.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| “We will tax the banks and pay for free childcare. The banks stay private.” | “The banks will be taken into public ownership.” |

Compare: Only the second changes who owns.

## Step 2: Marxism, and Marxism–Leninism: the class or the party

Marxism explains more than it plans. Workers make more value than they are paid, the owner keeps the rest, and the system breeds its own crises. The end is no classes and no state. Marxism–Leninism adds a plan to seize and hold power: a small disciplined party rules for the workers, and the state takes the industries.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| “One class lives on what the other produces.” | “One party will govern for the workers until the new society is ready.” |

Compare: The first explains how one class lives on another. In the second the party acts.

## Step 3: Democratic socialism, and social democracy

Democratic socialism is real public ownership of the main industries, won through elections and kept inside the rules. Social democracy keeps private owners and limits them with welfare, workers' rights and taxes: never who owns the factory. Many call social democracy "socialism"; this course uses the ownership test.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| “We keep private firms. We tax the rich to pay for free college and higher pensions.” | “We will win the election and put the main industries into public ownership.” |

Compare: Both use elections. Question 1 splits them.

## Step 4: Anarchism, and market socialism

Anarchism is socialist economics with the state as an enemy too: workers' councils or unions own and run everything, with no state or party above them (through unions it is called syndicalism). Market socialism puts firms in social hands, owned by their own workers or by society, then lets them compete at real prices. Socially owned firms competing in markets are market socialism, not democratic socialism. Democratic socialism is public ownership of the main industries, won through elections.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| “Councils of workers will run the mills. No parliament, no minister and no party will give them orders.” | “Each firm belongs to its workers. Firms still compete for customers at market prices.” |

Compare: Both give the firms to the workers. Only the first throws out the state.

## Step 5: The telltale answers of this family, and what is not socialist

Marxism: class is the unit and class conflict the engine. Ownership may go unsaid, because Marxism is an analysis.
Marxism–Leninism: a party rules for the class, and the state owns the businesses.
Democratic socialism: social ownership, won through elections.
Social democracy: private ownership stays, with welfare and regulation.
Anarchism: worker ownership and no state.
Market socialism: worker-owned or socially owned firms competing in markets.
Not socialist: private owners, with fewer rules and lower taxes. Marxism needs only the class story, no plan. If the words never show a branch's answers, say too little to tell.

## Step 6: A worked example

“Our party will win a majority and bring the railways, the power grid and the big banks into public ownership. Every step will pass through parliament and the courts. If the voters turn us out, we will go.”
From an invented party platform.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**Question 1. Who would own the businesses and farms?**
- Private owners
- The state
- The workers, in councils or co-ops
- Private owners, directed by the state
- The words do not say

The working, one step at a time:
1. Question 1. Find who would own the businesses. The railways, the power grid and the banks go “into public ownership”: the state would own them. Question 1: the state. That is socialism, since the owners change.
1. Question 4. Find what is wanted for elections. The party will “win a majority”, every step “will pass through parliament and the courts”, and it will leave if the voters turn it out. It keeps elections and uses them. Question 4: keep them and widen them.
1. Check the telltale answers, then name the branch. Social ownership won through elections is democratic socialism's telltale. A party-run state would be Marxism–Leninism; private owners would be social democracy.

Then the answer: The state / Keep them and widen them / Democratic socialism. The big industries go into public ownership (Question 1: the state), won through votes and courts (Question 4: keep elections). Social ownership won by election is democratic socialism.
