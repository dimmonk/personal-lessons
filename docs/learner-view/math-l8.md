# Basic Math: Circles, boxes and volume

Revision 1, live. Part: Circles, boxes and volume. Where the subject ends up: Work out the number a situation needs (a total, a price per one, a scaled amount, a percent or the price before one, how much paint or soil to buy, how high a ladder reaches, what savings grow to or a loan costs) and tell whether it is sensible, when a receipt, a recipe, a sale tag, a room or an offer hands you some numbers and a question and nobody names the method.

## The why

Soil comes in bags of cubic feet, mulch by the cubic yard, and a round rug’s pad by the square foot. The sizes you measure are in feet and inches.
Here you find a circle’s area and a volume, change the units, and work out how much to buy.

## Step 1: Circles, then volume

A circle’s area is π × radius × radius. (The distance around a circle, π × the distance across, is a length, not its area.) The radius is half the distance across: a rug 8 ft across has a radius of 4 ft, so its area is 3.14 × 4 × 4 = 50.24 square feet. π is about 3.14; for a rough answer use 3.
Volume is how much space: the area of the bottom × the depth. A planter 6 ft by 2 ft filled 1 ft deep holds 6 × 2 × 1 = 12 cubic feet.
Use one unit. A depth in inches becomes feet ÷ 12: 10 inches is 0.83 ft. A cubic yard is 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft, which is 27 cubic feet, so cubic feet ÷ 27 gives cubic yards.
Then, as with paint, divide by what one bag holds and round up.

A plan drawn to one scale, each side written in ft: a circle, the distance across written 8 ft (Rug); 6 ft by 2 ft (Planter).

A wrong idea, marked wrong: “A cubic yard is 3 cubic feet.”
The right line: A yard is 3 feet long, but a cubic yard is 3 feet long, 3 wide and 3 high: 27 cubic feet.

## Step 2: A worked example

A plain table, "Cedar planter box":

> Inside  |  6 ft long, 2 ft wide
> Fill with soil  |  10 inches deep
> Potting soil  |  1.5 cubic feet a bag, $9.97

A plan drawn to one scale, each side written in ft: 6 ft by 2 ft (Soil, 10 in deep).

The working, one step at a time:
1. Say what is asked and what is given. Asked: the soil in cubic feet, then the bags. Given: 6 by 2 ft, 10 inches deep, and a bag’s size. The price does not matter here.
1. Make a rough answer in your head. 10 inches is about 0.8 ft. About 6 × 2 × 0.8 = 9.6 cubic feet.
1. Name the kind of problem and draw it. Volume is the area of the bottom × the depth, all in feet: 10 inches ÷ 12 = 0.83 ft.
1. Work it out exactly on the calculator, then look back. On the calculator: 6 × 2 × 10 ÷ 12 = 10 cubic feet. Look back: near the rough 9.6.
1. Work out how many to buy, and round up. 10 ÷ 1.5 = 6.67, so 7 bags: always round up.

Then the answer: 9.6 cubic feet / 10.00 cubic feet / 7. 6 × 2 × 10 ÷ 12 = 10 cubic feet; at 1.5 a bag, 6.67, so 7 bags: always round up.
