# Singing: Looking after your voice

Revision 1, live. Part: Looking after your voice. Where the subject ends up: Sing a tune you have just heard, or a line of a song you know, in tune and at a height that suits your voice, with high notes that come out light instead of shouted; and when a line goes wrong, say which of a few things went wrong and what to try, or that nothing did.

## The why

A voice gets tired and hoarse, like anything you use. Most of the time rest is all it needs. A few signs mean it needs a doctor, and you should know them before you need them.
If singing hurts, stop. Pain when you speak or swallow means a doctor right away.

## Step 1: Rest, and when to see a doctor

Hoarse means any change in how your voice sounds: rough, husky, airy, weak.
Most hoarseness gets better with rest. After a cold it usually goes in 7 to 10 days.
Resting means using your voice less and gently. Whispering is not rest: avoid it, along with shouting and clearing your throat over and over.
Not getting better after 7 to 10 days, whatever started it, or no cold to explain it: see a doctor. Sooner if you smoke.
By four weeks at the latest, an ear, nose and throat doctor should look at it.
Do not wait at all with any of these: coughing up blood, trouble swallowing or breathing, a lump in your neck, pain when you speak or swallow, or a voice you have lost, or that has changed so much it is hard to use, for more than a few days.
And see a doctor if your voice keeps you from singing, teaching or doing your work.

Sam’s voice is hoarse five days into a cold, getting a little better, with none of those signs: rest, and watch it. If it were no better after 10 days, he should see a doctor. If he had a lump in his neck on day two, right away.

## Step 2: Everyday habits

Warm up first: a gentle hum, or humming through a straw, makes the voice work more easily.
Drink water through the day. It is a cheap habit that may help a little, not a fix for a hoarse voice. For a dry throat, humid air helps.
Do not sing too long or too loudly at once. Short practice on most days does more than one long session.

## Step 3: A worked example

Sam had a cold, and five days on his voice is still hoarse, though a little better each day. Nothing hurts when he talks or swallows, he is breathing fine, and he has no lump in his neck.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**What should they do?**
- Rest your voice and give it time
- See a doctor about it
- See a doctor right away

The working, one step at a time:
1. Any warning sign? No pain, no blood, no lump, no trouble swallowing or breathing.
1. How long, and what started it? Five days, after a cold, and getting better: under the 7 to 10 days.

Then the answer: Rest your voice and give it time. No warning sign, five days after a cold and getting better: rest your voice and give it time. If it is not getting better after 7 to 10 days, see a doctor.
