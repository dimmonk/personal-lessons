# Scams & Social Engineering: When it pushes

Revision 1, live. Part: When it pushes. Where the subject ends up: When a message, call or offer asks you to act, you stop, see what it wants, and check it through a channel you already had before doing anything, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, you make the first calls at once.

## The why

A call or chat that pushes harder each time you hold back is built to keep you from thinking. So is a message that lands while you are busy.
You practice ending it, out loud, and deciding while doing something else.

## Step 1: When it pushes, stop

Hurry, fear, a threat, “tell no one”, “stay on the line”: each is a reason to stop, not to act. Honest callers give you time.
Your line: “I don’t do this on the phone. I’ll call back on the number I have.” Then hang up and call that number, if it uses the name of someone you deal with. If not, just hang up.
Holding back without ending it only brings the next push.
A call you placed yourself, to a number you already had, is different. It may hurry you too, and you stay with it: you started it.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: Hanging up is rude. I will hear them out.
The right line: Ending a call costs nothing. People who stop early mostly lose nothing.

## Step 2: A worked example

You bank with Calderwood. Its number is on your card.

An incoming call, the caller's words as a transcript: "Calderwood fraud team", Incoming call · 7:45 PM.

> Someone is draining your account as we speak.
> Move your savings to the safe account I give you, now. Don’t hang up, or I can’t protect you.
> And don’t tell anyone at the branch: one of them is in on it.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**What do you do?**
- Do what it says
- Check first, a way I already had
- Leave it

The working, one step at a time:
1. See what it wants. It wants your savings moved, now.
1. Notice the push. A loss happening now, an order to stay on the line, and to tell no one. Each is a reason to stop.
1. See whether you deal with them. It uses your bank’s name, and you have the number on your card.
1. Choose. Say “I’ll call back on the number I have,” hang up, and call the card’s number.

Then the answer: Check first, a way I already had / Hang up and call the number on my card. Hurry, fear, “don’t hang up” and “tell no one” are each a reason to stop. A safe account settles it, and it uses your bank’s name, so you hang up and call the card’s number.
