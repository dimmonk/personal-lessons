# Geography: The Northeast

Revision 1, live. Part: The Northeast. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A story takes place in New Hampshire, or someone commutes from New Jersey to New York. The Northeast is small and crowded, so its states are the easiest to mix up. In about fifteen minutes you will place its nine states, by finding their part first, name the ones most often mixed up and say what touches four of them.

## Step 1: How to find a state

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? What touches it? What water is it on? Here the area is the Northeast, in two parts: New England in the top right corner (Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut) and the Mid-Atlantic states below it (New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania). Find the part first, then the state. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water.

## Step 3: A worked example

Vermont


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of The United States with Vermont lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? Vermont is in New England, the top right corner. Start from the Hudson River, placed already.
1. Touches: what is next to it? Follow the Hudson up to its top; Vermont is the narrow state just east of there, along Canada.
1. Water: what water is it on? It has no coast. Tap inside it.

Then the answer: Vermont. Tell it from New Hampshire: Vermont is wide at the top and narrow at the bottom, with no coast.
