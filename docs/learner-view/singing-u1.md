## Part 1 of 3: Nothing wrong, the top notes, and the air

### 1. Before you blame your voice, say what bothered you

*Unit One · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 3 · Card 1 of 22*

[reviewers only: card kind `orient`, id `orient-first`]

Before you decide your voice is bad, say what actually bothered you about the line. It is always one of the five things below. Each has a different fix, and often nothing is wrong at all.

You sing along in the car and the chorus comes out wrong. Your first thought is “I can’t sing.” But “wrong” can mean five different things: a high note that turned into a shout, air that ran out, a note that was off, a sound you dislike, or nothing at all except that you are not the singer on the record.

Each one is fixed a different way, and one of them needs no fix. If you guess, you can work hard on the wrong thing, like singing quieter when the trouble was your breath. This unit teaches the one question to ask before you try anything.

What bothers you about it?
- How the top notes come out → the trouble starts where the notes go up
- How the air holds up → the air runs out, leaks out, is forced out, or is grabbed mid-word
- Whether you are on the note → you are not sure you are singing the song’s note
- How the words sound when they come out → the notes and the air are fine, and the sound of the words bothers you
- Only that it is not the record’s voice → nothing is wrong: your voice is just not the singer’s voice

The unit has three parts, and you can stop after any of them.

1. Nothing wrong, the top notes, and the air
2. The note, the sound, and two lines that show two things
3. Putting it together, then the drill

Each starts from a real story. After each one you answer a quick question, and the reason is shown right away. Nothing here is graded. A miss only decides what comes back.

### 2. Only that it is not the record’s voice

*Unit One · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 3 · Card 2 of 22*

[reviewers only: card kind `meet`, id `meet-fine`]

First, the one people forget: sometimes nothing is wrong at all.

*Marta and the old record*

> Marta, 68, sings along to her favorite old record while she cooks. ⟦Every note is where it should be, every word is clear, nothing in her throat is tight, and she has air to spare⟧. She still frowns: '⟦The woman on the record sounds so much richer than I do⟧.'

Marta’s voice is doing its job. What bothers her is a comparison with the woman on the record, and that singer has a different voice, a studio, and as many tries as she wanted. Nothing in Marta’s own singing is wrong, so there is nothing to fix.

People call this “bad singing” all the time. It is the first thing to rule out, before you change anything about how you sing.

**How to spot it**

1. **Check the notes: Marta hits every one.** If a note were off, the trouble would be about the note.
2. **Check for anything tight, sore or short of air: Marta’s throat is loose and she has air to spare.** Any of those would put the trouble in the top notes or the air.
3. **Check the words: every one of Marta’s is clear.** Words that are hard to follow would put the trouble in the sound.
4. **Find the complaint: Marta only wishes she sounded richer, like the woman on the record.** A complaint that is only a comparison with someone else’s voice means nothing is wrong.

This is **“Only that it is not the record’s voice”**. Marta’s voice needs no fix.

**What to do when you meet it**

1. **Leave your voice alone and sing it your way.** A different voice from the record’s is not a fault.
2. **If the song sits wrong for your voice, move it lower or higher.** Karaoke apps and backing tracks have a setting for it, usually called pitch or transpose.
3. **Next time something bothers you, say what it was before you change anything.** Most fixes for the other answers would only make Marta sing worse.

### 4. How the top notes come out

*Unit One · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 3 · Card 4 of 22*

[reviewers only: card kind `meet`, id `meet-high`]

Next, when the trouble starts where the notes go up.

*The flip at the open mic*

> At an open mic night, Dario sings a chorus that climbs to its last word. ⟦The low part of the line is steady⟧, and he has air to spare. Then ⟦on the high word his voice flips with a jolt into a thin, airy sound⟧.

Dario’s line was steady until it climbed. Then his voice flipped with a jolt. The top of a line is where voices often go wrong, in several ways: a shout, a flip like Dario’s, a clamped throat, or a note that is not there. A light, thin top note that feels weak to you belongs here too, because the top notes are what you are unsure of.

Each of these is fixed differently, and the light one needs no fix at all. For now, only notice that the trouble is at the top.

**How to spot it**

1. **Find the part of the line that climbs: Dario’s chorus climbs to its last word.** The trouble you describe has to start there.
2. **Check the low part: Dario’s is steady.** If the low part went wrong too, the trouble would be more than the top notes.
3. **Listen to what happens at the top: his voice flips into a thin, airy sound.** A shout, a clamp, a missing note and a light sound are each heard differently.

This is **“How the top notes come out”**. Dario’s low notes were fine.

**What to do when you meet it**

1. **Sing the line again at talking volume, and let the top go lighter even if it feels thin.** Getting louder to reach a note is a common way for a top note to go wrong.
2. **If the top is still tight, stop for the day.** Singing should never hurt.
3. **If the note is still not there when you sing it lightly, move the song lower.** Your voice is fine; the song sits higher than it can reach today.

### 6. How the air holds up

*Unit One · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 3 · Card 6 of 22*

[reviewers only: card kind `meet`, id `meet-breath`]

Next, when the air runs out, leaks out, is forced out, or is grabbed mid-word.

*The hymn with no room to breathe*

> At church, Walter sings a hymn with a long line. His notes are right, but he ⟦runs out of air before the end of the line, grabs a breath in the middle of a word⟧, and the line comes out in chopped pieces.

Walter’s notes are right, but his air does not last, and he grabs a breath where he should not. Singing is a steady stream of air turned into sound, so when the air goes wrong the line goes wrong. It can go wrong in four ways: a quick breath that runs out, air that leaks out with the sound, air that is shoved out hard, or breaths taken wherever you run out, like Walter’s.

Each has its own fix, and a breath that lasts needs none. For now, only notice that the air is the trouble.

**How to spot it**

1. **Listen for the air running out: Walter is out of air before the line ends.** A voice with air left at the end of the line is not the problem here.
2. **Listen for breaths in the middle of words: Walter grabs one mid-word.** A planned breath comes between words.
3. **Check the notes: Walter’s are right.** If a note were off, the trouble would be about the note, not the air.

This is **“How the air holds up”**. Walter’s voice is fine; his breaths are the trouble.

**What to do when you meet it**

1. **Mark in the lyrics where you will breathe: at a comma, at the end of a line, or before a long phrase.** A planned breath beats grabbing air where you run out.
2. **Put a hand on your belly and breathe in: it should move out while your shoulders stay still.** A low breath holds more air than a high one.
3. **Take a breath at a marked spot even before you need it.** An early breath is a quiet one, and it leaves air for the long part.

## Part 2 of 3: The note, the sound, and two lines that show two things

### 9. Whether you are on the note

*Unit One · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 2 of 3 · Card 9 of 22*

[reviewers only: card kind `meet`, id `meet-pitch`]

Next, when you are not sure you are singing the song’s note.

*The third note*

> Kenji is learning a song in the car to sing at his sister's wedding. The chorus is easy to reach and he has plenty of air, but ⟦its third note always sounds a shade off to him. He is not sure it is the song's note⟧, and he keeps sliding up into it to find out.

Kenji is not asking whether his top notes shout or his air lasts: both are fine. His doubt is about one note, whether it is the song’s note. “A shade off” can mean he is a little under it, a little over it, sliding up into it, or hunting for it. It can also mean the note is right and only sounds odd to him.

Each of those is fixed differently, and the last one needs no fix. For now, only notice that the doubt is about the note itself.

**How to spot it**

1. **Find the doubt: Kenji is not sure the third note is the song’s note.** The doubt is about one note, not about the whole line.
2. **Check the top notes and the air: the chorus is easy to reach and Kenji has plenty of breath.** If either were the trouble, that would be the answer instead.
3. **Notice how it is off: a shade, with a slide up into the note.** A small miss is a problem with the note, and a shout or a gap is not.

This is **“Whether you are on the note”**. Kenji’s voice is working; one note is in doubt.

**What to do when you meet it**

1. **Play the song’s note on a piano app, or pause the recording on it, and hold it.** You need the real note to compare with.
2. **Sing your note next to it and listen: is yours lower, higher or the same?** Your ear can tell which way you are off when the two notes sit side by side.
3. **Before you sing the next note, hear it in your head.** A note you can already hear is much easier to land on.

### 15. How the words sound when they come out

*Unit One · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 2 of 3 · Card 15 of 22*

[reviewers only: card kind `meet`, id `meet-tone`]

Next, when the notes and the air are fine, and the sound of the words bothers you.

*The dull sound*

> In the choir, Owen ⟦sings with his mouth barely open⟧. His notes are right and he has air to spare, but ⟦the sound is dull and stays back in his throat, and the singers next to him cannot make out his words⟧.

Owen’s notes are right and his air lasts. What is wrong is the sound that comes out: dull, stuck in his throat, with words his neighbors cannot follow. The sound of a word is shaped after the voice makes it, by the mouth, the tongue and the lips, so a mouth that stays shut gives a dull sound.

The same answer covers a pinched sound, words that run together, and a voice that only sounds strange on a recording. Each is fixed differently, and the recording one needs no fix.

**How to spot it**

1. **Check the notes and the air first: Owen’s are right.** Then the trouble is not the note or the air.
2. **Listen to the sound: Owen’s is dull and stays back in his throat.** A pinched sound, run-together words and a strange recorded voice are each heard differently.
3. **Look at the mouth: Owen’s barely opens.** A mouth that stays shut is a common reason for a dull sound.

This is **“How the words sound when they come out”**. Owen’s notes and air are fine.

**What to do when you meet it**

1. **Look in a mirror and open your mouth about two fingers wide on “ah”.** A mouth that opens gives a sound that carries.
2. **Hold your nose shut and sing “ah”: if the sound changes a lot, too much of it is going through your nose.** That tells you whether the sound is pinched.
3. **Record yourself and ask whether a listener could write down the words.** A recording is the only way to hear it from outside.

### 18. The one question to ask first

*Unit One · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 3 of 3 · Card 18 of 22*

[reviewers only: card kind `question`, id `q-first`]

Here is the question and its five answers in one place.

**The question:** **“What bothers you about it?”**

**Its answers**

- **“How the top notes come out”**
  - Give this answer when the trouble starts where the notes go up: at the top of the line the voice shouts, flips, clamps, cannot reach the note, or goes light in a way you are not sure of.
- **“How the air holds up”**
  - Give this answer when the trouble is with the air: it is gone before the line ends, grabbed in the middle of a word, heard leaking out with the sound, or driven out hard, or you suspect the way you breathe.
- **“Whether you are on the note”**
  - Give this answer when what you doubt is the note itself: whether the note you sing is the song’s note, because it sounds a shade off, you slide into it, you hunt for it, or you simply are not sure.
- **“How the words sound when they come out”**
  - Give this answer when the notes and the air are fine, and what bothers you is the sound: pinched and nasal, dull and muffled, words that run together, or a voice that only sounds strange recorded.
- **“Only that it is not the record’s voice”**
  - Give this answer when the notes are right, nothing is tight, sore or short of air, the words are clear, and the only complaint is that it does not sound like the singer on the record.

**Why it matters**

Each of the four goes wrong in a different place and has a different fix, so this answer decides what to ask next. It says where to look, not that something is wrong: in each of the four, the voice may turn out to be doing fine. When a line shows two of them, give the one higher in the list, because fixing it usually fixes the other.

Skip this question and you fix the wrong thing: you sing quieter when the trouble was your breath, or you practice notes that were already right. Each answer sends you to a different fix, and one of them needs no fix at all.

**How to answer it**

1. **Play the line again, or say aloud what happened.** Start from what you heard, not from what you think of your voice.
2. **First look for “How the top notes come out”.** Fixing the top usually fixes the others.
3. **Next look for “How the air holds up”.** Air that gives out is a common reason a note sags.
4. **Next look for “Whether you are on the note”.** Only when the top and the air are fine does a note need work.
5. **Next look for “How the words sound when they come out”.** This one stands alone: it is the sound once the notes and the air are fine.
6. **If none of those four fits, it is “Only that it is not the record’s voice”.** Then nothing is wrong, and nothing needs a fix.
7. **Say the exact words that show your answer.** If you cannot find them, you do not have an answer yet.

**When two answers both seem to fit**

Some lines show two things at once: a top note that goes flat because it is shouted, or a line that goes flat at the end because the air gave out. Each line gets one answer, and the one higher in the list wins. The test for each pair is below.

- Whether you are on the note or Only that it is not the record’s voice: Is a note in doubt? Or are the notes right, and I only wish I sounded like someone else?
- How the top notes come out or How the air holds up: What goes wrong first: my voice as the line climbs, or my air?
- How the air holds up or Whether you are on the note: Is a note off from the start of the line, or only once the air begins to run out?
- Whether you are on the note or How the words sound when they come out: Do I doubt a note? Or am I sure of the notes, and it is the sound I dislike?
- How the top notes come out or Whether you are on the note: Did my voice shout, flip, clamp or fail at the top of the line? Or is the note the only thing in doubt?
- Only that it is not the record’s voice or How the top notes come out: Does something happen at the top of the line that I would want to fix? Or is the only complaint that I am not the singer?
- Only that it is not the record’s voice or How the air holds up: Is the air short, noisy or grabbed anywhere in the line? Or does it last, with nothing to fix?
- Only that it is not the record’s voice or How the words sound when they come out: Can I name something wrong with the sound itself? Or is it only that it is not the singer’s sound?
- How the top notes come out or How the words sound when they come out: Does the sound go wrong only at the top of the line? Or is it that way from the first note?
- How the air holds up or How the words sound when they come out: Can I hear air escaping or running short? Or is there plenty of air and the sound is still pinched, dull or blurred?

### 21. What to carry away

*Unit One · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 3 of 3 · Card 21 of 22*

[reviewers only: card kind `recap`, id `recap-first`]

You have now answered the first question on your own.

**This unit’s questions and answers**

What bothers you about it?
- How the top notes come out
- How the air holds up
- Whether you are on the note
- How the words sound when they come out
- Only that it is not the record’s voice

**For each name: what to look for, and the question to ask when you spot it**

- **“How the top notes come out”**: a line that climbs, and at the top something you noticed: a shout, a flip, a tight throat, a note that is not there, or a lighter voice you are unsure of.
- **“How the air holds up”**: air that gives out before the line ends or is grabbed in the middle of a word, air you can hear leaking with the sound, air driven out hard, or a breath you suspect is wrong.
- **“Whether you are on the note”**: a note you are not sure is the song’s note: it sounds a shade off, you slide into it, you hunt for it, or you doubt it without knowing why.
- **“How the words sound when they come out”**: notes and air that are fine, and a sound you dislike: pinched and nasal, dull and muffled, words that run together, or a voice that only sounds strange on a recording.
- **“Only that it is not the record’s voice”**: the right notes, nothing tight, sore or short of air, words that are clear, and a complaint only that it does not sound like the singer on the record.

**To carry away**

- Before you decide your voice is bad, say what bothered you, and find the exact words that show it. Each answer has a different fix.
- None of the four is a verdict. In each, the voice may turn out to be doing fine, and **“Only that it is not the record’s voice”** is a real answer that you will need as often as the other four.
- A light, thin top note that feels weak, and your own voice on a recording, are two common false alarms. Check them before you fix anything.
- A top note that goes flat because it is shouted is about the top notes. A line that goes flat at the end because the air gave out is about the air.
- When a line shows two things, the one higher in the list wins: the top notes, then the air, then the note itself.
