# Psychology: Moves made on someone

Revision 2, draft (the screen carries the draft line). Part: Moves made on someone. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can hear a story about someone, or catch one in your own head, and say what is going on, whether it is one moment or a lasting pattern, the name that fits or that there is too little to tell, and what to do about it, when someone reaches for "narcissist", "gaslighting" or "toxic".

## The why

Your sister says her ex "gaslit" her. A coworker calls a hard question "DARVO". Sometimes they are right; often it was one bad moment.
In about 19 minutes: five moves people make, the ordinary twin of each, and what to do.

## Step 1: The questions, for moves

Five questions, name last: 1 what is going on, 2 one moment or over and over, 3 what it does for them, 4 the nearest look-alike, 5 what would prove you wrong. A move is named by its whole sequence and what it does, never by one uncomfortable moment.
"Ordinary": the facts show a normal reason (a hard time, a fair point, a slip put right); nothing to fix. "Too little to tell": too few facts to see anything; find out more first.

## Step 2: Defenses

Denial (refusing a fact), projection (seeing your own feeling in others), rationalization (a reason made up afterward), displacement (a feeling sent to a safer target), intellectualization (talking in facts, not feelings).
Question 3, what it does: a defense shields a person from their own pain; it is not aimed at you. In a hard time it is ordinary.

## Step 3: Gaslighting

Gaslighting: over and over, for a long time, a person's memory or senses denied until they start doubting themselves. Response: trust your record; write things down, check with someone outside.
It escalates, often across a power gap: a boss, a parent.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| “I never said that.” You show the text. “Huh. You're right.” | “I never said that, you always get confused.” A year of it, and you now doubt yourself first. |

Compare: Only the second repeats and plants the doubt.

## Step 4: DARVO, and its near cousin

DARVO: in one exchange, all three: deny the act, attack the person raising it, and recast the accuser as the one at fault. Response: bring it back to the one fact, calmly. Do not take the bait.
Attack and change the subject: caught out, they bring up something else the accuser did, without the full flip of victim and offender. Response: bring it back to the one fact, calmly.
The story must show it happened; one part alone is not DARVO.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| Your bike came back flat, his helmet on it. “I never touched it. You always blame me. I'm the one being attacked here.” | The same bike: “I never touched it. Anyway, you lost my charger.” |

Compare: Only the first flips who is the victim.

## Step 5: Love-bombing

Love-bombing, then the cold turn: fast, intense devotion far beyond how well they know you, then criticism or coldness once you depend on them. Response: slow down, and keep your own friends and plans.
Speed alone is common; without the cold turn, too little to tell.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| On the third date he says she is "the one". When she keeps a night with friends, he goes quiet for days. | After a month she is crazy about him; he wants to go slower. Both keep their friends. |

Compare: Only the first outruns what he could know, then turns cold. The second is ordinary.

## Step 6: Projection, as a move

Projection: they accuse someone else of the very feeling or impulse they show themselves. Response: stay with the one fact, calmly. Do not argue the feeling.
Both must show: their own act, and no fact behind it.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| He has been late to every family dinner this month. He tells his sister, always on time, "You never respect anyone's time." | His sister has been late to every dinner this month. He says the same. |

Compare: Only the second has a record behind it.

## Step 7: When it is not safe

Safety comes before naming. If the story shows fear of the other person, or control (a phone read, friends picked, a door blocked), get outside help: a therapist, or in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline, 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788. Wanting more time together is a preference, not control.

## Step 8: The trap: everything becomes manipulation

What is wrong with each claim?
"He disagreed with me, that's gaslighting": one disagreement, no repeat, no doubt.
"That's literally DARVO", about a calm explanation: no false denial, no attack, no flip.
"He love-bombed me", about two warm years: the warmth fit how well he knew her, and never turned cold.

## Step 9: A worked example

For three years, whenever she brings up a plan they made, the trip, the loan to his brother, her husband says they never talked about it, and that she "invents things". She now begins every talk with "I might be wrong, but".

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**Question 1. What is going on here?**
- A mind talking itself into something
- A move made on someone
- A lasting way someone is
- An ordinary reaction or slip: none of these

The working, one step at a time:
1. Question 1. What is going on? His words are aimed at her and change what she believes about her own memory: a move made on someone, not a mind talking itself into something.
1. Question 2. One moment, or over and over? "For three years", "whenever she brings up a plan": over and over, for months or years.
1. Questions 3 and 4. What does it do, and what is the nearest look-alike? It does one job: she stops trusting her own memory. The look-alike is an ordinary disagreement, which ends when someone checks and leaves nobody doubting themselves. All three signs are here: the talks denied, over and over, and the doubt. Gaslighting.
1. What to do, and Question 5. What would prove it wrong? Her memory is what is attacked, so the answer is a record: write things down as they happen, and check with someone outside. She shows no fear of him, so the move's own response fits. The read would be wrong if her records often prove him right.

Then the answer: A move made on someone / Over and over, for months or years / Gaslighting / Trust your record: write things down and check with someone outside. Denying the talks themselves, for three years, until she doubts herself before she speaks: gaslighting. A record is the answer.
