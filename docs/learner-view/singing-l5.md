# Singing: Steps and leaps

Revision 1, live. Part: Steps and leaps. Where the subject ends up: Sing a tune you have just heard, or a line of a song you know, in tune and at a height that suits your voice, with high notes that come out light instead of shouted; and when a line goes wrong, say which of a few things went wrong and what to try, or that nothing did.

## The why

A tune is notes and the gaps between them. Singers who struggle to sing in tune tend to make big jumps too small: they fall short of the far note.
When you sing along with a recording, both notes have to match the recording’s. So here each note is checked against its own note, and both have to be on.

## Step 1: Warm-up

A hum; a line on the screen follows your voice. It is not scored and can be skipped.

#### Try 1: Warm-up (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Warm up your voice. Hum a slow slide up and down, or hum through a straw. Keep it easy and quiet. There is nothing to match.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 8 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- It is not scored, stored or counted, and there is a "Skip the warm-up" link. It ends with "Warm-up done".
- Stored: nothing.

Then the next step: a warm-up has no break screen.

## Step 2: Hear the gap first

**Hear it**

A step is a small move between two notes, a jump a big one. In the third sound, the far note stops a shade short of where it should be.

Buttons, one for each sound; nothing sounds until one is tapped. A tap plays it (the line under them reads "Playing. Tap to stop."), a second tap stops it, and moving on stops it:
- "A small step"
- "A big jump"
- "A jump that falls short"

Before you sing, hear both notes in your head and notice whether it is a small step or a big jump: the first groups tell you which. On a big jump, aim for the far note on purpose.

Checked against the song, two notes can go five ways:
Both notes on.
Fell short on the jump: the first note on, the far note short of it. That is under after a jump up, and over after a jump down. It is the usual miss of singers who struggle with pitch.
Went past on the jump: the first note on, the far note beyond it. That is over after a jump up, and under after a jump down.
First note off, far note on: the jump started from the wrong place and still landed.
Both notes off the same way: the jump was the right size but started from the wrong place.
Whenever the first note is off, fix it first, and the jump follows.

## Step 3: A worked example

The chorus of Max’s song opens with a big jump up. He checks each note against a piano app. The first note matches. The top note sounds a little lower than the piano’s, and he has to move up to reach it.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**What happened on the jump?**
- Both notes on
- Fell short on the jump
- Went past on the jump
- First note off, far note on
- Both notes off the same way

The working, one step at a time:
1. Check the first note. It matches the piano, so the jump starts from the right place.
1. Check the far note. It is a little under after a jump up: the jump was too small.

Then the answer: Fell short on the jump. First note on, far note short of it: he fell short on the jump. Hear both notes in your head first and aim for the far one on purpose.

## Step 6: Tries

4 tries, listed, with help (the line and the notes drawn while singing). A missed try comes back at least three tries later, with a new note, until it is on the note.

When no range is stored yet, this group opens with the range exercise, and the group follows it:

#### First: Find your range (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Find your range. You will hum a slide up, then a slide down. Keep it easy: stop at the notes that still feel comfortable.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 7 seconds + 7 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- The two windows say: "Slide up to the highest note that is easy, and hold it." then "Now slide down to the lowest note that is easy, and hold it." The lowest and highest steady notes are saved as the learner's range, once. If the two ends are too close together: "I could not find two different easy notes. Slide further each way and hold each end for a moment, then try again." and nothing is saved.
- After the try: "Your range is saved". Reason: Every note you practice sits inside the range you just sang, so none is too high or too low.
- Nothing heard: "I did not hear you. Check that the microphone is on, then try again." The try is not counted and can be made again.
- Stored: numbers only (how much range was found); no sound is recorded or sent anywhere.

#### Try 10: Sing both notes (a sung question; the notes are made fresh from the learner's range, inside it)

**Listen to the two notes, a small step apart, then sing them back one after the other on “oo”.**

- While singing: an empty strip with the words "No line this time. Listen, sing, and see how you did afterwards.".
- After the try, in words, one line for each note: On the note, A shade under, A shade over, Well under, Well over. The strip then shows the note and the voice, with or without the line. Reason: Hear both notes in your head first. On a big jump, people who struggle to sing in tune most often fall short of the far note.
- After a miss, a line for each note that was off says which way to move and how far, for example "Your voice sat a shade under the note: sing a little higher, less than a half-step." or "Your voice sat well over the note, about two half-steps: sing that much lower.".
- Nothing heard: "I did not hear you. Check that the microphone is on, then try again." The try is not counted and can be made again. A miss comes back three tries later with a new note.
- Stored: numbers only (how far off each note was, in whole hundredths of a half-step); no sound is recorded or sent anywhere.

Check tries 35 to 38: the same question, each with a new note drawn from the range.

## The result

A line such as "10 of 10: passed" or "8 of 10: not yet" (all questions and tries together), then each rule above shown as met or not met. The answer to each was shown as it was given.
