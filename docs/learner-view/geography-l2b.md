# Geography: The big rivers

Revision 1, live. Part: The big rivers. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A barge is stuck on the Mississippi, or the Colorado is running dry. A river is named in the news more often than most states, and it runs through several. In about thirteen minutes you will tap ten big rivers, each found from where it ends, and name the four most often mixed up.

## Step 1: How to find a river

A river runs through many places, so you find it from where it ends. Area: which region does it run through? Touches: which bigger river or sea does it run into? Water: is that the Gulf of Mexico, the Atlantic or the Pacific? Find where it ends, then follow it back. A river shares its name with a state now and then (the Mississippi, the Ohio): here it is the river you tap, the line on the map, not the state. Next, the rivers on three screens; none of it stays up.

## Step 2: Three rivers of the middle

The Mississippi runs down the middle to the Gulf; the Missouri comes in from the northwest and the Ohio from the east.

## Step 4: Four rivers of the West

The Columbia ends at the Pacific in the northwest, and the Snake runs into it; the Colorado runs southwest to Mexico; the Rio Grande runs along the Mexican border to the Gulf of Mexico.

A map of The United States, north up, with no names on it. Lit: Colorado River, Columbia River, Snake River, Rio Grande River. The learner can pinch to zoom, drag to move it and use the buttons to zoom in, zoom out and show the whole map.

## Step 5: Tell them apart

Tell apart: The Missouri and the Mississippi meet at St. Louis: the Mississippi runs straight down the middle to the Gulf; the Missouri comes in from the northwest, across the Plains.

Tell apart: The Ohio and the Tennessee: the Ohio is the long river that runs west from the Northeast to the Mississippi; the Tennessee is the shorter one below it that loops south and then back north into the Ohio.

Tell apart: The Snake and the Columbia: the Columbia is the big one that ends at the Pacific; the Snake is the one that bends across from the east and runs into it.

## Step 6: A worked example

Mississippi River


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of The United States with Mississippi River lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? The Mississippi runs through the middle of the country, from the Midwest down through the South.
1. Touches: what is next to it? It starts in Minnesota at the top, by the Canadian border, and runs straight down.
1. Water: what water is it on? It ends in the Gulf of Mexico at the bottom. Tap its line in the middle of the map.

Then the answer: Mississippi River. The Mississippi runs straight down the middle of the country, from near the Canadian border to the Gulf of Mexico.

## Step 7: A worked example

Ohio River


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of The United States with Ohio River lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? The Ohio runs through the middle of the country, along the bottom of the Midwest. Find where it ends first.
1. Touches: what is next to it? It ends in the Mississippi, partway down: the first big river that comes in from the east, below where the Missouri comes in from the west.
1. Water: what water is it on? Its water is the Mississippi it runs into, and through it the Gulf of Mexico. From there follow the Ohio back up, east and a little north, toward the Northeast, and tap that line.

Then the answer: Ohio River. The Ohio runs west from the Northeast, along the bottom of the Midwest, and runs into the Mississippi.
