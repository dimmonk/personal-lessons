# Singing: Match a note

Revision 2, live. Part: Match a note. Where the subject ends up: Sing a tune you have just heard, or a line of a song you know, in tune and at a height that suits your voice, with high notes that come out light instead of shouted; and when a line goes wrong, say which of a few things went wrong and what to try, or that nothing did.

## The why

Most trouble singing in tune is not the ear. It is the link from the note you hear to the note your voice makes, and copying notes you have just heard is the way to practice that link.
Hear the note in your head before you sing it. At first a line follows your voice, so you can watch yourself land on the note. Then the line goes away, and you say whether you were under, on or over before you see the result. That is how you learn to judge your own voice.

## Step 1: Warm-up

A hum; a line on the screen follows your voice. It is not scored and can be skipped.

#### Try 1: Warm-up (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Warm up your voice. Hum a slow slide up and down, or hum through a straw. Keep it easy and quiet. There is nothing to match.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 8 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- It is not scored, stored or counted, and there is a "Skip the warm-up" link. It ends with "Warm-up done".
- Stored: nothing.

Then the next step: a warm-up has no break screen.

## Step 2: How a try goes

Each try has three steps:
1. Tap Hear the note and listen to all of it.
2. Hear it again in your head, silently, for a moment, until you could hum it back.
3. Sing it on “oo” (as in “moon”) as soon as the note stops and the screen says Sing now. Aim at the note itself from the first moment, and keep going until the screen moves on. A voice that slides into the note or wanders before it settles is named for it, and does not count as on.

How far off you were is told in half-steps. A half-step is the smallest step between two notes of a tune: the two notes that take turns at the start of the Jaws theme are a half-step apart, as are two notes right next to each other on a piano, counting the black ones. On the note here means within half of a half-step. A shade under or over is off by more than that but less than a whole half-step; well under or over is off by more.

**Hear it**

The two notes of the Jaws theme, a half-step apart. That is the size of the step the results talk about.

Buttons, one for each sound; nothing sounds until one is tapped. A tap plays it (the line under them reads "Playing. Tap to stop."), a second tap stops it, and moving on stops it:
- "A half-step: the Jaws theme"

The line is your voice. The bar is the note. Aim to put the line on the bar. If you miss, the result says which way you were off and about how far. Notice it, hear the next note in your head, and aim for it.

In the second group there is no line. When you finish, say whether you were under, on or over, then see what the app measured. Every note sits inside your own range.

## Step 3: Tries

8 tries, listed, with help (the line and the notes drawn while singing). A missed try comes back at least three tries later, with a new note, until it is on the note.

When no range is stored yet, this group opens with the range exercise, and the group follows it:

#### First: Find your range (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Find your range. You will hum a slide up, then a slide down. Keep it easy: stop at the notes that still feel comfortable.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 7 seconds + 7 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- The two windows say: "Slide up to the highest note that is easy, and hold it." then "Now slide down to the lowest note that is easy, and hold it." The lowest and highest steady notes are saved as the learner's range, once. If the two ends are too close together: "I could not find two different easy notes. Slide further each way and hold each end for a moment, then try again." and nothing is saved.
- After the try: "Your range is saved". Reason: Every note you practice sits inside the range you just sang, so none is too high or too low.
- Nothing heard: "I did not hear you. Check that the microphone is on, then try again." The try is not counted and can be made again.
- Stored: numbers only (how much range was found); no sound is recorded or sent anywhere.

#### Try 2: Match the note (a sung question; the notes are made fresh from the learner's range, inside it)

**Listen to the note, then sing it back on “oo”.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Hear the note". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), the app plays the note in a soft hum-like tone, not a voice (1.5 seconds), ignoring the microphone while it plays and for a quarter of a second after, then the learner sings in a window of 1.9 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the note as a bar and the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- After the try, in words: On the note, A shade under, A shade over, Well under, Well over, Sliding up into it, Sliding down onto it, Hunting for the note. The strip then shows the note and the voice, with or without the line. Reason: Hear the note in your head before you sing it. Your voice finds it faster when you know the sound you are aiming for.
- After a miss, a line for each note that was off says which way to move and how far, for example "Your voice sat a shade under the note: sing a little higher, less than a half-step." or "Your voice sat well over the note, about two half-steps: sing that much lower."; a note that slid onto the note says "Your voice started under the note and slid up into it. Aim at the note itself, so you are on it from the start.", and one that wandered up and down says "Your voice wandered up and down before it settled. Hear the note in your head before you sing; if it still wanders, slow down.".
- Nothing heard: "I did not hear you. Check that the microphone is on, then try again." The try is not counted and can be made again. A miss comes back three tries later with a new note.
- Stored: numbers only (how far off each note was, in whole hundredths of a half-step); no sound is recorded or sent anywhere.

Tries 3 to 9: the same question, each with a new note drawn from the range.

Then a screen: you can stop here, your place is kept.

Your note was higher than it sounded to you."

## Step 5: Away from the phone

Play one note of a song you know on a piano app. Hear it in your head, then sing it.
