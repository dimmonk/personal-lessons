# Singing: Check before you fix: on, under, over, scooping, guessing

Revision 1, live. Part: Check before you fix: on, under, over, scooping, guessing. Where the subject ends up: Sing a tune you have just heard, or a line of a song you know, in tune and at a height that suits your voice, with high notes that come out light instead of shouted; and when a line goes wrong, say which of a few things went wrong and what to try, or that nothing did.

## The why

A note that sounds wrong to you can be fine. And a note that really is off can be off in different ways, each with its own fix.
So check before you fix: hold the song’s note steady, sing yours beside it, and notice which way you have to move.

## Step 1: Warm-up

A hum; a line on the screen follows your voice. It is not scored and can be skipped.

#### Try 1: Warm-up (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Warm up your voice. Hum a slow slide up and down, or hum through a straw. Keep it easy and quiet. There is nothing to match.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 8 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- It is not scored, stored or counted, and there is a "Skip the warm-up" link. It ends with "Warm-up done".
- Stored: nothing.

Then the next step: a warm-up has no break screen.

## Step 2: The note check

Rosa’s sister says a line of hers sounded low. Rosa plays the line’s first note on a piano app and holds it, then sings her own note beside it. Hers sounds lower, so she moves her voice up until the two sound like one. Because she had to move up, she knows she was under.

That is the note check: a steady note (a piano app, or a long note of the recording while it plays), your note beside it, and which way you have to move. If you do not have to move at all, the note was fine. A reaction, yours or a friend’s, can be right or wrong: the check decides.

## Step 3: On, under, over

**Hear it**

Each sound plays the song’s note and your note together. When they match they sound like one. When yours is a shade off you hear a fast flutter or roughness, which slows and fades as yours moves onto the note.

Buttons, one for each sound; nothing sounds until one is tapped. A tap plays it (the line under them reads "Playing. Tap to stop."), a second tap stops it, and moving on stops it:
- "On the note"
- "A shade under, then moving up"
- "A shade over, then moving down"

A shade under stays a little low the whole way, less than a half-step, and you move up to match. Any note sung under is called flat, and any note over is called sharp. The fix for both is the same: check against a steady note and move to it.

## Step 4: Sliding in, and hunting

**Hear it**

Listen to how each note starts. The first slides up from below and arrives late, the second lands right on the note, and the third wanders up and down until it finds it.

Buttons, one for each sound; nothing sounds until one is tapped. A tap plays it (the line under them reads "Playing. Tap to stop."), a second tap stops it, and moving on stops it:
- "Sliding up into it"
- "Landing on it"
- "Hunting for the note"

| A | B |
|---|---|
| A shade under: low from start to end, until you move it. | Sliding up into it: starts low, then arrives on the note by itself within a moment, late. |

Compare: Both start low. Only one stays there. Aim at the note itself, so you are on it from the start.

A note can also start above and slide down onto it: sliding down onto it, the same fault from above. Hunting often means the note was not in your head before you sang. The fix is the second check: hear it first. If it still wanders, slow down.

Next, a singer whose chorus sounded wrong to him and to his daughter. Watch what decides it: the check, not the reaction.

## Step 5: A worked example

On the drive home Hector sings along with a song, and the chorus sounds wrong to him. His daughter says, “Dad, that one sounded funny.” At home he plays the chorus’s first note on a piano app and holds it, then sings his own note beside it. The two sound like one from the start, and he does not have to move at all.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**Where did the note land?**
- On the note
- A shade under
- A shade over
- Sliding up into it
- Hunting for the note

The working, one step at a time:
1. Put the reaction aside. It sounded wrong to him and funny to his daughter. That might be right or wrong; only the check can say.
1. Hold the song’s note steady. He played the chorus’s first note on a piano app, so he had a steady note to sing against.
1. Sing yours beside it and notice which way you move. The two sounded like one from the start, and he did not move at all.

Then the answer: On the note. His note and the song’s note sounded like one with nothing to move: on the note. This time the reaction was wrong, so he leaves it alone.

## Step 7: Now land on it

Now you match single notes. Aim at the note itself, so you are on it from the first moment. The app listens to how each note starts and whether it wanders: a voice that slides into the note, or hunts for it, is named for it, and does not count as on. First with the line, then without it, saying where you landed before you see it.

## Step 8: Tries

4 tries, listed, with help (the line and the notes drawn while singing). A missed try comes back at least three tries later, with a new note, until it is on the note.

When no range is stored yet, this group opens with the range exercise, and the group follows it:

#### First: Find your range (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Find your range. You will hum a slide up, then a slide down. Keep it easy: stop at the notes that still feel comfortable.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 7 seconds + 7 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- The two windows say: "Slide up to the highest note that is easy, and hold it." then "Now slide down to the lowest note that is easy, and hold it." The lowest and highest steady notes are saved as the learner's range, once. If the two ends are too close together: "I could not find two different easy notes. Slide further each way and hold each end for a moment, then try again." and nothing is saved.
- After the try: "Your range is saved". Reason: Every note you practice sits inside the range you just sang, so none is too high or too low.
- Nothing heard: "I did not hear you. Check that the microphone is on, then try again." The try is not counted and can be made again.
- Stored: numbers only (how much range was found); no sound is recorded or sent anywhere.

#### Try 8: Match the note (a sung question; the notes are made fresh from the learner's range, inside it)

**Listen to the note, then sing it back on “oo”.**
