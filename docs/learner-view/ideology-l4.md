# Political Ideologies: Fascism and Nazism

Revision 1, draft (the screen carries the draft line). Part: Fascism and Nazism. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can read a post, a speech, a platform or a quote and name the ideology it speaks for, or say there is too little to tell, by answering the deciding questions, when someone argues a position or throws a label like "socialist" or "fascist".

## The why

Fascism is the most abused word on the list. It is thrown at anyone harsh, and it also fits some things exactly.
In about fourteen minutes you will learn what it means, the marks to look for together, and what it is told apart from: Nazism, an ordinary dictatorship, reaction and conservatism.

## Step 1: What fascism is, and the cluster of marks

Fascism is a mass movement against liberals and against Marxists. Its core story: the nation has decayed or been humiliated, and will be reborn through unity, discipline and often violence. A leader is said to be the people's will, and each person serves the nation as one body.
No one mark decides, so look for several together:
1. A lost golden age and a nation to be reborn.
2. An enemy both weak and about to win.
3. Praise of action and violence.
4. A leader who is the people's will, with elections for show.
5. Mass mobilization.
6. Class struggle thrown out for national unity.
7. Corporatism: employers and workers organized into bodies the state recognizes. Its strong form, the state directing owners and ending free unions, is fascism's (national populism may too).
8. War as proof of strength.
It is a family resemblance, not a checklist.

## Step 2: Marks and methods: what any dictatorship does

A plain dictator wants power and quiet. Censorship, banned parties, torture, repression and a bare leader cult are methods any dictatorship uses.
Fascism's own marks separate it from that: a leader who claims to BE the nation's will, so parliaments are theater; compulsory state bodies replacing free unions while owners keep their firms (corporatism); mass mobilization, such as a uniformed youth movement; and a doctrine of purifying struggle.
Mobilization never separates fascism from Leninism, which mobilizes too. Violence alone is a method; violence praised as purifying the nation is a mark.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| “Stay home, stay quiet, and the army will keep order.” | “Every child into the youth movement, to serve the reborn nation.” |

Compare: The first wants quiet, as any dictator might. The second demands that everyone join in: a mark of fascism.

## Step 3: Fascism, Nazism, reaction and conservatism

Nazism is fascism with biological race, a natural rank, in place of the nation-state as the unit, with hatred of Jews at its center and killing whole peoples as policy.
A reactionary wants back an old order that is gone. A conservative keeps what still stands and changes it slowly. A fascist wants something new, made through national rebirth, and will smash the old elites.
Fascism: the nation, its decline and rebirth, and a leader who replaces elections.
Nazism: race, as a natural rank, is the unit.
Conservatism: inherited institutions kept, and changed gradually.
Reaction: an old order that is gone, brought back.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| “The crown and the church must return. We seek restoration, not revolution.” | “Sweep the old lords away. We will forge a new people out of the ruin.” |

Compare: The first brings back the old order: reaction. The second breaks it to make something new: fascism.

## Step 4: "The Nazis were socialists": Question 1 and Question 2 settle it

Some say "The Nazis were socialists" because the party's name had "Socialist" in it. That claim is wrong, and the questions show it. Question 1: ownership stayed private, and the regime directed private industry, banned free unions and destroyed workers' parties. Question 2: the unit was race, built against class, and Marxism was the declared enemy. A party's name is marketing; the answers decide.

## Step 5: A worked example

“Our nation was great once. Weak parliaments and foreign ideas dragged it down. It will be reborn. The leader knows what the people want, so there is no need for votes, and every young person will march in the movement. Class war is a foreign poison. The owners keep their factories, and they answer to the state.”

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**Question 1. Who would own the businesses and farms?**
- Private owners
- The state
- The workers, in councils or co-ops
- Private owners, directed by the state
- The words do not say

The working, one step at a time:
1. Question 1. Find who would own the businesses. “The owners keep their factories, and they answer to the state”: private owners, directed by the state.
1. Question 2. Find who the story is about. Class war is “a foreign poison”: the nation.
1. Question 3. Find what drives history. “Great once”, “dragged it down”, “It will be reborn”: decline and rebirth, the core of fascism.
1. Question 4. Find what it wants done with elections. “No need for votes”: abolish them for a leader.
1. Check the telltale answers, then name the family. The nation, its decline and rebirth, and a leader who replaces elections: fascism's telltale answers, with a marching youth and class war thrown out as the rest of the cluster.

Then the answer: Private owners, directed by the state / The nation / The nation's decline and rebirth / Abolish them for a leader or a movement / Fascism. The cluster is all here: the rebirth story, the nation as one body, a leader in place of votes, young people marching, class war rejected, private firms answering to the state. Fascism.
