# Political Ideologies: Today's two

Revision 1, draft (the screen carries the draft line). Part: Today's two. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can read a post, a speech, a platform or a quote and name the ideology it speaks for, or say there is too little to tell, by answering the deciding questions, when someone argues a position or throws a label like "socialist" or "fascist".

## The why

Two movements are argued about most today, and mislabeled most: a nation-first populism, and a politics of fixing unequal results between groups.
In about twelve minutes you will learn each one's five answers, and tell the first from fascism and the second from Nazism.

## Step 1: National populism, with its five answers

National populism is a thin populism fastened to a national host. Question 1: private owners, directed toward national goals with tariffs and the screening of foreign buyers (the mild end of "directed", which fascism also uses). Question 2: the real people and the nation against a cosmopolitan elite ("cosmopolitan" and "globalists" are the speaker's words for an elite said to care more about the world or foreign money than about home). Question 3: the nation was sold out from above and outside. Question 4: it works inside elections but attacks the courts, the press and the civil service as unrepresentative, without abolishing the vote. Question 5: a sovereign, united nation, restored.
It lacks four things fascism has: a rebirth myth, a leader who replaces elections, a marching mass movement, and a cult of purifying violence. Directed owners do not separate the two; these four, and the vote, do.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| “The establishment sold us out. Vote for us, and vote us out if we fail.” | “Parliament is a stage. We do not want votes. The nation will be reborn.” |

Compare: Both rage at the elite. Question 4 splits them: the first keeps the vote.

## Step 2: Identity-egalitarianism, with its five answers

Question 1: markets largely accepted. Question 2: identity groups such as race and gender, in the egalitarian direction, the ranking unjust and to be undone. Question 3: neutral-looking rules keep old disadvantage. Question 4: keep elections and widen them. Question 5: equal outcomes between groups, with no fixed end.
It is not Marxism: class and ownership are not its root. It is not classical liberalism: it says equal rules are not enough. It is not Nazism, which calls the rank natural. Listen for structural, systemic, equity. Scholars dispute whether it grew from the Marxist tradition or on its own; the questions classify it either way.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| “Barriers rooted in race keep producing unequal outcomes. Find them and tear them down.” | “Each race has its rightful rank. Keep the racial order.” |

Compare: Both are about race. The first calls the ranking unjust and to be undone; the second, natural and to be kept.

## Step 3: Populism alone names nothing, and the two rows

The people against an elite, with nothing more, is too little to tell. The host shows in the nation, the owners and the vote.
National populism: the people and the nation against an elite, pursued inside elections.
Identity-egalitarianism: identity groups as an unjust rank to undo.

## Step 4: A worked example

“The people who work and pay have been sold out by a small circle in the courts, the press and the ministries. We will take those back for the nation. Firms will stay private but will build what this nation needs, and foreign buyers will be screened. We will win it at the ballot box, and the voters can throw us out if we fail.”
A campaign speech, invented.

- The state

The working, one step at a time:
1. Question 1. Find who would own the businesses. “Firms will stay private but will build what this nation needs.” Owners keep their firms and the state points them at national goals. Question 1: private owners, directed by the state.
1. Question 2. Find who the story is about. “The people who work and pay” and “the nation” against “a small circle”. Question 2: the people against an elite.
1. Question 4. Find what is wanted for elections. The courts, the press and the ministries are attacked, but the vote stays: “win it at the ballot box”, “the voters can throw us out”. Question 4: keep the vote, attack the courts, the press and the civil service. Fascism would replace elections.
1. Check the telltale answers, then name the ideology. The people and the nation against an elite, inside elections: national populism. None of the four things fascism adds is here.

Then the answer: Private owners, directed by the state / The people against an elite / Keep the vote, but attack the courts, the press and the civil service / National populism. Directed private firms (Question 1), the people and the nation against an elite (Question 2) and a vote kept while the courts, press and ministries are attacked (Question 4): national populism.
