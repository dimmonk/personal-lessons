# Basic Math: Growth year by year

Revision 1, live. Part: Growth year by year. Where the subject ends up: Work out the number a situation needs (a total, a price per one, a scaled amount, a percent or the price before one, how much paint or soil to buy, how high a ladder reaches, what savings grow to or a loan costs) and tell whether it is sensible, when a receipt, a recipe, a sale tag, a room or an offer hands you some numbers and a question and nobody names the method.

## The why

Savings at 5% a year for 10 years grow by more than 50%, and a rent that rises 4% a year doubles in about 18 years. Most people guess low, because they add where it multiplies.
Here you tell the two kinds of growth apart and work each one out.

## Step 1: Adding or multiplying

Two ways an amount grows. Adding $50 each year: $1,000, $1,050, $1,100. Growing 5% a year: $1,000, $1,050, $1,102.50, because each year’s 5% is of a bigger amount.
So first decide which: the same dollar amount each year adds; a percent each year multiplies, by 1.05 for 5%.
Adding: start + the amount × the years. Multiplying: start × the multiplier, once for each year. The xʸ button does the years in one go: $1,000 at 5% for 10 years is 1.05 xʸ 10 × 1000 = 1,628.89.
Rough, for multiplying: it doubles in about 72 ÷ the percent years. At 5%, about every 14 years: $1,000 is about $2,000 after 14 years and $4,000 after 28.
A rate given as APY is the growth in a whole year, with the interest added in.
How many years until it reaches a goal? Rough: a goal a bit more than double takes a bit longer than doubling, about a fifth longer. Then try that number of years on the calculator, and the years next to it: the answer is the first year it gets there.

A table, year by year: Year | Adds $50 | Grows 5%; 0 | $1,000 | $1,000; 1 | $1,050 | $1,050; 2 | $1,100 | $1,102.50; 3 | $1,150 | $1,157.63; 10 | $1,500 | $1,628.89.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: “5% a year for 10 years is 50% more.”
The right line: That adds 5% of the start each year. Each year’s 5% is of a bigger amount: 1.05 xʸ 10 is 1.63, 63% more.

## Step 2: A worked example

An offer, "Saving for later":

> Put in now  |  $5,000
> Savings account, interest added to the balance  |  4% a year (APY)
> Monthly fee  |  none

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**Each year, does it add the same amount or multiply by the same number?**
(the options come in an order that depends on the seed of the question)
- It adds the same amount each year
- It multiplies by the same number each year

The working, one step at a time:
1. Say what is asked and what is given. Asked: the savings after 18 years, if it keeps growing this way. Given: $5,000 now and how it grows each year. The last line does not matter.
1. Decide how it grows. A percent each year, of a bigger amount each time: it multiplies by 1.04 a year.
1. Make a rough answer in your head. It doubles in about 72 ÷ 4 = 18 years, so 18 years is one doubling: about $10,000.
1. Work it out exactly on the calculator, then look back. On the calculator: 1.04 xʸ 18 × 5000 = shows $10,129.08. Look back: near the rough $10,000, and more than adding 4% of $5,000 each year would give.

Then the answer: It multiplies by the same number each year / $10,000 / $10,129.08. It multiplies by 1.04 each year: 1.04 xʸ 18 × 5,000 = $10,129.08.
