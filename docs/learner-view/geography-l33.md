# Geography: The whole world, mixed

Revision 1, live. Part: The whole world, mixed. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

In real life a place is never named next to its neighbors: a story jumps from Ohio to Senegal to Vietnam, and you have to find each one at once. In about sixteen minutes you will see each kind of question done once more, answer a mixed run of questions, and then take a check of 30 questions on places from every part: names tapped, lit places named, flags, neighbors and water, towns you have never met, and pairs of cities.

## Step 1: Every kind of question once more

No new rule. Every question is the same one: find the bigger area first, then what touches the place, then its water, and for a pair of cities, measure it against pairs you know. Here is each kind done once for you.

## Step 2: A worked example

Nevada


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of The United States with Nevada lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? Nevada is in the West's mountain half, inland. Start from the West Coast, placed already.
1. Touches: what is next to it? The long state down the coast is California; Nevada is the one just east of it, in the middle of the coast.
1. Water: what water is it on? No sea or ocean touches it. Tap inside it.

Then the answer: Nevada. It is the one just east of California, with a slanted west side.

## Step 7: A worked example

How far is San Francisco from Los Angeles?

A map of The United States, north up, with no names on it, opening on one part of it (zoom 2.2, round the point 140 across and 268 down, of 1000 by 523.1), where the button for the whole map goes back to that part. **About how far apart are they, in a straight line?**
- Under 150 miles
- 150 to 500 miles
- 500 to 1,500 miles
- 1,500 to 3,000 miles
- 3,000 to 6,000 miles
- Over 6,000 miles

The working, one step at a time:
1. Find both cities on the map. San Francisco and Los Angeles are both lit and named. Look at the pair, not at each city alone.
1. Compare the pair with pairs you know. Pairs to measure against, in straight-line miles: Austin to San Antonio about 70; Washington, D.C. to New York City about 200; Dallas–Fort Worth to Chicago about 800; Los Angeles to Chicago about 1,740; New York City to Paris about 3,630; Los Angeles to Sydney about 7,500. San Francisco to Los Angeles is farther apart than Austin to San Antonio and than Washington, D.C. to New York City, and much nearer than Dallas–Fort Worth to Chicago.
1. Pick the distance band. The straight line is about 350 miles: 150 to 500 miles.
1. Turn it into a trip. By road: 350 miles at 60 miles an hour is about 5.8 hours. By air: 1 hour on the ground and in the climb, plus 350 miles at 500 miles an hour, is about 1.7 hours, a short flight. So: a drive of 3 to 9 hours, or a short flight.

Then the answer: 150 to 500 miles / A drive of 3 to 9 hours, or a short flight. San Francisco to Los Angeles is about 350 miles in a straight line, which is the band 150 to 500 miles. By road: 350 miles at 60 miles an hour is about 5.8 hours. By air: 1 hour on the ground and in the climb, plus 350 miles at 500 miles an hour, is about 1.7 hours, a short flight.
