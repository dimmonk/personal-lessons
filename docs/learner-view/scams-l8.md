# Scams & Social Engineering: About you

Revision 1, live. Part: About you. Where the subject ends up: When a message, call or offer asks you to act, you stop, see what it wants, and check it through a channel you already had before doing anything, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, you make the first calls at once.

## The why

Some messages want your details: your Social Security number, your birthday, your address, the answers to your security questions.
You see each script beside a real request for details, and choose what to do.

## Step 1: A message that wants who you are

| A | B |
|---|---|
| A text message: "Ashgrove Bank", Text message.  > Enter your Social Security number and date of birth at the link by tonight. > (the link in it, drawn as words and never one that can be followed: ashgrove-verify.example/id) | A pop-up on a screen: "Penhallow CU", In the app, after you tapped “Open a savings account”.  > To open your savings account, enter your Social Security number. > [a button: "Continue"] |

Compare: The first comes to you. In the second, you opened the app and chose to open an account.

With your number, your birthday and your address, someone can pass as you: open a card or a phone account in your name, or take your tax refund. Give them inside something you started: an app or site you opened, a call you made to a number you already had.

## Step 2: The friendly stranger’s questions

| A | B |
|---|---|
| A chat, with its history: "Fun Quizzes", Message on a social app.  > (Fun Quizzes, at the left) Tell us your first pet’s name and the street you grew up on. | A pop-up on a screen: "Alderwick", In the app, as you make your account.  > Pick a security question and type your answer. > [a button: "Save"] |

Compare: The quiz collects the answers. The second is where you choose them.

A first pet, a childhood street, a first car: these are the answers to common security questions. When you choose an answer yourself, make it one nobody could guess, like a second password.

## Step 3: A call that wants to confirm you

| A | B |
|---|---|
| An incoming call, the caller's words as a transcript: "Calderwood Bank", Incoming call.  > Give me your full card number, the date on it and your birthday. | An incoming call, the caller's words as a transcript: "Calderwood Bank", Your call to the number on your card.  > First, what is your date of birth? |

Compare: The same name. You placed the second call, on your own number.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: They only want my birthday, not money, so there is no harm.
The right line: Your details are how someone passes as you. When they call you, hang up and call the number you had.

## Step 4: A worked example

You bank with Ashgrove Bank, and its app is on your phone.

A text message: "Ashgrove Bank", Text message · 12:40 PM.

> We must confirm your identity to keep your account open.
> Enter your Social Security number and date of birth at the link by tonight.
> (the link in it, drawn as words and never one that can be followed: ashgrove-verify.example/id)

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**What do you do?**
- Do what it says
- Check first, a way I already had
- Leave it

The working, one step at a time:
1. See what it wants. It wants your Social Security number and your birthday.
1. See whether you started it. No. You were not opening anything or filling in any form.
1. See whether you deal with them. Yes: it uses the name of your bank, and you have its app.
1. Choose. Check first, in the Ashgrove app. Leave the link alone.

Then the answer: Check first, a way I already had / The Ashgrove app, opened myself. You did not start it, and it uses the name of your bank, so you check in the app you already have. Your number and birthday are what open accounts in your name.

## The check

9 new questions, no help. No answer is shown until the end. The questions come in mixed order.

You have 20 seconds for each. The clock only shows the time: nothing moves on until you tap Next. A call is not on a clock.

To pass:
- None did what a scam asked
- None checked through the link, number or reply the message gave
- At most 1 wrong among real messages
