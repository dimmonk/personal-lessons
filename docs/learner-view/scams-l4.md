# Scams & Social Engineering: Your device

Revision 4, live. Part: Your device. Where the subject ends up: When a message, call or offer asks you to act, you stop, see what it wants, and check it through a channel you already had before doing anything, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, you make the first calls at once.

## The why

Some messages want your phone or computer itself: a pop-up that tells you to call, a call that wants to see your screen, a file nobody expected.
You see each script beside the real thing it copies, and choose what to do.

## Step 1: A warning that tells you to call

| A | B |
|---|---|
| A pop-up on a screen: "Security alert", On a web page.  > Warning: your computer has a virus! > Call Security Desk at 1-555-0147 now. > [a button: "Call now"] | A banner over another task: "Your security program", On your computer.  > Weekly scan finished. No threats found. |

Compare: One tells you to call a number. The other only tells you what your own program did, and wants nothing.

A real security pop-up never tells you to call a phone number. Whoever answers is nobody you have an account with, so the rule says leave it: close the page.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: I called them myself, so I know who I am talking to.
The right line: Calling the number a pop-up gave you is not starting it yourself. The pop-up chose who answers.

## Step 2: A call about your computer, or a refund

| A | B |
|---|---|
| An incoming call, the caller's words as a transcript: "Tech Care billing", Incoming call · 3:30 PM.  > You overpaid $300. > Open a website so I can see your screen and send it back. | An incoming call, the caller's words as a transcript: "Kestrel Mobile", You called · 2:05 PM.  > Kestrel Mobile, this is Jo. How can I help? |

Compare: Who started it? In the real call, you did: you called the number on your bill.

Real tech companies won’t call you first about a problem with your computer, and nobody sending you a refund needs to see your screen. Pressing Allow on the page a caller sends you to hands him your screen. If the caller uses the name of a company you deal with, hang up and call the number on your bill. If not, just hang up.

## Step 3: A file or an app nobody expected

| A | B |
|---|---|
| A text message: "Marcy", Text message · 7:40 PM.  > Look at these photos from the party! > (the link in it, drawn as words and never one that can be followed: photo-party.example/view) | A text message: "Rosa (sister)", Text message · 5:10 PM, after you asked at lunch.  > Here is Mom’s soup recipe. > (the link in it, drawn as words and never one that can be followed: recipes.example/soup.pdf) |

Compare: Did you request it? Rosa’s you did, at lunch. Marcy is nobody you know.

Open a file or install an app only when you requested it. If someone you know sends one you did not request, check with them on the number you have, because their account can be taken over. If you do not know the sender, leave it. The file you requested, from the person you requested it from, in the form you expected, is safe enough to open.

## Step 4: A worked example

You are reading a recipe on your laptop.

A pop-up on a screen: "Security alert", On a web page.

> Warning: your computer has a virus! Do not turn it off.
> Call Security Desk at 1-555-0147 now.
> [a button: "Call now"]

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**What do you do?**
- Do what it says
- Check first, a way I already had
- Leave it

The working, one step at a time:
1. See what it wants. It wants you to call a number, where someone will want onto your computer.
1. See whether you started it. You did not. You were reading a recipe.
1. See whether you deal with them. No. Security Desk is nobody you have an account with.
1. Choose. Leave it. Close the page.

Then the answer: Leave it. You did not start it and you have no account with Security Desk, so you leave it. A real security pop-up never tells you to call a phone number.

## Step 5: Questions

6 questions, listed, with each inner pair asked one after the other, in a random order. A missed question comes back at least three questions later, until it is right. After each answer: the right answer, the deciding words, the reason, and one line on the learner's own choice if it was wrong.

## Step 6: Questions

## The check

9 new questions, no help. No answer is shown until the end. The questions come in mixed order.

You have 20 seconds for each. The clock only shows the time: nothing moves on until you tap Next. A call is not on a clock.

To pass:
- None did what a scam asked
- None checked through the link, number or reply the message gave
- At most 1 wrong among real messages
