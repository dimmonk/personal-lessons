# US Civics & History: Who acted, and at which level

Revision 1, live. Part: Who acted, and at which level. Where the subject ends up: Answer the citizenship interview’s civics questions aloud from memory, and, reading a news story about a US law, order or ruling, say who acted, whether it was theirs to do, who can stop it, and whether to take the story as it stands or check who is behind it first.

## The why

A news story about the government names someone who did something: a council, a governor, a judge, the president. Before you decide what you think, find out which government that is and what it did.
It is easy to think the president did it, even for a school bus stop or a parking fee. A city, a state and a court each act on their own.

## Step 1: Two questions first

Find who the story names. Then put two questions to yourself.
Which level acted: the whole country (federal), one state, or a city, town or county (local)? What did it do: write a rule (vote on it), carry a rule out (do the work or enforce it), or judge a dispute (decide who is right)?

A plain table, "Who does what, at each level":

> Level  |  Writes  |  Carries out  |  Judges  (the column heads)
> Federal  |  Congress  |  The president, the Cabinet and federal agencies  |  Federal courts
> State  |  The state legislature  |  The governor and state agencies  |  State courts
> Local  |  The city council, the county board, or the school board for its schools  |  The mayor, the police, the fire department, public works and parks  |  Local courts

## Step 2: A wrong idea

A wrong idea, marked wrong: “The president decided it.” It is the first thing many people think, even about a school bus stop or a parking fee.
The right line: Look at who the story says acted. A story may name the president for a speech or a wish while a city council, a governor or a judge does the acting. Each works for their own level; the president works for the federal one only.

## Step 3: A worked example

A news story, from "The Lakeview Times":

> # Mayor sends plows to school routes first
>
> Snow fell all night in Ridgefield, and the roads were still covered by morning. The mayor of Ridgefield ordered the city’s snow plows to clear the roads around schools first. Parents said the buses ran on time.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**Which level of government acted?**
- Federal: the whole country
- State: one state
- Local: a city, town or county

The working, one step at a time:
1. Find who acted: The story says the mayor of Ridgefield ordered the city’s snow plows to clear the roads around schools first.
1. Name the level: The mayor of Ridgefield is part of a local government, which works for one city, town or county.
1. Name what they did: That is carrying out a rule or a law.

Then the answer: Local: a city, town or county / Carries it out: does the work or enforces the rule. A mayor runs a city, and sending the plows is the city doing its work. No president is in this story.

## Step 4: The same topic, two levels

| A | B |
|---|---|
| Congress voted to add money for school lunches. | The Brookside school board voted to start classes later. |

Compare: Both are about schools. The first is the whole country; the second is one town’s board.

#### Item 1

A news story, from "The Harbor Gazette":

> # Congress adds money for school lunches
>
> The House and the Senate both voted on Thursday to add money to the national school lunch program. The bill now goes to the president. Lunch workers said the extra money will help schools buy fresh fruit.

**Which level of government acted?**
- Federal: the whole country (right)
- State: one state — if chosen: Congress is part of the federal government, which works for the whole country.
- Local: a city, town or county — if chosen: Congress is part of the federal government, which works for the whole country.

**What did the one who acted do?**
- Writes the law or the rule (right)
- Carries it out: does the work or enforces the rule — if chosen: Congress voted to add money to the national school lunch program. That is writing a rule or a law, not carrying out a rule or a law.
- Judges: settles a dispute or says what a law means — if chosen: Congress voted to add money to the national school lunch program. That is writing a rule or a law, not judging a dispute.

After the answer: the deciding words are marked. Reason: Congress writes the country’s laws, and a vote on a bill is writing one. That is federal.
