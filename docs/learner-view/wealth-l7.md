# Wealth Preservation: Mix and drift, and your own hands

Revision 1, live. Part: Mix and drift, and your own hands. Where the subject ends up: Run a planner's yearly check on your own US accounts: list everything, see what money you need soon is exposed and what single loss could ruin you, find the certain leaks (yearly costs in dollars, a missed match, tax in the wrong place, foreign filings) and the risks you did not choose (one big bet, a drifted mix), check who inherits and who can act, and change only what the check calls for.

## The why

Dana wrote down 65% stocks. After two good years her stocks are 74.5% of what she invests, and she never noticed.
Prices move the mix for you. Here you check it against the one you chose, and act only on a line decided in advance, never on the news.

## Step 1: The rule

Add up the stocks in your invested accounts (401(k), IRA, brokerage), then everything in them: stocks, bonds and cash held there. Leave the bank accounts out: they are your buffer. Stocks ÷ everything × 100 is your stock share.
Hold it against the share you chose. Under 5 points off: leave it. 5 points or more: move the gap, points × everything ÷ 100, from the side that is over to the side that is short. Five is a common line, not a law.
Make the trade inside a 401(k) or an IRA, where a sale is not taxed. A target-date fund is one fund of stocks and bonds that re-mixes itself as you age: if it is all you hold, leave it.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: “Stocks are falling. I should sell before they fall further.”
The right line: Nobody knows which comes first, more fall or the rebound, and the people who trade most earn less. The line decides: after a big fall it moves money into stocks, not out.

A plain table, "The mix Dana wrote down" (a tab named "The mix", one of a household's papers: one open at a time, the first at the start, and the one with a marked line after the answer):

> Part  |  Share  (the column heads)
> Stocks  |  65%
> Bonds  |  35%
> (Of the money in the 401(k) and the brokerage account. The bank savings are the cash buffer.)

The working, one step at a time:
1. Add up the stocks in the invested accounts, and everything in them. Leave the bank out. $52,000 + $30,000 = $82,000 of stocks, out of $52,000 + $28,000 + $30,000 = $110,000.
1. Divide the stocks by everything invested. $82,000 ÷ $110,000 = 74.5%.
1. Hold it against the mix chosen: 5 points or more off is the line. 74.5% against 65%: 9.5 points over. Change it.
1. Work out the money to move. 9.5% of $110,000 = $10,450.
1. Trade where a sale is not taxed. Inside the 401(k): about $10,450 from the US Stock Fund to the Bond Fund.

Then the answer: 74.5% / Change it / $10,450 / In the 401(k): sell some of the US Stock Fund and buy the Bond Fund. Stocks are $82,000 of $110,000, 74.5%, 9.5 points over the 65% Dana chose. Moving about $10,450 from stocks to bonds inside the 401(k), where a sale is not taxed, brings it back.
