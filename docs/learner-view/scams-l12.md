# Scams & Social Engineering: The first hour

Revision 1, live. Part: The first hour. Where the subject ends up: When a message, call or offer asks you to act, you stop, see what it wants, and check it through a channel you already had before doing anything, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, you make the first calls at once.

## The why

If money, a password, your device or your Social Security number has already gone out, what you do first decides how much you get back.
You learn the first step for each, then do it on new situations.

## Step 1: If money went out

Call the bank or company the money went through, on its own number, at once: your bank, the payment app, the wire company, the crypto company, or the gift card company on the number on the back of the card.
Tell them a scammer tricked you, and request that they reverse it. Only they can try to stop it, and only quickly. Keep a gift card and its receipt.
A stolen card: call the bank at once, then follow up in writing. For a debit card, what you owe depends on how fast you tell them; for a credit card it is at most $50.

## Step 2: If a password or your device went out

A password: make a new one, in the app or on the site you open yourself. Then turn on two-step sign-in, and change the password anywhere you used the same one.
Your device: update your security software and run a scan, and stop signing in to accounts on it until then. Then new passwords, with two-step sign-in.

## Step 3: If your Social Security number went out

Go to IdentityTheft.gov: it tells you the steps, and if your number has been used, it gives you a recovery plan. Freeze your credit at each of the three credit bureaus, which is free. Get your credit reports and look for accounts you do not know.

## Step 4: Then

Report a scam at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. An offer to get your money back for a fee is another scam.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: It is gone, so there is no hurry now.
The right line: Right away is when the company can still try to stop the money, and when a new password still shuts someone out.

## Step 5: A worked example

An hour ago a caller said he was Ashgrove’s fraud team, and you moved $1,800 by bank transfer to the “safe account” he gave. You have just called the number on your card and learned it was not your bank.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**What do you do first?**
- Call the bank or company the money went through, on its own number
- Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov
- Wait a day to see what happens
- Message whoever took it and tell them to send it back
- Pay someone who offers to get it back

The working, one step at a time:
1. See what went out. Money, $1,800, by bank transfer.
1. Find who can stop it. Only the bank it went through, Ashgrove, and only at once.
1. Do that first. Call Ashgrove on the number on your card, now.
1. Then the next two. Request that they reverse the payment, then report the scam at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Then the answer: Call the bank or company the money went through, on its own number / Tell them a scammer tricked me, and request that they reverse the payment; Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Money that went out is stopped, if at all, by the bank it went through, and only at once. Then you request that they reverse it, and report the scam.
