# Basic Math: Counting ways and chances

Revision 1, live. Part: Counting ways and chances. Where the subject ends up: Work out the number a situation needs (a total, a price per one, a scaled amount, a percent or the price before one, how much paint or soil to buy, how high a ladder reaches, what savings grow to or a loan costs) and tell whether it is sensible, when a receipt, a recipe, a sale tag, a room or an offer hands you some numbers and a question and nobody names the method.

## The why

A test that finds 90% of the people who have a condition comes back positive: most people guess a 90% chance they have it, and for a rare condition that is far too high.
Here you count ways by multiplying, and you work out chances by counting people.

## Step 1: Multiply the choices; count the people

Counting the ways: when you pick one of each, multiply the choices. 4 shirts, 3 pants and 2 pairs of shoes make 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 outfits. A lock with 4 wheels of 10 digits has 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 = 10,000 codes; trying one every 5 seconds takes 50,000 seconds, and an hour is 60 × 60 = 3,600 seconds.
Chances are easiest as counts of people: picture 1,000 people and follow them.
At least once: count the opposite, the people who miss every time, and take them from 1,000. 1 in 4 scratch tickets wins, so 3 in 4 lose. Of 1,000 people with 2 tickets each, 750 lose the first, and 3 in 4 of those, 562.5, lose the second too. So about 563 win nothing and about 437 win at least once.
A test result: say 10 in 1,000 have it, and the test finds 9 of them. Of the other 990, 5% test positive anyway: about 50. So 59 test positive, and only 9 of them have it: about 15%, not 90%.

Of 1,000 people who take the test: a row of 1,000 dots for each count, the first ones marked and read left to right, top to bottom: Have it, 10 in 1,000; Have it and test positive, 9 in 1,000; Do not have it but test positive, 50 in 1,000.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: “The test is right 90% of the time, so a positive means a 90% chance.”
The right line: Count the people: many positives can come from the many who do not have it, and for a rare condition most do.

## Step 2: A worked example

A plain table, "Screening test: what the numbers mean":

> People your age who have it  |  20 in 1,000
> If you have it, the test is positive  |  80% of the time
> If you do not, it is positive anyway  |  5% of the time
> Results  |  in 3 days

The working, one step at a time:
1. Say what is asked and what is given. Asked: if you test positive, the chance you have it. Given: how many have it, and how often the test is positive with it and without it. The days for results do not matter.
1. Make a rough answer in your head. 80% of the 20 who have it test positive: 16. About 5% of the rest, about 50, test positive without it. 16 of about 70 positives is about 23%.
1. Count it out in 1,000 people. 20 in 1,000 have it, and 80% of them test positive: 16. The other 980 do not, and 5% of them test positive anyway: 49. So 16 + 49 = 65 test positive.
1. Work it out exactly on the calculator, then look back. On the calculator: 16 ÷ 65 × 100 = shows 24.6%. Look back: below 80%, because 49 of the 65 positives do not have it: most of them.

Then the answer: 23% / 65 people / 24.6%. Of 1,000 people, 16 have it and test positive and 49 test positive without it: 16 ÷ 65 = 24.6%.

A picture follows:
Of 1,000 people who take the test: a row of 1,000 dots for each count, the first ones marked and read left to right, top to bottom: Have it, 20 in 1,000; Have it and test positive, 16 in 1,000; Do not have it but test positive, 49 in 1,000.
