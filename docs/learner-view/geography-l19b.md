# Geography: The flags of Europe, the Balkans and the south

Revision 1, live. Part: The flags of Europe, the Balkans and the south. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A flag in a news photo, on a truck or at a match tells you whose it is only if you can read it. In about fifteen minutes you will read the flags of the twelve countries of the Balkans and the south, by the stripes, the colors and the mark, and tell the ones that look alike: Slovenia, Croatia and Serbia; Albania and Montenegro; Bosnia and Herzegovina and Kosovo.

## Step 1: How to read a flag

Read it the same way every time. First count the stripes and see which way they run, lying flat or standing upright, or whether there are none: that splits the flags into groups, so you search a few and not all twelve. Then say the colors in order, from the top or the left, since look-alikes often share colors in a different order. Then look for a mark, a shield, an eagle, a sun, stars or a shape, which settles a flag the stripes do not. Then check the one line that tells it from the flag it is mistaken for: that is where the misses happen. Last, find the country with the three questions: its area, what touches it, its water. Next, the twelve flags in three groups; none of it stays up.

## Step 2: Flags of flat stripes

Look for about a minute at each of three groups. Say each name as you read its flag. These five have stripes lying flat: three of them have red, white and blue in different orders, Bulgaria has white, green and red, and Greece has nine stripes.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇸🇮 Slovenia; 🇭🇷 Croatia; 🇷🇸 Serbia; 🇧🇬 Bulgaria; 🇬🇷 Greece.

Slovenia: Three flat stripes, white on top, then blue and red, with a small shield on the upper left that holds a white mountain. Croatia and Serbia both have red on top.

Croatia: Three flat stripes, red on top, then white and blue, with a red-and-white checkered shield in the middle. Serbia has blue in the middle and its shield on the left.

Serbia: Three flat stripes, red on top, then blue and white, with a shield on the left. Croatia has white in the middle and a checkered shield.

Bulgaria: Three flat stripes, white on top, then green and red, with nothing on them.

Greece: Nine flat stripes, blue and white in turn, with a white cross on a blue square at the top left.

## Step 3: Red flags

These four are mostly red, and each has its own mark in the middle or on the side. Malta is half white and half red.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇦🇱 Albania; 🇲🇪 Montenegro; 🇲🇰 North Macedonia; 🇲🇹 Malta.

Albania: Red with a black double-headed eagle in the middle. Montenegro is red with a gold eagle and a gold border.

Montenegro: Red with a thin gold border and a gold double-headed eagle in the middle. Albania is red with a black eagle and no border.

North Macedonia: Red with a yellow sun in the middle, its rays reaching the edges.

Malta: Two upright halves, white on the left and red on the right, with a small cross edged in red in the top left corner.

## Step 4: Flags with no stripes: blue and white

These three have no stripes: two blue flags with stars, and one white flag.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇧🇦 Bosnia and Herzegovina; a flag no emoji draws, drawn here: blue, with six white stars in an arc above a gold map of the country Kosovo; 🇨🇾 Cyprus.

Bosnia and Herzegovina: Blue with a big yellow triangle and a row of white stars along its slanted edge. Kosovo has a blue flag too, with six white stars in an arc above a gold map of the country.

Kosovo: Blue with six white stars in an arc above a gold map of the country. Bosnia and Herzegovina is blue too, with a yellow triangle.

Cyprus: White with a copper-colored shape of the island above two green olive branches.
