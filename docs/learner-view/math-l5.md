# Basic Math: Percents forward

Revision 1, live. Part: Percents forward. Where the subject ends up: Work out the number a situation needs (a total, a price per one, a scaled amount, a percent or the price before one, how much paint or soil to buy, how high a ladder reaches, what savings grow to or a loan costs) and tell whether it is sensible, when a receipt, a recipe, a sale tag, a room or an offer hands you some numbers and a question and nobody names the method.

## The why

A tip, a raise, a sale, a rent increase: each is a percent of something. The mistakes that cost money come from taking the percent of the wrong amount.
Here you find the whole first, make a rough answer from easy percents, then use the calculator.

## Step 1: Find the whole, then the percent

A percent is a part out of 100. The whole, 100%, is the amount the percent is of: the subtotal for a tip, your pay for a share of it, the old rent for a rise.
Rough answers come from easy percents. 10%: move the decimal point one place left, so 10% of $64.50 is about $6.45. 20% is double that; 15% is 10% and half of it again. 1% is a hundredth, so 4.5% is four and a half of the 1%s. 25% is a quarter, 50% is half. A rough answer catches big slips; a small one needs the look back.
Exact answers come from the multiplier: 18% is 0.18, so 18% of $64.50 is $64.50 × 0.18. Up 4% is 104%, so × 1.04; up 4.5% is 104.5%, so × 1.045. 30% off leaves 70%, so × 0.70.
For a small change, a raise or a sale, the rough answer is of the part that changes, the raise or the amount off: the new amount is then the old one plus or minus it. A share as a percent is part ÷ whole × 100. A change as a percent is the change ÷ where you started × 100.

A bar for the whole, "A $64.50 subtotal is 100%", cut into parts as wide as their percents: An 18% tip 18%, The rest of the subtotal 82%.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: “Rent went from $1,000 to $1,100: that is $100 out of $1,100, about 9%.”
The right line: A change is measured from where you started: $100 out of $1,000 is 10%.

The working, one step at a time:
1. Say what is asked and what is given. Asked: a tip of 18% on the subtotal. Given: the subtotal, $64.50. The tax and the total do not matter here.
1. Make a rough answer in your head. With benchmarks: 10% of $64.50 is $6.45 (move the decimal point one place left). 18% is a bit less than 20%, double 10%, $12.90: about $12.00.
1. Name the kind of problem and draw it. A percent of an amount: the subtotal is the whole, 100%, and the tip is 18% of it.
1. Work it out exactly on the calculator, then look back. With the multiplier: 18% is 0.18, so $64.50 × 0.18 = $11.61. Look back: the two ways agree, and the tip is smaller than the subtotal.

Then the answer: $12 / $11.61. 18% of $64.50 is $64.50 × 0.18 = $11.61. The benchmark way, from 10%, lands near it.
