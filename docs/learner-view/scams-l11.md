# Scams & Social Engineering: Your plan, and all of it

Revision 1, live. Part: Your plan, and all of it. Where the subject ends up: When a message, call or offer asks you to act, you stop, see what it wants, and check it through a channel you already had before doing anything, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, you make the first calls at once.

## The why

Real messages and scams come mixed, of every kind, while you are busy. One plan, made now, helps you stop at the right moment.
You write your plan, practice on everything at once, and then take the test you took at the start, on new messages.

## Step 1: The whole rule

What does it want? Money, a sign-in or a code, your details, or your phone or computer.
Did I start it, or is it exactly what I agreed, the amount or how it is worked out, and the way to pay? Then do it.
If not: do I deal with them, or know the person? Check first, through a way I already had. A government office counts: it has your records.
If neither, leave it. If it is someone you know only online, a friend or match you have never met (not a buyer you deal with once), and they want money, also tell someone you know in person.

## Step 2: One plan, for when a request comes

Write one plan in your own words. Start from the moment: a message that wants money, a code, your details or your device. Then say what you will do. Only you can see it. Do not type a number from a card or an account.

- When this happens (for example: someone I did not call asks for a code, money or my sign-in)
- Then I will (for example: end it and call back on the number I have)

Buttons: "Save my plan" (both boxes must be filled in; the plan is kept as one sentence, "If <when>, I will <what>.", with the day it was saved) and "Skip for now". Once saved: "Your plan is saved. You will see it again before the practice that rehearses it." and "Change my plan". A group of questions that rehearses the plan shows it once first: "Your plan, before you start", "This is the plan you wrote. Practice it on the next messages.", and the sentence, when there is one.

## Step 3: Questions

13 questions, shuffle, each question on a 20 second clock (the clock only shows the time, counted on the frame clock: at zero the question stops, whatever is not answered is a timeout, a miss that is not a slip, and the screen waits for Next; a live call or chat is not timed), with the learner's saved plan shown once first when there is one. A missed question comes back at least three questions later, until it is right. After each answer: the right answer, the deciding words, the reason, and one line on the learner's own choice if it was wrong.
