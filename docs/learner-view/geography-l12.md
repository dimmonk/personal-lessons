# Geography: Canada

Revision 1, live. Part: Canada. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A news story says a wildfire has closed the highways in Alberta, or that ice is breaking up on Hudson Bay. If Canada is one big blank to you, you cannot tell how far away it is, who is next to it or whether it is on the coast. In about fifteen minutes you will place the ten provinces, the three territories and Hudson Bay on a blank map, by finding the area each sits in first, then name the look-alikes and say what touches some of them and what water they are on.

## Step 1: How to find a province

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? That tells you where on the map to look. What touches it? Its neighbors pin the exact spot, and for a province one neighbor is often the United States. What water is it on? Coast or inland is often the point of the story. Canada has ten provinces and three territories, in five areas, west to east. British Columbia is alone on the Pacific coast. The three Prairie provinces, Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba, sit side by side above the United States. Ontario and Quebec are the two big ones in the middle, around the Great Lakes and Hudson Bay. The Atlantic provinces are on the east coast. The three territories, Yukon, the Northwest Territories and Nunavut, cover the north. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water.

## Step 2: The Pacific coast and the Prairies

East of it, side by side above the United States, are the three Prairie provinces: Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba.

The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? Saskatchewan is one of the three Prairie provinces, which lie side by side just above the United States. Start from Montana and North Dakota, which you placed in The West and The Midwest.
1. Touches: what is next to it? Alberta is the Prairie province against British Columbia, on the west; Manitoba is the one on the east. Saskatchewan is the one in the middle, above the east end of Montana and North Dakota.
