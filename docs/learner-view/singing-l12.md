# Singing: Find the cause

Revision 1, live. Part: Find the cause. Where the subject ends up: Sing a tune you have just heard, or a line of a song you know, in tune and at a height that suits your voice, with high notes that come out light instead of shouted; and when a line goes wrong, say which of a few things went wrong and what to try, or that nothing did.

## The why

This is the whole thing at once. A line goes wrong, or seems to, and you find out why, the way you have practiced: in order, one check at a time.
Then the tune test: new tunes, sung back with no line.

## Step 1: Warm-up

A hum; a line on the screen follows your voice. It is not scored and can be skipped.

#### Try 1: Warm-up (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Warm up your voice. Hum a slow slide up and down, or hum through a straw. Keep it easy and quiet. There is nothing to match.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 8 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- It is not scored, stored or counted, and there is a "Skip the warm-up" link. It ends with "Warm-up done".
- Stored: nothing.

Then the next step: a warm-up has no break screen.

## Step 2: The seven checks, once more

Go in order and stop at the first check the story does not clear. That check is the cause, and its fix is the one thing to try:
1. Range: a note will not come, even on “oo” at an easy loudness, or comes out only as a rumble or air. Move the song.
2. Heard: the voice wandered looking for a note that was not in your head. Hear it first.
3. The note: under or over at any loudness, or falling short where the line climbs. Check it against a steady note and move to it. A note that is over only when you sing it loud, and on when you sing it lighter, passes this check: it goes to the next one.
4. Effort, only where the line climbs or gets loud: pushing (louder, sharp or strained), squeezing (tight and strangled, not louder) or cracking (a sudden break into a thin sound). Sing it lighter, on “oo” or through a straw first.
5. Air: the notes are right but the air runs out, is gasped, or is taken mid-word. Plan your breaths.
6. Sound: everything else is right, but the words are nasal, muffled or mumbled. Work on the words.
7. Nothing wrong: it just does not sound like the record. Leave it alone.

Pain or a hoarse voice comes before all of them: stop and rest. A new airy sound that does not clear counts as hoarse. None of the stories here has pain or a hoarse voice.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| The note check: under, loud or light. | The effort check: over or strained only when loud, on when light. |

Compare: Sing it lighter and listen again. If that puts it on the note, the effort raised it.

## Step 3: A worked example

Nina sings a song she knows well at karaoke. The verses match the recording. In the chorus she gets louder as the line climbs, and the top note comes out strained and a shade over. On “oo” at an easy loudness, the same note comes out clean and on.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**Which check finds what went wrong?**
- Is the song in my range?
- Did I hear the note before I sang it?
- Is the note on, under or over?
- Does it go sharp, strained or break only where the line climbs or gets loud?
- Does the air last the line?
- How do the words sound?
- Nothing is wrong

The working, one step at a time:
1. Range: does the top note come? On “oo” at an easy loudness it comes out clean, so it is in range.
1. Heard: did she know the note? She knows the song well and the verses match, so yes.
1. The note: off at any loudness? No: over only when loud, on when light. That clears the note check.
1. Effort: off only where it climbs and gets loud? Yes, over and strained there: the fourth check.
1. The fix for effort. Lighter and softer, on “oo” first.

Then the answer: Does it go sharp, strained or break only where the line climbs or gets loud? / Sing it lighter and softer, on “oo” or through a straw first, sliding up through any break. Sharp and strained only where it climbs loud, clean sung lighter: the effort check. Sing it lighter and softer, on “oo” first.
