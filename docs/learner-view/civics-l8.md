# US Civics & History: Whole stories

Revision 1, live. Part: Whole stories. Where the subject ends up: Answer the citizenship interview’s civics questions aloud from memory, and, reading a news story about a US law, order or ruling, say who acted, whether it was theirs to do, who can stop it, and whether to take the story as it stands or check who is behind it first.

## The why

A real story puts all four questions to you at once. Put them in this order, every time: who acted and at which level, was it theirs to do, which check is at work, and who is telling you.
Then you can say what happened, whether it was allowed, what comes next, and how far to trust the story.

## Step 1: The four questions

A plain table, "In this order":

> Question  |  What to do  (the column heads)
> 1. Who acted?  |  Name the level (federal, state, local) and what they did (wrote it, carried it out, judged it).
> 2. Was it theirs to do?  |  Find what lets them: a power the Constitution lists for Congress, a state power, what the state hands down to a city, or for the president their own powers and branch, and outside it what Congress said. Not settled only for the president or an agency; in court only when a court has the lawsuit now.
> 3. Which check is at work?  |  The one under way or set to happen: a veto, a lawsuit in court, a new law, a higher level’s law, the voters, an amendment. Not one the story calls far off.
> 4. Who is telling you?  |  Take a story as it stands only when both hold: you have already checked who is behind the source, or it is the original record you went to yourself, and what it says is ordinary. If you cannot place the source, or the story is alarming or sudden, check before you rely on it: leave the page and see what others say. Alarming or sudden means a big change to many people’s lives, money or rights, out of nowhere: every bank account frozen, all cash ended. The usual work of government is ordinary, even when it starts soon: a vote, a new fee, a road closed, new benches in a park.

## Step 2: A worked example

A news story, from "The Harbor Gazette":

> # President sends disaster teams to the coast
>
> The president ordered federal disaster teams to every town on the coast on Monday after a hurricane. A law Congress passed lets the president send federal help when a governor asks for it, and the governor asked. Some lawmakers say they will pass a new law that changes how the disaster money is spent, and a vote is planned for next week. A few residents said they might sue over slow help.

You read the Harbor Gazette most days, and last month you looked up who owns it.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**Which level of government acted?**
- Federal: the whole country
- State: one state
- Local: a city, town or county

The working, one step at a time:
1. Find who acted: The story says the president ordered federal disaster teams to the coast after a hurricane.
1. Name the level: The president is part of the federal government, which works for the whole country.
1. Name what they did: That is carrying out a rule or a law.
1. Find what lets them: A law of Congress lets the president do this when a governor requests it, and the governor did.
1. Find the check at work: Lawmakers have planned a vote on a new law that changes how the money is spent. A lawsuit is only talk.
1. Find who is telling you: You have checked this source, and the story is the ordinary work of government: both halves hold.

Then the answer: Federal: the whole country / Carries it out: does the work or enforces the rule / Theirs to do / Lawmakers passing a new law that changes or cancels it / Take it as it stands. The president, who leads the federal executive branch, acted under a law of Congress, and Congress can change that law.

>
> The House and the Senate passed a spending bill on Friday, with $5 billion for bridges. One senator mused about an amendment on a talk show. The Constitution gives Congress the power to tax and spend for the country. The president said she will veto the bill unless the bridge money is cut.

- State: one state — if chosen: Congress is part of the federal government, which works for the whole country.
- Local: a city, town or county — if chosen: Congress is part of the federal government, which works for the whole country.

**What did the one who acted do?**
- Writes the law or the rule (right)
- Carries it out: does the work or enforces the rule — if chosen: Congress passed a spending bill with $5 billion for bridges. That is writing a rule or a law, not carrying out a rule or a law.
- Judges: settles a dispute or says what a law means — if chosen: Congress passed a spending bill with $5 billion for bridges. That is writing a rule or a law, not judging a dispute.

**Was it theirs to do?**

- Not theirs to do — if chosen: The power is theirs, and nothing in the story stands against it.
- Not settled: the president or an agency reaching outside its branch where Congress has said nothing — if chosen: The story shows the power they act on, so nothing is left open.
- In court: a court is deciding it now — if chosen: No lawsuit about it is before a court now.

**Which check does the story show at work?**
- A veto, and a vote to override it (right)
- A court — if chosen: No lawsuit about it is before a court now.
- Lawmakers passing a new law that changes or cancels it — if chosen: No one in the story is at work on a new law now.
- A higher level’s law: federal over state and local, state over local — if chosen: No higher level’s law is running into it in the story.
- The voters — if chosen: No election in the story is coming where this is the question.
- An amendment to the Constitution — if chosen: One senator only mused about an amendment on a talk show; no one is working on one.

**Take it as it stands, or check before you rely on it?**
- Check before you rely on it (right)
- Take it as it stands — if chosen: Taking it as it stands needs both: a source you have already checked (or the original record), and an ordinary story. One of them is missing here.

After the answer: the deciding words are marked. Reason: Congress used its power to tax and spend, and the president’s veto could stop the bill.
The interview question this rests on: Name one power of the U.S. Congress. Accepted: Writes laws; Declares war; Makes the federal budget.
The working:
- Find who acted: The story says Congress passed a spending bill with $5 billion for bridges.
- Name the level: Congress is part of the federal government, which works for the whole country.
- Name what they did: That is writing a rule or a law.
- Find what lets them: Taxing and spending for the country is one of Congress’s listed powers.
- Find the check at work: The president can refuse to sign the bill, and Congress can pass it over the veto only with two-thirds of both houses.
- Find who is telling you: You cannot place the source. Leave the page and see who is behind it before you rely on it.
