A voice that is warmed up, watered and rested sings better tonight, and a hoarse voice gets worse if you push it. These are the facts worth holding, so that you can do the right thing without looking anything up.

### 2. Warm up first

*Unit Six · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 2 · Card 2 of 30*

[reviewers only: card kind `concept`, id `con-warmup`]

First, the five minutes before you sing anything that matters.

*Marcus at karaoke*

> Marcus gets to the karaoke bar at nine, orders a drink and sits down. When his name is called an hour later, he walks up and goes straight into the loudest song he knows, the one with the big high chorus. By the second chorus his voice is tight and scratchy, and the top notes will not come out. The next morning his voice feels tired and low.

Marcus asked a voice that had been sitting quiet for an hour for the loudest, highest part of the night. It tightened. A warm-up wakes the voice up first.

A warm-up takes about five minutes. Start with a hum. Then a lip trill: let your lips flutter loose as you blow air through them and make a sound. Then a gentle slide, one sound gliding slowly up and down. Sing all of it softly, in the easy middle of your voice: the notes you can sing without any effort.

The loud parts and the high notes wait until after the warm-up. Do it before anything that matters, karaoke included.

### 8. Water and your voice

*Unit Six · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 2 · Card 8 of 30*

[reviewers only: card kind `concept`, id `con-water`]

Next, what water does for your voice, and what dries it out.

*Ines before the concert*

> Ines sings in a choir. On the morning of the concert her voice feels dry, so five minutes before the first song she drinks a whole bottle of water. It changes nothing: her voice still feels dry on the first line. All week she has hardly drunk any water, and the rehearsal room has a heater that blows dry air.

Ines’s bottle of water did nothing for her voice. What you drink never touches your voice on its way down. It helps over hours, so water has to be drunk through the whole day.

Some things dry the voice out: dry air, smoke and alcohol. The heater in Ines’s rehearsal room was one of them.

A dry throat right now is a different job. Breathe steam or humid air (a hot shower is an easy way), and take a sip of water for your mouth.

## Part 2 of 2: A hoarse or tired voice, and practice

### 14. A hoarse or tired voice

*Unit Six · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 2 of 2 · Card 14 of 30*

[reviewers only: card kind `concept`, id `con-hoarse`]

Next, what to do when your voice is already hoarse or tired.

*Theo’s hoarse morning*

> Theo spends Saturday night at a loud party, shouting over the music and singing along to every song. On Sunday morning his voice is low and rough. All day he clears his throat, again and again, because it feels as if something is stuck there. That evening a friend asks him to sing one song together, and he says, ‘It will warm up as I go.’ It does not: by the end of the song his voice is rougher than it was in the morning.

Theo did two things that made his voice worse: he cleared his throat all day, and he sang when his voice was telling him to stop. A hoarse or tired voice needs the opposite.

It needs rest: no singing, and as little talking as you can. Do not push through it. When your throat feels thick, do not keep clearing it; swallow, or take a sip of water instead.

Singing should never hurt. If it does, stop for the day. Hoarseness that lasts more than two or three weeks, with no cold to explain it, is for a doctor, not for more practice.

### 22. Practice that sticks

Last, how to practice so that a song stays learned.

*Nadia’s Sunday hour*

> Nadia wants to sing a song at her daughter’s birthday on Saturday. On Sunday she practices for a whole hour, and by the end she sings the chorus the way she wants. Then she is busy for three days. On Wednesday she tries it again and most of it is gone: the tune wanders and she has to start over. She did not record any of it, so she has nothing to listen back to.

Nadia’s hour on Sunday did not stick. Ten to fifteen minutes on most days beats an hour once a week.

Work on one thing at a time, such as the first line or one tricky note. Record yourself and listen back: that is the only way to know whether it worked.

Learn a new song slowly, in the easy middle of your voice, on one sound (“la” or “oo”) before you add the words. Pick songs that sit where your voice is easy, and move the others lower.

### 29. What to carry away

*Unit Six · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 2 of 2 · Card 29 of 30*

[reviewers only: card kind `recap`, id `recap-care`]

You have now met every fact in the unit. Here they are together, with what to carry away.

**The warm-up**

- **How long does a warm-up take?** Five minutes is enough
- **What do you sing in a warm-up?** Soft hums, lip trills and slides, in the easy middle
- **When do the loud parts and the high notes come?** After the warm-up
- **Before which singing do you warm up?** Anything that matters, karaoke too

**Water, dry air and a dry throat**

- **When do you drink water for your voice?** Through the whole day, not only before you sing
- **What dries your voice out?** Dry air, smoke and alcohol
- **Your throat is dry right now. What helps?** Steam or humid air, and a sip of water for your mouth

**Rest, and when to stop**

- **What does a hoarse voice need?** Rest: no singing, and as little talking as you can
- **Your voice is hoarse and the song is not done. What do you not do?** Push through it
- **Your throat feels thick. What do you do instead of clearing it?** Swallow, or sip water
- **Singing starts to hurt. What do you do?** Stop for the day
- **When is hoarseness a matter for a doctor?** After more than two or three weeks, with no cold to explain it

**How to practice**

- **How much should you practice?** Ten to fifteen minutes, most days
- **How many things do you work on at once?** One at a time
- **How do you find out whether practice worked?** Record it and listen back
- **How do you learn a new song?** Slowly, in the easy middle, on “la” or “oo” first
- **Which songs do you pick, and what about the others?** Ones that sit where your voice is easy; move the others lower

**To carry away**

- Warm up for five minutes before you sing anything that matters, karaoke included, and keep it soft and easy until you are done.
- Water works over hours, so drink it through the day. For a dry throat right now, breathe steam or humid air and take a sip for your mouth.
- A hoarse voice needs rest. Do not push through it, do not keep clearing your throat, and stop for the day if it hurts.
- Ten to fifteen minutes most days beats an hour on Sunday. Work on one thing at a time, and record it.

### 30. A plan, if you want one

*Unit Six · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 2 of 2 · Card 30 of 30*

This card is for the night you sing and the morning your voice is rough. Fill it in or leave it.

A plan is one line: if this happens, I will do that. The minute before you sing, or the morning your voice is rough, is a bad time to work out what to do, and a good time to follow what you decided in advance.

The lines below are examples. You can use one, change it, or write your own two lines. Nothing is saved until you press the button.

- it is a night I know I will sing, warm up for five minutes first, softly, before any loud part or high note
- it is a hoarse morning, rest it and clear nothing: no singing, little talking, and a sip of water when my throat feels thick
- my throat is dry right now, breathe steam or humid air and take a sip of water for my mouth
- I want to learn a new song, practice ten or fifteen minutes most days, slowly on “la”, and record it
- singing starts to hurt, stop for the day

## After the unit: what comes back on later days
