# Geography: The frame of the United States

Revision 1, live. Part: The frame of the United States. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

Someone says a storm is coming up the Gulf Coast, or that they drove along the Rockies. If the country is one blank shape to you, neither tells you where. In about fifteen minutes you will tap its four regions, three coasts, four big ranges and the five Great Lakes: the frame every state hangs on.

## Step 1: How to find a place in the United States

The same three questions as for the world. Area: what bigger area is it in? Here the areas are the country's four regions, the West, the Midwest, the South and the Northeast. Touches: what is next to it? Water: what water is it on, the Pacific, the Atlantic, the Gulf of Mexico or a Great Lake? A coast is a shore; a range runs along a region; a lake sits on the Canadian border. The lower 48 means the states joined together, without Alaska and Hawaii. Next, one screen each for the regions and coasts, the ranges and the lakes; none of it stays up.

## Step 5: A worked example

The Gulf Coast


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of The United States with The Gulf Coast lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? A coast is a shore, the line where the land meets the water. This one is in the South, along the bottom of the country.
1. Touches: what is next to it? It runs from Texas in the west round to Florida in the east.
1. Water: what water is it on? Its water is the Gulf of Mexico, the sea at the bottom of the map. Tap the shore of that sea.

Then the answer: The Gulf Coast. The Gulf Coast is the shore on the Gulf of Mexico, from Texas round to Florida.

Rocky Mountains


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**

The working, one step at a time:

1. Touches: what is next to it? They run from Montana at the Canadian border down to New Mexico, about a third of the way in from the Pacific.
1. Water: what water is it on? They touch no sea: they are high inland. Tap the long range in the West.

## Step 7: A worked example

Lake Michigan


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of The United States with Lake Michigan lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? The Great Lakes sit in the Midwest and the Northeast, on the Canadian border, and run west to east.
1. Touches: what is next to it? Lake Michigan is the one that hangs down from the border, the only one wholly inside the United States.
1. Water: what water is it on? It is itself the water: the long lake pointing south, second from the left. Tap it.

Then the answer: Lake Michigan. Lake Michigan is the one Great Lake wholly in the United States, between Wisconsin, Illinois, Indiana and Michigan.
