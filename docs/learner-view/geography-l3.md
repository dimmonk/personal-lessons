# Geography: The West

Revision 1, live. Part: The West. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A friend is moving from Oregon to Colorado, or a fire is burning near the Nevada line. If the West is one big blank to you, you cannot picture either. In about fifteen minutes you will place the thirteen states of the West, Alaska and Hawaii among them, by finding the half of the West each sits in first, then name the ones most often mixed up and say what touches four of them.

## Step 1: How to find a state

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? What touches it? What water is it on? Here the area is the West, in two halves: the Pacific side (Washington, Oregon and California, with Alaska and Hawaii drawn in boxes) and the mountain half inland, from Montana down to New Mexico. Find the half first, then the state. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water.

## Step 3: A worked example

Nevada


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of The United States with Nevada lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? Nevada is in the West's mountain half, inland. Start from the West Coast, placed already.
1. Touches: what is next to it? The long state down the coast is California; Nevada is the one just east of it, in the middle of the coast.
1. Water: what water is it on? No sea or ocean touches it. Tap inside it.

Then the answer: Nevada. It is the one just east of California, with a slanted west side.
