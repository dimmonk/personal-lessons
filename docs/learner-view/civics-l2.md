# US Civics & History: In numbers, and why

Revision 1, live. Part: In numbers, and why. Where the subject ends up: Answer the citizenship interview’s civics questions aloud from memory, and, reading a news story about a US law, order or ruling, say who acted, whether it was theirs to do, who can stop it, and whether to take the story as it stands or check who is behind it first.

## The why

Many questions at the interview want a number or a reason: how many senators, how long a term, why a state has two. A number is easier to keep when you know the reason behind it.
Read the two tables once. Then you hear or read each question, say your answer out loud, and type it. You see every answer the list accepts.

## Step 1: The numbers side by side

A plain table, "The numbers":

> Body  |  How many  |  How long  |  Who picks them  (the column heads)
> Senate  |  100: two from each state  |  6 years  |  Citizens from their state
> House of Representatives  |  435 voting members  |  2 years  |  Citizens from their congressional district
> President  |  1  |  4 years, and no more than two terms  |  The Electoral College: electors from each state
> Supreme Court  |  9 justices; a majority of five (5) decides a case  |  For life  |  The president, with the Senate’s approval

The president’s Cabinet is made of Cabinet-level positions, mostly the heads of the federal departments: the Secretary of State, the Secretary of the Treasury, the Secretary of Labor, the Attorney General and others.
Look across each row once, then try the questions. If you are not sure, tap Not yet to see the answers.

## Step 2: The reason behind each

A plain table, "Why it is that way":

> The fact  |  The reason  (the column heads)
> Every state has two senators  |  Equal representation for small states. It came from the Great Compromise (also called the Connecticut Compromise): the House counts people, the Senate gives every state two seats.
> House terms are only 2 years  |  To more closely follow public opinion.
> Big states have more House members  |  Because of the state’s population: some states have more people.
> A president serves two terms at most  |  Because of the 22nd Amendment, to keep the president from becoming too powerful.
> The Electoral College elects the president  |  It decides who is elected president. It is a compromise between the popular election of the president and congressional selection: a middle way between a vote of all the people and Congress choosing.
> Justices serve for life  |  To be independent of politics, and to limit outside political influence.

The reasons are written here as the official list words them, so the words you learn are the words it accepts. Mind one pair: a senator represents the “people” or “citizens” of their state, but the list wants “citizens” for who elects them.
