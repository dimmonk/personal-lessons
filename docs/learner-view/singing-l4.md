# Singing: Hold it steady

Revision 1, live. Part: Hold it steady. Where the subject ends up: Sing a tune you have just heard, or a line of a song you know, in tune and at a height that suits your voice, with high notes that come out light instead of shouted; and when a line goes wrong, say which of a few things went wrong and what to try, or that nothing did.

## The why

Most people who sing for fun land near the note on average. But when researchers checked whether people sang the same note the same way each time, about half of those with no music training did not: the same note came out a little different on each try.
So one good try is not the test, and the checks here take several. Songs also often end a line on a long note, which has to stay where you put it. Holding a note trains that: land on it, keep your ear on it, and bring it back when it drifts.

## Step 1: Warm-up

A hum; a line on the screen follows your voice. It is not scored and can be skipped.

#### Try 1: Warm-up (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Warm up your voice. Hum a slow slide up and down, or hum through a straw. Keep it easy and quiet. There is nothing to match.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 8 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- It is not scored, stored or counted, and there is a "Skip the warm-up" link. It ends with "Warm-up done".
- Stored: nothing.

Then the next step: a warm-up has no break screen.

## Step 2: What steady means

Steady here means on the note, within half a half-step as before, without a break, for the seconds the try names. The app times the longest stretch you stay on.

**Hear it**

The first stays on one note. The second starts on the same note, starts to slip before a second is out, is off the note by about a second and a half, and ends a shade under. That one would not count as a 2-second hold, and the app would say it sank.

Buttons, one for each sound; nothing sounds until one is tapped. A tap plays it (the line under them reads "Playing. Tap to stop."), a second tap stops it, and moving on stops it:
- "Holding steady"
- "Sinking under"

A held note can go four ways: it stays steady; it sinks under as it goes on; it rises over as it goes on; or it was off from the first moment and stays off. Often it wanders off and comes back. Check it the way you checked a single note: against a steady one. If it does more than one, name the first thing you hear: a note off from the start is off from the start, whatever it does next.

Keep the loudness even from start to end. Pushing a note louder tends to make it go sharp, though a note can swell and stay on, or rise with no swell at all: only the check tells. If it drifts, nudge it back while you hold; do not stop.

## Step 3: A worked example

Ana holds the last note of “Happy Birthday” beside a piano app playing that same note. At first the two sound like one. After about a second they start to sound rough together, and by the end her note is a little lower than the piano’s.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**How did the held note go?**
- Steady on the note
- Sank under as it went on
- Rose over as it went on
- Off the note from the start
- Wandered off and back

The working, one step at a time:
1. Check the start. At first the two sound like one, so she started on the note.
1. Check the end. By the end hers is a little lower: it moved during the hold.

Then the answer: Sank under as it went on. On at first, lower by the end: it sank under as it went on. Keep your ear on the note for the whole hold, and nudge it back up as soon as you hear it drop.

## Step 5: Now hold it

The check wants 2 seconds. These groups go on to 4, so 2 will feel easy. Each starts with the line and then goes without it. Without the line you say where your note ended, under, on or over, before you see it. If the result says it sank or rose, that is which way to nudge it next time.

## Step 6: Tries

4 tries, listed, with help (the line and the notes drawn while singing). A missed try comes back at least three tries later, with a new note, until it is on the note.

When no range is stored yet, this group opens with the range exercise, and the group follows it:

#### First: Find your range (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Find your range. You will hum a slide up, then a slide down. Keep it easy: stop at the notes that still feel comfortable.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 7 seconds + 7 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- The two windows say: "Slide up to the highest note that is easy, and hold it." then "Now slide down to the lowest note that is easy, and hold it." The lowest and highest steady notes are saved as the learner's range, once. If the two ends are too close together: "I could not find two different easy notes. Slide further each way and hold each end for a moment, then try again." and nothing is saved.
- After the try: "Your range is saved". Reason: Every note you practice sits inside the range you just sang, so none is too high or too low.
- Nothing heard: "I did not hear you. Check that the microphone is on, then try again." The try is not counted and can be made again.
- Stored: numbers only (how much range was found); no sound is recorded or sent anywhere.
