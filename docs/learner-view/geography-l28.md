# Geography: East Asia and mainland Southeast Asia

Revision 1, live. Part: East Asia and mainland Southeast Asia. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

The news says a typhoon has hit Taiwan, the Mekong is running low, or a border crossing between the two Koreas has closed. If East Asia and Southeast Asia are one blur to you, you cannot picture where, who is next door, or what water is near. In about sixteen minutes you will place the eleven countries from Mongolia to Vietnam, with the Yangtze and the Mekong, on a blank map, by finding the area each sits in first, then name the four most often mixed up and say what touches them.

## Step 1: How to find a country

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? That tells you where on the map to look. What touches it? Its neighbors pin the exact spot, and they are what the news names. What water is it on? Coast or inland is often the point of the story. Here the areas are five. China and Mongolia fill the north and middle of East Asia. The Korean peninsula and Japan are its east edge. Taiwan is the island off China's southeast coast. Then Southeast Asia's mainland, in two views: Myanmar and Thailand in the west, and Laos, Cambodia and Vietnam in the east. The world map of part 1c calls all of Southeast Asia one part, islands included; here we take only its mainland, and part 29 takes the islands. Find the area first, then the country. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water.

## Step 5: Mainland Southeast Asia, the west: Myanmar and Thailand

Southeast Asia is the part of Asia below East Asia, and its mainland is five countries (Malaysia's strip hangs down below Thailand and comes in part 29).
