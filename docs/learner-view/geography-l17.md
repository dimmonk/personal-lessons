# Geography: Europe, north

Revision 1, live. Part: Europe, north. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A story names Estonia, Finland and the Baltic Sea, or says a ship left Ireland for Britain. If the north of Europe is one blur to you, you cannot picture who is next to whom. In about fifteen minutes you will place the ten countries of northern Europe on a blank map, by finding the area each sits in first, then name the four most often mixed up and say what touches each.

## Step 1: How to find a country

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? That tells you where to look. What touches it? Its neighbors pin the spot, and they are what the news names. What water is it on? An island, a coast or the inland is often the point of the story. These ten countries are the world map's Northern Europe, here split into three smaller areas so each is easier to find. The British Isles are two islands off the northwest coast: Ireland and the United Kingdom (some people in Ireland prefer to say Britain and Ireland). The Nordic countries are five: Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Finland and Iceland. The Baltic states are three small countries in a row on the Baltic Sea: Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania. Find the area first, then the country. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water. It takes about fifteen minutes: four views, three questions done for you, then the ten countries found, four named and four with what touches them.

## Step 2: The British Isles

Look for about half a minute at each of four views. Say each name as you find it. None of it stays up: next you find places on a map with no names. The United Kingdom is the eastern one, Great Britain, plus the top corner of the island of Ireland. The Irish Sea lies between them.
