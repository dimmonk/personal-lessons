# Geography: East Africa and the Horn

Revision 1, live. Part: East Africa and the Horn. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A story names Ethiopia and Eritrea, or says a ship was stopped in the Gulf of Aden, or that aid is crossing into South Sudan. If East Africa is one blur to you, you cannot picture who is next to whom. In about fifteen minutes you will place the twelve countries of East Africa and the Horn, with the Nile and Lake Victoria, on a blank map, by finding the area each sits in first, then name the four most often mixed up and say what touches each.

## Step 1: How to find a country

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? That tells you where to look. What touches it? Its neighbors pin the spot, and they are what the news names. What water is it on? A coast, an island or the inland is often the point of the story; here water means a sea, an ocean or one of the big lakes (Victoria, Tanganyika, Malawi), so a river or a small lake does not count. East Africa has four areas. The Horn is the point of land in the northeast: Ethiopia, Eritrea, Djibouti and Somalia. South Sudan lies along the Nile, south of Sudan. The Great Lakes are around Lake Victoria: Uganda, Rwanda and Burundi, with Kenya and Tanzania on the Indian Ocean beside them. Two island countries lie off the coast: Seychelles and Comoros. All twelve are East Africa on the world map of part 1b, but that map's East Africa runs on south to Mozambique: part 25 takes those southern countries (Mozambique, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Malawi, Madagascar and Mauritius) with the rest of the south. Find the area first, then the country. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water. It takes about fifteen minutes: four views, three questions done for you, then the fourteen places found, four named and four with what touches them.

## Step 2: The Horn

Look for about half a minute at each of four views. Say each name as you find it. None of it stays up: next you find places on a map with no names. The Horn is the point of land in the northeast that juts out into the Indian Ocean. Ethiopia is the big country inland. Eritrea, Djibouti and Somalia are its coast: Eritrea on the Red Sea in the north, tiny Djibouti at the mouth of the Red Sea, and Somalia along the tip.

## Step 4: The Great Lakes, Kenya and Tanzania

Lake Victoria is Africa's biggest lake, shared by Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania. Rwanda and Burundi are two small countries beyond its west shore, Rwanda to the north. Burundi lies on Lake Tanganyika, the long lake on its west, and Rwanda does not. Kenya and Tanzania are the two big countries on the Indian Ocean, Kenya the northern one.
