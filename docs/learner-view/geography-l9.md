# Geography: The cities east of the Mississippi

Revision 1, live. Part: The cities east of the Mississippi. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A report from Pittsburgh, a flight to Atlanta, a hurricane warning for Miami: if the city is a blank to you, so is the story. In about sixteen minutes you will place the 23 big cities east of the Mississippi on a blank map, by finding the state first and then the part of it and the water, then name the four most often mixed up from the lit dot.

## Step 1: How to find a city

The same questions as for the cities of the west. The state first, since you have placed every state; then the part of the state; then the water it stands on, a Great Lake, a river or the ocean. East of the Mississippi means by state: Wisconsin, Illinois, Kentucky, Tennessee, Mississippi and every state east of them, in six views. This east and west split is made only for these cities: the world map you met first puts all of them in one part, Northern America. The Northeast corridor is the one place where order helps: the big cities come up the coast one after another, Washington, D.C., Baltimore, Philadelphia, New York City, Boston. After every answer the feedback gives you the state and the part of it, the water, and what tells the city from the one it is mixed up with.

## Step 2: The Great Lakes

Look for about a minute at each of six views. Say each name as you find it. None of it stays up: next you find the cities on a map with no names.
