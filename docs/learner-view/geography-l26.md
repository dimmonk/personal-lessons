# Geography: South Asia

Revision 1, live. Part: South Asia. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A flood on the Indus, a border closed between India and Pakistan, a climbing team on a Himalayan peak: the news from South Asia names places most of us cannot picture. In about fifteen minutes you will place the eight countries of South Asia, with the Himalayas, the Indus and the Ganges, on a blank map, by finding the area each sits in first, then name the four most often mixed up and say what touches each.

## Step 1: How to find a country

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? That tells you where to look. What touches it? Its neighbors pin the spot, and they are what the news names. What water is it on? A coast, an island or the inland is often the point of the story; here water means a sea or an ocean; a river or a lake does not count. South Asia has four areas. The subcontinent is the great triangle of land south of the Himalayas: India in the middle, with Pakistan on its west and Bangladesh on its east. The northwest is the mountainous country of Afghanistan, above Pakistan. The Himalayas are the wall of mountains along the north, with Nepal and Bhutan in them. Two island countries lie in the Indian Ocean: Sri Lanka and the Maldives. Find the area first, then the country. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water. It takes about fifteen minutes: four views, three questions done for you, then the eleven places found, four named and four with what touches them.

Pakistan is the large country on the west, on the Arabian Sea; Afghanistan is the inland one above it; Bangladesh is the small one on the east, at the head of the Bay of Bengal.
