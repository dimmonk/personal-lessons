# Singing: Before you blame your voice: your range

Revision 1, live. Part: Before you blame your voice: your range. Where the subject ends up: Sing a tune you have just heard, or a line of a song you know, in tune and at a height that suits your voice, with high notes that come out light instead of shouted; and when a line goes wrong, say which of a few things went wrong and what to try, or that nothing did.

## The why

Most people who think they cannot sing can. In a study of more than 16,000 adults, only about 1 or 2 in 100 had true tone deafness. Yet about 1 in 5 of the others said they had no ear for music.
So the feeling is not the test. When a line goes wrong, run a few checks, in order, and fix one thing at a time. Here are the checks, then the first one: is the song in your range?

## Step 2: Seven checks, in order

Sam sings a song at karaoke and the chorus falls apart. He decides he has a bad voice. Start somewhere else: go through these checks in order, and stop at the first answer that explains it. The order puts first the causes that change the result most. It is a sensible order, not one a study has tested.

1. Is the song in my range? Mistakes grow on notes far from the ones you sing with ease.
2. Did I hear the note before I sang it? You cannot aim at a note that is not in your head.
3. Is the note on, under or over? Check it against a steady note. How it felt does not count.
4. Does it go sharp or strained only where the line climbs or gets loud? Sharp means over the note, too high. Pushing harder makes a note go sharp and sound strained. That is effort, not your ear.
5. Does the air last the line?
6. How do the words sound? Nasal, muffled or mumbled comes last.
7. Nothing is wrong. It just does not sound like the record.

Fix one thing at a time, the biggest first. And whatever you find, pain or a hoarse voice means stop and rest.

## Step 3: Range first

Lena sings along to a song on the radio. The verses are easy. The chorus jumps above the highest note she can sing with ease, and she has to shout to get there. Her ear is fine: that song sits too high for her voice. Moved lower, it fits.

Your range is the stretch from the lowest to the highest note you can sing with ease. To test a note, sing it on “oo” (as in “moon”) at an easy loudness, about as loud as you talk. A note in your range comes out without forcing. A note above it will not come without straining or shouting, however you try it. A note below it comes out as a rumble or only air. When a song goes outside your range, move the song. That is not a fault in you.

To move a song: many karaoke machines and music apps have a setting that moves the whole song lower or higher. Or pick a version of the song sung by a lower or higher voice, or, with friends, take the other part.

Now find yours. Hum a slide up to your highest easy note and hold it, then slide down to your lowest easy note and hold it. Easy notes, not the highest you can force.

## Step 4: Tries

1 try, listed. A missed try comes back at least three tries later, with a new note, until it is on the note.

#### Try 2: Find your range (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Find your range. You will hum a slide up, then a slide down. Keep it easy: stop at the notes that still feel comfortable.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 7 seconds + 7 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- The two windows say: "Slide up to the highest note that is easy, and hold it." then "Now slide down to the lowest note that is easy, and hold it." The lowest and highest steady notes are saved as the learner's range, once. If the two ends are too close together: "I could not find two different easy notes. Slide further each way and hold each end for a moment, then try again." and nothing is saved.
- After the try: "Your range is saved". Reason: Every note you practice sits inside the range you just sang, so none is too high or too low.
- Nothing heard: "I did not hear you. Check that the microphone is on, then try again." The try is not counted and can be made again.
- Stored: numbers only (how much range was found); no sound is recorded or sent anywhere.

Then a screen: you can stop here, your place is kept.

## Step 5: A worked example

Lena found her range with the hum: her easy notes run from a low hum up to a note a little above her talking voice. She sings along to a song she loves. The verses sit comfortably. The chorus climbs past the top of her easy hum. She tries the top note at an easy loudness on “oo”, and still she cannot get there without forcing.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**Is the song in their range?**
- In range: go on to the next check
- Too high: move it lower
- Too low: move it higher

The working, one step at a time:
1. Check the lowest notes. The verses sit comfortably, so the bottom of the song fits her range.
1. Check the highest notes. The chorus goes past the top of her easy hum.
1. Try the top note at an easy loudness, on “oo”. It will not come without forcing. A note in her range would come out without forcing.

Then the answer: Too high: move it lower. The top of the chorus will not come without forcing, even on “oo” at an easy loudness, so the song is too high for her; her voice is not the problem. Move it lower, then go on to the next check.

## Step 7: Away from the phone

Take a song you like to sing. Sing its lowest and highest notes on “oo” at an easy loudness. If one will not come without forcing, try the song lower or higher.
