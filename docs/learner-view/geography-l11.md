# Geography: The capitals of the South and the Northeast

Revision 1, live. Part: The capitals of the South and the Northeast. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

The news says "Albany raised a tax" or "Tallahassee passed a bill", and means the state. If you take the capital for the famous city, New York City or Miami, you misplace the story. In about sixteen minutes you will place the 25 capitals of the South and the Northeast on a blank map, by finding the state first, then the part of it, then name five from the lit state.

## Step 1: How to find a capital

A state's capital is where its government sits, and the news uses its name for the whole state. It is often not the biggest or the best-known city, so the city you know is the wrong answer for many states. The questions are the same as for any place: the state first, then the part of it, and one more: which bigger city is it mixed up with? The 25 capitals of the South and the Northeast come in five views, grouped as the states lessons group the states (Delaware and Maryland count as the South; the world map you met first does not divide the United States). Six are cities you already placed (Austin, Oklahoma City, Nashville, Atlanta, Raleigh and Boston); they come back as capitals. You tap a capital as its dot; the other way you see the state lit and type its capital. After every answer the feedback gives you the capital, its state and the part of it, and the bigger city it is not.
