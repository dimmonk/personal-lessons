# Geography: The Midwest

Revision 1, live. Part: The Midwest. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

The news says floods hit Iowa and Missouri, or a plant is closing in Ohio. If the middle of the country is a blur, you lose the story. In about fifteen minutes you will place the twelve states of the Midwest, by finding its half first, name the ones most often mixed up and say what touches four of them.

## Step 1: How to find a state

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? What touches it? What water is it on? Here the area is the Midwest, in two halves: the Great Lakes states (Ohio to Minnesota, each touching a lake) and the Plains states west of them (the two Dakotas down to Kansas, with Iowa and Missouri between the rivers). Find the half first, then the state. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water.

## Step 3: A worked example

Iowa


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of The United States with Iowa lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? Iowa is in the Midwest, on the Plains side. Start from the Mississippi, placed already.
1. Touches: what is next to it? Iowa is the state just west of the river, about a third of the way down from Canada.
1. Water: what water is it on? It sits between two rivers, the Mississippi on its east and the Missouri on its west, and touches no lake or sea. Tap inside it.

Then the answer: Iowa. It sits between two rivers, the Mississippi on its east and the Missouri on its west. Not Idaho, far west in the mountains.
