# Singing: Short tunes

Revision 1, live. Part: Short tunes. Where the subject ends up: Sing a tune you have just heard, or a line of a song you know, in tune and at a height that suits your voice, with high notes that come out light instead of shouted; and when a line goes wrong, say which of a few things went wrong and what to try, or that nothing did.

## The why

A short tune is where the notes, the steady hold and the jumps come together.
Most people who sing for fun sing a tune more in tune when they slow down. So learn a new tune slowly, then sing it at its speed.

## Step 1: Warm-up

A hum; a line on the screen follows your voice. It is not scored and can be skipped.

#### Try 1: Warm-up (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Warm up your voice. Hum a slow slide up and down, or hum through a straw. Keep it easy and quiet. There is nothing to match.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 8 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- It is not scored, stored or counted, and there is a "Skip the warm-up" link. It ends with "Warm-up done".
- Stored: nothing.

Then the next step: a warm-up has no break screen.

## Step 2: Slow, then at speed

Hear the whole tune in your head first, then sing it note by note. The last note is long: keep it steady for 2 seconds.

The groups go: three notes played slowly, first with the line and then without; four notes played slowly; four notes at their speed; then five notes at their speed. Each try says when it is played slowly.

If you lose the tune partway, do not push on. Go back to the slow speed, hear the tune again, and sing the part you lost on its own before the whole tune.

Checked note by note, a short tune can go five ways:
On all the way.
One jump fell short: every note on except the far note of a big jump, which stops short of it.
The long last note sank: all on until the last, which slips lower while it is held.
Lost the tune partway: sure at first, then unsure of the next note, and the voice wanders.
The whole tune sat off the same way: every note under, or every note over, or drifting further off as it goes. Check the first note against a steady one; if the first was right, check each note after it the same way, slowly.
If more than one shows, fix the first one you hear.

## Step 3: A worked example

Zoe learns the first line of a lullaby, five notes with a big jump up in the middle. She checks it note by note against a piano app. The first two notes match. The note after the jump is a little under. The rest match, and the last note holds.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**Where did the tune go wrong?**
- On all the way
- One jump fell short
- The long last note sank
- Lost the tune partway
- The whole tune sat off the same way

The working, one step at a time:
1. Check each note, not only the last. Two on, then the note after the jump under, then the rest on.
1. Find where it went wrong. The one miss is the far note of the big jump, a little short of it.

Then the answer: One jump fell short. Only the far note of the jump was off, and short of it: one jump fell short. Hear that jump on its own first and aim for the far note on purpose.
