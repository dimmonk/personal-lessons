# Geography: The flags of South America

Revision 1, live. Part: The flags of South America. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A flag in a news photo, on a shirt at a match or on a ship tells you whose it is only if you can read it. In about twelve minutes you will read the flags of the twelve countries of South America, by the stripes and the colors, and tell the ones that look alike: Colombia, Venezuela and Ecuador, and Argentina and Uruguay.

## Step 1: How to read a flag

Read it the same way every time. First see which way the stripes run, flat or upright: that splits most of these flags in two. Then say the colors in order, from the top, or the left if upright, since look-alikes share colors. Then look for a mark, a star, a sun or a coat of arms, which settles what the stripes do not. Then check the one line that tells it from the flag it is mistaken for: that is where the misses happen. Last, find the country on the map by the three questions: its area, what touches it, its water. Next, the twelve flags in three groups. French Guiana is not here: it is a part of France and flies the French flag.

## Step 2: Flags of yellow, blue and red stripes

Look for about a minute at each of three groups. Say each name as you read its flag. These three have flat stripes of the same three colors.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇨🇴 Colombia; 🇻🇪 Venezuela; 🇪🇨 Ecuador.

Colombia: Three flat stripes, yellow, blue and red, the yellow twice as wide as each of the others, with no mark on it. Venezuela and Ecuador have the same colors with a mark.

Venezuela: Three equal flat stripes, yellow, blue and red, with a small coat of arms at the left of the yellow one and an arc of eight white stars on the blue one. Colombia has no mark; Ecuador has a larger coat of arms in the middle and no stars.

Ecuador: Three flat stripes, yellow, blue and red, the yellow twice as wide, with a coat of arms in the middle of the flag. Colombia has no mark; Venezuela has stars on the blue.

## Step 3: Other flat stripes

These five have flat stripes too, in other colors. Bolivia (red, yellow, green), Paraguay (red, white, blue) and Suriname (a wide red middle) have no look-alike here; their colors tell them.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇧🇴 Bolivia; 🇵🇾 Paraguay; 🇦🇷 Argentina; 🇺🇾 Uruguay; 🇸🇷 Suriname.

Bolivia: Three flat stripes, red, yellow and green from the top; the yellow one may carry a coat of arms.

Paraguay: Three flat stripes, red, white and blue, with a round emblem in the white one (a different emblem on each side of the flag).

Argentina: Three flat stripes, light blue, white and light blue, with a yellow sun in the white one. Uruguay has nine stripes.

Uruguay: Nine flat stripes, white and blue in turn, with a white square in the top corner holding a yellow sun. Argentina has three stripes.

Suriname: Five flat stripes, green, white, a wide red, white and green, with a yellow star in the red.

## Step 4: Upright stripes, shapes and a corner

These four are built in four different ways: Peru has upright stripes, Brazil a diamond, Chile two halves with a square in the corner, Guyana two arrowheads. Each has a mark that no other flag here has.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇵🇪 Peru; 🇧🇷 Brazil; 🇨🇱 Chile; 🇬🇾 Guyana.

Peru: Three upright stripes, red, white and red; the white one may carry a small coat of arms.

Brazil: A green flag with a big yellow diamond, and in it a blue circle with white stars and a white band.

Chile: Two flat halves, white on top and red below, with a blue square in the top corner holding one white star.

Guyana: A green flag with a long yellow arrowhead pointing to the right, and a red arrowhead inside it, with thin white and black edges.
