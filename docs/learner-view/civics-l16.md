# US Civics & History: The interview

Revision 1, live. Part: The interview. Where the subject ends up: Answer the citizenship interview’s civics questions aloud from memory, and, reading a news story about a US law, order or ruling, say who acted, whether it was theirs to do, who can stop it, and whether to take the story as it stands or check who is behind it first.

## The why

At the interview an officer puts up to 20 of the 128 questions to you aloud. You answer from memory, with no choices to pick from. Twelve right passes, and the officer stops once you have 12 right or 9 wrong.
Here you take the same test: 20 questions drawn at random from all 128. Aim for 16, so a bad day still passes. A week later you take a second one, on other questions.

## Step 1: Before you start

Some answers change with elections and appointments: the president, your senators and the others. At the interview you give the name of the person serving on that day. Check them on the page below.

A value looked up, never memorized: "The page that posts the answers that change with elections and appointments": uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates. It prints "Looked up 2026-10-11" and "Source: https://www.uscis.gov/citizenship/find-study-materials-and-resources/check-for-test-updates", says to check the source, and after a year says to look it up again.

Your own answers are on the subject’s page, under “Your answers and interview date”; change them there if one is out of date. A question about your own answers is left out of the mock until you have entered it, so you can start now either way. After the mock, each question you missed names where to go over it.
