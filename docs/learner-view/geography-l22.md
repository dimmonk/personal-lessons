# Geography: West Africa, the coast

Revision 1, live. Part: West Africa, the coast. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A story says a ship left Ghana, or that thousands crossed from Guinea into Sierra Leone. If the coast of West Africa is one blur to you, you cannot picture who is next to whom. In about fourteen minutes you will place the eleven countries and the islands of the West African coast on a blank map, by finding the area each sits in first, then name the four most often mixed up and say what touches each.

## Step 1: How to find a country

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? That tells you where to look. What touches it? Its neighbors pin the spot, and they are what the news names. What water is it on? Coast, inland or island is often the point of the story. West Africa is the bulge on the west side of Africa. These twelve places, eleven countries and the islands of Cape Verde, all have a coast on the Atlantic, and they are part of the world map's West Africa. They fall in four areas. The west corner is where the coast faces west: Senegal, the Gambia and Guinea-Bissau. Below it the coast runs southeast: Guinea, Sierra Leone and Liberia. Then the coast turns and runs east along the Gulf of Guinea: Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin and Nigeria. The fourth area is Cape Verde, islands out in the Atlantic. The countries behind the coast, such as Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger, have no coast and are not asked here. Mauritania, north of Senegal, is also in West Africa and has an Atlantic coast; it is on the map of the Sahel that comes next. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water. It takes about fourteen minutes: three views, three questions done for you, then the twelve places found, four named and four with what touches them.

## Step 2: The west corner and Cape Verde

Look for about a minute at each of three views. Say each name as you find it. None of it stays up: next you find places on a map with no names. Senegal is at the far west tip of the mainland. The Gambia is a thin strip inside it, along a river. Guinea-Bissau is the small country just south of Senegal. Cape Verde is a group of islands far out in the Atlantic, west of Senegal, with no neighbor on land.

## Step 4: The Gulf of Guinea coast

At the end of Liberia the coast turns and runs east along the Gulf of Guinea. Five countries share it, west to east: Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin and Nigeria. Togo and Benin are thin. Burkina Faso lies behind Ghana, Togo and Benin and has no coast.
