# Scams & Social Engineering: What does it want?

Revision 4, live. Part: What does it want?. Where the subject ends up: When a message, call or offer asks you to act, you stop, see what it wants, and check it through a channel you already had before doing anything, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, you make the first calls at once.

## The why

A text, an email, a call or a pop-up lands on your phone, and it looks real or it does not. Before anything else, find out what it wants from you.
You tap the line that wants something, then say what it wants.

## Step 1: Five things a message can want

A message only becomes something to decide when it wants something from you. People who are good at this get suspicious when a message wants them to do something. How it looks cannot settle it, because a scam can copy all of it.
Here you only name what a message wants. A real bill wants money too: what to do about it comes in the next lessons.

Money. To pay, send or buy something: a bill, a fee, a transfer, gift cards. A sign-in or a code. To type your password, or to type or read out a code. A code works like a password. Your details. Your address, birthday, Social Security number or card number. Your phone or computer. To install an app, open a file, or let someone see your screen. Nothing. It only tells you something.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: It has the right logo and my name, so it must be real.
The right line: How it looks tells you nothing, because a scam can copy all of it. What it wants is what you can read.

## Step 2: A worked example

You have an account with Ashgrove Bank. A text comes in while you eat lunch.

A text message: "Ashgrove Bank", Text message · 12:40 PM.

> We saw a sign-in from a new phone.
> Sign in at the link now to keep your account open.
> (the link in it, drawn as words and never one that can be followed: ashgrove-verify.example/in)

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**What does it want?**
- Money
- A sign-in or a code
- Your details
- Your phone or computer
- Nothing

The working, one step at a time:
1. Read every line.: The first line tells you something. The second line tells you to do something.
1. Find the line that wants something from you.: “Sign in at the link now” is the one.
1. Say what it wants.: Signing in means typing your password. So it wants a sign-in.

Then the answer: A sign-in or a code / Sign in at the link now to keep your account open. The second line is the one that wants something: your sign-in, typed on the page the link opens. How polished the text looks does not change what it wants.
