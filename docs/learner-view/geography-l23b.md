# Geography: The flags of the Sahel and Central Africa

Revision 1, live. Part: The flags of the Sahel and Central Africa. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A flag in a news photo or at a match tells you whose it is only if you can read it. In about twelve minutes you will read the twelve flags from Mauritania to the two Congos and tell the ones that look alike.

## Step 1: How to read a flag

Read it the same way every time. Count the stripes and see which way they run, upright, flat or across the corner: that alone splits these flags into groups. Say the colors in order, from the left or the top: look-alikes share colors in a different order. Look for a mark, a star, a crescent or a triangle. Check the line that tells it from the flag it is mistaken for: that is where the misses happen. Last, find the country on the map by its area, what touches it and its water; part 23 placed the twelve. Next, the twelve flags in four groups; none of it stays up.

## Step 2: Flags of upright stripes

Look at each of four groups for about a minute. These stripes stand upright, and all three have yellow.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇲🇱 Mali; 🇹🇩 Chad; 🇨🇲 Cameroon.

Mali: Three upright stripes, green, yellow and red, with the green on the left. Chad has blue on the left.

Chad: Three upright stripes, blue, yellow and red, with the blue on the left. Mali has green on the left.

Cameroon: Three upright stripes, green, red and yellow, with a yellow star in the red. Mali has yellow in the middle and no star.

## Step 3: Flags of flat stripes

These four lie flat.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇳🇪 Niger; 🇬🇦 Gabon; 🇬🇶 Equatorial Guinea; 🇸🇹 São Tomé and Príncipe.

Niger: Three flat stripes, orange, white and green, with an orange circle in the white.

Gabon: Three flat stripes, green, yellow and blue, with nothing else on them. Equatorial Guinea has red and a triangle.

Equatorial Guinea: Three flat stripes, green, white and red, with a blue triangle at the left and an emblem in the white.

São Tomé and Príncipe: Three flat stripes, green, a wider yellow and green, with two black stars and a red triangle at the left.

## Step 4: Flags with a star or a crescent

These three are flat bands with a star; Mauritania's star sits over a crescent.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇧🇫 Burkina Faso; 🇲🇷 Mauritania; 🇨🇫 Central African Republic.

Burkina Faso: Two flat bands, red over green, with a yellow star in the middle. Mauritania is green with a crescent.

Mauritania: Green with a red band along the top and the bottom, and a yellow star above an upward-pointing yellow crescent. Burkina Faso has no crescent.

Central African Republic: Four flat bands, blue, white, green and yellow, cut by an upright red band, with a yellow star at the top left.

## Step 5: Flags with a diagonal band

Only the two Congos have a band across the corner.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇨🇬 Republic of the Congo; 🇨🇩 Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Republic of the Congo: A yellow band cut diagonally between green at the top left and red at the bottom right.

Democratic Republic of the Congo: Sky blue with a red diagonal band edged in yellow, from the bottom left to the top right, and a yellow star at the top left.
