# Basic Math: Packs, leftovers and lining up

Revision 1, live. Part: Packs, leftovers and lining up. Where the subject ends up: Work out the number a situation needs (a total, a price per one, a scaled amount, a percent or the price before one, how much paint or soil to buy, how high a ladder reaches, what savings grow to or a loan costs) and tell whether it is sensible, when a receipt, a recipe, a sale tag, a room or an offer hands you some numbers and a question and nobody names the method.

## The why

Hot dogs come 10 to a pack and buns 8 to a pack, buses seat 52, and party bags should all be the same. Whole things do not split, so the answer has to come out even.
Here you round up to hold everyone, make equal groups with nothing left, and buy packs that come out even.

## Step 1: Whole things: round up, groups and packs

Whole things: 230 people ÷ 52 seats = 4.42 buses. The part after the point is people with no seat yet, so book 5 buses: to hold everyone, round up, as when you buy paint. 4 full buses carry 4 × 52 = 208, so 22 ride the fifth, which has 52 − 22 = 30 empty seats.
Equal groups with nothing left over: the number of groups must divide both amounts. Try what divides the smaller amount, from the biggest down, until one also divides the other. 24 pencils and 36 stickers: 24 does not divide 36, 12 does. So 12 bags, each with 2 pencils and 3 stickers.
Packs that come out even: count up by the bigger pack until the smaller one divides it. Hot dogs in 10s, buns in 8s: 10, 20, 30, 40, and 40 ÷ 8 = 5. Buy 40: 4 packs of hot dogs and 5 of buns.
Telling them apart: “the most, all the same” is groups, so split down, and the answer is no bigger than the smaller amount; “the fewest that come out even” or “next together” is packs, so count up, and the answer is at least the bigger pack. To list what divides a number, try 1, 2, 3 … up to its middle: 30 is 1 × 30, 2 × 15, 3 × 10, 5 × 6.
Why it works: every whole number is built by multiplying primes, the numbers only 1 and themselves divide (2, 3, 5, 7, 11 …). 10 = 2 × 5 and 8 = 2 × 2 × 2, and 40 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 5 holds the pieces of both and nothing more.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: “Hot dogs in 10s and buns in 8s: buy 10 × 8 = 80 of each.”
The right line: 80 comes out even but is twice what you need: 40 is the first number both packs divide.

## Step 2: A worked example

A plain table, "Field trip to the science museum":

> Students and adults going  |  230
> Each bus  |  seats 52
> Each bus, for the day  |  $650
> Leaves  |  8:15 a.m.

The working, one step at a time:
1. Say what is asked and what is given. Asked: how many buses to book, then the empty seats on the last one. Given: 230 people and 52 seats a bus. The cost and the time do not matter here.
1. Make a rough answer in your head. About 230 people ÷ about 50 seats: about 4.6 busloads.
1. Work it out exactly on the calculator, then look back. 230 ÷ 52 = 4.42: 4 full buses and some people left over, so 5 buses. Always round up, or people stay behind. Look back: near the rough 4.6, rounded up.
1. Look at the last bus. 4 full buses carry 4 × 52; the other 22 ride the last bus. On the calculator: 5 × 52 − 230 = shows 30 empty seats.

Then the answer: 4.6 busloads / 5 buses / 30 empty seats. 230 ÷ 52 = 4.42, so 5 buses: 4 full, and 22 people on the last, which leaves 30 seats empty.
