# Scams & Social Engineering: Bills and payments

Revision 1, live. Part: Bills and payments. Where the subject ends up: When a message, call or offer asks you to act, you stop, see what it wants, and check it through a channel you already had before doing anything, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, you make the first calls at once.

## The why

Some scams copy a bill you really owe, or a payment you really make. The money goes to the wrong place.
You see each script beside the real payment, and choose what to do.

## Step 1: A bill whose bank details changed

| A | B |
|---|---|
| An email: "Brightline Roofing", Email.  > Subject: **Your invoice** > > Our bank has changed. Pay the $4,200 to the new account below. | An email: "Brightline Roofing", Email.  > Subject: **Your invoice** > > Invoice attached: $4,200, to the account on your quote. |

Compare: The same bill. The first changes where the money goes.

A change of where to send money is checked every time, on the number you already had for them: the one on the quote, the lease or the bill. Never on the email’s number or a reply.

## Step 2: A small fee by link

| A | B |
|---|---|
| A text message: "Westway Tolls", Text message.  > You have an unpaid toll of $6.85. Pay today at the link. > (the link in it, drawn as words and never one that can be followed: westway-tollpay.example/pay) | A pop-up on a screen: "Westway Toll Pass", In the app, after you tapped “Add money”.  > Add $25 from your saved card? > [a button: "Add $25"] |

Compare: The first comes to you with a link. The second answers what you just tapped.

A small sum makes it feel not worth checking. The page is after your card number, not the $6.85.

## Step 3: An official who wants gift cards or crypto

| A | B |
|---|---|
| An incoming call, the caller's words as a transcript: "Marlow County Sheriff", Incoming call.  > Pay the $800 fine in gift cards in the next hour, or officers come to your home. | A pop-up on a screen: "Marlow County Treasurer", On the county site, typed from your tax bill.  > Property tax due by November 30: $1,240. Pay from your bank account. > [a button: "Pay"] |

Compare: The first wants gift cards in an hour. The second is a page you opened, with weeks to pay.

No government office or honest company insists you pay in gift cards, crypto or a wire, and an honest one gives you time. A government office has your records, so treat its name like your bank’s: hang up, and call it on a number you know, from a letter it sent or its own website that you type.

## Step 4: The buyer who pays too much

| A | B |
|---|---|
| An email: "Buyer", Email.  > Subject: **Your bike** > > I sent a check for $750 by mistake. Deposit it and send back $450. | A chat, with its history: "Seller on Bramblecote Market", Chat, after your offer of $40.  > (Seller on Bramblecote Market, at the left) Deal at $40. Tap Pay in the app. |

Compare: The first sends you more than the price and wants some back. The second is the price you offered, in the app.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: The money showed up in my account, so the check is good.
The right line: It can take weeks for a bank to find a fake check. Then you pay all of it back, and the money you sent is gone.

## Step 5: A worked example

Brightline Roofing put a new roof on your house this week. The signed quote says to pay $4,200 to the account printed on it, and has Brightline’s phone number.

An email: "Brightline Roofing", Email · Thursday 4:10 PM.

> Subject: **Your invoice**
>
> Thanks again for choosing Brightline.
> Our bank has changed. Please pay the $4,200 to the new account below, not the one on the quote.
> Call us at 1-555-0148 with any questions.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**What do you do?**
- Do what it says
- Check first, a way I already had
- Leave it

The working, one step at a time:
1. See what it wants. It wants $4,200, paid to a new account.
1. See whether it is what you agreed. You agreed to pay the account on the quote. This wants a different one.
1. See whether you deal with them. Yes: Brightline did your roof, and you have its number on the quote.
1. Choose. Check first: call Brightline on the quote’s number. The email’s number and a reply both go to whoever wrote it.

Then the answer: Check first, a way I already had / Call Brightline on the number printed on the signed quote. A new account to pay was not what you agreed, and it uses the name of a company you deal with, so you check on the number on the quote. A change of where to send money is the line to check, every time.

## Step 6: Questions

6 questions, listed, with each inner pair asked one after the other, in a random order. A missed question comes back at least three questions later, until it is right. After each answer: the right answer, the deciding words, the reason, and one line on the learner's own choice if it was wrong.

#### Item 1

You have a Westway Toll Pass account and its app is on your phone.

A text message: "Westway Tolls", Text message · 7:48 AM.

> You have an unpaid toll of $6.85.
> Pay today at the link to avoid a $50 late fee.
> (the link in it, drawn as words and never one that can be followed: westway-tollpay.example/pay)

**What do you do?**
- Do what it says — if chosen: The page took your card number, and charges you never made began to show up.
- Check first, a way I already had (right)
- Leave it — if chosen: You are safe, but it uses the name of your toll account, and the app would have told you in a minute.

**How do you check?** (opens only after a certain answer)
- The Westway app on my phone (right)
- The link in the text I got — if chosen: The link came with the message, so the sender decides where it goes. A copy of a real page looks the same.
- Reply to the text and get the toll’s date — if chosen: You would be asking the sender, who answers its own question.

After the answer: the deciding words are marked. Reason: You did not start it, and it uses the name of an account you have, so you go in through the app. A small sum makes it feel not worth checking, and that is the point: the page wants your card number.

## The check

9 new questions, no help. No answer is shown until the end. The questions come in mixed order.

You have 20 seconds for each. The clock only shows the time: nothing moves on until you tap Next. A call is not on a clock.

To pass:
- None did what a scam asked
- None checked through the link, number or reply the message gave
- At most 1 wrong among real messages
