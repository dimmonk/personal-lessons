# US Civics & History: The 1900s to now

Revision 1, live. Part: The 1900s to now. Where the subject ends up: Answer the citizenship interview’s civics questions aloud from memory, and, reading a news story about a US law, order or ruling, say who acted, whether it was theirs to do, who can stop it, and whether to take the story as it stands or check who is behind it first.

## The why

Nineteen questions cover the 1900s and after: the wars and why the country went in, hard times at home, and the fight for civil rights.
A war is easier to remember with its reason, and an event with its place in time. Use the same moves as before.

## Step 1: The wars, and why the country went in

A plain table, "Each war with its reason":

> When  |  War  |  Why the United States went in  (the column heads)
> 1917  |  World War I  |  Germany attacked U.S. ships; “to support the Allied Powers (England, France, Italy, and Russia)” against the Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, and Bulgaria)
> December 7, 1941  |  World War II  |  Japanese attacked Pearl Harbor; “to support the Allied Powers (England, France, and Russia)” against the Axis Powers (Germany, Italy, and Japan)
> June 1950  |  Korean War  |  To stop the spread of communism: communist North Korea attacked the South
> 1964 and 1965  |  Vietnam War  |  To stop the spread of communism: Congress backed the president in 1964, and the first ground combat troops went in 1965
> 1990 and 1991  |  Persian Gulf War  |  To force the Iraqi military from Kuwait: Iraq took Kuwait in August 1990, and the U.S.-led war began in January 1991
> October 2001  |  War in Afghanistan, part of the War on Terror  |  Terrorists attacked the United States on September 11, 2001
> After 2001  |  War in Iraq  |  Another conflict after the attacks; the list gives no reason for it

| A | B |
|---|---|
| World War I: Germany attacked U.S. ships. | World War II: Japanese attacked Pearl Harbor. |

Compare: Both began, for the United States, with an attack, and both times it joined the side called the Allied Powers. Keep the attacker with its war: Germany and ships for the first, Japan and Pearl Harbor for the second.

## Step 2: At home

A plain table, "The country between and after the wars":

> When  |  What happened  (the column heads)
> 1920, after World War I  |  With the 19th Amendment, all women get the right to vote by law.
> 1929  |  The Great Crash, the stock market crash of 1929, starts the Great Depression. Say what it was in the list’s words: the longest economic recession in modern history.
> The Depression and World War II  |  Franklin Roosevelt is president.
> World War II and after  |  Dwight Eisenhower: a general during World War II, then the 34th president, at the end of the Korean War
> The Cold War  |  The main rival is the Soviet Union; the main concerns are communism and nuclear war.
> The civil rights movement  |  Say what it did in the list’s words: fought to end racial discrimination (treating people worse for their race). Martin Luther King, Jr.: fought for civil rights; worked for equality for all Americans.

Two more questions are not tied to a date. An American Indian tribe: Cherokee, Navajo, Sioux, Apache and many more. An American innovation: the light bulb, the automobile, the airplane, the assembly line, landing on the moon.
