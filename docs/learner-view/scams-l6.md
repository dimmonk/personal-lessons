# Scams & Social Engineering: Money to strangers

Revision 4, live. Part: Money to strangers. Where the subject ends up: When a message, call or offer asks you to act, you stop, see what it wants, and check it through a channel you already had before doing anything, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, you make the first calls at once.

## The why

Some of the money that is lost goes to someone met only online, to a fee that is said to release a prize, a loan or a job, or to someone who offers to get lost money back.
You see each script beside what is real, and choose what to do.

## Step 1: Someone you only know online

| A | B |
|---|---|
| A chat, with its history: "Mei", Chat on the hobby forum.  > (Mei, at the left) I made $4,000 this month with a trading app. Put in $500 at this link. > (the link in it, drawn as words and never one that can be followed: trade-mei.example/join) | A text message: "Luis (brother)", Text message, after you agreed at dinner to pay half.  > Half the pizza came to $20. Send it in PayLane like you said. |

Compare: You know Luis and agreed this with him. Mei you know only online, and you agreed nothing.

Someone you know only online is not someone you know: their name, photos and stories all came from them, and a friend in common you also met online changes nothing. So send no money, and tell someone you know in person. Crypto sent to their wallet or app has none of a card’s protections.

## Step 2: A fee to get something

| A | B |
|---|---|
| An email: "Prize Office", Email · Friday.  > Subject: **You won** > > Congratulations, you won $10,000. Pay a $200 fee to claim it. > (the link in it, drawn as words and never one that can be followed: prize-office-claim.example/fee) | A text message: "Harbor Café", Text message, after you bought a raffle ticket there.  > Your ticket won the $50 gift card! Reply with your name to collect it. |

Compare: The first wants you to pay before you get anything. The second you entered yourself, and it costs nothing.

Real prizes are free. A fee called “insurance” before a loan is the trick. No honest employer has you deposit a check and send part of it on.

## Step 3: A fee to get your money back

| A | B |
|---|---|
| An incoming call, the caller's words as a transcript: "Fund Recovery", Incoming call · 11:30 AM.  > We saw your report, and we can get your $800 back. Pay a $150 fee first. | Tell your bank, on the number on your card. Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Neither one charges you. |

Compare: The first wants money to get money back. The second costs you nothing.

Nobody honest who calls you wants a fee up front to get your money back. That offer is another scam: hang up.

## Step 4: Someone you know wants money

A sister, friend or grandson wants money you had not agreed. It uses a name you know, so you check: call them on the number you already have. When they answer on that number, in their own voice, and the story fits, that settles it. If anything is off, call someone else in the family.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: It came from my sister’s own number, so it must be her.
The right line: Phones and chat accounts can be taken over, so a text from her number proves nothing. Your call to the number you have is the check.

## Step 5: A worked example

You met Mei on a hobby forum three months ago. You have never met her or heard her voice.

A chat, with its history: "Mei", Chat on the hobby forum.

> (Mei, at the left) I made $4,000 this month with a trading app.
> (Mei, at the left) Put in $500 at this link and I will show you how.
> (the link in it, drawn as words and never one that can be followed: trade-mei.example/join)

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**What do you do?**
- Do what it says
- Check first, a way I already had
- Leave it

The working, one step at a time:
1. See what it wants. It wants $500, put in through a link.
1. See whether you started it. You did not. A friend you only know online came with a way to make money.
1. See whether you know her. Only online. Her name, her photos and her app all came from her, so she does not count as someone you know.
1. Choose. Leave it: send nothing. Then tell someone you know in person.

Then the answer: Leave it / Tell someone I know in person about it. Someone you know only online is not someone you know, so you leave it. Because she wants money, you also tell someone you know in person.

## Step 6: Questions

4 questions, listed, with each inner pair asked one after the other, in a random order. A missed question comes back at least three questions later, until it is right. After each answer: the right answer, the deciding words, the reason, and one line on the learner's own choice if it was wrong.

#### Item 1

You have been chatting with Daniel on a dating site for two months. You have never met.

A chat, with its history: "Daniel", Chat on the dating site.

> (Daniel, at the left) I am stuck at the airport with no wallet. I need $900 for a ticket.
> (Daniel, at the left) Please send gift cards. I will pay you back.

**What do you do?**
- Do what it says — if chosen: The gift cards were emptied in minutes, and then he needed more.
- Check first, a way I already had — if chosen: There is nothing of yours to check with: Daniel is the only one who can answer about Daniel.
- Leave it (right)

**What else do you do?** (opens only after a certain answer)
- Have them prove who they are first — if chosen: Any proof would come from them, the same as everything else you know about them.
- Tell someone I know in person about it (right)
- Keep chatting, but send nothing — if chosen: Every message gives them another try. The ones who lose nothing are mostly the ones who stop.
- Report the profile to the site, and leave it at that — if chosen: Reporting helps the site, but it does not tell anyone who can look at it with you. Tell someone you know in person too.

After the answer: the deciding words are marked. Reason: You have only met him online, so you leave it, and you tell someone you know in person. Gift cards settle it: no honest person is paid that way.

## The check

9 new questions, no help. No answer is shown until the end. The questions come in mixed order.

You have 20 seconds for each. The clock only shows the time: nothing moves on until you tap Next. A call is not on a clock.

To pass:
- None did what a scam asked
- None checked through the link, number or reply the message gave
- At most 1 wrong among real messages
