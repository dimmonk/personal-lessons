# Geography: The continents and the oceans

Revision 1, live. Part: The continents and the oceans. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A story says a ship sank in the Indian Ocean, or that a storm crossed the Atlantic. If you cannot see where that is on the world, the story has no place to sit. In about twelve minutes you will learn the way to find any place, and use it to tap the seven continents and the five oceans every later place sits in.

## Step 1: How to find any place

You will learn where places are with no map in front of you, the way a story names them. For any place, answer three questions, in this order.

1. Area: what bigger area is it in? A country is in a part of a continent, a state is in a region of the country. The area tells you where on the map to look, so you start there, not with the place itself.

2. Touches: what is next to it? Its neighbors pin down the exact spot, and they are what the news uses: "near the border with France", "just over the Nevada line".

3. Water: what water is it on? An ocean, a sea, a lake, or none. Whether a place is on the coast or inland is often the point of the story: a storm, a port, a ship.

First come the biggest areas of all, the continents and the oceans: every later place sits in one of them. After each answer the feedback says where the place sits, what is beside it and its water.

## Step 3: A worked example

Africa


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of The world with Africa lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? A continent is the biggest area there is, so start from the whole map: Africa is the big one in the middle.
1. Touches: what is next to it? Europe, the small continent you just saw named, sits just above it; Asia, the biggest, joins it at the top right.
1. Water: what water is it on? The Atlantic, the ocean you just saw between the Americas and Africa, is on its left, and the Indian Ocean on its right. Tap inside Africa.

Then the answer: Africa. Africa is the big continent at the middle of the map, south of Europe across the Mediterranean, between the Atlantic and the Indian Ocean.
