# US Civics & History: Symbols and holidays

Revision 1, live. Part: Symbols and holidays. Where the subject ends up: Answer the citizenship interview’s civics questions aloud from memory, and, reading a news story about a US law, order or ruling, say who acted, whether it was theirs to do, who can stop it, and whether to take the story as it stands or check who is behind it first.

## The why

The last ten questions are about symbols and holidays. Most of them point back to what you have learned: the flag’s stripes to the colonies, Independence Day to 1776.
Read the table once, then answer from memory.

## Step 1: Symbols, and what they stand for

A plain table, "Symbols of the country":

> Symbol  |  What to say  (the column heads)
> The capital  |  Washington, D.C.
> The Statue of Liberty  |  New York Harbor; Liberty Island. The list also accepts New Jersey, near New York City, and on the Hudson River: the island is near both shores, and its park’s address is in Jersey City, New Jersey.
> The flag’s 13 stripes  |  They represent the original colonies: 13 original colonies
> The flag’s 50 stars  |  One star for each state: 50 states
> The national anthem  |  The Star-Spangled Banner
> E Pluribus Unum, the first motto  |  Out of many, one

## Step 2: Holidays

A plain table, "National holidays":

> Holiday  |  What it is  (the column heads)
> Independence Day, July 4  |  A holiday to celebrate U.S. independence from Britain: the country’s birthday
> Others  |  New Year’s Day, Martin Luther King, Jr. Day, Presidents Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Labor Day, Columbus Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving Day, Christmas Day

| A | B |
|---|---|
| Memorial Day: a holiday to honor soldiers who died in military service. | Veterans Day: a holiday to honor people who have served in the U.S. military. |

Compare: Memorial Day is for those who died; Veterans Day is for everyone who served.
