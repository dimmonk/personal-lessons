# Singing: Lighter, not louder

Revision 1, live. Part: Lighter, not louder. Where the subject ends up: Sing a tune you have just heard, or a line of a song you know, in tune and at a height that suits your voice, with high notes that come out light instead of shouted; and when a line goes wrong, say which of a few things went wrong and what to try, or that nothing did.

## The why

When a line climbs, most people sing it louder to get up there. That habit is what goes wrong.
A higher note does need more breath pressure. But more pressure also pushes the note higher, so a top note sung harder tends to go sharp, and loud high singing sounds strained. The way up is lighter, not louder.

## Step 1: Warm-up

A hum; a line on the screen follows your voice. It is not scored and can be skipped.

#### Try 1: Warm-up (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Warm up your voice. Hum a slow slide up and down, or hum through a straw. Keep it easy and quiet. There is nothing to match.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

## Step 2: Effort, or the note?

This is the fourth check. Range comes first: try the top note on “oo” at an easy loudness, about as loud as you talk. If it will not come without straining or shouting, the song is too high, and singing lighter cannot fix that. Move the song lower.

If it does come, sing the line as you usually do and check the top note against a steady note, such as a piano app:
It goes sharp or strained only when you sing it loud, and on “oo” at an easy loudness it is on: that is pushing. The cause is effort. Sing it lighter and softer, on “oo” first, then the words.
It comes out easily but sits under, loud or soft: that is falling short. The cause is the note, the third check: check it against a steady note and move up to it.
It comes out light and on the note: a light, easy top. Leave it alone.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| Pushing: over, or strained, only when loud. | Falling short: under, at any loudness. |

Compare: Which way it is off, and whether singing lighter changes it. If a pushed note is also a shade over, fix the pushing first: it is what made the note go sharp.

Humming, singing through a straw, or singing on “oo” make the voice work more easily, and make it easier to slide high smoothly. That is why a high note is tried on “oo” first.

## Step 3: A worked example

Jamal sings a karaoke song whose chorus climbs. He sings louder and louder to get up there, and his neck tightens. Checked against a piano app, the top note is a shade over. Sung on “oo” at about the loudness he talks, the same note lands on it.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**What is happening at the top?**
- A light, easy top
- Pushing
- Falling short of the note
- Out of range

The working, one step at a time:
1. Is the note in his range? On “oo” at an easy loudness it comes out, so yes.
1. When is it off? Only when he sings it loud, and then it is over: the pushing raised it.

Then the answer: Pushing. In range, over only when he sings it loud, on when he sings it lighter: that is pushing. Sing it lighter and softer, on “oo” first, then the words; moving to the note would not fix what made it go sharp.

## Step 5: Now sing it lightly

The app first wants one note sung loudly, then the same note at the loudness you talk at. That shows it what loud and talking mean for your voice. Then it plays notes from the top of your range: sing each one back on the note and no louder than you talk.

The app measures two things on each try: whether the note is on, and whether it was louder than your talking note. A try that was too loud says so, and it does not count, even on the note. Light is not a whisper: about as loud as talking. The first group is on “oo”, with the line. The second is on “oo” without the line, and you say where you landed before you see it. The third is on “ah”, the open sound most words use.

## Step 6: Tries

4 tries, listed, with help (the line and the notes drawn while singing). A missed try comes back at least three tries later, with a new note, until it is on the note.

When no range is stored yet, this group opens with the range exercise, and the group follows it:

#### First: Find your range (a sung question; nothing to match)

**Find your range. You will hum a slide up, then a slide down. Keep it easy: stop at the notes that still feel comfortable.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Start". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), then the learner sings in a window of 7 seconds + 7 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- The two windows say: "Slide up to the highest note that is easy, and hold it." then "Now slide down to the lowest note that is easy, and hold it." The lowest and highest steady notes are saved as the learner's range, once. If the two ends are too close together: "I could not find two different easy notes. Slide further each way and hold each end for a moment, then try again." and nothing is saved.
- After the try: "Your range is saved". Reason: Every note you practice sits inside the range you just sang, so none is too high or too low.
- Nothing heard: "I did not hear you. Check that the microphone is on, then try again." The try is not counted and can be made again.
- Stored: numbers only (how much range was found); no sound is recorded or sent anywhere.

#### Try 8: Sing it lightly (a sung question; the notes are made fresh from the learner's range, inside it)

**Listen to the note, then sing it back lightly, no louder than you talk.**

- Before the tap: one button, "Hear the note". The microphone is off and nothing sounds. The screen says: The microphone listens only while you sing. The sound is checked on this device and thrown away: nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
- On the tap: the microphone turns on (it stays on for the group), the app plays the note in a soft hum-like tone, not a voice (1.5 seconds), ignoring the microphone while it plays and for a quarter of a second after, then the learner sings in a window of 1.9 seconds.
- While singing: the strip shows the note as a bar and the voice as a line, drawn as it is sung.
- Before the first of these in a group: two notes to set the scale, neither scored. "First, listen to the note and sing it loudly. That tells the app how loud is loud for you." then "Now listen again and sing the same note at the loudness you talk at."
- After the try, in words: On the note, A shade under, A shade over, Well under, Well over, Sliding up into it, Sliding down onto it, Hunting for the note. The strip then shows the note and the voice, with or without the line. Reason: A top note that is easy sounds light. If it comes out loud, ease off the volume and keep the note.
- After a miss, a line for each note that was off says which way to move and how far, for example "Your voice sat a shade under the note: sing a little higher, less than a half-step." or "Your voice sat well over the note, about two half-steps: sing that much lower."; a note that slid onto the note says "Your voice started under the note and slid up into it. Aim at the note itself, so you are on it from the start.", and one that wandered up and down says "Your voice wandered up and down before it settled. Hear the note in your head before you sing; if it still wanders, slow down.".
- Nothing heard: "I did not hear you. Check that the microphone is on, then try again." The try is not counted and can be made again. A miss comes back three tries later with a new note.
- Stored: numbers only (how far off each note was, in whole hundredths of a half-step); no sound is recorded or sent anywhere.
