# Scams & Social Engineering: Your accounts

Revision 4, live. Part: Your accounts. Where the subject ends up: When a message, call or offer asks you to act, you stop, see what it wants, and check it through a channel you already had before doing anything, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, you make the first calls at once.

## The why

Some messages want your accounts: a page that copies a sign-in, a code someone wants read back, a sign-in you did not start, an app that wants your texts.
You see each script beside the real thing it copies, and choose what to do.

## Step 1: A page that copies your sign-in

| A | B |
|---|---|
| A text message: "Calderwood Alert", Text message · 2:14 PM.  > Your card has been locked. > Confirm your sign-in now to unlock it. > (the link in it, drawn as words and never one that can be followed: calderwood-secure.example/confirm) | Open your bank’s app, or type its address yourself. You start the sign-in. |

Compare: In the first, the message starts it. In the second, you do.

A page you reach from a message can look exactly like the real one, and keep what you type. When it uses the name of an account you have, sign in your own way, never through the link.

## Step 2: A code read back

| A | B |
|---|---|
| An incoming call, the caller's words as a transcript: "Fraud team", Incoming call · 6:15 PM.  > Read me the code we just texted you. | A text message: "Calderwood Bank", Text message · 7:45 AM.  > Your sign-in code is 702 118. Type it on the page you are on. |

Compare: A real code is typed by you, on the page you opened yourself. It is never read to anyone.

A code is only for you to type, on a page or app you opened yourself. Never read it to anyone, whatever the story: the one asking is often the one who made it come.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: It is my bank on the phone, so reading them the code is fine.
The right line: Your bank never needs a code read back. Hang up and call the number on your card.

## Step 3: A sign-in you did not start

| A | B |
|---|---|
| A pop-up on a screen: "Fernbrook", On your phone · 3:10 AM.  > Is this you signing in to Fernbrook? Tap Approve. > [a button: "Approve"] | A pop-up on a screen: "Fernbrook", On your phone · just after you typed your password.  > Is this you signing in to Fernbrook? Tap Approve. > [a button: "Approve"] |

Compare: The same words. Only one is a sign-in you are doing right now.

Approve lets whoever is signing in through. If you are not signing in, someone else is trying to, often with your password: deny it, then change the password in the app, opened yourself. That is checking first: you go to the account your own way and put it right. Never approve to make the requests stop.

## Step 4: An app that wants your texts

| A | B |
|---|---|
| A pop-up on a screen: "PicGlow", On an Android phone, in an app you got from an ad.  > Allow PicGlow to read your text messages? > [a button: "Allow"] | A pop-up on a screen: "Scanly", In an app from your app store, after you tapped “Scan”.  > Allow Scanly to use your camera? > [a button: "Allow"] |

Compare: Did it answer something you just tapped? Scanly’s did. PicGlow’s answers nothing you did.

Allow something when it answers what you just tapped. On an Android phone, an app that reads your texts can read the codes your bank sends you; if it wants that out of nowhere, don’t allow it, and delete the app.

## Step 5: A worked example

You listed a couch for sale on Bramblecote Market. A buyer you do not know writes in the app’s chat.

A chat, with its history: "Buyer on Bramblecote Market", Chat about the couch.

> (Buyer on Bramblecote Market, at the left) Hi, I want the couch. To check you are a real seller, I sent a code to your phone.
> (Buyer on Bramblecote Market, at the left) Tell me what it says.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**What do you do?**
- Do what it says
- Check first, a way I already had
- Leave it

The working, one step at a time:
1. See what it wants. It wants a code that was sent to your phone.
1. See whether you started it. You did not request a code. The buyer made it come.
1. See whether you deal with them. No. The buyer is a stranger in a chat.
1. Choose. Leave it. Stop replying, and never read out the code.

Then the answer: Leave it. You did not start it and you do not know the buyer, so you leave it. A code is only for you to type, on a page you opened yourself. Never read it to anyone.

## The check

9 new questions, no help. No answer is shown until the end. The questions come in mixed order.

You have 20 seconds for each. The clock only shows the time: nothing moves on until you tap Next. A call is not on a clock.

To pass:
- None did what a scam asked
- None checked through the link, number or reply the message gave
- At most 1 wrong among real messages
