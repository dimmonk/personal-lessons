# Geography: Island Southeast Asia and the Pacific

Revision 1, live. Part: Island Southeast Asia and the Pacific. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A cyclone is forecast for Vanuatu, a ferry is lost in the Philippines, or Australia and New Zealand meet in a final. If these islands are a scatter of dots to you, the news tells you little. In about fifteen minutes you will place the twenty countries of island Southeast Asia and the Pacific on a blank map, by finding the area each sits in first, then name the four most often mixed up and say what borders each of them.

## Step 1: How to find an island country

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? That tells you where on the map to look. What touches it? Almost every place here is an island, so no country touches it by land and the islands beside it pin the spot instead. The only land borders are on the big islands of Borneo, Timor and New Guinea, and on the mainland between Malaysia and Thailand. What water is it on? A sea or an ocean, which tells you which way to look. The areas are three. Island Southeast Asia runs from Malaysia and the Philippines across to Timor-Leste. Papua New Guinea, Australia and New Zealand lie to its south and east. Beyond them the Pacific island states are dots in three groups: Melanesia, Micronesia and Polynesia. These are the world map's groups of part 1c, cut finer. Its Southeast Asia was the mainland and the islands together; here the islands have a part of their own. Its Oceania had only Australia and New Zealand and Melanesia, and Papua New Guinea is in Melanesia there too, though here it stands with its neighbors on New Guinea. Micronesia and Polynesia are the United Nations' names for the island states that map was too small to show. Find the area first, then the island. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water.

## Step 2: Malaysia, Indonesia and the Philippines

Look for about a minute at each of five views. Say each name as you find it. None of it stays up: next you find places on a map with no names. Malaysia is in two parts: the strip at the south end of the mainland and the north of the big island of Borneo. Tiny Singapore is a dot at the tip of the strip. Brunei is on Borneo's north coast. Indonesia is the huge chain of islands across the south. The Philippines are in the north-east, on the South China Sea. The dot east of them is Palau, the first Pacific island state.

## Step 3: Timor-Leste and Papua New Guinea

## Step 6: The north Pacific: Micronesia

Micronesia is the group of island states in the north and central Pacific. From west to east: Palau (on the first map), the Federated States of Micronesia and the Marshall Islands. Nauru is the single tiny island just south of the Marshall Islands.

Vanuatu

The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? Vanuatu is one of the Pacific island states in Melanesia, the south-west Pacific. Start from Papua New Guinea, which the second map named, and look east.
1. Touches: what is next to it? No country touches an island state by land. The islands beside it pin the spot: the Solomon Islands are the nearest to Papua New Guinea, Vanuatu is the middle one of the three, south-east of them, and Fiji is the farthest east.

Then the answer: Vanuatu. Tell it from the Solomon Islands and Fiji: Vanuatu is the middle one of the three, south-east of the Solomon Islands and west of Fiji.
