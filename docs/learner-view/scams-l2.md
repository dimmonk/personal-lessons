# Scams & Social Engineering: Your own way in

Revision 4, live. Part: Your own way in. Where the subject ends up: When a message, call or offer asks you to act, you stop, see what it wants, and check it through a channel you already had before doing anything, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, you make the first calls at once.

## The why

Some messages you check before you act: ones you did not start, that use the name of a company you deal with or a person you know. The check goes wrong when you check with the sender.
You see such a message, and pick the way to check it that the sender cannot steer.

## Step 1: What a message gives you belongs to the sender

A link, a phone number, a reply button, a badge number, “I’ll transfer you”: whatever comes with a message was put there by the sender.
A scammer can make a fake badge with a friend’s phone number on it. If you call that number, the friend answers.
A number your phone shows can be faked, and a searched number can be a scammer’s ad. Neither is a way you already had. Real messages are checked the same way.

A wrong idea, marked wrong: The number on my screen is my bank’s, so the call must be my bank.
The right line: Scammers can change the number your screen shows. The number on the back of your card is the one to call.

## Step 2: Your own ways in

The company’s app, from your own app store. The number on your card, a bill or a letter. A web address you type, copied from a bill or letter. A number you saved before the message came. A secret word you agree with your family face to face. If a caller says he is family in trouble and cannot say the word, hang up and call him on the number you have.

No card or bill? The company’s app. A government office: its own website, at the address on a letter from it (most end in .gov).
In the check, one pick of what the message gave you fails it.

## Step 3: A worked example

A man calls and says he is from the county police.

An incoming call, the caller's words as a transcript: "County police", Incoming call · 3:40 PM.

> This is Detective Moss, badge 4417.
> There is a warrant for you. Call 1-555-0132 and have a card ready to pay the fine.

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**How do you check it?**
- Hang up, type the county’s own website address myself, and call the number it lists
- Check the badge number he gave me
- Call the number he gave me

The working, one step at a time:
1. See what the message gave you. A badge number and a phone number. Both came from the caller, so both are his.
1. Find a way you already had. The police are an agency that has your records, so you check. The county’s own website, at an address you type yourself, lists the real number.
1. Use that way. Hang up and call the number you found yourself. A fake badge can carry a friend’s phone number.

Then the answer: Hang up, type the county’s own website address myself, and call the number it lists. Everything he gave you, the badge and the number, came from him. A number you find yourself, on a site you typed, is not his to choose.

## Step 4: Questions

4 questions, listed, with each inner pair asked one after the other, in a random order. A missed question comes back at least three questions later, until it is right. After each answer: the right answer, the deciding words, the reason, and one line on the learner's own choice if it was wrong.

#### Item 1

You have a Calderwood Bank card.

A text message: "Calderwood Alert", Text message · 2:14 PM.

> Your card has been locked.
> Call 1-555-0161 now to unlock it.

**How do you check it?**
- The number I find by searching for them on my phone — if chosen: Scammers buy ads and put up pages so that their page or number is the one a search shows. It is not a way you already had.
- The number printed on the card (right)
- Call the number the text gave me — if chosen: The number came with the message, so whoever answers it is on the sender’s side.

After the answer: the deciding words are marked. Reason: The number in the text is the sender’s, and a searched number can be a scammer’s ad. The number on your card was there before the text.

## Step 6: Your own ways in

Do each of these on your own phone. Tap Done when you have. Nobody checks: it only helps you remember what is ready. Each of these is a row with "Done now" (the day is kept) and, once done, "Not done":

- Save my bank’s number from the back of my card
- Check that my bank’s and my main shops’ apps are on my phone
- Agree a secret word with my family

Only you can see your marks.

## The check

8 new questions, no help. No answer is shown until the end. The questions come in mixed order.

You have 15 seconds for each. The clock only shows the time: nothing moves on until you tap Next. A call is not on a clock.

To pass:
- At least 7 right
- None checked through the link, number or reply the message gave
