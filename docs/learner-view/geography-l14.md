# Geography: The Caribbean

Revision 1, live. Part: The Caribbean. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A hurricane is forecast to cross Jamaica, then Cuba, or a cruise ship calls at Saint Lucia. If the Caribbean is a scatter of dots to you, the news tells you little. In about fifteen minutes you will place the fourteen countries and islands of the Caribbean on a blank map, by finding the group each sits in first, then name the four most often mixed up and say what borders Haiti and the Dominican Republic, the only two of these that share a land border.

## Step 1: How to find an island

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? That tells you where on the map to look. What touches it? Most of these places are islands, so no country touches them by land; the island or the group beside it pins the spot instead. What water is it on? The Caribbean Sea, or the Atlantic outside it. The world map you met first calls all of these islands the Caribbean; here they split into three groups. The four big islands, the Greater Antilles, run across the north: Cuba, Jamaica, Hispaniola, one island shared by Haiti and the Dominican Republic, and Puerto Rico. The Bahamas lie out in the Atlantic, north of Cuba and east of Florida. The small islands curve in an arc, the Lesser Antilles, from Puerto Rico south toward Venezuela, with Trinidad and Tobago at the end. Find the group first, then the island. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water.

## Step 2: The seas

Look for about a minute at each of five views. Say each name as you find it. None of it stays up: next you find places on a map with no names. The Caribbean Sea is the warm sea in the middle, with Central America and Venezuela on its south and west and the islands along its north and east. The Atlantic Ocean lies outside the islands, to the north and east. The Gulf of Mexico is the sea north of Cuba, between Mexico and Florida.

## Step 5: Puerto Rico and the arc of small islands, north

Puerto Rico, east of the Dominican Republic, is the last of the four big islands. East of it the small islands curve south in an arc, the Lesser Antilles. Saint Kitts and Nevis and Antigua and Barbuda are at the north end, then Dominica, then Saint Lucia. The islands left out between them are not countries: Guadeloupe and Martinique belong to France, Montserrat to Britain. Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, just below Saint Lucia, is on the next view.

## Step 7: A worked example

Jamaica


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of The Caribbean with Jamaica lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? Jamaica is one of the four big islands, the Greater Antilles. Start from Cuba, the long island just south of Florida, which the first map named.
1. Touches: what is next to it? No country touches an island by land. What is near it pins the spot: Jamaica is the small island south of Cuba, with Haiti and the Dominican Republic east of it.
1. Water: what water is it on? It sits in the middle of the Caribbean Sea, the sea named across the map. Tap the island.

Then the answer: Jamaica. It is the small island alone in the middle of the Caribbean Sea, south of Cuba and west of Haiti.
