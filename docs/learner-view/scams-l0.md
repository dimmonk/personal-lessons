# Scams & Social Engineering: Before you start

Revision 1, draft (the screen carries the draft line). Part: none. Where the subject ends up: When a message, call or offer asks you to act, you stop, see what it wants, and check it through a channel you already had before doing anything, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, you make the first calls at once.

## The why

Before the lessons, ten things the way they arrive on your phone, so that you can see later how far you have come.
Do what you would really do.

## Step 1: How this goes

Messages and a call arrive one at a time, as they do on your phone. Each time, pick what you would really do: do what it says, check first through a way you already had, or leave it.
Nothing here is graded, and you see no answers until the end.

## The baseline

Ten things, the way they arrive on a phone. Do what you would really do. Nothing here is graded, and you see no answers until the end. You have 20 seconds for each. When time runs out, the question stops and counts as a miss. Nothing moves on until you tap Next. A call is not on a clock.
It is not passed or failed: no rule is shown, nothing is shown per question, and the questions come in mixed order. It is stored as tries in the context "baseline", never counted in the results by lesson or in the schedule of a topic, and taken once.

#### Check question 9

Your phone rings. You do not know the number.

**A caller says he is your bank. What do you say?**
- An incoming call from "Fraud team" (Incoming call · 6:15 PM). The turns arrive one after another; the learner picks a reply each time and the next turn follows the reply. Pick what you say. The call goes on from there. Nothing is marked until it ends, and a call is not on a clock.
- turn 1, the other side says: "This is the fraud team at Calderwood Bank. There is a payment on your account that you did not make." "To stop it, I need the code we just texted you." [pushes: It frightens you and hurries you.]
  - reply "Read out the code": the call ends (not this one): The code let them into your account, and they moved your money out in minutes. [slip: did what a scam asked]
  - reply "Say you will call the bank yourself": goes on to turn 2, where the pressure comes back
  - reply "Hang up and call the number on my card": the call ends (right): You called the number on your card. The bank had not called, and your account was safe.
- turn 2, the other side says: "Do not hang up. If you do, your account will be frozen and the money gone." [pushes: It threatens you to keep you on the line.]
  - reply "Give in and read out the code": the call ends (not this one): The code let them into your account, and they moved your money out in minutes. [slip: did what a scam asked]
  - reply "Say you need a moment to think": goes on to turn 3, where the pressure comes back
  - reply "Hang up and call the number on my card": the call ends (right): You called the number on your card. The bank had not called, and your account was safe.
- turn 3, the other side says: "I am a licensed officer. If you refuse to help me, you may be charged." [pushes: It uses a title to make you doubt yourself.]
  - reply "Give in and read out the code": the call ends (not this one): The code let them into your account, and they moved your money out in minutes. [slip: did what a scam asked]
  - reply "Hang up and call the number on my card": the call ends (right): You called the number on your card. The bank had not called, and your account was safe.
- After it ends: the lines of every turn that pushed are marked and named ("It pushes:"), and the reply where it could have ended the right way is named ("You could have ended it at your first reply: “the reply”." or "You ended it as soon as you could.").

After the answer: the deciding words are marked (none named). Reason: No bank needs you to read out a code it just texted you, or to stay on the line. Hanging up and calling a number you already had ends it at once, however hard it pushes.

#### Check question 10

Yesterday a caller said he was your bank, and you sent him $1,200. You have just worked out that it was not your bank.

**What do you do?**
- Tap the three steps in the order you would do them. The first one is what counts. The steps are offered mixed (never already in the right order): "Call my bank on the number on my card"; "Change my password on the real site"; "Report it"; "Message the caller to get the money back". Each tapped step is numbered; "Start again" clears the taps; the answer is the order tapped once 3 are.
- Right: Call my bank on the number on my card, then Change my password on the real site, then Report it (only the first step counts). After a miss: "You put: …" and why the order matters: A bank can sometimes stop or recall a payment, but only in the first hours. So the bank comes before anything else, and the caller is the last person to contact.

After the answer: the deciding words are marked (none named). Reason: Your bank first, on a number you already had, because time counts. The password and the report follow.
