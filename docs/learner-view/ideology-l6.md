# Political Ideologies: The mixed drill

Revision 1, draft (the screen carries the draft line). Part: The mixed drill. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can read a post, a speech, a platform or a quote and name the ideology it speaks for, or say there is too little to tell, by answering the deciding questions, when someone argues a position or throws a label like "socialist" or "fascist".

## The why

Out in the world a quote does not come labeled with the question it needs. You start with the right question, pick the family, and sometimes say the words are not enough.
In about fourteen minutes you will hold the whole table, practice on mixed passages, then try twelve new ones on your own. The check has one question on each of the twelve, the family, and ten right pass.

## Step 1: The order, and the whole table

Put the five questions in order: who owns, who the story is about, what drives history, what to do with elections, where it ends. Questions 1 and 2 sort most passages fastest; the rest settle the close calls. Then name a family only on its telltale answers, or say too little to tell:
Marxism: class is the unit and class conflict the engine. Ownership may go unsaid, because Marxism is an analysis.
Marxism–Leninism: a party rules for the class, and the state owns the businesses.
Democratic socialism: social ownership, won through elections.
Social democracy: private ownership stays, with welfare and regulation.
Anarchism: worker ownership and no state.
Market socialism: worker-owned or socially owned firms competing in markets.
Fascism: the nation, its decline and rebirth, and a leader who replaces elections.
Nazism: race, as a natural rank, is the unit.
Classical liberalism: the individual, private ownership, a limited state.
Conservatism: inherited institutions kept, and changed gradually.
Reaction: an old order that is gone, brought back.
National populism: the people and the nation against an elite, pursued inside elections.
Identity-egalitarianism: identity groups as an unjust rank to undo.

## Step 2: Three checks before you name

Race or identity: is the rank natural and to be kept, or unjust and to be undone? Methods are not content: a leader cult, secret police, censorship and violence appear in every dictatorship, and mobilization separates fascism from a quiet dictator, never from Leninism. Names are marketing: check the name against the answers. A right label reached by tone is luck, so check your reasons against the telltale answers too.

## Step 3: Too little to tell: say it, and say what you would need

When the passage does not show a family's telltale answers, say so and name what would settle it. Do not use it as a way out: when the answers are there, name the family.

| A | B |
|---|---|
| “Ordinary families are tired of being talked down to by those in charge. We stand with them.” | “Ordinary families are tired of being talked down to by those in charge. Our nation comes first: foreign buyers will be screened, and the voters will decide whether we stay.” |

Compare: The first gives a people and an elite and nothing more: too little to tell. The second adds the nation, directed owners and the vote, so it can be placed.

## Step 4: A worked example

“The Democratic People's Front, the party that leads the working class, will hold all power for now. The mills, the mines and the banks pass to the state and stay there until classes have disappeared.”

Before the working, the learner may commit to a choice (not scored, not stored):
**Question 1. Who would own the businesses and farms?**
- Private owners
- The state
- The workers, in councils or co-ops
- Private owners, directed by the state
- The words do not say

The working, one step at a time:
1. Question 1. Find who would own the businesses. “The mills, the mines and the banks pass to the state.” Question 1: the state.
1. Question 2. Find who the story is about. The Front is “the party that leads the working class”, and the end is “until classes have disappeared”. Question 2: class.
1. Question 4. Find what the words want done with elections. The Front “will hold all power for now”, with no vote mentioned. Question 4: go beyond elections, a party rules for the workers.
1. Check the telltale answers, then name the family. A party rules for the class, with the state owning the businesses: Marxism–Leninism.
1. Check the name against the answers. The name says “Democratic”. The answers say one party holds all power. Names are marketing, so the answers win.

Then the answer: The state / Class: owners against workers / Go beyond them: a party rules for the workers / Marxism–Leninism. The state takes “the mills, the mines and the banks” (Question 1), the story is the working class and the party that leads it (Question 2), and the Front “will hold all power” (Question 4). That is Marxism–Leninism, whatever the name says.

## Step 7: What this cannot do

These words are also used as weapons: people throw "fascist" and "socialist" to condemn, not to describe. Describing and condemning are different jobs.
Fascism's definition is contested. Some scholars stress the rebirth myth, some the stages, some a list of family traits, and some limit the word to interwar Europe. Whether identity-egalitarianism grew from the Marxist tradition or on its own is disputed too.
Applying all this to living parties is contested ground. The questions tell you what to look for, not a verdict. Non-Western, religious and expert-rule systems are only lightly covered.
