# Geography: Europe, central and east

Revision 1, live. Part: Europe, central and east. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

The news says a pipeline through Slovakia has stopped, or that fighting is near the Moldovan border. If Central and Eastern Europe is one gray blur to you, you cannot picture where, who is next door, or what water is near. In about fifteen minutes you will place the nine countries from Poland to Russia and the Black Sea on a blank map, by finding the area each sits in first, then name the four most often mixed up and say what touches them.

## Step 1: How to find a country

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? That tells you where on the map to look. What touches it? Its neighbors pin the exact spot, and they are what the news names. What water is it on? Coast or inland is often the point of the story. On the world map all nine of these countries are Eastern Europe; here that region is split into three smaller areas so each is easier to find. Central Europe is four countries: Poland in the north, Czechia and Slovakia side by side below it, and Hungary below them. The south-east is Romania, curving round the Carpathians to the Black Sea, with small Moldova beside it. The east is the wide plain of Belarus, Ukraine and Russia, the biggest country of all. Find the area first, then the country. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water.

## Step 2: Central Europe: Poland, Czechia, Slovakia and Hungary

Look for about 40 seconds at each of three views. Say each name as you find it. None of it stays up: next you find places on a map with no names. Poland is the big country in the north. Czechia and Slovakia lie side by side below it, Czechia in the west and Slovakia in the east. Hungary is the plain below Slovakia, inside the curve of the Carpathians. Germany and Austria, from the map of western Europe, are on their west.
