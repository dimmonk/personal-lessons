# Geography: The South, from Texas to Kentucky

Revision 1, live. Part: The South, from Texas to Kentucky. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A hurricane is heading for Louisiana, or a cousin lives in Tennessee. If the South is one shape to you, you cannot follow either. In about fifteen minutes you will place the eight states from Texas to Kentucky, by finding their row first, name the ones most often mixed up and say what touches four of them.

## Step 1: How to find a state

The same three questions as for any place. What bigger area is it in? What touches it? What water is it on? Here the area is this part of the South, in two rows: the Gulf states along the Gulf of Mexico (Texas, Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama) and the inland row above them (Oklahoma, Arkansas, Tennessee, Kentucky). Find the row first, then the state. After every answer the feedback gives you the three: Area, Touches, Water.

## Step 3: A worked example

Arkansas


Above the map: **Tap it on the map.**
The map of The United States with Arkansas lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which bigger area is it in? Arkansas is in the South's inland row, above the Gulf states. Start from the Mississippi River at the Gulf of Mexico, both placed already.
1. Touches: what is next to it? Follow the river up from the Gulf: the first state on its west bank is Louisiana; the next one up is Arkansas.
1. Water: what water is it on? It has the river on its east side and touches no sea. Tap inside it.

Then the answer: Arkansas. It is just west of the Mississippi River, between Oklahoma and Tennessee.
