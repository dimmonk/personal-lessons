# Geography: The capitals of the West and the Midwest

Revision 1, live. Part: The capitals of the West and the Midwest. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

The news says "Sacramento passed a law" or "Springfield is debating the budget", and means the state. If you cannot say where the capital is, or take it for the big city next door, you misplace the story. In about sixteen minutes you will place the 25 capitals of the West and the Midwest on a blank map, by finding the state first, then the part of it, then name five from the lit state.

## Step 1: How to find a capital

A state's capital is where its government sits, and the news uses its name for the whole state. It is often not the biggest city, so the city you know is the wrong answer for many states. The questions are the same as for any place: the state first, then the part of it, and one more: which bigger city is it mixed up with? The 25 capitals of the West and the Midwest come in six views, grouped as the states lessons group the states (the world map you met first does not divide the United States). Seven are cities you already placed (Sacramento, Phoenix, Denver, Salt Lake City, Honolulu, Columbus and Indianapolis); they come back as capitals. You tap a capital as its dot; the other way you see the state lit and type its capital. After every answer the feedback gives you the capital, its state and the part of it, and the bigger city it is not.

## Step 2: The Pacific states

Look for about a minute at each of six views. Say each name as you find it. None of it stays up: next you find the capitals on a map with no names. Washington's capital is Olympia, at the south end of Puget Sound. Oregon's is Salem, south of Portland. California's is Sacramento, inland. None of the three is the state's biggest city.

## Step 8: A worked example

Sacramento


Above the map: **Tap this state capital on the map.**
The map of The United States with Sacramento lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Area: which state is it in? California's government sits in Sacramento: "Sacramento passed a law" means California. California is the long state down the Pacific coast, which you placed in The West.
1. Touches: where in the state, and next to what? It is not Los Angeles or San Francisco, the coast cities of The cities west of the Mississippi. The capital is inland, in the north-center of the state, northeast of San Francisco.
1. Water: what water is it on? It stands on the Sacramento River, a river too small to show on this map, away from the ocean. Tap that dot.

Then the answer: Sacramento. Tell it from Los Angeles and San Francisco: Sacramento is the capital, inland; the bigger cities are on the coast.
