# Geography: The flags of western Europe

Revision 1, live. Part: The flags of western Europe. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A flag in a news photo, on a truck or at a match tells you whose it is only if you can read it. In about eight minutes you will read the flags of the ten larger countries of western Europe, by the stripes and the colors, and tell the pairs that look alike: the Netherlands and Luxembourg, Belgium and Germany, France and Italy.

## Step 1: How to read a flag

Read it the same way every time. First count the stripes and see which way they run, upright or flat: that alone splits most of these flags in two. Then say the colors in order, from the left or from the top, since look-alikes often share colors in a different order. Then look for a mark, a shield or a cross, which settles a flag the stripes do not. Then check the one line that tells it from the flag it is mistaken for: that is where the misses happen. Last, find the country on the map, using the three questions: the area it is in, what touches it, the water it is on. Next, the ten flags in three groups, each with its line; none of it stays up.

## Step 2: Flags of upright stripes

Look for about a minute at each of three groups. Say each name as you read its flag. The stripes here stand upright.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇫🇷 France; 🇮🇹 Italy; 🇧🇪 Belgium.

France: Three upright stripes, blue, white and red, with the blue on the left.

Italy: Three upright stripes, green, white and red, with the green on the left. France has blue where Italy has green.

Belgium: Three upright stripes, black, yellow and red. Germany's three stripes lie flat.

## Step 3: Flags of flat stripes

The stripes here lie flat.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇳🇱 Netherlands; 🇱🇺 Luxembourg; 🇩🇪 Germany; 🇦🇹 Austria.

Netherlands: Three flat stripes, red, white and a deep blue. Luxembourg's blue is lighter.

Luxembourg: Three flat stripes, red, white and a light blue. The Netherlands' blue is deeper.

Germany: Three flat stripes, black, red and gold. Belgium's three stripes stand upright.

Austria: Three flat stripes, red, white and red.

## Step 4: Flags with a shield or a cross

These three have a mark that no other flag here has.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇪🇸 Spain; 🇵🇹 Portugal; 🇨🇭 Switzerland.

Spain: Three flat stripes, red, yellow and red, the yellow one twice as wide, with a shield on the left.

Portugal: Two upright bands, green on the left and a wider band of red, with a gold sphere and a shield where they meet.

Switzerland: A white cross on a red background.
