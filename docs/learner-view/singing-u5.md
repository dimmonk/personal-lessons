## Part 1 of 3: The voice everyone else hears, and a pinched sound

### 1. When your voice sounds off, two quick checks tell you what to change

*Unit Five · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 3 · Card 1 of 16*

[reviewers only: card kind `orient`, id `orient`]

The voice you hear from inside your head is not the one everyone else hears. And when the sound really is off, two quick checks tell you which of three things to change.

You sing a line into your phone and play it back, and it sounds thin and not like you. A friend at karaoke says your voice sounds pinched. Your choir director cannot make out your words, and you cannot tell why.

These sound alike, but they are four different things. One is only how your voice sounds from outside, and there is nothing to fix. The other three each have their own fix. Hold your nose shut and sing an “ah”, look in a mirror, and you can tell which one you have in a minute.

**What Unit One taught, in one place.** The first question is **“What bothers you about it?”** Its answers:

- **“How the top notes come out”**: give this answer when the trouble starts where the notes go up: at the top of the line the voice shouts, flips, clamps, cannot reach the note, or goes light in a way you are not sure of.
- **“How the air holds up”**: give this answer when the trouble is with the air: it is gone before the line ends, grabbed in the middle of a word, heard leaking out with the sound, or driven out hard, or you suspect the way you breathe.
- **“Whether you are on the note”**: give this answer when what you doubt is the note itself: whether the note you sing is the song’s note, because it sounds a shade off, you slide into it, you hunt for it, or you simply are not sure.
- **“How the words sound when they come out”**: give this answer when the notes and the air are fine, and what bothers you is the sound: pinched and nasal, dull and muffled, words that run together, or a voice that only sounds strange recorded. **This unit is about these stories.**
- **“Only that it is not the record’s voice”**: give this answer when the notes are right, nothing is tight, sore or short of air, the words are clear, and the only complaint is that it does not sound like the singer on the record.

Two things are marked separately: the name you give a story, and your answers to the questions on the way to it.

### 2. Your voice from outside

*Unit Five · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 3 · Card 2 of 16*

[reviewers only: card kind `meet`, id `meet-recorded`]

Start with the one that needs no fix, so you know what the other three are not.

*The lullaby playback*

> Mia is learning a lullaby for her baby niece, so she records one verse on her phone. Every note is right and her breath lasts the whole verse. ⟦On the playback her voice sounds thin and high⟧, and she hardly knows it is hers. She sings the line again in front of the mirror: ⟦her mouth opens easily, every word is clear⟧, and ⟦when she holds her nose shut on an “ah” the sound barely changes⟧.

Mia’s voice is fine. What surprised her is where she was listening from. When you sing, you hear yourself from inside your head, partly through bone, and that makes your voice sound deeper and fuller. A recording leaves that out. What you hear on the playback is the voice everyone else hears, thinner and higher.

Two quick checks show that the sound itself is fine. The nose check: hold your nose shut on an “ah” and listen for a change. The mirror check: look in a mirror to see how far your mouth opens.

**How to spot it**

1. **Hold your nose shut and sing an “ah”: Mia’s sound barely changes.** A sound going through the nose would change a lot.
2. **Look in a mirror and sing: Mia’s mouth opens easily.** A mouth that barely opens gives a dull sound.
3. **Listen to the words: every word on Mia’s recording is clear.** If the words ran together, a listener would lose them.
4. **Find where the complaint comes from: only the playback, where her voice sounds thin and high.** The playback is where the surprise comes from, not your singing.

This is **Your voice from outside**. Nothing is wrong here. It has a name so that you do not start fixing a voice that is fine.

**What to do when you meet it**

1. **Leave your singing alone.** Nothing in it needs fixing.
2. **Listen to recordings of yourself often.** The surprise wears off as you get used to the voice everyone else hears.
3. **Judge a recording by the checks, not by the surprise: nose held, mouth in the mirror, words clear.** A recording can sound odd and still be fine.

You may also hear this called “the recording surprise” or “how everyone else hears you”. Those words mean the same thing here, and from now on this unit uses one name: **Your voice from outside**.

### 4. A nasal sound

*Unit Five · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 1 of 3 · Card 4 of 16*

[reviewers only: card kind `meet`, id `meet-nasal`]

Now a sound that really is off, and the nearest one to the voice you just met.

*The red light*

> Raj is singing along to a slow song in his car. Every note is right and his breath lasts, but ⟦he can hear that his voice is pinched, as if it comes out of his nose⟧. At a red light ⟦he holds his nose shut and sings an “ah”: the sound changes a lot⟧. His mouth is open and the words are clear.

Raj’s notes and his breath are fine. What is off is the sound: after his voice makes it, too much of it goes out through his nose, and that makes it pinched. The back of the roof of your mouth decides how much goes that way.

Holding your nose shut is the check: if the sound changes a lot, it was going through the nose.

**How to spot it**

1. **Hold your nose shut and sing an “ah”: Raj’s sound changes a lot.** A big change means the sound was going through the nose.
2. **Listen as you sing, not on a recording: Raj hears the pinch live, in the car.** A voice that sounds odd only on a recording is **Your voice from outside**.
3. **Check that nothing else is the trouble: Raj’s mouth is open and the words are clear.** The pinch is the one thing that is off.

This is **A nasal sound**. Too much of the sound is going out through the nose.

**What to do when you meet it**

1. **Yawn, and feel the back of the roof of your mouth lift.** That lifted feeling is what you want to keep while you sing.
2. **Sing an “ah” keeping that lift. Then sing the line on “ah” alone, and then with the words.** Add the words only once the “ah” sounds right.
3. **Keep the “m” and “n” sounds short.** Those two sounds go through the nose, so long ones bring the pinch back.
4. **Hold your nose shut again and sing the line.** If the sound changes less than before, it is working.

You may also hear this called “honky” or “singing through your nose”. Those words mean the same thing here, and from now on this unit uses one name: **A nasal sound**.

## Part 2 of 3: A dull sound, and words that run together

### 7. A muffled sound

*Unit Five · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 2 of 3 · Card 7 of 16*

[reviewers only: card kind `meet`, id `meet-muffled`]

Next, a sound that is off for a different reason: the trouble is in the mouth, not the nose.

*The bathroom mirror*

> Ines is getting ready for her first open mic night. In front of the bathroom mirror she sings the opening line of her song. Every note is right and her breath lasts, but ⟦her mouth barely opens⟧. ⟦The sound is dull and stays back in her throat⟧, and her sister in the next room ⟦cannot make out the words⟧.

Ines’s voice is fine. After the voice makes the sound, your jaw and tongue shape it, and Ines’s mouth stays almost shut, so the sound sits back in her throat. That makes it dull, and her sister cannot pick out the words. It is common in shy singers.

**How to spot it**

1. **Look in a mirror and sing a line: Ines’s mouth barely opens.** This is the second check: how far the mouth opens.
2. **Listen to the sound: Ines’s is dull and stays back in her throat.** A pinched sound that seems to come out of the nose is **A nasal sound**.
3. **Ask a listener, or play it back, whether the words can be made out: Ines’s sister cannot.** If the mouth is open and the sound is clear, the trouble is somewhere else.

This is **A muffled sound**. The sound is there, but the mouth is not open enough to let it out.

**What to do when you meet it**

1. **Put two fingers between your teeth and sing “ah”.** It shows you what an open mouth feels like.
2. **Rest the tip of your tongue behind your lower front teeth.** That stops the tongue from being pulled back into the throat.
3. **Sing to a mirror.** You can see the mouth open instead of guessing.
4. **Think a bright “ee”.** It pulls the sound toward the front of your mouth.

You may also hear this called “a swallowed sound” or “singing into your chest”. Those words mean the same thing here, and from now on this unit uses one name: **A muffled sound**.

### 9. Mumbled words

*Unit Five · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 2 of 3 · Card 9 of 16*

[reviewers only: card kind `meet`, id `meet-mumbled`]

Last, a sound that is clear and still does not get through: here the words themselves are the trouble.

*The river song*

> Wes is at karaoke, singing a fast song about a river. Every note is right and his breath lasts. ⟦His mouth is open and his voice rings out clear⟧, but ⟦the ends of his words disappear⟧. On “we walked the river road all night” his friends at the back hear only “we wal… the ri-ver ro… all ni…”, and ⟦they cannot tell what he is saying⟧.

Wes’s mouth is open and his sound is clear, so the mouth and the nose are not the trouble. The trouble is the consonants: the small sounds, like t, d and s, that start and end the words. Your lips and teeth make them at the front of your mouth. His are soft or missing, so the words run into each other and a listener loses them.

**How to spot it**

1. **Check that the sound is clear: Wes’s mouth is open and his voice rings out.** A dull sound with the mouth barely open is **A muffled sound**.
2. **Listen to the ends of the words: on “we walked the river road all night”, his t, d and s sounds are gone.** Those small sounds are what make one word stand apart from the next.
3. **Ask a listener to write the words down: Wes’s friends cannot.** If a listener can write them down, the words are getting through.

This is **Mumbled words**. The sound is fine. It is the small sounds around the words that are missing.

**What to do when you meet it**

1. **Speak the words in rhythm, and overdo every consonant.** Overdoing them shows you what finishing a word feels like.
2. **Finish the end of every word, especially the t, d and s.** The ends are where a listener hears one word stop and the next start.
3. **Sing the line again, keeping the consonants.** Now the clear sound and the clear words arrive together.
4. **Record it and ask whether a listener could write the words down.** If they could, the words get through.

You may also hear this called “lazy diction” or “swallowing the consonants”. Those words mean the same thing here, and from now on this unit uses one name: **Mumbled words**.

## Part 3 of 3: The question, one whole story, then the drill

### 12. The question to ask every time

*Unit Five · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 3 of 3 · Card 12 of 16*

[reviewers only: card kind `question`, id `q-how`]

Here is the question and its four answers in one place.

**The question:** **“What do the words sound like?”**

**Its answers**

Each answer leads to one name, and so rules out the other three.

- **“Open and clear, and only strange on a recording”**
  - Give this answer when the sound is open, the words can be followed, holding your nose shut changes little, and it bothers you only on a recording, where your voice is thinner and higher than it sounds from inside.
  - It leads to **Your voice from outside**.
- **“Pinched, as if coming out of the nose”**
  - Give this answer when the sound is pinched and seems to come out of the nose, and holding your nose shut on an “ah” changes the sound a lot.
  - It leads to **A nasal sound**.
- **“Dull and stuck in the throat, with the mouth barely open”**
  - Give this answer when the mouth barely opens, the sound is dull and stays back in the throat, and the words are hard to make out.
  - It leads to **A muffled sound**.
- **“Clear in sound, but the words run together”**
  - Give this answer when the sound itself is open and clear, but the consonants are soft or missing, so the words run into each other and a listener loses them.
  - It leads to **Mumbled words**.

**Why it matters**

Each sound comes from a different place and has a different fix: the roof of the mouth, the jaw and the tongue, the lips and the teeth, or nothing but your own ears. How the singer on the record sounds does not change the answer.

How much you dislike the sound does not decide it. Two checks you can do in a minute, and the words themselves, do.

**How to answer it**

1. **Hold your nose shut and sing an “ah”.** If the sound changes a lot, it is **A nasal sound**.
2. **If it changes little, look in a mirror and sing again.** A mouth that barely opens, with a dull sound, is **A muffled sound**.
3. **If the mouth is open and the sound is clear, ask whether a listener could write the words down.** If the consonants are soft or missing and they could not, it is **Mumbled words**.
4. **If the words come through and you only dislike a recording, stop.** That is **Your voice from outside**, and there is nothing to change.
5. **Find the exact words that show it: what you hear, the mouth in the mirror, the words a listener loses.** If you cannot find them, you do not have an answer yet.

**When two answers both seem to fit**

Two of these can feel alike. Each pair below has one thing to check that tells it apart.

- A nasal sound or Your voice from outside: Hold your nose shut and sing an “ah”: does the sound change a lot, or hardly at all?
- A muffled sound or Mumbled words: Look in a mirror: is the mouth barely open and the sound dull, or is it open and clear with the words still running together?
- A nasal sound or A muffled sound: Is the sound pinched, with a big change when you hold your nose shut? Or is it dull, with the mouth barely open?
- Mumbled words or Your voice from outside: Could a listener write the words down, or is your voice itself the only surprise?

### 15. What to carry away

*Unit Five · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 3 of 3 · Card 15 of 16*

**This unit’s questions and answers**

What do the words sound like?
- Open and clear, and only strange on a recording → Your voice from outside
- Pinched, as if coming out of the nose → A nasal sound
- Dull and stuck in the throat, with the mouth barely open → A muffled sound
- Clear in sound, but the words run together → Mumbled words

**For each name: what to look for, and the question to ask when you spot it**

- **Your voice from outside**: an open sound, words that can be followed, little change when you hold your nose shut, and a complaint that only comes from a recording.
- **A nasal sound**: a pinched sound that seems to come out of the nose, and a big change in the sound when you hold your nose shut on an “ah”.
- **A muffled sound**: a mouth that barely opens, a dull sound that stays back in the throat, and words that are hard to make out.
- **Mumbled words**: an open, clear sound, consonants that are soft or missing, and words that run into each other.

**To carry away**

- Before you change anything, check what you are hearing: hold your nose shut and sing an “ah”, look in a mirror, and ask whether a listener could write the words down.
- A big change with your nose held is **A nasal sound**. A mouth that barely opens, with a dull sound, is **A muffled sound**. An open mouth and a clear sound with the words running together is **Mumbled words**.
- The voice you hear from inside your head is not the one everyone else hears. A voice that sounds odd only on a recording, with a clear sound and clear words, is **Your voice from outside**, and nothing needs fixing.
- A recording can sound odd and still be fine. Judge it by the checks, not by the surprise.

### 16. A plan, if you want one

*Unit Five · rev 1 · Draft: not yet read by a newcomer · Part 3 of 3 · Card 16 of 16*

[reviewers only: card kind `plan`, id `plan`]

This card is for what you will do about it. Fill it in, or leave it.

A plan is one line: if I notice this, then I will do that. Decide it now, because the middle of a song is the worst moment to think. These are examples to start from: use one, change it, or write your own.

- my voice sounds thin or strange on a recording, hold my nose shut on an “ah”, check that the words are clear, and if both are fine, leave my singing alone and record again next week
- my singing sounds pinched, as if it comes out of my nose, yawn, sing an “ah” keeping the back of my mouth lifted, and check again with my nose held
- my mouth barely opens in the mirror, or a listener says my singing sounds dull, put two fingers between my teeth and sing “ah”, rest my tongue behind my lower front teeth, and sing to a mirror
- a friend cannot make out my words, speak the words in rhythm, overdo every consonant, then sing again and record it

Or write your own: If …, then I will …
