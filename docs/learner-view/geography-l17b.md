# Geography: The flags of Europe, north

Revision 1, live. Part: The flags of Europe, north. Where the subject ends up: After this subject you can put a named place on a map with no names, say what it borders and what water it is on, name a place that is lit, tell a country by its flag, and say roughly how far it is from you, when a place is named in the news, by a person or in a travel plan with no map in sight.

## The why

A flag in a news photo, on a ferry or at a match tells you whose it is only if you can read it. In about eleven minutes you will read the ten flags of northern Europe and tell the five Nordic crosses apart.

## Step 1: How to read a flag

Read every flag the same way. First look for a cross or for stripes: that sorts these flags into three kinds: the five Nordic flags are crosses that reach the edges, the three Baltic flags are flat stripes, and the last two are each different, the United Kingdom's slanted crosses and Ireland's upright stripes. Then say the colors in order, since look-alikes share colors in a different order. Then check the one line that tells it from the flag it is mistaken for: that is where the misses happen. Last, find the country on the map by the three questions. Next, the ten flags in four groups; none of it stays up.

## Step 2: Nordic crosses with red

Look for about half a minute at each of four groups. Say each name as you read its flag. The five Nordic flags are one family, a cross that reaches all four edges, off to the left; only the colors tell them apart. These three have red in them.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇩🇰 Denmark; 🇳🇴 Norway; 🇮🇸 Iceland.

Denmark: Red with a plain white cross. Norway adds a blue cross inside the white one.

Norway: Red with a blue cross edged in white. Denmark's cross is plain white; Iceland has a blue field with a red cross.

Iceland: Blue with a red cross edged in white. Norway's colors the other way round: Norway has a red field with a blue cross.

## Step 3: Nordic crosses in blue, yellow and white

The other two of the family.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇸🇪 Sweden; 🇫🇮 Finland.

Sweden: Blue with a yellow cross. Finland's flag is white with a blue cross.

Finland: White with a blue cross. Sweden's flag is blue with a yellow cross.

## Step 4: Flat stripes

The three Baltic flags have no cross.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇪🇪 Estonia; 🇱🇻 Latvia; 🇱🇹 Lithuania.

Estonia: Three flat stripes, blue, black and white, from the top.

Latvia: Dark red with a thin white stripe across the middle.

Lithuania: Three flat stripes, yellow, green and red, from the top.

## Step 5: Ireland and the United Kingdom

The last two have no cross like the Nordic five and no flat stripes.

The flags, each about 64 pixels wide with its name beside it: 🇮🇪 Ireland; 🇬🇧 United Kingdom.

Ireland: Three upright stripes, green, white and orange, with the green on the left. Italy's flag is the same except that its third stripe is red.

United Kingdom: Blue, with a red upright cross edged in white and red and white diagonal crosses behind it. No other flag here has diagonal crosses.

## Step 6: A worked example

A flag, about 64 pixels wide, with no name beside it: 🇳🇴


Above the map: **Tap the country with this flag.**
The map of Europe, north with Norway lit and the tap shown on it.
The working, one step at a time:
1. Read the flag: how many stripes, which way they run, which colors, and any mark. No stripes: it is a cross that reaches the four edges, placed toward the left. The field is red and the cross is blue, edged in white.
1. Tell it from the flag it is mistaken for. Denmark has the same shape with a plain white cross on red. Iceland has the same colors the other way round, a blue field with a red cross. Norway is red with the blue cross.
1. Find the country. Norway is the long Nordic country down the west side of Scandinavia, on the Norwegian Sea. Tap it.

Then the answer: Norway. Red with a blue cross edged in white. Denmark's cross is plain white; Iceland has a blue field with a red cross.
