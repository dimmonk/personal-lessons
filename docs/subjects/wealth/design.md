## Gate 1. Kind of learning and evidence

**Kinds**, from what the learner must do in real life:
- **Procedure** (the core): find the right line on a real US document and turn it into a number (yearly cost in
  dollars, a share, a drift, a trade, a shortfall against the match).
- **Judging**: decide change or leave alone (a cheaper fund of the same kind exists or not; a drift inside or outside the
  band; a holding that is one company or five hundred).
- **Habit / behavior**: make the change, and repeat the check once a year. Mostly one-off actions plus one yearly
  routine, not a daily habit.
- *Knowing facts* is deliberately small: contribution limits, thresholds and tax rules change yearly and are looked up at
  the moment of use from a dated reference, not memorized.

**Evidence**: `docs/research/acting-on-your-own-money.md` (F1–F9, researched for this subject: financial education and
behavior change); `docs/learning-science.md` P10, P17, P19 (worked examples, fading, feedback for procedures) and P26
(legitimate cases, reminders, plans); `docs/research/review-after-lessons.md` R4, R8, R9 (review). Note: the
learning-science document assumed diagnostic classification; only its procedure and feedback principles are used here,
not the key-and-outcomes format.

**The principles that drive the design:**
1. **Teach at the decision, act straight after** (F1 Fernandes et al. 2014; F2 Kaiser & Menkhoff 2017). Every lesson
   ends on the learner's own accounts; a lesson is offered at its trigger (new job, new account, yearly statement).
2. **Count actions, not answers** (F3 Bhattacharya et al. 2012: personalized advice was mostly not followed). A lesson
   is not finished until the change is made or dated.
3. **Prefer fixes that then run themselves** (F4 Madrian & Shea 2001; Thaler & Benartzi 2004, weak causal evidence).
   The fixes taught are set-once (automatic contribution, target-date or one-fund holding, named beneficiary); the only
   repeated behavior is the yearly check.
4. **One rule of thumb per part, with one threshold and one action** (F6 Drexler et al. 2014). No finance theory beyond
   what one step needs.
5. **Every cost in dollars a year; change only when a same-kind cheaper option exists** (F5 Choi, Laibson & Madrian
   2010; Hastings & Tejeda-Ashton 2008).
6. **Worked example, then completion, then alone, with explained feedback** (learning-science P10, P17, P19).
7. **Include what should be left alone** in every practice set, so the learner learns to discriminate, not to change
   everything (P26 requires 1; Burke et al. 2022).
8. **Sample first, own numbers second** (F9 Walkington: personal context helps; and sample statements have known answers,
   so feedback can be exact).
9. **A dated plan for a one-off change, never a savings target** (F7 Milkman et al. 2011 for; Loibl & Scharff 2010 and
   Carrera et al. 2018 against goal-amount plans).
10. **Bring it back with reminders and review in the real form** (F8 Karlan et al. 2016; Burke et al. 2022; R4, R8, R9):
    the yearly check is scheduled; the weekly review shows new statements to work, mixed across parts.

Question asked: if the learner did only what these principles describe, would they be closer to the end result? Yes:
each one is about doing the check and the change, not about knowing more about money.

## Gate 2. End result

- **After this subject you can** run the yearly check on your own US accounts: work out what each costs you a year in
  dollars, your biggest single bet, how far your mix has drifted, whether you take the full employer match, which
  foreign holdings need a US filing, and who inherits each account; then make the changes it calls for and leave alone
  what it clears.
- **Real moment**: once a year, and when you start a US job, open an account or move money: your own statements, the
  plan's fee disclosure, the beneficiary pages and your will, open in front of you.
- **Real-world test** (something the learner does):
  1. On a new sample household (5 to 6 documents the learner has not seen: a 401(k) statement and its fee disclosure, an
     IRA or brokerage statement, a pay stub with the plan's match rule, a foreign account summary, a beneficiary page):
     the yearly cost in dollars per account within 10%; the largest single holding's share of the whole and the
     stock/bond split within 2 points; the dollars a year short of the full match within 10%; and every flag correctly
     raised or cleared: switch a fund, rebalance, raise the contribution, foreign filing needed, beneficiary missing or
     out of date. At least one item in the household is sound and must be left alone.
  2. On the learner's own accounts: the same sheet filled in; every change it calls for dated with a when and where, done
     within 30 days (marked by the learner), and the next check scheduled in 12 months.
- **What is out**, so the subject stays short:
  - Choosing your ideal mix, picking investments, timing the market. The check uses the mix you chose, or a target-date
    fund's own.
  - Earning, budgeting, debt, building a US credit history, buying a home, insurance pricing.
  - Filing taxes; cross-border tax planning. The subject only flags foreign holdings that need a US filing or that the
    US taxes harshly (foreign funds) and the action is booking a cross-border tax preparer.
  - Trusts, LLCs, estate-tax planning, Social Security, Medicare, annuities in detail (an annuity appears only as a cost
    line to find).
  - Memorizing limits and thresholds: they change yearly and are read from a dated reference when used.

Question asked: if the learner passed this test, would they be closer to keeping their money? Yes: these are the quiet
leaks (fees, concentration, drift, missed match, foreign-filing penalties, accounts going to the wrong person), found on
their own documents and fixed.

## Gate 3. Practice, and whether the app can deliver it

**Method.** The real moment's own action: a real-looking US document, the learner finds the line, types the number,
the app checks it, the learner decides change or leave. For each step: a worked example on a sample statement (the line
marked, the arithmetic shown), one with the last step left to the learner, then two new statements alone, one of which
should be left alone (principles 6, 7). Then the same step on the learner's own statement (principle 8), and the change
made now if it can be done in a few minutes on the account's website, or dated with a when and where (principles 2, 9).

**Time.** About 12 to 15 minutes a lesson: one to two minutes reading (what this line is, the one rule), about seven on
sample statements, about five on the learner's own account and the action. Reading is never more than the rule and the
line.

**Feedback.** On samples: immediate, exact, from the app's own calculation: the right number, the line it comes from
highlighted on the document, the arithmetic, and why change or leave (P19). On the learner's own numbers the app cannot
know the truth; it recomputes from what was typed and shows the arithmetic, warns on values outside a plausible range (an
expense ratio over 2%, a share over 100%), and shows the matching line on a sample statement of the same kind as a model
to compare against.

**Delivery check.** What the app must be able to do, and whether it can today (checked in `public/app`):
| Needed | Today |
|---|---|
| Show a realistic US document (statement, fee disclosure, pay stub, beneficiary page) and highlight a line on it after an answer | No. Cards are text; no document view |
| Take a typed number and score it against a tolerance, with worked feedback | No. Drills are choices; inputs are free-text notes |
| A private "my accounts" sheet on the device: account type, balance, holdings, fees, match rule, beneficiary yes/no; never account numbers or logins | No form; `localStorage` helpers exist (`public/app/helpers.js`) |
| The calculator behind the sheet: cost in dollars, share of one holding, mix and drift, rebalancing trade, match shortfall | No |
| A dated action list with done marks, one reminder per date, and the yearly check scheduled | Partly: plan text inputs (`cards.js`, `review.js`) and the review schedule exist; no dated actions or yearly item |
| Offering a lesson at its trigger (new job, new account) and holding the own-account step until then | No |
| Reading numbers automatically from a PDF or screenshot | Not needed and not to be built: finding the line is the skill |

Everything marked "No" is built before the pilot lesson. Can the app take numbers from the learner's own statements?
Only by the learner typing them (5 to 15 numbers a check), which is the practice itself; that input does not exist yet.

Question asked: if the learner did only this practice, would they be closer to the end result? Yes: it is the check
itself, first on samples with exact feedback, then on their own accounts, ending in the change.

**Map both ways.** Lessons 1 to 8 below serve parts 1 to 8, one each; no lesson without a part. Parts 2 to 7 are the
six items of the gate 2 test; part 1 is what each of them reads from; part 8 is the test as a whole and its "done and
repeated" clause. If every part passes, the gate 2 test passes.

Question asked: if the learner passed only these checks, would they be closer to the end result? Yes: each is one line
of the yearly check, done on documents.

## Gate 5. Each lesson

Every lesson: the rule and the line (1 to 2 minutes), a worked sample, a completion sample, two samples alone (one to
leave), then own accounts and the action. About 12 to 15 minutes. Offered at its trigger where one exists. The weekly
review gives new sample documents for parts 2 to 7, mixed (R8, R9); the yearly reminder brings back lesson 8 on the
learner's own accounts (principle 10).

1. **Your accounts on one sheet** (part 1). Do: read a sample household's five documents and fill the sheet (type,
   balance), 6 min; then fill it for your own accounts, 6 min. Feedback: each type and balance checked, the line shown.
   End check: part 1. Why: 6, 8. Without it no later number can be computed.
2. **What it costs a year** (part 2). Do: read the rule ("expense ratio times balance, plus fee lines; switch only to a
   same-kind fund that costs less"), 1 min; worked sample, completion sample, two alone (one cheap index fund), 7 min;
   own: each account's dollars a year, then the switch made on the plan site or dated, 6 min. Feedback: dollars, the fee
   disclosure line highlighted, the cheaper same-kind fund in the menu named. End check: part 2. Why: 5, 6, 7, 2, 9.
3. **Your biggest single bet** (part 3). Do: rule (one company over 10% of the whole is a bet you did not choose to
   make), 1 min; samples including employer stock and RSUs spread over two accounts and a broad index fund to clear,
   7 min; own share, the sell-down plan dated if over, 4 min. Feedback: the share, the holdings added across accounts.
   End check: part 3. Why: 4, 6, 7, 2.
4. **Mix and drift** (part 4). Do: rule (outside 5 points, trade back; trade inside sheltered accounts first; a
   target-date fund does this for you), 2 min; samples including one under 5 points and one target-date to leave,
   7 min; own split against your chosen mix, the trade done or dated; option to move to a set-once fund, 5 min.
   Feedback: the split, the trade in dollars and which account it belongs in. End check: part 4. Why: 3, 4, 6, 7.
5. **The full match** (part 5). Offered when a US job starts. Do: rule (contribute at least what takes the whole
   match), 1 min; three sample pay stubs and match rules, one already full, 6 min; own: the rate changed now on the plan
   site, or dated to before the first payroll, 4 min. Feedback: rate needed and dollars left a year, the match sentence
   and the stub line highlighted. End check: part 5. Why: 1, 3, 2, 9.
6. **Foreign holdings** (part 6). Offered before the first US tax year. Do: rule (foreign accounts together over the
   reporting threshold need a yearly filing; foreign-registered funds are taxed harshly in the US; fix: a cross-border
   tax preparer), with the threshold read from a dated reference, 2 min; three sample households, one with nothing to
   flag, 5 min; own: total and flags, the booking dated, 4 min. Feedback: the total, which item crosses, why the fund is
   flagged. End check: part 6. Why: 1, 4, 7, 2.
7. **Who inherits** (part 7). Do: rule (the beneficiary on the account beats the will; name a primary and a backup on
   every account), 1 min; a sample household with a missing one, an ex-spouse and one sound account, 5 min; own: each
   account checked on its website and fixed there now (usually a few minutes), will and power of attorney dated with a
   lawyer if missing, 6 min. Feedback:
   which accounts, why "estate" or an ex-spouse is a problem. End check: part 7. Why: 2, 3, 7, 9.
8. **The whole check** (part 8). Do: a new sample household, all six items, scored, 10 min; then your own sheet
   refreshed, the change list with dates, the yearly check scheduled, 8 min. Feedback: item by item as in lessons 2 to 7.
   End check: the gate 2 test. Why: 2, 10. Comes back every year.

Rejection test, per lesson: the main activity is the part's real action (finding a line, computing, deciding, acting);
reading is at most two minutes; each end check measures the part; and each answers yes to "if the learner did only this,
would they be closer to the end result?". Lesson 2 is the pilot (largest, most common leak; needs the document view,
number input and own-accounts sheet first).

## Audit of the existing lessons (2026-10-10)

Read after gates 1 to 5 were written: `public/subjects/wealth/` (subject, key, units) through the learner views
`docs/learner-view/wealth-u1.md` to `u5.md`. All five units have the same shape: a short story about someone else's
money, a tap on the words that show it, a choice among named answers, side-by-side "telling them apart" pairs, a drill
of about 32 to 44 choice items, a recap, and an optional if-then plan card. No unit asks the learner to read a
statement, type a number, or look at their own accounts. The suspicion in the draft is confirmed.

| Unit | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| U1 Before you change anything (the five kinds of loss, the baseline) | **goes** | Serves no part: naming "what could lose this money" in strangers' stories is not a step of the check. The baseline judges stories, so it measures nothing in gate 2's test |
| U2 Money going out every year (fees, tax placement, deferring gains, using a loss, spending rate) | **changes** (fee cards only, for part 2); the rest **goes** | The fee arithmetic and the "a flat fee for real work is fine" contrast are right for part 2, but the practice is tapping words and naming "Switch to index funds", never computing dollars from a fee disclosure. Tax placement, gains, loss harvesting and spending rate serve no part |
| U3 When one thing is most of your money (selling down, capping a loss, business safety nets, umbrella insurance, LLCs, loans) | **changes** (single-company share only, for part 3); the rest **goes** | The share-of-the-whole rule fits part 3, practiced as labeling, not as adding up a holding across accounts. Loss-capping contracts, LLCs, business owners and loans are outside the end result |
| U4 Prices falling at the wrong time (cash for spending, a bond per bill, rebalance by a rule, already covered) | **changes** (the rebalance card, for part 4); the rest **goes** | The 5-point rule, "sell in the sheltered account first" and the dollar arithmetic are what part 4 needs, but the learner never finds a split or works out a trade. Cash-years and bond-per-bill are retirement-income planning, which is out |
| U5 Handing it over (will, beneficiary form, power of attorney, gifts, trusts, family rules) | **changes** (the three papers, for part 7); the rest **goes** | "The beneficiary form beats the will" is the core of part 7, but the learner never opens a beneficiary page. Estate tax, gifts, trusts and family rules are out |

Missing entirely: part 1 (the list of your accounts), part 5 (the employer match), part 6 (foreign holdings under US
rules), part 8 (the whole check, done and repeated), and every step of the practice gate 3 requires (documents, typed
numbers, own accounts, dated actions, the yearly return).

**Overall verdict: rebuild from scratch.** What can be kept is wording, not lessons: the fee arithmetic (U2 card 3), the
flat-fee contrast (U2 card 6), the share of the whole (U3), the written rebalancing rule (U4 card 12) and the beneficiary
form (U5 card 2) can be mined for the one-to-two-minute rule text in lessons 2, 3, 4 and 7. The key, the specimens, the
named answers and the drills go.

**The three most important problems**
1. **The wrong task.** About 190 choice items ask the learner to label what is wrong with strangers' money in the
   subject's own invented names ("Money going out every year", "Switch to index funds"). The real moment is the
   learner's own statement, and nothing in the subject practices reading one or computing its number (F1, R8).
2. **No action.** Each unit ends with an optional if-then card; nothing is changed, dated or checked again on the
   learner's accounts, which is where the evidence says financial education fails (F1, F3, F7).
3. **The wrong scope for this learner.** It spends most of its time on tools that rarely apply (loss-capping
   contracts, one LLC per property, estate-tax gifts and trusts, a bond per bill, tax-loss harvesting) and leaves out
   the quiet leaks of someone starting out in the US: the employer match, foreign accounts and funds under US rules,
   and simply listing what you have.
