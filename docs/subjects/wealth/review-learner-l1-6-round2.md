## The way of deciding, in my words (written before the first practice question)

- L1: List every account with its total. For each, find the line that says who pays if the holder fails (FDIC bank, SIPC brokerage, neither for a 401(k)). Add accounts in one name at one bank (and one kind of ownership) and compare to the bank limit. Over it means move the extra to another bank. A fall in prices is never paid by anyone.
- L2: Cash is checking plus savings, minus any savings already set aside for a bill due within 5 years. If a near bill sits in stocks, move it. Otherwise divide cash by a month of spending: under 3 build, 3 to 6 leave, over 6 with no near bill invest the rest.
- L3: Percent times balance gives dollars for each fund, plus the plan's flat fee. Change a fund only if another fund of the same kind (the Kind column) on the same list costs less. Otherwise leave it.
- L4: Turn the stub's dollars into a percent of pay. Find where the match stops (the end of the last step). Below that, the gap times the match rate is the money left on the table, so raise the rate. At or above it, leave it. For saving order, strike steps that do not apply, then use the fixed order.
- L5: Bonds belong in the 401(k)/IRA and stocks in the brokerage account, swapped only when the 401(k) holds stocks to trade. Inside a 401(k) a sale is untaxed, so switch now. In a brokerage account compare the tax saved by waiting past one year with the extra fee while waiting. A repurchase of the same fund within 30 days either side voids the loss.
- L6: Add one company's stock across every account (funds of hundreds of companies do not count), divide by the whole list including bank savings, compare to 10%. Over it, sell down to 10%, starting in the 401(k) where a sale is untaxed.

## Defects (all polish unless marked M)

### L1
1. Joint account limit is not taught. Rule says a joint account "is counted on its own" but never says how much it is covered for. Every joint balance in the items is small, so nobody stalls, but a beginner will wonder. Fix: one clause giving the joint-account rule, or keep joint balances plainly small and say so.
2. "Held in a trust" is jargon, explained only by "apart from your employer". Acceptable, but a one-line gloss helps.
3. The checks "Is there anything to change?" can be answered by spotting any single bank total over 250,000 without reading names. Reasoning is still required for the two-bank and joint decoys (items 3, 4, check 6, 7), so this is only mild.

### L2
4. Typo in the data: "In 1 years" (check 12 table and its reason and working). Fix: "In 1 year".
5. Working line "Add up the cash, the bill's money put aside" reads oddly in the cases where nothing is set aside, and a second read is needed. Fix: "Add up the cash, leaving out money set aside for a bill."
6. The rule does not say what to do if cash is under 3 months and a near bill sits in stocks. Not tested, no stall, but the "First the bills" order implies it. One clause would close it.
7. Options for "What does X's cash need?" are in a fixed order (not shuffled as elsewhere). The right answer still moves position, so no pattern.

### L3
8. (M, small) The rule and the fee sheet footnote say "your quarterly statement shows" the administrative fee, but the sample statement shows only balances; the fee is on the fee sheet. A beginner looks for it in the wrong paper. Fix: change the footnote to "this sheet shows it" or add a fee line to the statement.
9. The brokerage-account tax point (a switch there is taxed) is in the rule but only practised in L5. Fine, but a reader may wonder why every L3 account is a 401(k). Cosmetic.

### L4
10. "Tap the six steps" tells the learner how many steps survive, so the count itself reveals how many to strike. Fix: drop the number from the prompt.
11. "Pay off the credit card" is unconditional on rate. Every card in the items is 22 to 26%, so "high" is never defined. Fix: state the rough line (anything well above what savings earn) or include a low-rate card item.
12. "Is there anything to change?" after the learner has already typed the dollars left is nearly automatic; the earlier typed answers decide it. Harmless.
13. Step 8 waits for "I started a job in the US". A learner without a job is held at that gate; I could not tell from the view whether the check is also gated. Confirm the check stays open.
14. Both Roth and traditional IRA, and income limits, are unmentioned; "not open to you" is the only hint. Fine for now.

### L5
15. (M) Months to wait is unexplained. A gain "10 months" old waits "3 months", one "11 months" waits "2". A beginner computes 12 minus 10 = 2 and then disagrees with the 3 the question hands them. The rule says "held more than a year" but never says the wait runs to 13 months. Fix: say "a gain is long-term after a year and a day, so count to month 13", or phrase "just over a year".
16. (M) "What does keeping the dearer fund 3 more months cost" has no formula in the rule and no wrong-answer feedback in the view. The help in item 4 shows value times fee gap times months over 12, but a learner who reaches item 7 or the check without the help gets only a bare number check. Fix: put the formula in step 5's rule and add feedback for the two likely slips (full-year cost; gain used as base).
17. (M) The swap rule says "Selling the bonds in the brokerage account is a sale: check its gain first", but no item ever gives a gain on those bonds, so the instruction is never used and the answer "Yes" ignores it. Fix: either give a gain in one swap item (with a decision) or state plainly that the lesson sets it aside.
18. The answer text "the stocks that move pay dividends, taxed at a lower rate" and "saves most of that" appear only after the answer; they are not in the rule. Never quantified. Fix: add one sentence to step 1.
19. Wash-sale loss: "Gains sold this year: None" is on every loss table but the rule never says why (losses first offset gains, then up to 3,000 of other income). Untested, so no stall. Also "a fund of another kind" is offered without explaining "same fund" (substantially identical).
20. "Sell or wait" gives only two options, but the rule itself offers a third (keep the fund, put new money into the cheaper one). Not defensible in the data given, so no ambiguity; just unused.
21. Wait advice ignores that the gain could shrink while you wait. Not taught; no question depends on it.
22. In 401(k) items the "Tax tables" are titled "for a brokerage account" beside a 401(k) statement; the right answer is "0 / sell now" every time. It can be passed by spotting "401(k)", which is the reasoning taught, so acceptable.

### L6
23. "Sell first in the 401(k)" is the right answer in every item, so it can be passed by position-free pattern (it is always the stock fund line). It is also the answer the rule's caution about employer stock leaving the plan seems to qualify. Fix: add one item where the sale needed exceeds the 401(k) holding (as in the worked example), or one where the 401(k) holds no employer stock.
24. The sale amount assumes the money stays on the list so the total does not change; not said. One clause.
25. Jargon met without gloss: Treasury bills, Form 1099-B (L5), money market account (L2), expense ratio is glossed. Fine.
