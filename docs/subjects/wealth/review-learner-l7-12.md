## l7 Mix and drift: PASS

My words, before the first question: add the stocks across the invested accounts (not the bank), divide by everything invested, compare with the share I chose. Under 5 points off, do nothing, whatever the news says. 5 or more off, move the gap from the side that is over to the side that is short, inside the 401(k) so no tax is owed. A fund that mixes itself needs nothing. Check: 5 of 5 by reasoning. News of a fall is a trap that the lesson names and then makes me resist in three of the five check items (10, 11, 12).

Defects (all polish or minor):
1. Polish, items 4, 6, 9, 11: "1 points off", "69%" beside "69.0%". Fix the singular and keep one decimal format.
2. Minor pattern, "Where would X make the trade": the right option is always "in the 401(k)" with the correct direction. It is not in the pass rule, so it does not decide. Fix: add one item where the over-weight fund sits only in the brokerage account.
3. Polish, Item 7 and check 12: "target-date fund" is explained only in the table footnote, and the rule says only "leave it". Say in the rule why (it re-mixes itself).
4. Polish: the 5-point line is never tested at 4.9 or 5.0. Add one item near the line.

## l8 A loss you could not pay: FAIL

My words: for each loss, compare the worst case with my savings. Small, keep it. Large and an insurer will carry it, pass it on. Large and nobody carries it (a price fall, a loan), avoid it. For lawsuits, add up what a claim could reach (all accounts but the 401(k), plus home value less mortgage), find the most insurance pays (limit plus any umbrella on top), and fix only if the first beats the second. Check: I answered the liability items by reasoning, 5 of 5.

Defects:
1. DECISIVE, pattern, steps 3 and 7 and check Q10 to Q12. The first half has only three stories: a small bill (keep), a lawsuit (pass), a margin loan (avoid). The numbers are always extreme ($1,250 against $62,000; $650,000 against $57,000; $110,000 against $55,000). I could answer all of them from the story type without comparing anything, and "without real harm" is never decided by a number. Fix: use each story type with sizes that flip the answer (a $3,000 repair with $2,500 saved; a lawsuit when savings are $2,000,000), and include a near-threshold amount.
2. Gap, steps 5 to 7: car and home limits are equal in every item, so "the limit" is never a choice. The sheet asks for both, and a real learner will meet two different numbers. Teach which one applies, and test it.
3. Polish: "LLC" is never spelled out and is explained only as "a claim against it stays with it".
4. Polish: step 1 measures a loss against savings, while step 4 measures a claim against everything owned. A beginner may wonder why the yardstick changed. One sentence would do.

## l9 Foreign holdings: FAIL

My words: add each account's highest balance; over $10,000 together means an FBAR. Form 8938 is a separate test, using two totals (December 31 and highest) against two lines that depend on filing status. A TFSA or a Canadian fund outside an RRSP goes to a cross-border preparer. Check: 4 of 4 right, but see 9.1.

Defects:
1. DECISIVE, coverage: Form 8938 needs "the December 31 total or the highest total", but in every practice and check item both totals fall on the same side of their lines. Checking one total, or only the larger line, answers every item correctly. The one case where the two disagree (the worked example, Dec 31 over $50,000, highest under $75,000) is never practised. Fix: add items where only one test trips, in both directions, for single and joint.
2. Stall, step 1: the Form 8938 line is shown as "over the first on the last day or the second at any time; married filing jointly, the third and the fourth", and the rule text gives no numbers. I needed three reads. Fix: a small table, single/joint by rows, "December 31" / "any time" by columns.
3. Polish: "Is there anything to do?" answered "Change it" for filing a report reads oddly.
4. Polish: "FinCEN" and "passive foreign investment company" are jargon, and "harsh rules" says nothing. The TFSA 1% a month line is never tested.

## l10 Who inherits: FAIL

My words: money in a 401(k), IRA or life policy goes to whoever its page names, not the will. Check each page against the family: someone named (not blank, not "my estate"), still the right person, and a backup. Also a will, a durable power of attorney, and an estate line of $15,000,000. Check: I got the page questions by looking, not by reading the family table (see 10.1).

Defects:
1. DECISIVE, pattern: in every page item exactly one page differs from the other two, or all three match. Picking the odd one out gives the right answer without reading the family history. Check Q11 and Q12 and practice items 1, 3, 5, 6, 10 all work this way. Fix: make some sets with all three pages stale, two stale, or one account alone, so the family table has to be used.
2. DECISIVE, pass bar: "at least 1 right" of the two paper questions, and one of them (everything on file) is answered "Neither". A learner who never looks for the power of attorney can pass. The check also never tests a missing backup or "my estate". Fix: require both paper questions, and cover all four page failures in the check.
3. Gap: the missing-backup rule appears in one practice item (6) and nowhere in the check.
4. Stall: "the plan's own rule decides who gets it" for a blank page is never explained.
5. Polish: "date the change" is unclear, "estate" is not defined, and the non-citizen spouse note is not tested.

## l11 The spending rate: PASS

My words: take yearly spending, subtract income that keeps coming (Social Security), divide what is left by the invested accounts (not the bank), and compare with 3.9%. At or under, leave it; over, spend less, work longer, or plan cuts. Next year's amount is this year's raised by prices, not a new share of the balance. After an early fall, spend a bit less for a while. Check: 4 of 4 by working it.

Defects:
1. Minor pattern, Item 4 and check Q9 second question: "spend a bit less" is the only option not contradicted by l7 and not absurd. How much less is never said. It does not decide, because only the typed amount counts in the pass rule.
2. Stall, polish: "By the plain rule" is first used in Item 4 with no definition.
3. Polish: 3.9% is given as one number for everyone, with no hint that it assumes a 30-year retirement.

## l12 The whole check: PASS (assuming lessons 2 to 6 are done)

My words: lay every paper out, hold each line against its own rule, change only the lines whose rule says so; a year with nothing to change is a good result. Check: I would pass by working each line; all-"Leave it" guessing fails because two of the three grids need changes.

Defects:
1. Gap: step 1 gives only the "under three months" half of the cash rule. Check Q12 offers "More cash than it needs: invest the rest", and the feedback says "three to six". Nothing says what to do above six, and no grid goes above six. Add the rule and an item.
2. Inconsistency: "10% of everything" uses all accounts (bank and Canada included); the mix uses invested accounts only. Nora's 20% cannot be reproduced from her invested accounts alone (21.8%). The answers are the same, but the numbers don't match what a learner computes. State the denominator.
3. Minor pattern: "Where would X sell first/trade" is always the 401(k) option (also l7).
4. Polish: "Big bills ahead" is None in every grid, so that rule is only tested in Q12. "1 months of $3,500" (Item 3, Q6), "1 points".
5. Polish: check Q8 to Q12 recall lesson content (turn an expense ratio into dollars, read a pay stub) that step 1 of this lesson doesn't restate; fine only for a learner who did the earlier lessons.
