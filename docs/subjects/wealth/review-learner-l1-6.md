# Learner review, wealth l1 to l6 (round 1)

Reviewer: cold beginner (adult project manager). Read only `docs/learner-view/wealth-l1..l6.md`.
Note: the views print the marked answers inline, so I could not cover them. I decided each answer from the rule text first, then compared. I got every practice and check question right by reasoning, but several only by pattern (listed under "P").

Kinds: U = something needed but not taught; S = own-accounts step (what to enter / what the result means); P = answerable by pattern; A = arguable answer; W = wording/feedback; J = jargon.

## L1 Your list, and what could lose it: FAIL
Way of deciding, in my words: list every account and total it; each bank or firm is covered only if it fails (FDIC bank, SIPC broker), never for falling prices; add up what I hold in one name at one bank and compare with $250,000; anything over has no cover, so move it.
Check score by reasoning: 3 of 3.
1. U, Step 2 and every "Change it": the remedy is never taught (move the excess to another bank or another ownership category). "Change it" is just a label.
2. U, Step 1: it says to write what pays for each account, but the 401(k) has no FDIC or SIPC. The worked answer line "The 401(k): $94,500 / $416,900 / Nobody / Change it / $12,400" is garbled and never says why "Nobody". Add one line: a 401(k) has neither; it is held for you by the plan.
3. U, the looked-up FDIC value says "per kind of ownership". Never explained (joint, retirement, trust). All samples are single-name.
4. S, Step 5 sheet: no field for the bank or firm name or whose name is on it, yet the rule groups by bank and owner. The sheet also asks fund costs and shows Canadian-account fields, which mean nothing here. I would not know what to enter or what "what pays" is based on.
5. P: "Change it" exactly when one bank total passes $250,000. "Nobody" is never right. No case has two banks, or a brokerage over its limit.
6. J: brokerage, holdings, SIPC are not glossed. Say where "Member FDIC" is found on a real statement.

## L2 Money you need soon: FAIL
Way of deciding: cash divided by a month's spending; under 3 months build up; over 6 with nothing coming, invest the rest; any big bill due within about 5 years must not sit in stocks.
Check score: 4 of 4.
1. U: moving the $45,000 to cash makes cash about 15 months, which then trips the "past six, invest the rest" rule. Never said that bill money is kept apart from the months-of-cash count.
2. U: what counts as cash (CDs, money market, an HSA?) and what a "usual month" includes (insurance, annual bills, taxes).
3. U/untested: under 3 months with a bill inside 5 years; a bill at exactly 5 years; a bill already held in cash; where "the rest" goes.
4. P: every bill sits in "Index 500 Fund (stocks)", so the years column alone decides. Add a bill already in savings.
5. S, Step 5: "the cash you can reach" is never defined; I would not know which of my accounts the sheet counts. Fund-cost fields are irrelevant here.
6. A: "more cash loses to rising prices" is stated as sure for 9 to 12 months with no job-risk caveat.
7. W: exact 3.0 and 6.0 are not placed.

## L3 What it costs a year, in dollars: FAIL
Way of deciding: percent times balance gives dollars, add the plan fee; switch only to a cheaper fund of the same kind on the same list.
Check score: 3 of 3.
1. P: the held fund's name decides. Names containing "Index" are always left, and Summit, Globe Select or Ridgeway are always changed. I never needed the Kind or the costs. Add an index fund that has a cheaper index twin, and a non-index fund that is cheapest of its kind.
2. W, dollar answers accept "within 10%". Leaving out the small fund still passes (Item 1: $193.65 vs $212.85; Check 6: $125.30 vs $130.00). Tighten to within $1 or a dollar-level tolerance.
3. U/conflict: "switching only costs you time" is false in a taxable account (selling taxes the gain; L5 says so). The sheet takes any account kind.
4. S, Step 5: real papers say "expense ratio" and "administrative fee", not "Yearly cost" and "Plan fee". Say these words once and where they print.
5. W: Check 4 and 5 are near copies of Item 2 (same two funds).

## L4 The full match, then the order of saving: FAIL
Way of deciding: put in at least the "up to" rate; dollars left = pay x (up to - mine) x cents per dollar. Then save in a fixed order: cash, match, card, HSA, IRA, more 401(k), brokerage.
Check score: 3 of 3.
1. U: tiered matches ("100% of the first 3%, 50% of the next 2%") and vesting (match can be forfeited if you leave) are common; neither the rule nor the sheet can handle them.
2. S, Step 8: a pay stub shows dollars per paycheck, but the lesson and sheet want "% of pay" and yearly pay. Teach the conversion, and say the match rule comes from the plan summary, not the stub.
3. U: why HSA, then IRA, then more 401(k)? Only memorized. One line each.
4. A, Check 9 (Mei): a 26% card against a 25-cent match, with 2 months of cash. Card first, or cash after the match, are defensible, and the lesson itself says "not a law".
5. W: "leave out the one that does not apply" means not eligible (HSA), done (Rosa's cash) and nothing owed (Ben's card). Only the first is taught. Seven buttons, six taps.
6. W: the 2026 limits (HSA, IRA, 401(k)) are shown, but no question uses them.

## L5 Taxes: where each fund sits, sell or wait, a loss used: FAIL
Way of deciding: bonds in the retirement account, stocks in the brokerage; sell a gain only once held over a year if that cuts the rate; do not buy the same fund back within 30 days of a loss sale.
Check score: 5 of 5.
1. S, Step 8: the sheet has no tax rate, interest paid, purchase date, gain or loss. The app "shows how each account is taxed", which gives me nothing to act on. Where do I find "tax rate on interest" (my bracket?) or "interest paid" (a tax form)? Two of three ideas have no own-account step.
2. U: swapping sells the bonds in the brokerage, which can itself trigger tax; the 401(k) may be too small; Roth accounts differ. "Saves $211.20" ignores the dividends and gains now taxed in the brokerage.
3. J: "wash sale" is never named, so I cannot recognise it on a real statement.
4. A: "wait only when waiting changes the rate" ignores that the gain may shrink and the dearer fund costs more meanwhile. Selling now is defensible.
5. P: placement questions: bond fund in the brokerage means change; otherwise leave.
6. W: three separate rules in one lesson (15 items), and "Buy ... again, 11 days before the sale" reads oddly.

## L6 Your biggest single bet: PASS (with fixes)
Way of deciding: add one company's stock across all accounts, divide by everything, over about 10% sell down to 10%; broad funds are not one bet.
Check score: 3 of 3.
1. U: "everything you have" does not say whether the home, a business or crypto count. Say "the accounts you listed".
2. U: which account to sell from (401(k) has no tax, brokerage may). "Could cost tax" appears only in wrong-answer feedback.
3. W: Check 6 and 7 give two different feedbacks for the same typed number (2.0% and 2.0%; 3.0% and 3.0%), so the learner cannot tell which applies.
4. J: Index 500 Fund is unlabelled in the 401(k); the rule says "thousands" of companies, Index 500 has 500.
5. S: the sheet has fund name only, so how the app knows two holdings are the same company is unclear.
6. P: "Change it" when over 10%.
