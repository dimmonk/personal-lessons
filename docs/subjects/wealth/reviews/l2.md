  I would have done.

- **Question 1 (help, leave).** 24,000 × 0.08% = 19.20; 45,500 × 0.03% = 13.65; + 180 = 212.85. Leave. Right. The help showed
  only the "find" step, so I did the dollars myself; good.
- **Question 2 (change).** Typed 1.05 and 0.07; 346.50 + 15.75 + 100 = 462.25; Change; International Index Fund (saves
  336.60). Right.
- **Question 3 (leave).** 0.03 and 0.06; 6.00 + 12.30 + 140 = 158.30; Leave. Right.
- **Could answer without learning anything.** By question 2 I noticed the pattern: if the fund I hold has "Index" in its
  name, the answer is Leave; if not, switch to the one with "Index" in its name. The Kind column always uses the identical
  words for same-kind funds, the bond held is always Total Bond Index Fund and always the cheapest bond. So the
  change/leave call and the switch can be got from the names alone, with no reading of costs. Every item, practice and
  check, has the same two-fund shape, the same plan names and the same fund names.
- **Confusing for me.**
  - "Yearly cost" is the lesson's word. My real 401(k) papers will say "expense ratio", "total annual operating expenses",
    "gross" and "net", maybe "per $1,000". The lesson never gives me any of those words, so I would not know which number
    on a real sheet is the "yearly cost".
  - "Kind" on a real sheet is a category such as "Large Blend", "Large Growth", "Foreign Large Blend", "Target Date 2055".
    The lesson only ever shows exact matches, so I do not know whether "Large Growth" and "Large Blend" count as the same
    kind.
  - "To within a tenth" in the pass rules: I first read it as 10 cents. It means within 10%.
- **On your own accounts.** Judging it as a beginner with real US accounts:
  - The sheet itself is easy to fill and the app does the multiplying, so if I had the right numbers I would get the right
    dollars.
  - Getting the numbers is the hard part, and the lesson does not help. "Your plan's fee sheet" is never named as a real
    paper or page (the yearly participant fee disclosure, or the plan website's investments/fees page, or each fund's
    page at the broker for an IRA). "Find the same lines on yours" just repeats the made-up sample with its made-up
    labels, so it teaches me nothing I can find on my own papers.
  - Account fees on a real statement come as a quarterly recordkeeping charge (needs ×4), and an adviser or robo-adviser
    fee comes as a percent of the whole account. The lesson only shows a flat "$96 a year", and the sheet's fee box is in
    dollars a year, so a percent fee would be left out or entered wrong. Part 2 names "advisory and account fees" as part
    of the job.
  - Most new 401(k) savers are put in a target-date fund. The sheet offers "A fund that mixes by your age", but the lesson
    never says what to do with one (leave it; a cheap S&P 500 fund is not the same kind).
  - Harm: the rule says switching "only costs you time". In a taxable brokerage account, selling a fund to switch can owe
    tax on its gains. A beginner who follows this lesson on a brokerage account could pay tax that it never warned about.
    Inside a 401(k), IRA or HSA the switch is free.
  - Verdict on this step: a beginner would likely stall at "which number is it, and where", or enter a gross ratio, a
    quarterly fee as yearly, or skip an advisory fee. Not reliably doable from what the lesson gave.

## The five answers

- **Gain: yes.** Turning a fund's percent cost and a flat fee into dollars a year for an account, on a statement and fee
  sheet, with no prior idea how. Also: same-kind-only switching.
- **Doing: yes.** Almost all the time is finding numbers, typing dollars and deciding; the reading is two short paragraphs
  and one worked example.
- **Check: partly.** It tests the dollar cost on new numbers, within 10%, which is the part 2 check. It does not test the
  change/leave judgment: the fund names give the answer ("Index" means leave, or the one to switch to), every item has
  the same two-fund shape and the same names, and no leave case has a held fund that is not called "Index" (the case the
  rule itself names: nothing of its kind costs less). The documents use the lesson's own labels, not the ones on a real
  fee disclosure, so it is not in the form of the real moment.
- **Clear: blocks, on the own-accounts step.** Places a beginner could not go on without outside help:
  1. Which number on real papers is the "yearly cost" (expense ratio; net or gross), and which paper or page it is on, for
     a 401(k) and for an IRA or brokerage account.
  2. How to judge "same kind" on real category names (Large Blend vs Large Growth; a target-date fund).
  3. How to enter a quarterly account fee or an advisory fee given as a percent of the whole account.
  4. That switching in a taxable account can owe tax (missing, and the lesson says the opposite).
  Smaller: "within a tenth" reads as 10 cents.
- **Waste:** "Find the same lines on yours" repeats the worked sample and adds nothing; replace it, do not keep it. Question
  3 repeats question 1's leave case in the same form; it could be the new kind of leave case below instead.
