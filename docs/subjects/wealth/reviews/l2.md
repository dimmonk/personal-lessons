## Before

A beginner adult new to US accounts. I know funds "have fees" and that a 401(k) is a work retirement account. I could not

**Your own accounts (judged as a beginner with a phone, 10 minutes, my own papers).**
- Cost in dollars: doable. The "where" lines tell me the 401(k) fee disclosure / investments page and a broker's fund
  page, to copy the net ratio, × 4 for a quarterly fee, and to turn an adviser's percent into dollars before typing.
  The app then does the multiplication. Two small gaps: the field says "Its yearly cost" not "net expense ratio" (the
  where-text covers it), and nothing says what to type for a holding that is not a fund (one company's stock, cash) —
  the field is required; "type 0" would do.
- Change or leave: not doable from what the lesson gave, outside the simplest 401(k).
  - The decision needs my fund's category and the cheaper funds of that category in my menu. The lesson never says where
    the category line is on real papers (a plan disclosure may call it "asset class" or "type"; a broker's fund page
    calls it the Morningstar category), and for an IRA or brokerage account it never says what my "menu" is or how to
    find same-category funds there. I would stall at "What will you change?" and probably press "Nothing to change".
  - The sheet's "What it holds" list (US stocks, Stocks outside the US, Bonds…) uses different words from the categories
    the lesson taught (Large Blend, Foreign Large Blend…), so the app cannot help me compare either.
  - If I did find a cheaper fund, nothing says how a switch is done: in a 401(k) the current balance and future
    contributions are changed separately, and changing only one is the common beginner miss. In a taxable account "check
    first" for tax does not say where (the gain or loss column on the statement).

## After

Things I can do now that I could not before, on the real task:
1. Turn a fund's net expense ratio into dollars a year on what I hold (Step 1 rule, worked example, Q1-Q7).
2. Pick the net ratio, not the gross, off a fee sheet (worked example step 1, the sheet's note, Q2/Q5 waiver rows).
3. Add the account's fee correctly: × 4 for quarterly, percent × whole balance for an adviser (Q2, Q3, Q5, Q7).
4. Decide change or leave by comparing only within the same category, including leaving an expensive fund that is the
   cheapest of its kind and leaving a target-date fund for a cheaper one of another date (Q3, Q4, Q7).
5. Total my own accounts' yearly cost in dollars (Step 5 sheet), but not yet decide or make a switch on my own IRA or
   brokerage account.

## The five answers

- **Gain: yes.** I can now work out what an account costs a year in dollars from its statement and fee sheets (funds' net
  ratios plus quarterly or percent fees) and tell a same-category switch from a false one.
- **Doing: yes.** After two short reading screens, nearly all the time is reading the papers, calculating and choosing.
- **Check: yes.** Three new accounts on fresh numbers, cost within 10%, change or leave on each, one cheap fund to leave
  (Q6), one costly-but-cheapest-of-its-kind to leave (Q7), one switch (Q5), in the same papers-in-tabs form as the real
  moment. Same fund names as practice, new numbers and new right answers.
- **Clear: no, two places block a beginner.**
  1. The own-accounts change step: no guidance on where the category is on real papers, what the "menu" is for an IRA or
     brokerage account and how to find same-category funds there, or how a switch is made (balance and future
     contributions in a 401(k); where to see a taxable gain). The cost half is doable; the change half is not.
  2. At phone width, the fee-sheet table breaks fund names mid-word (Fund column ~48 px vs ~125 px for "Net expense
     ratio"); the name is the matching key on every item.
  Non-blocking: reuse of "Lakeside Manufacturing 401(k)" in Q4; "4 of 5" result example vs a 3-account check; no "type 0"
  for a stock or cash row; gross ratio passes the dollar tolerance in some items.
- **Waste:** little. The "find the net ratio, type it in" form on every practice item could drop after the first two
  (the dollar answer already needs it); the bond line could vary so it is not dead weight.

## Fixes needed

1. Own-accounts "where" lines: say where the category is (plan disclosure: the category / asset-class column; broker fund
   page: the "category" line, often "Morningstar category"); say the menu for a 401(k) is the disclosure's fund list and,
   for an IRA or brokerage account, funds of the same category at that broker (its fund search filtered by category).
   Consider letting the sheet take the category as words, so it matches what the lesson taught.
2. Own-accounts change: one line on how a switch is made — in a 401(k), move the current balance and change future
   contributions too; in a taxable account, look at the gain on the statement before selling.
3. Fee-sheet table at phone width: keep fund names whole (give the Fund column room; wrap the long headers instead of
   the names).
