# Wealth Preservation: plan (build-subject step 4, 2026-10-22)

Evidence: `docs/research/wealth.md` (W1-W36) and `docs/learning-science.md` (P, D, R ids). Nothing here is drawn from another
subject. Not personalized advice: the subject teaches how planners and economists reason; the learner applies it to their own sheet.

## What you'll be able to do, and when

**After this you can** run a planner's yearly check on your own US accounts: list everything, see what money you need soon is
exposed and what single loss could ruin you, find the certain leaks (yearly costs in dollars, a missed match, tax in the wrong
place, foreign filings) and the risks you did not choose (one big bet, a drifted mix), check who inherits and who can act, and
change only what the check calls for, **when** you start a US job, open or move an account, marry, divorce or have a child, a
statement or tax form arrives, and once a year.

**The test** (pass mark is ours, a judgement call; thresholds from the 2026-10-10 design record):
1. A new sample household of 6-7 US documents (401(k) statement and fee sheet, pay stub and match rule, brokerage statement,
   bank statement, a Canadian account summary, beneficiary pages, insurance declarations): yearly cost per account within 10%;
   largest single holding and stock/bond split within 2 points; match shortfall within 10%; months of spending in cash within half
   a month; every flag raised or cleared right (switch, sell down, rebalance, raise the rate, buffer short, cover a ruinous loss,
   foreign filing, PFIC/TFSA, beneficiary missing or stale, papers missing, spending rate high). **Every sound item left alone.**
2. Your own sheet: filled, every change dated with where, done within 30 days (your mark), next check in the calendar.

## The way of thinking (the planner's loop)

1. **See everything before changing anything**: every account, its protection, the cash, the cover, the papers (W1, W22).
2. **Compare the current course with a named alternative; "leave it" is a real answer** (W2). Change only for a gain you can show.
3. **Protect first what you cannot afford to lose**: money needed soon stays out of what can fall (capacity is not nerve, W18,
   W19, W28); a loss you could not pay is transferred, a small one kept (W20).
4. **Take what is certain before betting on forecasts**: costs (W6-W10), the match (W23), taxes (W25-W26). Returns are not yours to
   pick; diversify the bet you did not choose (W13, W14).
5. **Decide the rules in advance, then let them run**: a written rebalancing band, set-once funds and contributions; your own
   trading is the usual leak (W15-W17). Think in today's dollars (W27).
6. **Check again at set times** (W3), and hand the specialist questions (cross-border filings, estate law) to a specialist (W31-W33).

## The lessons (four units, twelve lessons)

Every lesson, by P10/P17/P19 and W36: one rule with its reason and the novice's wrong idea (P29); a worked document; one with the
last steps left (`support: { leave }`); new documents alone, **one to leave alone in every set** (W2, P26; the facet `call`); then
your own data (`own`) ending in a change made now or dated (W35; `actions`, .ics). Checks are new documents, `feedback: 'at-end'`.
Reading is the rule and the line only (P6). From unit B on, sets `mix` earlier lessons (P22, R9).

**Unit A. See it all**
1. **Your list, and what could lose it.** All accounts, type, balance, what protects each (FDIC, SIPC, neither, and that neither
   covers a fall). Check: a sample household's papers as `document` tabs → a `form` ask (type, balance) per account; `choose` the
   protection. Own: `sheet`. *Why:* planner step 1 (W1, W22).
2. **Money you need soon.** Months of spending in cash; known bills inside about five years sitting in stocks; too little, enough,
   or idle cash losing to inflation. Check: generator (`number` months, `choose` call), one household that is fine. Own: needs a
   spending figure and a bills list (gap 2). *Why:* capacity before tolerance (W18, W19, W27).

**Unit B. The certain leaks**
3. **What it costs a year, in dollars.** The built pilot (`l2` now, part 2; renumbered when parts change). *Why:* W6-W12.
4. **The full match, then the order of saving.** Rate that takes the whole match and dollars a year short (`document` paystub +
   matchrule, generator); then the rungs put in order (`order` ask) with a case where a rung does not apply. Own: rate changed on the
   plan site or dated; trigger `job`. *Why:* W23, W24.
5. **Taxes: where each fund sits, sell or wait, a loss used.** Tap the misplaced fund across two accounts; a sale's tax now versus
   waiting; a loss sale with a buy-back inside 30 days (`choose`, `tap`, a `number` for tax saved). Own: placement flags from the sheet
   (gap 1). *Why:* W25, W26.

**Unit C. Risks you did not choose**
6. **Your biggest single bet.** One company's share across all accounts (employer stock, RSUs); a broad index fund cleared.
   Generator `number` + `choose`. Own: share; sell-down dated. *Why:* W13, W14 (10% is a judgement call).
7. **Mix and drift, and your own hands.** Split across accounts, drift against your chosen mix, the trade and which account takes
   it; leave under 5 points or a target-date fund; a news-of-a-fall case (`article`) where the rule says hold. *Why:* W15-W17.
8. **A loss you could not pay.** The frequency-severity matrix on cases (retain, reduce, transfer, avoid); net worth against
   liability limits (umbrella); an LLC for a rental or business only; a business's safety nets; a loan that could force a sale.
   `choose` with a policy's limits shown as a `document` of form `table` (there is no declarations form; `table` suffices), `number` for the gap. Own: dated action to read your limits. *Why:* W20, W21.

**Unit D. US rules, handover, the long run**
9. **Foreign holdings under US rules.** Highest balances summed against the FBAR and 8938 thresholds (`ref` blocks, dated);
   flags for a Canadian fund (PFIC), a TFSA, an RRSP (no flag: automatic deferral). `document` form `foreign`. Own: booking a
   cross-border preparer dated; trigger before the first US tax year. *Why:* W31-W33.
10. **Who inherits, and who can act.** Each beneficiary page: missing, "estate", an ex-spouse, no backup; a will and power of
   attorney; a trust's job; estate tax checked and left. `document` form `beneficiary`, `tap`, `choose`. Own: fixed on the site or
   dated. *Why:* W29, W30.
11. **The spending rate.** This year's draw over the pot; next year's in today's dollars; inside the research range or not; a
   bad first decade and flexing. Generator, `ref` for the current estimate. No own step until a trigger (gap 3). *Why:* W27, W28.
12. **The whole check, done and repeated.** The test above; own sheet refreshed; changes dated; next check `repeat: 'year'`.
   *Why:* planner step 7 (W3); the yearly return is the spaced return (P24, P26 requires 2).
