# Stand-in learner review: Math, lesson 1 "Ballpark and check" (rev 2)

Reviewer: a fresh stand-in (did not build the lesson). Date: 2026-10-12. Seen: the packet (end result, real moment, the part's row and
check), docs/learner-view/math-l1.md, public/subjects/math/l1.lesson.js, gen-totals.js, items-ballpark.js, and the engine's number and
calculator code (public/app/lessons/number.js), run in node to confirm. Not seen: the design record, research, lesson standard, any
earlier review.

## Before

As a beginner adult with a phone: I can add a receipt on the calculator, slowly, and I trust whatever it says. I can eyeball a short cart
very roughly, but I have no method and no habit of making a rough answer first. I would not think to check a printed total, and if I did,
I would re-add it exactly or not at all. Splitting a bill, I go straight to the calculator. I would not catch a calculator answer ten
times too big unless it was absurd.

## Notes, item by item (worked as a learner)

- **Step 1, Round first, then add.** Clear and short. The rounding rule (50 cents up) is stated with two examples; quantities and the
  split each get one example. Used later: all of it. The line on the calculator doing × before + is needed for the order items and is
  true (confirmed: 5 × 11.09 + 3 × 9.09 + 3 × 7.99 + 4 × 6.89 = gives 134.25).
- **Step 2, worked example.** Easy to follow; ends on the rough answer $23 and says what a wrong total would look like ($2.34, $234.30).
  Taught me the compare move I used later.
- **Q1 (cart).** 5 + 18 + 1 + 4 + 6 = 34; exact 34.95 on the calculator. Fine.
- **Q2 (order with quantities).** 55 + 27 + 24 + 28 = 134 in my head. Right, but this was the hardest screen: four products held in my
  head and added. Step 1's example has only one quantity line, and nothing says "keep a running total as you go". A beginner can do it,
  slowly. Exact 134.25 by typing the sum as written.
- **Q3 (split by 7).** Needs knowing 7 × 8 = 56. The teaching shows only 6 (an easy table). A beginner who does not know the 7s would
  round to 55 or 60 and still land within 20%, so not blocking. Exact 7.92.
- **Q4 to Q6.** Same forms, fresh numbers, no help line. 27 / 26.85; 140 / 135.22; 8 / 8.07. By here the rounding was quick; the
  quantity order was still the slow one.
- **Step 5, Check a printed total.** Clear rule: a gap of $10 or more, or a whole line, is a mistake; a dollar or two is rounding. The
  tax-line note is useful for real receipts. The last paragraph (where totals go wrong most) is not used by any item.
- **Q7, Q8 (right totals).** Rounded 27 vs 27.85, 28 vs 28.45: right. Easy once the rule is known.
- **Q9, Q10 (wrong totals, a missed line).** 31 vs 17.06, 33 vs 21.16: wrong. "By about how many dollars": 31 - 17 = 14 and 33 - 21 = 12,
  both accepted (within $1 or 25% of 13.39 and 11.69). Finding that the gap matches one line is a good move; nothing in the check uses
  it.
- **Answerable without learning?** The plain cart estimates, partly: an adult can eyeball a 5-line cart within 20%. The quantity order,
  the split and the printed-total verdicts need the moves the lesson teaches. A blind guess at the three verdicts in the check passes 1
  time in 8; a flat estimate cannot pass because the check's carts, orders and splits run from about $6 to well over $100.

## The end check (done without the lesson open)

- Cart: 4 + 4 + 17 + 5 + 4 = 34 (exact 34.85): within 20%.
- Wide cart: 8 + 6 + 32 = 46 (exact 45.17): within.
- Order: 16 + 12 + 10 + 14 = 52 (exact 52.79): within.
- Split by 5: 130 / 5 = 26 (exact 25.95): within.
- Split by 4: 124 / 4 = 31 (exact 31.40): within (128 / 4 = 32 would be too).
- Printed total 27.55, lines round to 28: right. Printed 24.45, lines round to 24: right. Printed 59.65, lines round to 30: wrong (a slipped
  tens digit).
- Result: 5 of 5 estimates, all three verdicts right: passed. The pass rule (at least 4 of 5 estimates within 20%; the wrong total caught;
  no right total doubted) matches the part's check.

## After

Abilities I did not have before, on the real task:
1. Make a rough total of a short receipt in my head before trusting the calculator, and use it to catch a calculator total that is off by
   a missed line or a misplaced decimal point (worked example, Q1, Q4).
2. Estimate an order with quantities by rounding the price and multiplying each line (Q2, Q5, check order).
3. Estimate a split share by rounding the bill to a number the group divides evenly (Q3, Q6, both check splits).
4. Check a printed total by rounding and adding the lines, and tell a dollar or two of rounding from a real error: a slipped digit ($10
   or more) or a missed line (Step 5, Q7 to Q10, check receipts).

## The five answers

- **Gain: yes.** Named above; the clearest new ones are checking a printed total and the round-to-divisible split.
- **Doing: yes.** After two short teaching screens, nearly all the time is typing estimates, typing exact totals on the calculator and
  judging receipts. Reading is under a fifth of the time.
- **Check: yes.** It tests both halves of the part (4 of 5 estimates within 20% with no calculator; the one wrong total of three caught),
  on receipts (the real moment's form), with fresh numbers and a wider cart than practice. One gap from the real moment: every check
  prompt names the method ("Round each price to the nearest dollar...", "Round the bill to a number that 5 divides evenly"). The real
  moment names none. Not blocking at lesson 1, because the rounding is the skill being tested and the hint does not give the number, but
  see the fixes.
- **Clear: yes, nothing blocks.** Places a beginner could stall, none fatal:
  - Q2 / Q5: four quantity lines multiplied and added in the head, with only a one-line example taught. Add one sentence to Step 1:
    "Keep a running total: add each line as you go."
  - Q3 split by 7: needs the 7 times table; the taught example is 6. A line such as "any nearby number works if it is easy to divide;
    $55 ÷ 7 is about $8" would help.
  - Length: about 15 to 20 minutes on a phone (each exact total is 20 to 40 calculator taps), more than ten. The "you can stop here"
    screens after Steps 3 and 4 make this workable.
- **Waste:** the last paragraph of Step 5 (where totals go wrong most) is used by no item and could go. The "by about how many dollars"
  follow-up is practice only and untested; keep it only if a later lesson uses finding the bad line. Step 4's exact totals could be
  dropped to estimate-only to save time, at some cost to the compare habit.

## Fixes (none blocking)

1. In the check, drop the method sentence from the estimate prompts ("About how much does this cart come to?" alone; "About how much does
   each person pay?" alone), so the check matches the real moment with no method named.
2. Step 1: add "keep a running total as you go" for orders with several lines.
3. Step 1: show a split with a harder table (7 or 8), or say any easy nearby number will do.
4. Cut the last paragraph of Step 5.
