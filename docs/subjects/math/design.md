# math: design record (build-subject skill)

Written 2026-10-10. Gates 2 and 3 are drafts waiting for the owner's approval; until then V69 keeps every lesson of this
subject as it is. The content that exists predates the gates and is audited against this record (gates 4 and 5, designed
blind) before anything is kept.

Approved by the owner: 2026-10-10, "approve all, keep going" (gates 2, 3 and the pilot, for every subject; the pilot is still to be tried on a phone, and what it shows comes back into this record).

**Note on the approval (2026-10-10).** The owner approved the draft lines for gates 2 and 3. The blind design below keeps
their meaning (the same kinds of everyday problem; worked examples, fading, mixed practice, feedback) and makes them
checkable: the answer is a typed number, an estimate comes first, and the test has numbers. Those three additions were
not in what the owner saw, so they need the owner's yes before gate 6 is built.

## Gate 1. Kind of learning and evidence

**Kinds.** Mainly *doing a procedure*: in real life the learner has to turn a situation into a calculation and get a
number out of it, on numbers they have never seen. Inside it sits one step of *judging situations*: seeing what kind
of problem this is (a rate, a percent, an area, growth over time), because that decides the procedure. The judging step
serves the procedure; it is never the end in itself. No facts to recall beyond the few set-ups, and no habit or body
skill.

**Evidence:** `docs/research/learning-everyday-math.md` (M1 to M10), with `docs/research/review-after-lessons.md`
(R1 to R9) and the sources of `docs/learning-science.md` P10, P17 to P22 (its "diagnostic classification" frame is not
used: here classification is one step inside solving).

**The principles that drive the design**
1. **A worked example with real numbers before the learner solves a new kind**, each step with its reason (M1).
2. **Fade it into a problem**: last step left to do, then more, then the whole problem; ask for the reason of a worked
   step (M2).
3. **Teach the structure of each kind as one fixed picture** (a two-row table for a rate, a 100% bar for a percent, a
   sketch with measurements for an area, a year-by-year table for growth) and read the whole problem, never key words
   (M3).
4. **Mix the kinds once each is introduced**, so the learner chooses the method from the problem (M4, R9).
5. **The answer is a typed number**, the real moment's own response; choosing among given numbers lets the learner
   skip the set-up (M5, R8).
6. **Feedback shows the full working with the learner's numbers and names the slip** when the answer matches a known
   wrong path (an upside-down ratio, the percent of the wrong whole, added percents, a missed unit change) (M6).
7. **A calculator does the arithmetic**; the learned part is the set-up and the check (M7).
8. **An estimate first, and the answer checked against it** (M8; a judgement call, scored so it can be tested).
9. **Kinds come back later as new problems**, until each is solved right in several sessions (M10, R2, R4).

Asked out loud: if the learner only learned to recognize the kinds (principle 3 without 1, 2, 5), would they be closer
to the end result? Not really: naming "this is a percent problem" does not produce the number. Recognition is kept only
as the first step of solving.

## Gate 2. End result

**After this subject you can** work out the number an everyday situation needs (a scaled amount, a percent, an area and
how much to buy, the cost of a loan or the growth of savings), set it up from the situation yourself, with a calculator
for the arithmetic, and tell whether the answer is sensible, **when** a receipt, a recipe card, a sale tag, a room or a
loan offer hands you a few numbers and a question and nobody tells you the method.

**The real-world test.** Six new problems, in mixed order, each a short situation with real figures in the form of the
real moment (a receipt, a recipe card, a sale tag, a room's measurements, a loan or savings offer): one from each of the
five kinds of gate 4 (parts 2 to 6) and one two-step problem (part 7). No label or hint of the method. For each, the
learner types a rough estimate, then the answer with its unit (calculator allowed). **Pass:** at least 5 of 6 answers
right to the rounding the problem states, and no answer more than double or less than half of the right one (the wild
answer is the mistake that costs money). Taken at the end of the subject, and again two weeks later on six new problems.

**Out**, so the subject stays short:
- Arithmetic by hand and speed drills (the calculator does it, M7); only rough mental rounding for estimates is taught.
- Algebra beyond the one unknown a ratio or a percent gives; equations, negatives, powers beyond growth over years.
- Geometry beyond rectangles, shapes made of rectangles, circles and boxes; right triangles and the ladder rule.
- Chance and statistics (the Statistical Claims subject).
- Deciding which loan or saving is wise (the Wealth subject): this subject gives the number, not the choice.
- Working out a loan's monthly payment (the lender states it); the learner works out what it costs in total.

## Gate 3. How it is practiced, and whether the app can deliver it

**Practice method.** The core is the real moment's own action: a short everyday situation, set up and worked out to a
typed number. For each kind of problem: one worked example with each step's reason and the kind's picture (principles
1, 3); then completion problems with the last step, then the last two steps, left to do, with one "why this step"
prompt (2); then whole problems. Every problem: type a rough estimate (no calculator), then the answer (calculator
allowed) (5, 7, 8). From the second kind on, part of every lesson is mixed problems of all kinds so far, unlabeled (4).
Wrong kinds come back in later sessions as new problems with new numbers (9).

**Time.** About 15 minutes a lesson: reading at most 2 (what this kind looks like, the picture), the worked example 2,
and about 11 solving. More than three quarters of the time is solving.

**Feedback.** Immediate, from the app, after each typed answer: right or wrong against the problem's stated rounding;
then the full working with the learner's own numbers, the picture filled in, and, where the answer matches a known wrong
path, one line naming that slip. The estimate is shown next to the exact answer. While practicing, an answer more than
double or less than half the learner's own estimate gets "your answer and your estimate disagree; look again" before it
is scored; this prompt is removed in end checks.

**Delivery check** (what the app must do; checked against the engine, `public/app/lessons/ask.js` and `drill.js`, not
the math content):

| Needed | Exists now? |
|---|---|
| A typed numeric answer, scored with a tolerance (the problem's rounding), units shown | **No.** A problem item is answered by picking one of `answer.choices`; the only typed fields are unscored notes |
| A typed estimate before the answer, scored as within half to double, and the "disagree" prompt | **No** |
| Known wrong answers per problem mapped to a named slip | **No** (the choices could carry this, but only for choices) |
| A worked example step by step, then completion (last steps blank, typed) | **Partly**: a "last step" item exists (`solve:'last'`), answered by choice |
| Many problems per kind with fresh numbers (a problem written once with number ranges, its answer and wrong paths computed), so practice, retries and review never run out or repeat | **No**: problems are fixed cases |
| Mixed, unlabeled problems of earlier kinds; wrong ones back later | **Yes** (A12 return items, the weekly review), but as choice items |
| A calculator on the problem screen, so the learner does not leave the app on a phone | **No** (a judgement call: switching apps mid-problem on a phone loses the problem) |
| The kind's picture (a two-row table, a 100% bar, a labeled sketch, a year table) drawn with the problem's numbers | **Unknown**; to check when gate 6 is built |

So the typed answer with tolerance, the estimate, the named slips and number-varied problems are built **before** any
lesson. Swapping in the existing multiple-choice item because it is there is the failure this skill exists to stop.

## Gate 4. The parts of the end result

Ordered so each builds on the ones before: part 1 is used in every later part; parts 3 and 4 need the rate set-up of
part 2; part 6 needs percents; part 7 needs them all.

| # | Part | Its check (a small version of the gate 2 test: new problems, typed, estimate first, no hint) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ballpark and check**: round the numbers of an everyday total (a receipt, price times quantity, a bill split) and get a rough answer in your head; check a total against it | 4 of 5 estimates within 20% of the exact answer, without the calculator; and of 3 shown totals, the one that is wrong (a slipped digit or a missed item) is caught |
| 2 | **Rates and scaling**: one amount per another (per person, per hour, per liter, per dollar, a map scale); scale a recipe, compare unit prices, convert a currency or a unit | 4 of 5 rate problems right, none outside half-to-double |
| 3 | **Percents forward**: a percent of an amount, what percent one amount is of another, the percent change from one amount to another | 4 of 5 right, none outside half-to-double |
| 4 | **Percents backward and stacked**: the price before a discount or tax, a discount then a tax, up then down by the same percent, percent against percentage points | 4 of 5 right, none outside half-to-double |
| 5 | **Areas, volumes and how much to buy**: rooms and walls (minus a door), shapes made of rectangles, a circle, a box; coverage per can or box; round up to whole units | 4 of 5 right including the rounding up, none outside half-to-double |
| 6 | **Money over time**: simple interest, growth compounded over years (with the calculator), the total cost of a loan from its payment and term, doubling time as an estimate | 4 of 5 right, none outside half-to-double |
| 7 | **Two steps and mixed**: problems that join two kinds (paint: area, then cans, then cost; a unit price after a discount), all kinds mixed and unlabeled | The gate 2 test |

**Map both ways.** Lessons 1 to 7 serve parts 1 to 7, one each; no lesson without a part. **Adds up:** the gate 2 test is
one problem of each of parts 2 to 6 plus one of part 7, each with part 1's estimate; if every part's check passes, the
learner has solved each kind cold, with an estimate, and mixed (part 7). Part 7's check is the gate 2 test itself.

## Gate 5. Each lesson

Every lesson has the same shape; the bulk is solving. From lesson 3 on, the mixed block contains every kind taught so
far, unlabeled. Feedback in every block is gate 3's. Estimate first on every problem from lesson 1 on.

**Lesson 1. Ballpark and check** (part 1) — the pilot.
- Do: read what rounding to one figure means, with one receipt worked (1 min); 6 everyday totals estimated in the head
  and typed, each followed by the exact total (5 min); 4 receipts or bills where a total is shown: right or wrong, and if
  wrong, by about how much (3 min); end check (4 min). About 13 minutes.
- Feedback: the estimate against the exact answer and how far off in percent; for a receipt, which line the error is in.
- End check: part 1's.
- Why: principles 5, 7, 8 (the check every later lesson leans on); the real moment is the receipt itself.
- If the learner did only this, closer? Yes: catching a wrong bill and knowing roughly what a cart costs is real use.

**Lesson 2. Rates and scaling** (part 2).
- Do: read what a rate is and the two-row table (1 min); one worked example, scaling a recipe from 4 to 7 servings, each
  step's reason, with a prompt "why divide by 4 first?" (2 min); 3 completion problems (unit price, hourly pay, fuel per
  distance), last step then last two steps typed (3 min); 6 whole problems, varied settings (a currency, a map scale, a
  unit change) (6 min); end check (4 min). About 16 minutes.
- Feedback: the table filled in; the named slip for the upside-down ratio ("you divided 4 by 7").
- End check: part 2's.
- Why: principles 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8.
- If only this, closer? Yes: rates are the most common everyday problem.

**Lesson 3. Percents forward** (part 3).
- Do: read the 100% bar: the whole is 100% (1 min); one worked example each for a percent of, a share as a percent and a
  percent change (3 min); 3 completion problems (2 min); 5 whole problems (5 min); 3 mixed problems with lesson 2 (2
  min); end check (4 min). About 17 minutes.
- Feedback: the bar filled in; slips named: the change divided by the new amount instead of the old, percent of the wrong
  whole.
- End check: part 3's.
- Why: principles 1 to 6, 4 for the mix.
- If only this, closer? Yes: a tip, a raise, a price change.

**Lesson 4. Percents backward and stacked** (part 4).
- Do: read why a discount and a tax do not add (1 min); worked examples: a price before a 20% discount, a discount then a
  tax (2 min); 3 completion problems (2 min); 5 whole problems, including "up 20%, then down 20%" and percentage points
  (5 min); 3 mixed with lessons 2 and 3 (2 min); end check (4 min). About 16 minutes.
- Feedback: the bar run backward; slips named: added the percents, took the percent of the price after the change.
- End check: part 4's.
- Why: principles 1 to 6; the forward/backward contrast is the kind the evidence on mixing targets (4).
- If only this, closer? Yes: the "what did it cost before?" and stacked-discount mistakes cost real money.

**Lesson 5. Areas, volumes and how much to buy** (part 5).
- Do: read the labeled sketch, square units, and rounding up to whole cans (1 min); worked example: paint for a room with
  a door, coverage per can (2 min); 3 completion problems (an L-shaped floor, a round rug, a planter box) (3 min); 5
  whole problems, some in mixed units (5 min); 3 mixed with earlier kinds (2 min); end check (4 min). About 17 minutes.
- Feedback: the sketch with the measurements; slips named: perimeter instead of area, a unit not converted, rounded down.
- End check: part 5's.
- Why: principles 1 to 6, 3 for the sketch.
- If only this, closer? Yes: buying paint, flooring, soil.

**Lesson 6. Money over time** (part 6).
- Do: read the year-by-year table and the calculator's power key (1 min); worked examples: simple interest; growth at 5%
  for 10 years; a loan's total cost from payment times months (3 min); 3 completion problems (2 min); 5 whole problems,
  including doubling time estimated by 72 divided by the rate and checked on the calculator (5 min); 3 mixed (2 min); end
  check (4 min). About 17 minutes.
- Feedback: the table's first years filled in; slips named: simple where it compounds, a monthly rate used as yearly,
  interest counted on the payment.
- End check: part 6's.
- Why: principles 1 to 7.
- If only this, closer? Yes: what a loan really costs and what savings grow to.

**Lesson 7. Two steps and mixed** (part 7).
- Do: one worked two-step example (paint: area, cans, cost) (2 min); 3 two-step problems with the first step done (3
  min); 8 problems of every kind, mixed, unlabeled, some two-step (9 min); then the gate 2 test (6 problems, about 6 min).
  About 20 minutes; may be split over two sessions with the test opening the second.
- Feedback: as before; for a two-step problem, which step went wrong.
- End check: the gate 2 test. It comes back two weeks later on six new problems.
- Why: principles 2, 4, 9; real problems seldom come one step at a time.
- If only this, closer? Yes: it is the end result.

**Review.** The weekly review for Math is new problems of the kinds done, mixed, typed, estimate first (R8, R9, M10),
never questions about the lessons.

## Audit of the existing lessons (2026-10-10)

Read after gates 1 to 5 were written: `public/subjects/math/` (key, specimens, the six units) and
`docs/learner-view/math-u1.md` to `u6.md`. The existing course is a key: a first question, "What is this problem
about?", with five families, and 23 named types of problem under them. Unit One teaches the families; Units Two to Six
teach one family each; the course ends on 35 specimens to be named.

| Unit | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| U1. Check what the problem is about (5 families) | **goes** | Sorting only: 20 cards, then 23 drill items that each ask "What is this problem about?" and never a number. It serves no part; in this design, seeing the kind is one step inside solving, and three of its five families are out |
| U2. Whole numbers (prime check, prime factors, greatest common factor, least common multiple, remainder, irrational numbers) | **goes** | Serves no part: none of these is a problem everyday life hands an adult (gate 2, out) |
| U3. A missing number (rearranging a formula, proportion, systems, quadratics) | **changes** (proportion only) | Proportion is part 2, and its worked example (12 kg of apples), "why this step" prompt, "check the direction" step and wrong answers named by slip are close to lesson 2. But the practice is three to five cards of reading per type, then a chain of "which kind" questions before a number picked from three. Rearranging (the price before a coupon) belongs in part 4 as percents backward. Systems and quadratics go |
| U4. Amounts over time (linear, exponential, how long, one-time change) | **changes** (exponential growth and "5% means times 1.05") | Growth compounded over years is part 6, and "going up 5% means multiplying by 1.05" belongs at the start of part 3. Same practice problem as U3. Logarithms as a procedure go (doubling time becomes an estimate by 72); "a one-time change" is not a kind a learner meets |
| U5. Counting ways and chances | **goes** | Serves no part: permutations and combinations are not everyday, and base rates belong in Statistical Claims |
| U6. Shapes (Pythagorean theorem, trigonometry, similar shapes, square-cube law) | **goes** | Serves no part as built: part 5 is rooms, walls, paint and how many to buy, and none of that is taught (the one paint problem is a specimen to be named, not solved). Map and photo scale move into part 2 as a rate |
| Specimens (35) and the full determination | **goes** | The course's last step names the type and the route of a problem; nothing is worked out. Gate 2's test is solving |

**Overall verdict: rebuild from scratch.** No unit is what gate 5 would have designed. Parts 1 (estimate and check),
3 and 4 (percents), 5 (how much to buy) and 7 (two steps) have no unit at all; percents, the most common everyday maths,
appear only as growth multipliers in U4. What can be reused as raw material, after it passes its gate 5 block: the
worked-example shape with a "why this step" prompt and a direction check, and the habit of writing each wrong answer as
a named slip, which becomes gate 3's known wrong paths. The proportion and compound-growth problems of U3 and U4 can be
mined for settings and numbers.

**The three most important problems**
1. **It teaches sorting problems, not solving them.** Unit One and the closing specimens never ask for a number, and
   every drill item in Units Two to Six asks two or three "what kind of problem is it" questions before the working. The
   real moment ends in a number; naming the type is at best one step toward it.
2. **The number is picked from three, never typed, and the app cannot take a typed number.** With three numbers on screen
   the learner can check each against the problem or rule out the odd one, which skips the set-up that is being learned
   (M5). Each unit also has 20 to 27 cards (8,000 to 10,000 words in the learner view) before about 12 drill items, so
   most of the time goes to reading, not solving.
3. **The content is a school syllabus, not everyday life.** About half of the 23 types (primes, greatest common factor,
   least common multiple, irrational numbers, systems, quadratics, permutations, combinations, trigonometry, the
   square-cube law) are problems an adult seldom meets, while the problems they meet weekly (a discount then a tax, a
   percent change, a unit price, paint for a room, checking a bill by a rough estimate) have no unit.
