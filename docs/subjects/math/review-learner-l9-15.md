# Learner review, Math L9 to L15 (round 1)

Role: cold beginner, learner views only (regenerated first; L1 skimmed for the method: say what is asked and given, rough answer, name the kind and draw it, exact on the calculator, look back).

Honesty note: the views print the marked answers inline, so I worked each problem in my head while reading, with the answer visible nearby. Scores below are "I reached this by reasoning and it matched", not blind scores. Items where I checked the arithmetic myself: ladder, 1.04^18, 1.04^36, 1.05^14, loan payments, test counts. All matched.

Verdicts: L9 PASS, L10 PASS, L11 FAIL, L12 PASS, L13 PASS, L14 FAIL, L15 PASS (with the L11 fix applied to its car-loan item).

## L9 Ladders, scale and size: PASS
Way of deciding, in my words: a ladder against a wall is a right triangle, the ladder is the longest side; to find the ladder add the two squares, to find the height or the foot take the foot's square off the ladder's, then root. Anything that grows in every direction grows by the factor times itself (area) or three times (volume). Check: 5 of 5 (21.30, 20.62, 2.56, 13.5, 21.71), and the rough parts.
1. Check 16, 17, 20 repeat practice items with the same numbers (22 ft ladder = Item 1, 20 ft / 5 ft = Item 5, 21 ft / 5.5 ft = Item 2). Exact answers can be recalled, not reasoned. Fix: draw check numbers from a different set.
2. Rough questions on the ladder are passable by typing the ladder length or the wall height (within 20% of 19.36 or 20.62). Fix: tighten to 10% for these, or ask "how much shorter than the ladder", or add a rough that cannot be answered by echoing a given.
3. Item 2 and checks 17/20: "foot 5.5 ft out, the safe way" with 21 ft up does not match the 1-in-4 rule taught (a 21.71 ft ladder would stand 5.43 ft out). Reads as sloppy; a careful beginner may doubt the rule. Fix: give "the foot is 5 ft out" without "the safe way", or make numbers consistent.
4. Pizza wrong-answer feedback exists for only one slip (typing the width ratio). Typing 0.44 or 3.6 gets nothing. Polish.
5. Possible fact for the research reviewer, not a learner defect: the usual 4-to-1 ladder rule is 1 ft out per 4 ft of height to the support, not of ladder length.
6. "Area", "volume", "square root" assumed known. Fine for an adult, polish only.

## L10 Growth year by year: PASS
Way of deciding: if the same dollars are added each year, start plus amount times years; if a percent each year, multiply by 1.0x once per year (xʸ), because each year's percent is of a bigger number; rough with 72 divided by the percent for doubling time. Check: 5 of 5.
1. Every "how many whole years" item has years = 72 divided by the rate exactly (18, 36, 36, 18, 36; only 2% and 4%). The answer can be reached from the rough rule alone, without trying 17 and 18. Fix: include 3%, 5%, 6% and goals that are not exact doublings (e.g. 1.5 times).
2. The rough rule only covers whole doublings (every rough item uses 12, 14, 18, 24 or 36 years). Nothing teaches a rough for, say, 10 years. A real situation will not be that tidy. Fix: one item with a non-doubling year count and a taught rough (e.g. 1.05 xʸ 10 is about 1.6).
3. "APY" is never explained. Polish: one clause, "the yearly rate with interest added".
4. The "adds or multiplies" choice is decided by "$ amount" versus "%" wording. That is the taught reasoning, fine, but add one case where a percent is of the start only (simple interest) or accept it as out of scope.

## L11 What a loan costs: FAIL
Way of deciding: cost = all the payments added up, minus what was borrowed; a yearly rate is a yearly rate, so a month's interest is a twelfth. Check: I can do the exact parts 5 of 5, but a natural rough answer fails.
1. Rough-step trap (decides the verdict). The worked example rounds $396.02 to $400, the nearest hundred. Following that, check 13 ($731.00 a month, 36 months, $23,500) gives about $700 x 36 = $25,200 less $23,500 = $1,700, against the right $2,816: more than 20% off, marked wrong. Item 1 ($473.65 to $500) fails the same way. The cost is a small difference of two big numbers, so rounding error is magnified, and nowhere is this taught. A beginner who reasoned correctly fails the rough part and cannot see why. Fix: teach "round the payment to two figures (nearest $10), because we subtract two big numbers", show one wrong coarse rounding with its result, and make the example round 396.02 to 396 or 400 with that reason stated.
2. The monthly rate rule is printed inside every card statement ("charged each month at the APR divided by 12"), so no question tests deciding yearly versus monthly. Fix: drop the note from some items so the lesson's own wrong idea is tested.
3. No question asks whether the loan is "sensible" or compares two offers, though the subject goal says "tell whether it is sensible". Polish for this lesson, noted for the design.
4. Rough tolerance is 20% of the cost, which is tight on small costs (about $46 on the washer); a rounding of $116 to $120 gives $720 - $650 = $70, 50% off. Same fix as 1.

## L12 Packs, leftovers and lining up: PASS
Way of deciding: whole things round up (buses); to split into equal groups with nothing left, the number of groups must divide both amounts, so try divisors of the smaller from the biggest down; to make two pack sizes come out even, count up by the bigger pack until the smaller divides it. Check: 5 of 5.
1. Each of the three kinds always appears with its own story (buses, party bags, cookout, timetable). A beginner can pick the method from the story and never learn to tell groups from packs when the story is new. The lesson says how to compute each but not how to tell them apart (groups: split down, answer no bigger than the smaller amount, "most"; packs/lining up: count up, "fewest", "next together"). Fix: add that sentence to Step 1 and one mixed item with a new story for each kind.
2. Check 17 repeats Item 1 (337 people, 48 seats); check 15 repeats Item 12 (8 and 12). Same fix as L9 item 1.
3. Grammar in feedback: "1 people on the last", "1 empty seats", "the other 1 ride". Polish.
4. "Why it works" (primes) is never used by any question. A beginner reads it as noise or as something to learn. Polish: cut or tie to a question.
5. Listing "what divides 30 from the biggest down" assumes I can list divisors; for 24 or 30 I did, but a weaker beginner will stall. Polish: one example showing how to list them.

## L13 Counting ways and chances: PASS
Way of deciding: choices of one of each multiply; for chances, picture 1,000 people; "at least once" is 1,000 minus the people who miss every time; for a test, count who tests positive (true plus false) and ask what share of those really have it. Check: 5 of 5.
1. Bike lock rough is hard to read ("an hour is 3,600 seconds, about 4,000... a bit more because 3,600 is less"). It stalled me for a second read. The 3,600 seconds per hour is only given in the help, never in Step 1, and the check has no help. Fix: state 60 x 60 = 3,600 in Step 1 and use a simpler rough (divide by 3,600 as "about 4,000, then add a tenth").
2. Check 17 repeats Item 6 exactly (30 in 1,000, 80%, 8%). Fix as L9 item 1.
3. The story gives the type away (clothes: multiply; ticket: opposite; test: counting people). Again no mixed-up case. Polish for this lesson; the mixed practice in L15 partly covers it.
4. Tickets are assumed independent and the odds are "1 in 5". Not stated. Polish.
5. Rounding in the 1,000-person count (58.8 to 59) is fine and tolerances cover it. No defect.

## L14 A missing number: FAIL
Way of deciding: to go backward through a formula undo the steps in reverse order (take off 32, then divide by 1.8); a pass versus pay-each-time breaks even at fee divided by the saving per time; a square with its corner missing, fill the corner in, root, then take off the path. Check: 5 of 5 by reasoning.
1. Answer leak in the plan (decides the verdict). In every backyard item the drawing's labels print the answer before I ask: "13.362291495737216 by 13.362291495737216 (Bed)" in Item 3, "11.620499351813308" in Item 5, "6 by 6" in Item 7, 9.489... in Item 12, and in the check 8.180339887498949 (Check 15) and 13.0996688705415 (Check 17). I can type 13.36 or 8.18 without any reasoning. L9 labels the unknown "?"; this lesson should too. Also the unrounded decimals are unreadable on screen. Fix: label the bed side "?" and show only the given path width and total area; reveal the figure with the answer in feedback, rounded.
2. "Summer pass: $40 a month, then a swim $3" is ambiguous ("then a swim" reads as a one-off). Step 1 words it correctly ("plus $4 a class"). Fix: "Summer pass: $40 a month, plus $3 each swim".
3. The break-even is 6.67 or 7.5 swims, but nobody can swim a fraction; the which-costs-less question always uses a number far from the break-even, so the whole-number edge is never met. Polish.
4. The Fahrenheit rough ("take off 30 and halve, add a tenth") needed two reads. Tolerance forgives skipping the tenth. Polish.
5. The calculator chain "77 - 32 / 1.8 =" works only on the left-to-right calculator taught in L9. Fine in this subject, polish to say so once more.

## L15 Two steps, mixed, and the test: PASS
Way of deciding: the same five steps; when the numbers given do not lead straight to the answer, find the number you need first (cans before cost, gallons before cost, tip before share), round up whole things; then the right method for the kind. Check (12): I got 12 of 12 by reasoning (1.75, does not scale and 113.95, 22.0, 96, 501 and 4, 1.11 and 1.5, 14.43, 34,883.43, 4,548.00, 5 and 51, 21.6, 8.75 and each time).
1. The lesson's own new skill is barely tested. The twelve check items (seeds 6 to 17) contain no cost-from-cans, no gas, no tip split; only paint area-to-cans (10) and mulch (11) are two-step. A beginner can pass 10 of 12 without having learnt Step 1 well. Fix: guarantee at least three of the twelve are the lesson's own two-step kinds.
2. Only five practice items (three listed, two mixed) before a 12-question test. Thin. Fix: add a block of three more two-step items with new situations (plane fare plus bag fees; tile count then cost).
3. No help on telling the kind apart across lessons; the test relies on remembering L1 to L14. Polish: a one-screen list of the kinds with the cue words.
4. Check 14 (car loan) carries the L11 rough-rounding trap (a rough of $450 or $400 x 60 fails). Fix with L11 item 1.
5. Check 8 rough: the typical wrong answer 18.1% lies within 20% of 22%, so it is accepted as a good rough, while the note under it calls it wrong. Polish: narrow the rough tolerance for percent questions.
6. Check 9 (50% off) is solvable by "double it" without the reverse-percent idea. Polish: vary the percent across seeds (25%, 40%).
