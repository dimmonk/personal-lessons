## The way of deciding, in my words (before the first practice question)

- L1: Say what is asked and what is given, ignore the rest. Round each figure to friendly dollars, add (or multiply/divide), get a rough number. Do the exact sum on the calculator. If the two are close, believe it. For a bill, round the lines and compare with the subtotal; a gap over about $3 means a mistake.
- L2: Swap ugly numbers for easy ones (47 x 66 becomes 50 x 70), simplify sums (four amounts near $110 are 4 x 110), use easy fractions for a share. Then nudge: if both numbers went up, the true answer is a bit lower.
- L3: Find the amount for one, then scale. Money and unit rates: going toward the side with the 1 means divide, away means multiply; check the direction makes sense.
- L4: Look for a part paid once (start charge, joining fee, shipping). None: scale it. One: scale only the growing part, add the fixed part back.
- L5: Find the whole (the 100%) first. Rough from 10%; exact from the multiplier (0.18, 1.04, 0.70). Share = part / whole. Change = change / where you started.
- L6: If the percent was taken from the old amount, divide by the multiplier to get back. Chained percents multiply in turn, never add. A change in percentage points is not a percent change; divide by the old rate.
- L7: Draw it. Area is length x width, walls are the distance around x height minus the door, L shapes are two rectangles, edging is the distance around. Use one unit (9 sq ft to a sq yd). Buying: divide, then round up.
- L8: Circle area is 3.14 x radius x radius, and the radius is half the way across. Volume is bottom x depth in feet (inches / 12). 27 cu ft to a cu yd. Divide by what a bag holds and round up (to the next half yard for mulch).

I could state all eight without stalling.

## Defects

### L1
1. (Stall risk, Items 2, 5, 8, 15, the M+ questions) The lesson names M+ and MR and the question shows "Clear the memory" as a key, but nowhere says to clear the memory before starting a new total. A beginner who ran one M+ question and then starts the next gets a total that includes the old memory, and none of the wrong-answer notes explains it. Fix: one sentence in Step 1 ("press Clear the memory before each new total"), plus a wrong-answer note.
2. (Polish, Step 6) The slip "a digit slips, so the sum is $10 or $20 too high" is described, but practice and check only ever show "a line left out" (Items 12, 13) or a flat $6.00 over (Check 23, 24). Add one digit-slip case.
3. (Polish, Check 22 to 24) The adds-up question is a two-way choice and the pass rule "none wrong" on a single "adds up" bill is a coin flip alone (1 in 8 to pass all three by guessing). Add a second "adds up" bill.
4. (Polish) "Shown after" example text uses invented numbers that do not match the question ("You said $38.01... 10% over") but that is clearly a template.

### L2
1. (Polish, Step 1) "Nudge" is taught but never graded, because the 20% tolerance accepts un-nudged answers. Fine for a beginner; the reason lines carry it.
2. (Polish, Item 4/8/11/17) "pay x your hours / all hours" is never stated as a rule before the exact question; the wrong-answer note tells it after. A beginner worked it out as "part out of the whole" from Step 1's "23 hours out of 45", so no stall, but it is one inference.

### L3
1. (Polish, Step 1) "Toward the side with the 1 divide, away multiply" is a memorised direction rule. The look-back sentence rescues it, and items always add a sensible-direction check, so I could reason it. A one-line "ask: should the answer be bigger or smaller?" in Step 1 would make it the main test.
2. (Polish, Item 7) Rough answer for km to miles ("a mile is about 1.6 km, so about 0.6 of the km") is a jump; 5 / 1.6 in the head is hard. A beginner using 5 / 2 = 2.5 just lands inside 20%. Fix: teach "about 1.6, so divide by 1.5 or 2".

### L4
1. (Design note, all scale / not-scale questions) The fixed part is always printed on the card with a "no charge / none / free" or a dollar amount, so a beginner can answer "does it scale" by spotting a dollar in that row, with or without the reasoning. Step 1 says fixed parts "hide", but only Check 13 (shipping) does. Fix: in practice and check, give two sample costs only and let the learner infer the fixed part in at least one question each.
2. (Polish, Check 13, 14, 15) Grammar from templates: "1 pounds", "a 8-mile ride", "however many data".

### L5
1. (Teaching gap, Items 9, 13, 16 and Step 1) 15%, 2.5%, 4.5% rough methods ("10% and half again", "4.5 of the 1%s") first appear in a worked-out line after a question, not in Step 1. A beginner could fall into a rough answer that misses the 20% window. Exact still catches it, so no stall. Fix: add 15% and half-percents to Step 1's easy-percent line.
2. (Polish) The "typed" feedback for a share question (370.5%) names pay / rent, fine, but there is no case for taking the percent of tax-included total on a share question.

### L6
1. (Polish, rough steps) The classic wrong method passes the rough step: adding 25% back gives $78.75 (within 20% of $84), and 10% up then 10% down leaves $145 (within 20% of $143.55). Only the exact step catches the error. The feedback covers it, so the beginner is told what decided. Consider a tighter tolerance (10%) on these rough questions.
2. (Polish, Item 2/6/14) "Receipt, the top torn off" with an expense-report framing is good; the title is slightly odd on screen.

### L7
1. (Feedback defect, Items 1, 6, 10; Check 13) The wrong-answer note for the can count says "Rounded down, the paint runs out before the second coat". For Item 6 and Check 13 the typed 2 is more likely "forgot to double for two coats, then rounded up" (375 / 350 = 1.07 rounds up to 2). The note blames the wrong step and tells the beginner nothing about the missed second coat. The check rule "None rounded down" then counts that miss as a rounding error. Fix: split the note: "2 is what one coat needs; you paint two coats, double first" when area / coverage is under 2 and the answer matches that.
2. (Polish, Items 1, 3, 7, 9; Check 14, 17) Several buy-quantity questions give the same answer under "round up" and "round to nearest" (1.97, 1.6, 0.56, 7.91, 9.73, 8.54). Check 13 (2.07) and Check 16 (1.2) do split the two, so the check holds. Practice would teach "round up" better with more fractions under .5.

### L8
1. (FAIL, Check 14, 15, 16) None of the three buy-quantity check questions can separate "round up" from "round to nearest": 5.56 (nearest 6), 6.54 (nearest 7), 0.85 yards (nearest half yard is 1). Check 13 is area only. So a beginner who rounds to the nearest passes the rule "None rounded down" and the check, without the reasoning the lesson is about (buy enough). Practice has some splitters (Item 8, 5.23; Item 10, 8.25; Item 11, 1.11 yards) but the check, the part that decides, has none. Fix: make at least two check quantities have a fraction under .5 (for example 5.2 bags, 0.6 yards, where nearest gives 5 and 0.5) so a nearest-rounder fails the rule.
2. (Polish, Item 7 and Check 16 "Shown after") The example reads "You said 0.4. The exact answer is 0.4 ... you were 10% over", which contradicts itself. The sample uses made-up numbers but reads wrong on screen. Show the learner's real answer or a clearly separate example.
3. (Polish, Items 3, 7, 11, 16) "1 cubic yards" appears in the typed-answer labels. Grammar from a template.
