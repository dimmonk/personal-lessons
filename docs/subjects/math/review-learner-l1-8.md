# Learner review, math l1 to l8 (round 1)

Reviewer role: cold beginner, learner views only. Method note: the learner views print the marked answers inline, so I could not truly cover them. I worked each rough answer and exact answer from the stated numbers first (and recomputed every printed total), then compared. Scores below are my own reasoning scores, not a run of the real check.

Verdicts: l1 PASS, l2 PASS, l3 FAIL, l4 PASS, l5 PASS, l6 PASS, l7 PASS, l8 PASS.

## Defects across several lessons

S1. Rough-answer tolerance changes. l1 and l2 say a rough answer is right "within about a fifth". From l3 on, every rough question accepts "from 0.5 to 2 times the exact answer". That accepts $300 for $546.51, or 12% for a 30% rent share. It teaches that sloppy is fine and contradicts the earlier lessons. Fix: keep one rule (within a fifth), or say in the text why it loosens.
S2. The "Shown after" line prints a dollar sign for things that are not money ("You said $15.40" for feet, miles, cups, percent). Seen in l3, l5, l6, l7, l8. Fix: use the answer's unit.
S3. Counts and units read oddly: "1 rolls", "1 cubic yards", "1 cans". Fix: singular/plural.

## l1 Ballpark and check: PASS

My way of deciding: say what is asked and what is given, round to friendly numbers for a rough answer, do it exactly, and check the two are close (within a fifth). For a bill, round the lines and compare with the subtotal; tax is added after, so a total a few percent above is fine. Check score by reasoning: 8 of 8.
1. "Subtotal" is used from Step 6 on and never explained (Step 6, items 10 to 13, 22 to 24). Fix: one line defining it as the lines added, before tax.
2. Step 6 says a gap of "$10, or a whole line" is a mistake but not how big a gap is too big. All practice bills are exact or off by a whole line or exactly $10, so I never had to judge a $2 gap. Fix: state the rule (more than about $2 after rounding) and include one near-miss bill.
3. "Round the bill to a number the group divides evenly" is vague when two numbers qualify (item 20: 119 or 126). Both pass inside a fifth, but a beginner stalls. Fix: add "pick the nearer one".

## l2 Friendly numbers: PASS

My way: change both numbers to easy ones, multiply or divide, then nudge up or down by which way you moved the numbers; for a part of something, use half, a third, a quarter and nudge. Check score: 5 of 5.
1. The "friendly" totals chosen are not the nearest ones and the reason still says "close to": $2,912.53 to $3,200 (2,800 is nearer; item 2), $5,096.62 to $5,600 (4,900 is nearer; item 5), $2,040.04 to $2,400 (1,800 is nearer; check 13). A learner who picks the nearer one gets a different, also valid, rough answer and wonders which is right. Fix: use the nearer multiple, or say any nearby one works.
2. "Make the sum simpler" (four 80s) and "a tenth" are taught in Step 1 and never asked. Fix: add one question each, or cut them.
3. Item 6 maps 10 of 12 hours to "three quarters". It is nearer five sixths, which is not in the list of easy fractions. Fix: choose numbers that sit near a listed fraction.

## l3 Rates and scaling: FAIL

My way: find the amount for one, then multiply. I could not state, before the first conversion question, how to tell whether to multiply or divide. Check score by reasoning: I would likely get the deals, recipe and map right and could miss the two conversions (check 16 and 18) with no rule to lean on; the only guard is a sense check I had to invent.
1. (Blocking) Direction of a conversion is never taught. Step 1 says only "check which way the rate goes". The worked example is a recipe. The first currency question (item 2) and every unit question (items 4, 9, 13, 18) need "1 dollar = 17.9 pesos, so pesos to dollars is divide". The rule appears only in wrong-answer feedback. Fix: add a worked currency or unit example to Step 2 with the rule (the rate gives how many of the other thing in one of this; to go to the unit that is "one", divide; and the bigger unit gives fewer) before item 2.
2. Rough rounding is unfriendly: $5 ÷ 4 shown as "about $1.30" (item 6) and $7 ÷ 4 as "about $1.80" (check 14). 1.25 and 1.75 are already easy. Fix: use the exact easy value.
3. Item 4 (first unit question) hides the "name the kind of problem and draw it" step under help (leave 2). Fix: show it for the first unit and currency questions.
4. Systemic S1, S2.

## l4 Proportional or not?: PASS

My way: look for a part paid once whatever the amount; if there is none, per one then times; if there is, scale only the growing part, then add the fixed part. Check score: 5 of 5.
1. The check is passable by recognition. Every item is one of four stories already seen in Steps 2 to 5 (cab, phone, pot roast, truck), and the fixed part is either printed "no charge" or a dollar figure, so the yes/no answer shows on the card. The rate is also on the card, so the known pair is never needed to find a hidden fixed part. Fix: add stories not seen (joining fee, delivery fee, call-out charge) and one item where only two totals are given and the learner must find the fixed part.
2. Pot-roast items say "cost" and "every dollar comes from the oven-time" for a time question (items 5, 15). Fix: use "time" and "every minute" wording.
3. Step 2's card does not show "4 miles cost $13.50" although the working lists it as given. Fix: show it on the card.
4. "less than 8 ÷ 4 times $13.50" (Step 2, item 2) took me three reads. Fix: "less than double $13.50".
5. "At least 4 right" is unclear: each item has two questions. Fix: say whether it is 4 of 5 items or 4 of 10 questions.

## l5 Percents forward: PASS

My way: find the whole (the amount the percent is of); easy percents (10%, double, half of 10%) for a rough answer; multiplier (0.18, 1.04, 0.70) for the exact; share = part ÷ whole; change = change ÷ start. Check score: 5 of 5.
1. Step 1 shows a multiplier only for whole-number percents (18%, 4%, 30%). Items 8 and 16 need 4.5% to 1.045 and 2.5% to 1.025, shown only in help and feedback. Fix: one line in Step 1.
2. Every story maps to one formula by its words (tip, rent share, lease rise, raise, off). Nothing makes me choose the whole from an unlabelled situation. Fix: add one item where the stem does not name the type.
3. Systemic S1, S2.

## l6 Percents backward and stacked: PASS

My way: the percent is of the earlier amount, so that amount is 100%; going backward, divide by the multiplier; stacked percents multiply in turn; points versus percent of the old rate. Check score: 5 of 5.
1. Item 9's reason says "a bit less than it started" for 50% up then 50% down ($110 to $82.50, a quarter lower). Fix: "less".
2. The convention that tax is charged on the sale price is stated only in help and feedback (items 3, 8, check 15). The tag alone could mean tax on the full price. Fix: say it in Step 1 or on the tag.
3. Step 2's receipt prints the line and "You paid" both as $63.00, which confused me about which one was the sale price. Fix: one line.
4. Systemic S1, S2.

## l7 Areas and how much to buy: PASS (with a major defect)

My way: draw it; area = length × width (cut-outs off, L-shapes as two rectangles); walls = distance around × height; edging uses the distance around; one unit (divide square feet by 9); divide by what one covers and round up. Check score: 5 of 5.
1. (Major) Printed working contradicts itself. Item 4: "23.83 square yards × $29 = $691.17" is $691.07 on the calculator; item 8: "16.61 × $31 = $514.94" is $514.91; check 15: "15.33 × $33 = $506.00" is $505.89. The right answers come from unrounded values, so a learner who follows the printed steps gets a different number. Fix: show the unrounded step (214.5 ÷ 9 × 29) or round the displayed product correctly.
2. (Major) The plan shown with the question gives away the key step. Wall questions print "46 by 9 (The four walls, laid flat)", and check 13 prints "48 by 8". The learner never has to find the distance around. Fix: draw the room (13.5 by 10.5) and let the learner build the strip.
3. Coats are not taught in Step 1; "two coats means double the area" first appears in the worked example. Fix: add a line to Step 1.
4. Flooring has no allowance for waste or cuts. A beginner will buy exactly 190 sq ft and run short; check with the research note.
5. Systemic S1, S2, S3.

## l8 Circles, boxes and volume: PASS (with a major defect)

My way: radius is half the way across; circle area = π × r × r (3 for a rough answer); volume = bottom area × depth in feet (inches ÷ 12); cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27; divide by one bag and round up. Check score: 5 of 5.
1. (Major) Same printed-arithmetic problem as l7. Item 4: "50.24 × 0.67 ft = 33.49" is 33.66; item 8: "12.56 × 0.83 = 10.47" is 10.42; item 15 / check 15: "19.63 × 0.67 = 13.08" is 13.15. Fix: show the depth as 8 ÷ 12 unrounded.
2. The mulch order is accepted "within 0.1" (items 3, 7, 11 and check 16). A learner who types 0.46 or 0.43, the unrounded volume, passes without rounding up to the half yard, which is the point. Fix: accept only 0.5, 1, 1.5 (exact match).
3. Circumference appears only in wrong-answer feedback ("the distance around the rug") and is never taught. Fix: one sentence, or drop that wrong-answer line.
4. Systemic S1, S2, S3.
