## L16 Circles and right triangles: PASS

In my own words: around = 3.14 × across (across = around ÷ 3.14). A square corner: side² + side² = slant², and 3-4-5 is the tape check.
A triangle's angles make 180, and so do the two angles on a straight line. For angles, pick sin, cos or tan by which side you have and which
you want: along × tan gives up, slant × sin gives up, slant × cos gives along.

Practice 14/14 worked before looking. Check 5/5 (113.04, 17.09, 106, 5.09, 16.96).

Deciding defects: none.

Polish:
1. Step 3, the 3-4-5 traps: "Marks 3 and 4 times 5 from the corner are 5 times 5 apart" took a second read. Fix: "15 and 20 are 3 and 4
   times 5, so the tape should read 5 × 5 = 25."
2. Item 7: a stair "climbing at 12°" and a wheelbarrow ramp at up to 40° are not real-looking. A stair is about 30 to 37°, and a
   wheelbarrow ramp is under about 15°. Fix: give each thing its own angle range.
3. Check 17: on a shelf bracket where "one side meets the wall at 48°, the other side meets the wall at 26°", I could not picture
   which corner is which, and there is no drawing. Fix: add the triangle figure, or use the truss or gable wording.
4. Items 6 and 11 drew the same 52° twice in one run. Fix: the fresh-numbers rule should already cover this, so check that it applies
   inside one lesson's mixed set.

## L17 Scale, slope and round volumes: PASS

In my own words: same shape means multiply by one ratio and never add (post ÷ its shadow = tree ÷ its shadow). On a plan, divide the plan
inches by the scale. Grade = rise ÷ run × 100. A drain's drop = feet × the fall per foot. A cylinder is 3.14 × r × r × height, and a cone
is a third of that, with every length in feet first. To go to a smaller unit, multiply (2.54 cm an inch, 7.48 gallons a cubic foot).

Practice 14/14. Check 5/5.

Deciding defects: none.

Polish:
1. Drain items: "Length of the level run" describes a pipe that must not be level. Fix: "Length of the pipe run".
2. Step 9 says about 30% of the questions come from earlier lessons, but the view lists only this lesson's items. Fix: no change for the
   learner; this is only how the view prints it.

## L18 Powers, roots and log scales: PASS

In my own words: powers first, then times and divide, then plus and minus, and on this calculator put the times part in first. × 10ⁿ moves
the point n places. To divide, divide the fronts and subtract the powers. √ gives a square's side, and a square's diagonal is side × √2
(use the button, not 1.4). On a log scale, a difference of d is 10 to the d (for decibels, d ÷ 10).

Practice 14/14. Check 5/5.

Deciding defects: none for the learner. The phone-calculator claim on Step 1 is a fact error, covered in the research review.

Polish:
1. Item 3 / Check 16: "capacitor 7.5 × 10² farads" and a "coil of wire" of up to millions of meters made me doubt the numbers. Real
   capacitors are microfarads (10⁻⁶). Fix: give each part a realistic range.
2. "xʸ" is used on Steps 3 and 7 and never said in words. Fix: add "(xʸ, to the power of)" the first time it appears.
3. Items 4 and 10: the trap "62,500 / 400,000: That divides the photo by the drive" is not what happened. That answer flipped the front
   numbers only. Fix: "That divides the front numbers the wrong way round: drive's front ÷ photo's front."

## L19 Primes and counting formulas: FAIL

In my own words: a prime check means trying the primes up to √n. An inch fraction is halved until the top is odd. First ask whether the
order matters. If it does, multiply n × (n − 1) × … for each place. If it does not, divide that by the orders of the ones chosen.

Practice 11/11. Check 5/5. But I answered every "Does the order matter?" from the table's title, not by reasoning (see 1).

Deciding defects:
1. **Items 3, 9; Check 14 ("The order to work in", "… | any order works"):** the row says "any order works", and the right answer is
   "The order matters". On a first read I took "any order works" to mean order does not matter. Two answers are defensible.
   Fix: drop "any order works" and ask "How many different orders could you do them in?"
2. **Every "Does the order matter?" (items 3 to 9; Checks 14 to 16):** the title names the answer. "The order to work in" and "Filling:
   first, second and third" always mean it matters, and "Picking k" / "a crew of" always mean it does not. The lesson's one decision can be
   passed by pattern. Fix: use neutral titles, and build pairs that look alike but differ only in whether order counts, for example
   "3 of 10 bidders, one for each of 3 different jobs" against "3 of 10 bidders to share one job", or "a 4-digit code" against "4 colors
   for a sample pack".

Polish:
3. "Count of part numbers in a batch" (the prime card) reads oddly. Fix: "Parts in a batch".
4. Check 15 (a lock code): the answer is always 5,040, which is also 7!. See the research review.
