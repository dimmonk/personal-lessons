## Before
After lessons 1 to 3 I can ask the five questions and name the socialist family. I could not tell fascism from an ordinary dictatorship, Nazism, reaction or conservatism. I would have said "fascist" for any harsh regime.

## The rule
Given before the first case (steps 1 to 3), in my words:
- Fascism is a cluster, never one mark: a nation reborn, a leader who is the will, mass mobilization, class struggle rejected, state-directed owners.
- Torture, censorship, banned parties and a bare leader cult are methods any dictator uses, so they decide nothing.
- Mass mobilization, a leader who claims to be the nation's will, state bodies in place of free unions, and violence praised as purifying do separate fascism from a dictator who wants quiet.
- Nazism is fascism with race as a natural rank as the unit.
- Reaction wants an old order back. Conservatism keeps what stands and changes it slowly. Fascism breaks the old order to make something new.
- A party's name ("Socialist") never counts; Questions 1 and 2 decide.

## Stuck

- Step 6, Question 6: "an ordinary dictatorship leaves owners and unions alone" is a little broad, because Leninist states also ban free unions. The item still follows the taught mark (state bodies plus owners keeping their firms), so it is not misleading.
- Question 1 of the practice is a new format (separates, or any dictatorship can do it). Step 2 prepares it, so I was not lost.
- Every screen was used. Nothing could be answered without learning, because the deciding words (youth movement, "purify", "I am what the nation wills", "state bodies") are exactly the taught marks.
- No contradiction with lesson 2's methods screen. Lesson 4 repeats it: "mobilization never separates fascism from Leninism", and Question 15 and Check 20 test it.
- Method before case: steps 1 to 4 come before the worked example and all items.
