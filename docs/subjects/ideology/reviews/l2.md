# Stand-in learner review: Ideology, lesson 2 "Socialist, or not?" (rev 2, draft)

Reviewer: a fresh stand-in, 2026-10-12. Told only the packet (end result, real moment, real-world test, the part 2 row). Read the learner
view, `l2.lesson.js`, `items-build.js`, `items-practice.js`, `items-check.js`, `items-reserve.js` and the rules in `subject.js`; read
`public/app/lessons/tap.js` and `rules.js` to see how a tap is scored. Did every item as a beginner adult on a phone with ten minutes.

## Before

If a post called a free-bus plan "socialist", I would go by the mood: free stuff paid by the government sounds socialist, so I would half
agree. I could not say what "social democrat" means, nor how socialist differs from communist, and "capitalist" to me meant "pro-business"
or "greedy". I could not have picked the sentence that settles it.

## Notes, in order

  three-step check is short and usable. The line "they do not have to say that business stays private" is a little abstract on first read;
  it only made sense after q-meals.
- **Step 2, the four labels.** Clear and used in every item after. Two needs per label is the right size for a phone.
- **Step 3, the pair.** Taught me the core line (tax pays vs. county owns) in one look. Good.
- **Worked example (w-power).** Clear; the steps match the three-step check.
- **Group 1.** p-fares, p-bus, p-jobs, p-fortunes all doable with the panel beside the words. Fine.
- **Group 2, places where a correct beginner tap is called wrong.** Tap scoring (`tap.js`) accepts several lines only for an *earned*
  label whose `has` puts its needs in two lines; every other item accepts exactly one line. That rejects sentences that meet a need or that
  rule the label out as well as the chosen line does:
  - **q-farms** ("communist", earned): "Everything would be owned in common and run by the state." is almost word for word need 1 ("owned in

    deciding words.
  - **q-rail** ("capitalist", not earned): "A public board would run it, and the board would decide the fares." breaks need 2 (the

    it is scored wrong ("That says how it would be run"). Two items earlier, q-steel taught me that the price-fixing line *is* the deciding

  - **q-sell** ("free-market", earned): "The state would only enforce safety rules and contracts." meets need 2's second half (the

## Balance between the two sides

The counts are equal (10 left, 10 right across the groups and check; 3 and 3 in the check, each side with one earned, one not earned and
one too-little), and every label is judged by the same needs. What each side is shown doing is not equal:

- Labels thrown **from the right** at opponents ("socialist", "communist"): 9 items counting the worked example, and 6 of them are not
  earned (w-power, p-fares, p-fortunes, q-banks, q-meals, k-leave), 2 earned (q-farms, k-water), 1 too little.
- Labels thrown **from the left** at opponents ("capitalist", "free-market"): 6 items, and 1 is not earned (q-rail), 2 earned (q-sell,
  k-rents), 3 too little to tell.
- Self-labels: right-leaning groups' "free-market" is never earned (q-steel not earned, k-unleash too little); left-leaning groups' own
  labels are earned twice (p-bus, q-coops) and not earned twice.
- Every teaching example (Step 1's first wrong idea, the Step 3 pair, the worked example) is a right-leaning commenter wrongly calling
  something socialist. No teaching example shows a left-leaning commenter misusing "capitalist" on a concrete plan.

A beginner leaves with "right-wing critics misuse 'socialist'; left-wing critics are vague or fair", and a "right critic, so not earned"
guess pays 6 times in 9. That is the opposite of "each label is judged on its own needs, whichever side throws it".

## After

Concretely, I can now:
- Tell a socialist plan from a social democrat one by asking who would own the industry, not who pays (Step 3 pair, w-power, p-fares,
  q-meals, k-leave).
- Turn down "communist" on a plan that takes a few industries but leaves the rest private, and name it socialist instead (q-banks).
- Turn down "free-market" on a plan that keeps firms private but fixes the price (q-steel), and accept it where firms are sold and prices
  are left to buyers (q-sell, k-rents).
- Say "too little to tell" on slogans instead of agreeing or arguing (p-jobs, q-economy, q-ordinary).
- Point to the sentence that settles ownership in most items.

## The five answers

- **Gain: yes.** Telling public or worker ownership of a main industry ("socialist") from taxes paying for services with business left
  private ("social democrat"), and few industries from nearly all ("communist"), on a labelled post. I had none of this before.
- **Doing: yes.** Two short teaching screens and a pair, then one worked example and 20 labelled posts; nearly all the time is spent judging
  posts in the real-moment form.
- **Check: no, as scored.** It tests the right ability in the real form on new plans, balanced by side, but the tap scoring rejects correct
  deciding sentences on k-rents (r1) and k-wage (g3), so a learner with the skill can fail. Its two thin items are also guessable by shape.
- **Clear: no.** A beginner cannot resolve q-farms (c2 rejected though it states need 1), q-rail (n2 rejected though the item's reason uses
  it, and q-steel taught the opposite), or the many items where a line scored wrong is then lit up as a deciding word.
- **Waste:** little. Step 1's sentence "they do not have to say that business stays private" can go once the tax-line rule below is
  settled; q-meals and k-leave say the same thing twice.

## Fixes needed (blocking)

1. **Accept every line that decides it.** For any verdict, accept each line that by itself meets a need (earned) or by itself rules the
   label out (not earned): at least q-farms c2, q-rail n2, k-rents r1, q-sell m3. Give `has` (or a list of accepted lines) the lines that do
   this, and let `meets`-style scoring apply to not-earned items too, not only earned ones with needs in two lines.
2. **Make `deciding` and the accepted taps one list.** A line highlighted as deciding must never be the line just called wrong. Either
   accept every `deciding` line or take the non-accepted ones out of `deciding` (and say what they add in the reason instead).
3. **Settle the tax line.** Either accept the "a tax pays for it" line whenever a post only has taxes paying for a benefit (k-wage g3, and
   g2's note must stop calling the social democrat sign wrong), or teach the rule "a line saying nothing changes hands beats the tax line"
   in Step 1 and the worked example. Accepting both is simpler and true to the needs.
4. **Balance what each side gets wrong.** Give left-leaning commenters concrete plans their "capitalist" or "free-market" does not fit
   (e.g. a subsidy-plus-price-cap plan, or a tax-funded service, called "capitalist"), give right-leaning commenters more earned
   "socialist" or "communist" labels, and make one teaching example (the pair or the worked example) a left-thrown misfire. Aim for the same
   earned / not earned / too-little split on each side's insults in the groups, as the check already has.

## Should fix (not blocking)

- Vary the shape of "too little to tell" posts: give some three or four lines with concrete but irrelevant detail (dates, a dollar total
  with no owner or price), and give some earned or not-earned posts only two lines, so the shape stops giving the answer.
- Make k-leave less of a copy of q-meals (a different funding form, or a benefit next to a line about regulation).
