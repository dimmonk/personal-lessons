## Before

As a beginner: "socialist" to me means the government pays for a lot of things, roughly "big welfare". "Communist" is
"socialist, but worse". "Capitalist" means pro-business or greedy. Shown "This is socialism" over a free-buses plan, I
would shrug or agree; I could not say what would have to be in the words for the label to fit, and I had never heard of
"too little to tell" as an answer. I could not name "social democrat" as the thing most of these plans are.

## Notes, item by item

- **The why, Step 1.** Clear and short. The wrong idea ("socialist means the government pays for things") lands: it is
  exactly what I believed. The three-step check (find what it would do, read the needs, decide) is easy to hold.
- **Step 2, the four panels.** Readable on a phone. The communist / socialist line ("a few main industries" vs "nearly
  everything") is the most useful sentence in the lesson. No matching wrong-idea card for the other side: nothing says
  "capitalist does not mean pro-business or greedy", though q-steel later tests exactly that.
- **Step 3, the pair.** Good: same comment, two plans, only one earns it. Taught me the line in one look.
- **Step 4, worked example (w-power).** Clear. The working repeats the reason almost word for word; it could be shorter.
- **Q1 p-fares, Q2 p-bus, Q4 p-fortunes.** Did them right. Each unearned one carries an explicit line "would stay
  privately owned / private contractors", so I learned to hunt for that line. That is the trick that got me through.
- **Q3 p-jobs (too little).** Right ("Nothing here decides it"). Confusing after the answer: the learner view says "the
  deciding words are marked", and the item marks j1 and j2, the very sentences I was just told decide nothing. Tapping
  either gives only "That is not the sentence.", with no reason.
- **Q5 q-sell (free-market, earned).** I tapped "The new owners could charge whatever fares riders will pay", because the
  panel's second need is prices. Marked wrong ("That is about fares, once the company is sold"), and then the reason
  itself quotes that sentence as meeting the price need. I could not tell why my line was wrong.
- **Q6 q-farms (communist, earned).** Same problem. "Everything would be owned in common and run by the state" is the
  panel's need 1 almost word for word, and is marked wrong; "One national plan would decide what is made…" is need 2 and
  gets "That is not the sentence." Only "would end private ownership of farms, factories and shops" counts.
- **Q7 q-coops, Q8 q-utilities, Q12 q-banks.** Right; Q8 (a group calling itself social democrat while nationalising
  utilities) and Q12 (banks and rail are socialist, not communist) taught me something I would use.
- **Q9 q-economy, Q11 q-ordinary (too little).** Right, and answerable without the economy labels at all: any post with
  no concrete plan is "too little". That is part 1's skill, fine to keep, but these teach nothing new here.
- **Q10 q-steel.** Hard and good: subsidies plus a fixed price is not free-market, and fits none of the four. I picked
  "Social democrat" first ("businesses stay private, regulated"); the feedback set me straight.
- **Small print ("Said by…").** Never the answer after the first time; free points, no learning. Harmless.

## The check, done without the lesson open

- k-water (socialist, earned): Earned. Tapped "The Clean Water Act would take over the state's three private water
  companies" (the takeover is the act). **Scored wrong**: the only accepted line is "They would be owned and run by the
  state as a public utility". My take-over line gets "That is not the sentence."
- k-rents (free-market, earned): Earned. After Q5 taught me the ownership line is the one, I tapped "The city would sell
  its 40 apartment buildings to private owners". **Scored wrong**: this time only the price line counts.
- k-drugs: Not earned, social democrat, "Drug companies and pharmacies would stay privately owned". Right.
- k-wage: Not earned, social democrat, "No company would change hands". Right.
- k-unleash, k-city: Too little, nothing decides. Right.

Result: 4 of 6, **not passed**, with every verdict and every name right. An item counts only when all three asks are
right (`rules.js`: `ok` is every ask ok), and the tap accepts one line (`items-build.js`: `right: [spec.tap]`) even when
the item's own `deciding` list and `has` map put the two needs in two different lines (q-sell, q-farms, k-water,
k-rents). Which of two needed lines is "the" one flips between items (ownership line in q-sell, price line in k-rents;
the owning line in k-water but the buying line in p-bus), so no rule a learner could learn predicts it.

## After

Things I can do now that I could not before:
1. Shown "socialist" or "communist" pinned on a plan that pays for a service or benefit with taxes, I say "not earned"
   and name it social democrat (w-power, p-fares, p-fortunes, k-drugs, k-wage).
2. Tell socialist from communist by how much is taken over: a few main industries vs nearly everything (q-banks, the
   Step 2 line).
3. Spot a label pinned on a slogan and say "too little to tell" instead of agreeing or arguing (p-jobs, q-ordinary).
4. Say a plan that subsidises private firms and fixes prices is not free-market, whoever calls it that (q-steel).

What I still could not do on a real post: judge a plan that pays for a benefit with taxes and **says nothing about
ownership**, which is most real "This is socialism" posts (a health plan rarely adds "hospitals stay private"). Every
unearned item in the lesson hands me an explicit "stays private / no company changes hands" line, and every "too
little" item is an empty slogan. The boundary the real moment sits on is never practised.

## Balance

The counts are even (side facet, 2 of 3 per side in the check), but the content is not:
- Accusations thrown from the right ("socialist", "communist" at someone else's plan): 8 items, 5 not earned on a
  concrete plan, 2 earned, 1 too little.
- Accusations thrown from the left ("capitalist", "free-market" at someone else's plan): 5 items, never not earned on a
  concrete plan: 2 earned, 3 too little.
- The left's misfires are only self-labels (q-utilities, k-wage); the right's are mostly accusations.
- The wrong-idea card refutes only the right-side misreading of a label; there is no card for "capitalist means
  pro-business or greedy".

A reader from the right would see the lesson as "your side's labels are wrong, theirs are right or merely vague". The
needs themselves are neutral and judged the same way on both sides; the selection of items is what tilts.

## The five answers

- **Gain: yes.** A beginner can now tell "socialist" pinned on a tax-paid benefit (not earned, social democrat) from
  public ownership of a main industry (earned), and socialist from communist by how much is taken over.
- **Doing: yes.** About three short cards and a pair, then 1 worked and 18 judged items; most of the time is judging in
  the real form.
- **Check: no, as scored.** The form is right (label on words, verdict, name, deciding words, new items, both sides), but
  the single-line tap fails correct reasoning on k-water and k-rents, so a learner who has the ability can fail it. Its
  unearned items are also easier than the real moment (each states ownership outright).
- **Clear: no.** Blocking: the tap on earned items whose two needs sit in two lines (q-sell, q-farms, k-water, k-rents);
  the learner cannot know which line is wanted, and the feedback contradicts itself (it quotes the "wrong" line as meeting
  a need). Not blocking but confusing: on "too little" items the "deciding words" marked after the answer are the lines
  the learner was just told decide nothing; the bare "That is not the sentence." on some lines gives no reason.
- **Waste:** the worked example's four-step working repeats its reason; the speaker small-print distractor teaches nothing
  after the first item; two of the practice "too little" items (q-economy, q-ordinary) re-practise part 1 only and could
  give way to the missing ownership-silent items below.

## Fixes needed

1. **Tap scoring on two-need items.** Either accept any line that meets a need of the label (the item already lists them
   in `has` / `deciding`), or rewrite those items so one line carries the whole decision. Remove the contradiction where
   the reason quotes a line the tap marked wrong. Apply to q-sell, q-farms, k-water, k-rents, and check p-bus the same way.
2. **Give every non-right line a reason.** Replace "That is not the sentence." with why (for example, on k-water a1:
   whatever the fix in 1 decides).
3. **On "too little" items, don't call the marked lines "deciding words"** after the learner was told nothing decides it;
   mark them as "says nothing about owners or prices", or mark nothing.
4. **Practise the real boundary:** add items (practice and check) where a tax-paid benefit is described with no line
   about ownership, and make the lesson say plainly which verdict that earns and why.
5. **Balance the content, not just the count:** add left-thrown "capitalist" / "free-market" accusations that are not
   earned on a concrete plan (for example on a plan that taxes firms to fund services, or subsidises and fixes prices),
   and add a one-line wrong-idea card for "capitalist means pro-business or greedy" beside the socialist one.
