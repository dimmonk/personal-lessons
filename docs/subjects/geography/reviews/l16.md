## Before

As the beginner in the brief: I could put France, Spain, Italy and Germany roughly in the right spot, Portugal probably. I could not tell Belgium from the Netherlands, could not place Luxembourg, Austria or Switzerland with confidence, and had never placed any of the five microstates. Honestly, as a model I already know all of it, so every answer below was in reach without the lesson. Where that matters I say so.

## Notes (in lesson order)

1. **Why this** (step 1). It's clear and has a real moment (a border strike in the news). It promises "know their flags" for fifteen countries. The lesson never teaches or asks the five microstates' flags, and teaches only one flag in all.
2. **Worked example, Belgium, tap** (step 2). This is useful: find France, then go north. The map is 318 px wide and readable. The five white circles of the microstates are on every map from here on, with nothing saying what they are until step 7.
3. **Worked example, Belgium's flag** (step 3). This is the only flag the lesson teaches ("three upright stripes, black, yellow and red"). It's fine as far as it goes.
4. **Worked example, Belgium's neighbors** (step 4). Confusing on a phone. The North Sea is lit in the same green as France, Germany and the Netherlands, so it reads as one huge green country from Scotland to Norway, and Corsica is lit too. Belgium itself is the dark shape in the middle. A beginner can't tell which green is water and which is land.
5. **Worked example, Switzerland, landlocked** (step 5). This is clear. Switzerland is again the dark shape among green neighbors, and it's small at this size.
6. **Worked example, lit France** (step 6). The screen has no question text, only a map and "Show the working". It taught nothing I used: France is the one country a beginner already knows.
7. **The named map** (step 7). **Too small to read on a phone.** At the default zoom, the country names render about 4 px tall and Luxembourg and the five microstates about 2 px. That's unreadable. After two taps on + the big names are legible, but Luxembourg and the microstate labels are still about 4 to 5 px, inside the white circles. Reading all five takes several more zooms and pans, and the screen allows "about a minute". This is the only screen that ever pairs a microstate with its place, and the names "do not come back".
8. **15 tap questions** (step 8). The map, the taps and the feedback work well. A right tap lights the place. A miss gives "The answer: X", lights X, and the question comes back about three later. I missed Austria and San Marino on purpose, and both came back. Two confusions:
   - When I tapped Monaco's circle for San Marino, the feedback said "You tapped: France", not "Monaco". A beginner who mixed up two circles isn't told which circle they picked.
   - For the tap questions on microstates, the answer is always one of five visible circles. That narrows a tiny-country question to a 1-in-5 choice. It's still a placing skill, but a cued one.

   My own knowledge did the work here. A real beginner would lean on the step 7 map, which they could barely read.
9. **10 "name the lit place" questions** (step 9). These are clear. The typed box and "My answer means the same" work. I typed "Holland" for Luxembourg on purpose: it was marked a miss, Luxembourg was shown, and it came back. Lit Luxembourg is a speck at the default zoom, so I'd have to zoom to see what is lit.
10. **10 flag questions** (step 10). **Not taught, and too small.** The app asked the ten larger countries' flags (Spain, Portugal, Italy, Luxembourg, Belgium, Austria, Switzerland, France, Germany, the Netherlands). Belgium's is the only one the lesson ever showed. Each flag is an emoji about 16 px wide.
    - At that size the Netherlands and Luxembourg flags look alike: the same red-white-blue stripes, only the blue differs.
    - Belgium against Germany is easy to mix up too, and nothing was said about which is upright and which is lying.
    - A beginner can only guess, then learn from "Not this one, the answer: X" with no reason given beyond the country's description.

    I got 10 of 10, all from my own knowledge.
11. **5 neighbor-and-water questions** (step 11). The app asked Spain, Italy, the Netherlands, Austria and Belgium. Tapping any one neighbor and one water is enough. The feedback lists every neighbor and water, including seas never named on the map (Balearic Sea, Gulf of Lion, Ionian Sea) and countries off the lesson's list (Czechia, Slovenia). That is fine as information.
12. **Learner view against the app.** The learner view's step 9 lists the flags of the five microstates plus Portugal, Spain, France, Belgium and the Netherlands. The app asked the ten larger countries instead and no microstate flags. Its step 10 lists Luxembourg, Germany, Austria, Switzerland and Italy, but the app asked Spain, Italy, the Netherlands, Austria and Belgium. The learner view doesn't match the lesson as served, or doesn't say the sets are drawn at random.
13. **Resuming.** Mid-lesson, reopening resumed at the right step, but a step restarts from question 1. After I reached step 12 (the check's start screen) and closed, reopening started again at step 1. That contradicts "your place is kept".

## After

What a beginner can now do that they could not before, tied to the step that taught it:
- Place Belgium from France, and say it is on the North Sea between France and the Netherlands (steps 2 and 4).
- Read Belgium's flag (step 3).
- Say Switzerland is landlocked among France, Germany, Austria and Italy (step 5).
- Place and name the ten larger countries on a blank map. This comes from the retrieval loop in steps 8 and 9, where a missed place comes back until it is right, not from the one-minute named map.
- Place the five microstates. This comes from step 8's feedback, which lights each circle when it is answered; step 7's labels were too small to read.
- Tell the ten larger flags apart, but only by trial and error in step 10. Nothing explained them.
- Name one neighbor and the water for each larger country (step 11, building on the place work).

## The five answers

- **Gain** (a model's judgement): moderate. The tap and name loops would really fix the ten larger countries and roughly the microstates for a beginner. The flags would be half-learned by guessing, and the neighbor skill is only "one neighbor plus the nearest water".
- **Doing**: yes. Every practice step is the real task: a blank map, a named place, a tap, with nothing marked before the answer.
- **Check**: yes. It asks the same abilities in mixed forms, unhelped, scored at the end. It can't be passed by position, except that a microstate tap question is a 1-in-5 choice among visible circles. A beginner could not pass it without the lesson. (A model can.)
- **Clear**: no. These places stop a beginner:
  - (a) The flag step asks nine flags that were never taught, as 16 px emoji where the Netherlands and Luxembourg, and Belgium and Germany, can't be told apart. **Blocking.**
  - (b) On the named map, the labels are about 4 px and the microstate labels about 2 px at the default zoom. They are still unreadable after two zooms, and this is the only screen that names the microstates. **Blocking for the microstates.**
  - (c) In step 4 the North Sea is lit in the same green as the countries, so it reads as land.
  - (d) A wrong microstate circle is reported as "You tapped: France".
  - (e) The step 6 worked example has no question text.
- **Waste**: Step 6's France example taught nothing a beginner lacks. The "Why this" promise of fifteen countries' flags is never met, since the microstates' flags are neither taught nor asked.

**Verdict: fails.** Doing and Check are yes and the check was passed, but (a) and (b) under Clear block a beginner.

## Fixes

1. **Teach the flags before asking them.** Add a screen, or a worked example per confusable pair, that shows each of the ten flags large (at least 64 px wide), with its name and the one feature that tells it apart:
   - Belgium's stripes stand upright, Germany's lie flat (black, red, gold).
   - The Netherlands has the darker blue, Luxembourg the lighter.
   - Austria is red-white-red.
   - Switzerland is a white cross on red.
   - Italy and France are green and blue against red.

   Show the flag in the questions at the same large size.
2. **Make the named map readable on a phone.** Labels should be at least 11 to 12 px on screen at the default zoom, scaling with zoom. Give the five microstates a readable callout, or a zoomed inset with their names and the country around each. Alternatively, show the named map in two or three zoomed views (Iberia; the Low Countries and the Alps; Italy) rather than one whole-region view.
3. **In step 4, draw water in a different lit treatment from countries** (a blue tint or hatch), and don't light islands outside the answer's countries.
4. **On a miss on a microstate circle, name the circle that was tapped** ("You tapped: Monaco"), not the country under it.
5. **Give the step 6 worked example its question line** ("What is the lit place called?"), or cut it.
6. **Make the learner view match the lesson as served:** the flag step's ten countries and the neighbor step's five. If those sets are drawn at random, say so. Also drop "know their flags" for the microstates from "Why this", or teach them.
7. **Keep the learner's place after step 11.** Reopening at the check should resume at the check, not step 1.
