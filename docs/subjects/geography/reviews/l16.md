## Before

The part's task: someone names a western European country, and I put it on a map with no names, name it when it is lit, tell it by its flag, and say one neighbor and its water.

As the beginner I am playing: I could tap France, Spain, Italy and Germany, and probably Portugal. I could name those same countries if they were lit. Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg were one blur to me. I could not have said whether Austria or Switzerland lay further east. I had not heard of San Marino or Liechtenstein as countries and could not place Andorra or Monaco. Of the flags I knew France, Italy, Germany and Switzerland. I could not have told Belgium's from Germany's, or the Netherlands' from Luxembourg's. I had never thought about which countries have no coast.

Plainly: as a model I already know all of this, and I cannot forget it. Every answer below was right partly because of that. Where it matters, I say what the lesson had shown by that point.

## After

What the beginner I am playing can now do, and the step that taught it:

- Tap Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg in order, north to south, and tell them apart: steps 1 and 6, drilled in step 7.
- Put Austria east of Switzerland and north of Italy, with Liechtenstein between them: step 6 (third map), step 7.
- Find all five tiny countries and say why each is where it is (the Pyrenees, the Riviera, the Alps, inside Italy near the Adriatic, inside Rome): step 6's leader lines and step 7's reasons.
- Tell Belgium's upright stripes from Germany's flat ones, and the Netherlands' deep blue from Luxembourg's light blue: steps 2 and 6, and the step 9 feedback.
- Recognize Austria's and Portugal's flags: step 6, step 9.
- Say which of these countries have no coast (Switzerland, Austria, Luxembourg, the tiny inland ones), and give Belgium and the Netherlands the North Sea and Portugal the Atlantic: steps 3 and 4, step 10.
- For the strike story: picture Belgium on the North Sea, with France south and the Netherlands north. That is the real moment the lesson opened with.

## Fixes

None needed to pass. These are worth making:

1. Keep the Landlocked button on screen on neighbor-and-water questions: above the map, or the map shortened so the button clears the Next bar.
2. Give Luxembourg (and the other small countries that are not rings) a forgiving tap area at the whole-map view, or open these questions zoomed on that area. Raise the contrast of the borders between countries.
3. Keep the map from jumping when the help line changes after the first tap: hold the line's height.
4. On lit-place questions at 390 px, bring the answer box above the fold, for example with a shorter map.
5. Move the Belgium neighbors worked example (step 3) after the map with names, or put names on it. Replace the France worked example (step 5) with one a beginner does not already know, such as the Netherlands or Austria lit.
6. In neighbor-and-water feedback, name the seas on the map, or light only the water that was tapped and the water that was expected.
