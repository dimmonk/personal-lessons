### Gate 1. Kind of learning and evidence
Written 2026-10-12, blind (nothing of this subject existed to read).

Kinds, from what the learner must do in real life:
- **Knowing facts**: a place is named and the learner knows where it is. "Where" means four things the moment needs: the
  unit it sits in (the state, the country, the region), what it borders, the coast or water it is on, and roughly how far
  it is from here. Nothing in real life asks for an outline, a flag, a population or a capital for its own sake; a
  capital matters only because the news names it to mean the state ("Sacramento passed", "Albany is weighing").
- **Judging situations**: a place the learner has never heard of, given as a story gives it ("a village in Oaxaca",
  "Mombasa, Kenya's port"), placed from its clues by the units it sits in; and two places named, how far apart they are
  (a few hours' drive, a day's drive, a flight, the other side of the world).

Evidence: `docs/research/knowing-where-places-are.md` (G1–G7, written for this gate: the survey of what adults cannot
place, the testing effect on maps and its transfer to the reversed question, the hierarchy in memory for places and its
errors, the straightening of remembered maps, map reading as a separate skill, the absence of evidence on distances);
`docs/research/review-after-lessons.md` (R1–R9); `docs/learning-science.md` P16, P19, P22, P24, P25, P30, used for its
sources only. Its framing of every subject as "diagnostic classification" with a key is **not** adopted: placing a place
is recall of a position, and the judging parts are two plain questions, not a key with named outcomes.

The principles that drive the design:
1. **The practice is the moment's form: a name, and the place tapped on a map with no names on it** (G1; R8: transfer is
   largest when the practice response matches the real one). The map is never studied twice: one minute with labels
   when a group is new, then recall only (G2, R1).
2. **Each place is practised both ways**, name to map and map to name, because testing transfers to the reversed
   question and the real moment comes in both (a name heard; a region on the phone's map) (G3).
3. **Feedback at once, with the unit, the neighbors and the water**: the right place lights up, and one line says where
   it sits ("Nevada: between California and Utah, no coast; Reno on its western edge"). That line is the memory the
   hierarchy stores (G4, R7, P19).
4. **The units first, then the places in them** (G4): the four US regions and the coasts before any state; a continent's
   parts before its countries; feedback always names the unit. A missed place returns at least three items later in the
   lesson until right (R2).
5. **Successive relearning, scheduled by the app**: a place returns on later days until right in three separate
   sessions, asked in the form it was not last asked (R2, R3, R4, P24).
6. **One map, one orientation, and the distortions taught on purpose** (G5): every map is north-up and the same
   projection; the places people line up wrongly (Europe and the United States, South America and North America, Reno
   and San Diego, the size of Africa) are contrast items with the true map after the answer (P12).
7. **Groups of about 15 to 20 places a lesson, mixed with earlier groups** as soon as there are two (R9, P22): a new
   group alone first, then with the due places of earlier lessons.
8. **Judging is practised on places never seen, a worked one first**, the clue that decides the region shown after
   (P10, P25); distances are answered as bands, never miles to the mile, and the true figure is shown with both places
   lit (G7).
9. **Reviews ask due places in the real form** (R8, R9): names to tap, regions to name, never a map to look at.

### Gate 2. End result
- **After this subject you can**, when a place is named, in the news, by a person or on a trip, know where it is: the
  region it sits in, what it borders, the coast or water it is on, and roughly how far it is from you.
- **Real moment**: a place named in a news story, in a conversation or in a travel plan, with no map in sight; and the
  phone's map, which shows where you are but not what a named place is near or what it belongs to.
- **Real-world test** (every part must pass):
  - *Placing*: 40 names drawn at random from every list of the subject (the 50 states and DC, the cities, the countries,
    the seas, rivers, mountain ranges and deserts), each tapped on a map with no names on it, pinch-zoom allowed. At
    least **34 of 40** in the right place (the right state or country; for a city or a feature, within its state or
    country and on the right side of it).
  - *Naming*: 10 places lit on the blank map, named from memory by typing. At least **8 of 10**.
  - Both parts on **two sittings at least a week apart**.
  - *Judging, places*: 10 places never practised, given as a story gives them ("a town in Oaxaca", "the port of
    Mombasa, Kenya", "Tyumen, in western Siberia"), placed by region on the blank map. At least **8 of 10**.
  - *Judging, distance*: 10 pairs of places (half inside the United States, half across the world); for each, the
    distance band (under 200 miles, 200 to 500, 500 to 1,000, 1,000 to 3,000, over 3,000) and the travel band (a drive of
    a few hours, a day's drive, a flight of a few hours, a long flight). At least **8 of 10** with both bands right.
- **What "right" is for a tap**: inside the right state or country. For a city, a river, a range or a sea: inside the
  unit it belongs to and in the right part of it (the app draws the unit split into its halves or thirds where the
  design says so, so "Reno is in western Nevada" is judged, not "Reno is 3 miles from here"). The pilot tests whether
  that feels fair on a phone.
- **Out**: map reading on the ground (the phone's map turns itself and finds you; G6); flags, outlines, populations,
  economies and climate; capitals of countries other than the ones the news uses as the government's name (Ottawa,
  Mexico City, Brussels, Beijing, Moscow, and the ones on each region's list); the geography of other planets, of the
  oceans' depths and of history (old borders, old names); navigation with a compass; towns under a quarter of a
  million people unless the news names them; the geography of the learner's own town (their own map does that).
  Canada's provinces are in (part 9) because the learner's own country is the one they will be asked about most, and
  because Americans' questions about it need an answer in US terms ("north of Montana").

### Gate 3. Practice, and whether the app can deliver it
**Method.** Facts: a name is shown; the learner taps where it is on the lesson's map, with no names on it; the right place
lights up at once with one line (the unit, the neighbors, the water); a miss shows the tapped spot and the right spot
together. The same places return in the lesson the other way: a region lit, the name typed from memory, the accepted
spellings forgiven as in Civics (principles 1 to 4). Each new group is shown first for one minute on the labeled map
(the only reading), then only recalled. Judging: a place never seen, given as a story gives it, with one tap on the
region and one line after on the clue that decided it; two places named, two bands chosen, then both lit on the map with
the figure (principle 8).

**Time.** In every lesson at least four fifths of the time is answering: tapping or naming. Reading is a minute at
most (the labeled map, and in the judging lessons one worked place).

**Feedback.** From the app, after every answer: the right place lit, the line of where it sits, and after a miss the
tapped spot beside it; for a distance, both places lit and the true figure. The end check gives none until the end.

**Delivery check** (what the app must do; each is built before the first lesson, or the lesson waits):
1. A `map` block: a map drawn as SVG from public-domain outlines (Natural Earth, simplified so that every data file
   stays under 800 lines), north-up, one projection for the world and one for the United States, pinch-zoom and pan on
   a phone, no names on it unless the block says `labels: true`. Each lesson's map is its region (the United States,
   Europe, the Middle East and North Africa), and the end check's is the world.
2. A `place` ask: the learner taps the map; right when the tap lands in the named region, or, for a point, inside the
   unit the item names and in the part of it the item names (`within: 'NV', part: 'west'`); the feedback lights the
   right place and marks the tap. Zoom does not count against the learner.
3. The reverse form on the same item: a region lit and a `text` ask with the accepted names (Civics' matching: case,
   punctuation, one small misspelling, "the" optional, alternatives such as "Türkiye / Turkey", "Czechia / Czech
   Republic", "DC / Washington, D.C.").
4. A per-item record across days and the schedule that brings a place back until right in three separate sessions,
   asked in whichever form it was not last asked (6, with the form chosen by the app).
5. A `choose` from the subject's `distance` and `travel` lists, and feedback that lights two places and prints the
   figure (straight-line miles, computed from coordinates in the data; the travel band is written in the item).
6. Content: the 50 states and DC with their regions and neighbors; about 40 US cities and the 50 state capitals, each
   with its state and the part of the state it is in; every country of the United Nations list plus Taiwan and Kosovo,
   grouped into the regions of parts 9 to 17, each with its neighbors and its water; about 30 seas, straits, rivers,
   ranges and deserts; and a bank of about 60 never-practised places and 40 distance pairs for the judging lessons and
   the end checks, so that every check uses places the learner has not met.
7. A mix rule (V76) so that every set holds places from at least two units of the lesson's region, and from lesson 3
   on a share of due places from earlier lessons.

Nothing here sends anything anywhere or asks for a permission: the map is data in the app, the taps are stored as the
item's answer (`{ x, y }` on the map, never a location of the phone), and no location service is ever asked for.

Order: the US map first (its units; G4), then the states region by region, then the cities and the capitals (which hang
on the states), then the world from the nearest neighbors outward, the water and mountains that tie the regions
together, then the two judging parts, which draw on everything, then the whole. Parts 2 to 5 hold 13 + 12 + 17 + 9 = 51
(the 50 states and DC), each once; parts 9 to 17 hold every country of the United Nations list, Taiwan and Kosovo,
each once, plus the features named; parts 19 and 20 are the two judging questions of the gate 2 test; part 21 is the
test itself. If every part passes and the returns hold, the gate 2 test passes.

"If the learner did only this, would they be closer to the end result?" Every fact part: yes, it is a share of the
places a story can name, placed in the moment's form. Each judging part: yes, it is one of the two questions asked
when the place is not one of the lists.

### Gate 5. Each lesson
Fact lessons share one shape, about 12 to 15 minutes: one minute on the labeled map of the lesson's region, grouped
by the unit the places sit in (P30); then each place shown by name and tapped on the blank map ("not yet" is allowed
and counts as a try), the right place lit at once with its line; after the group, the same places the other way (a
region lit, the name typed); misses come back later in the lesson until right; from lesson 3 on, the due places of
earlier lessons mixed in; then the part's check. Judging lessons, about 12 minutes: one worked place, ten places
answered with feedback, the part's check.

1. **The map of the United States** (part 1). Do: the labeled map for a minute; the coasts, the four regions and the
   Great Lakes tapped, then the ranges and rivers, then the time zones (a `choose`: which zone is Denver in?); each the
   other way. Feedback: the place lit and its line ("the Ohio: from Pittsburgh west into the Mississippi at Cairo,
   Illinois; the line between the Midwest and the South for most of its length"). Check: part 1, 20 of 22. Why:
   principles 1 to 4, 6. **The pilot.** It carries the map block, both asks and the lines; what the owner's try shows
   (is a tap on a river fair? is the zoom needed?) comes back into this record before lesson 2.
2. **The West** (part 2). Do: 13 states, each tapped, then named; the Pacific states first, then the Mountain states,
   then Alaska and Hawaii on the inset; the pairs people mix (Idaho and Iowa by name, Wyoming and Colorado by shape) asked
   back to back. Feedback: "Nevada: between California and Utah, no coast; Las Vegas in its southern tip, Reno on the
   California line." Check: part 2, 12 of 13. Why: principles 1 to 4, 7.
3. **The Midwest** (part 3). Do: 12 states, the Great Lakes states first (the lakes from lesson 1 as due items), then
   the Plains states; the four with an "I" (Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Idaho) in one mixed set. Check: part 3, 11 of 12.
   Why: principles 1 to 5, 7.
4. **The South** (part 4). Do: 17, the Gulf states first, then the inland South, then the Atlantic South with DC; the
   Carolinas and the Virginias as pairs; the Mississippi and the Ohio due. Check: part 4, 15 of 17. Why: as lesson 2.
5. **The Northeast** (part 5). Do: 9 states, New England as a group, then the three on the Mid-Atlantic; the small ones
   on the zoomed map (Rhode Island, Connecticut, New Jersey, Delaware from part 4 due). Then the whole lower 48 once,
   mixed, as the first whole-map set. Check: part 5, 8 of 9. Why: as lesson 2; delivery item 1 (zoom).
6. **The cities west of the Mississippi** (part 6). Do: about 23 cities, each a tap inside the state and the part of
   it ("Houston: southeast Texas, by the Gulf"), the other way by name; the Texas four and the California four as
   groups. Check: part 6, 20 of 23. Why: principles 1 to 4; gate 2's rule for a point.
7. **The cities east of the Mississippi** (part 7). Do: about 23, the Great Lakes cities, the Ohio valley, the South's
   cities, Florida's three, the Northeast corridor (Washington to Boston) in order. Check: part 7, 20 of 23. Why: as
   lesson 6.
8. **The capitals, west** (part 8, first half). Do: the 25 capitals of the West and Midwest as "the name the news uses
   for the state": shown "Sacramento" and the state tapped, then the dot inside the state; the other way "California's
   government sits in …" typed. The ones already placed as cities are due items, not new. Check: 22 of 25. Why:
   principles 1 to 5.
9. **The capitals, east** (part 8, second half). Do: the 25 of the South and Northeast the same way; the traps
   (Albany not New York City, Tallahassee not Miami, Harrisburg not Philadelphia, Springfield not Chicago, Jefferson
   City not Kansas City) asked as pairs. Check: 22 of 25. Why: as lesson 8; P12.
10. **Canada and Mexico** (part 9, first half). Do: the ten provinces and three territories, each placed and named,
    with the US state south of each as its line ("Saskatchewan: north of Montana and North Dakota"); Mexico's six
    border states with the US state across the line, then Mexico City, Jalisco, Oaxaca and Yucatán. Check: 17 of 19.
    Why: principles 1 to 4; gate 2 on Canada.
11. **Central America and the Caribbean** (part 9, second half). Do: the seven countries of the isthmus in order from
    Mexico to Colombia; the islands from Cuba east and south; the Gulf, the Caribbean and the Panama Canal as water.

12. **South America** (part 10). Do: 13 countries and three features; the Pacific side first, then the Atlantic side,
    Brazil's size shown against the United States; the trap "Chile's coast is east of Florida's" asked and shown. Check:

13. **Europe, west and north** (part 11). Do: about 24; the Iberian pair, France and the Low Countries, the German
    speaking three, the British Isles and their parts, the Nordic five; the North Sea and the Baltic; the trap "Rome is
    as far north as New York" shown. Check: part 11, 21 of 24. Why: principles 1 to 4, 6.
14. **Europe, east and south** (part 12). Do: about 27; the Baltic three, Poland to Hungary, the Balkans as a group on
    the zoomed map, the Black Sea states, Russia's two parts. Check: part 12, 24 of 27. Why: as lesson 13.
15. **The Middle East and North Africa** (part 13). Do: about 26; the Levant, the Gulf states on the zoomed map, Iran

16. **West Africa** (part 14, first half). Do: the coast from Senegal to Nigeria in order, then the Sahel states behind

    Africa and the two states inside and beside it, Madagascar and the islands; the Kalahari. Check: 12 of 14. Why: as

20. **South and Central Asia** (part 16). Do: 22; the subcontinent first, then the five -stans on the zoomed map, then
    the Caucasus three; the Himalayas, the Indus, the Ganges, the Caspian. Check: part 16, 20 of 22. Why: as lesson 15.
21. **East Asia** (part 17, first half). Do: China, Mongolia, the two Koreas, Japan, Taiwan; the Yangtze, the Taiwan
    Strait; the Koreas as a pair. Check: 7 of 8. Why: as lesson 15.
22. **Southeast Asia and the Pacific** (part 17, second half). Do: the mainland five, the island states, the Strait of
    Malacca and the South China Sea, then Australia, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea and the island groups; Hawaii's
    place among them as a due item. Check: 18 of 20. Why: as lesson 15.
23. **The world's water, mountains and deserts** (part 18). Do: about 27, the ones not yet placed; the oceans and the
    seas between them, the straits and canals as the narrow places ships must pass, the ranges, the deserts, the rivers;
    the ones already placed come back due. Check: part 18, 24 of 27. Why: principles 1 to 5, 7.
24. **How far** (part 19). Do: one worked pair (Los Angeles to New York: about 2,450 miles, a five-hour flight, a
    four-day drive); ten pairs inside the United States, then ten across the world, each two bands, feedback with both
    places lit and the figure; the time zones and the date line used in the travel band. Check: part 19, 10 new pairs,
    8 right. Why: principle 8; G7.
25. **Placing a place you have never heard of** (part 20). Do: one worked place ("Mombasa, Kenya's port": Kenya is on
    Africa's east coast, so its port is on the Indian Ocean, south of the Horn); ten unseen places given as a story
    gives them, placed by region; then the five traps as contrast items with the true map after. Check: part 20, 10 new
    places, 8 right. Why: principles 6, 8; G4, G5.
26. **The whole world** (part 21). Do: 40 names and 10 lit regions from every list, mixed, on the world map; then the
    judging questions; feedback at the end. Check: the gate 2 test; a second sitting a week or more later. Why:
    principles 2, 5, 9.

The weekly review for Geography: the due places from every finished lesson, mixed, asked in the form they were not
last asked (a name to tap, a region to name), plus two unseen places and one distance pair (R8, R9).
