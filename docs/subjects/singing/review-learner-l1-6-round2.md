# Learner review, singing lessons 1 to 6, round 2

Reader: cold beginner (project manager), learner views only, regenerated before reading. I cannot sing: sung tries were judged by their instructions and feedback words only. Written questions were answered blind, then checked.

## Verdicts

| Lesson | Verdict | Written check, blind | Way of deciding, in my words (written before the first practice question) |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Range | PASS | 5/5 | Sing the hard note on "oo" at talking loudness. If it comes out only by forcing it is too high; if only air or a rumble, too low; if it comes clean, the range is not the problem, go to the next check. |
| L2 Match a note | PASS (sung only; no written check) | n/a | Hear the note, replay it silently in my head, aim at it from the first moment, then judge under/on/over myself before the app says. |
| L3 On, under, over | PASS | 5/5 | Do not trust the reaction. Hold a steady note, sing beside it, and see which way I must move: none = fine, up = under, down = over, arrives late by itself = sliding in, wanders = did not have the note in my head. |
| L4 Hold it steady | PASS | 5/5 | Compare the start and the end of the hold with a steady note: same all the way = steady; on then lower = sank; on then higher (often louder) = rose; wrong from the first moment and unchanged = off from the start. |
| L5 Steps and leaps | PASS | 5/5 | Check each of the two notes against its own note. First on and far note short = fell short; both off the same way and amount = wrong start; both on = leave it. |
| L6 Short tunes | PASS | 5/5 | Check every note, find where it went wrong: one far note off, last note slipping, or lost partway (go slow, learn in parts); all matching = leave it. |

Nothing below is a stall that blocks a beginner from understanding or passing by reasoning. The items marked (priority) are the ones I would fix first.

## Defects (D = the beginner would be confused or misled; P = polish)

### L1
1. D (priority) Step 3 and Items 3, 5, 7, 8: "move the song" never says how. A beginner has no way to move a song lower. Fix: one line, e.g. "Change the key in the karaoke or music app, sing it an octave lower, or pick a different song."
2. P Step 3: "oo" is never explained. Fix: "oo as in 'moon'".
3. P Step 2 lists seven checks but the lesson uses only the first; check 4 uses "effort" and "sharp" before they are taught. Fix: show checks 2 to 7 greyed as "coming up", or define sharp in a line.
4. P Pattern: wherever someone is worried or someone else comments (Items 4, 6; Check 10, 13) the answer is "In range". A beginner can learn that "worry means in range". Fix: see cross-lesson item A.

### L2
1. P Step 2: "On the note is within half of a half-step" and "a shade is off by less than a half-step" overlap as written (a shade under includes on). Fix: say "a shade = more than half a half-step but less than one".
2. P The result words "Sliding up into it" and "Sliding down onto it" are listed here before they are taught (L3). The miss line explains the one that matters, so no stall.
3. P The view does not say what cues the start of singing after the tone, or whether the note can be replayed during the silent step. Fix: state both on screen.
4. P Jaws theme as the only half-step example assumes the learner knows it. Fix: add one more familiar pair, or say "two neighbouring piano keys".
5. Not judged: whether the sung check (4 of 5 on the note) is fair for a beginner; I cannot sing.

### L3
1. D (priority) Step 2, worked example, Away from the phone: "pause the recording on the note and hold it" cannot work, because a paused recording is silent. The piano app does work. Fix: remove "recording paused" everywhere (also L4, L5, L6 stories and Away from the phone) or say "play the note again and again, or use a piano app".
2. P "Sliding down onto it" is a result word but never taught here.
3. P Item 7 hinges on one word ("deliberately moves up") to separate "a shade under" from "sliding up into it". Defensible, but a second read. Fix: add "the whole two seconds" cue to the feedback of both.
4. P Feeling-prefix pattern (Item 6, Check 18, Hector): see cross-lesson item A.

### L4
1. P Step 5: "twice the check's 2 seconds, so 2 seconds comes easily" reads as nonsense on first pass. Fix: "The check asks for 2 seconds. These ask for 4, so 2 will feel easy."
2. P Loudness cue: both "rose over" stories (Items 4, Check 27) mention swelling louder; a beginner can answer "louder means rose" without checking. Item 7 (no loudness) breaks the pattern once. Fix: add one swelling story where the note stays steady, and one rising story with no loudness.
3. P The four outcomes leave out a hold that is off from the start and then also drifts. Fix: add one line saying "name the first thing you hear".

### L5
1. D (priority) Step 2 says two notes "can go three ways". A far note that overshoots, or a first note off with the second on, fits none of them. A beginner with a real result like that has no category. Fix: add "Something else: one note off, say which" or add the missing cases and cover them in questions.
2. P Step 2 says "each try tells you which" (step or jump), but the last group and Check try 32 say "a step or a jump apart". Fix: tell it there too, or remove the sentence.
3. P The sung feedback names each note's direction but not which of the three lesson categories it is; Step 5 leaves the learner to work it out. Fine for the pass; a one-line "this looks like: fell short" would close the loop.
4. P "by about the same amount" appears in every "both off the same way" story (Item 3, 6; Check 28, 31), so that phrase alone gives the answer. Fix: vary the wording.
5. P Feeling-prefix pattern (Item 5, Check 29): see item A.

### L6
1. P Heading duplicated: a "Step 13: The check" and a later "The check". Merge them.
2. P Four outcomes do not cover two problems at once (a short jump and a sinking last note), the likeliest real result. Fix: add "more than one: fix the first one you hear".
3. P Each story echoes its option's own words (long last note slides lower = "long last note sank"). A beginner can pass by word match. Fix: describe observations with different words from the option names, as Item 2 does.
4. P Feeling-prefix pattern (Item 5, Check 27): see item A.

### Across lessons
A. P (priority for the checks) Every story that begins with a worry, a feeling or a friend's comment has the answer "nothing wrong" (L1 Items 4, 6, Check 10, 13; L3 Item 6, Check 18; L4 Items 5, Check 28; L5 Items 5, Check 29; L6 Items 5, Check 27). A beginner can learn "worry means fine" without reasoning. The lessons should also include a story where the feeling is right and the check finds the fault (e.g. "she felt she was low; she moves up to match: a shade under"), at least once in each check.
B. P Option positions and answer lengths show no pattern. Right answers are spread across positions. No defect.
C. P Warm-up (hum or straw), "Away from the phone" tasks and "piano app" assume the learner has an app; no name or where to get one is given.
