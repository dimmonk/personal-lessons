## The way of deciding, in my words (before the first practice question)

- l7: Ask if the top note comes at all on "oo" at easy loudness (no: out of range, move the song). If it comes, ask whether it is off only when loud (pushing: sing lighter) or under at any loudness (falling short: hear the note, move up) or fine (leave it).
- l8: Same range-first, then listen to what the voice does on the way up: smooth and thin is fine, jolt is cracking, tight but not louder is squeezing, louder and sharp is pushing, easy but under is falling short.
- l9: Judge the breath by what it does, not how the body moves: air left at the end is fine; small noisy last-second gulp is a gasp; singing on after the air is gone is pressing on; full breath in the wrong place is mid-word; hearing air in the sound is leaking. Plan breaths at line ends.
- l10: Only after notes, top and air are clear. Nose-held test and a recording: nasal, muffled (dull, mouth shut, words lost), mumbled (clear sound, soft word edges), or just surprise at hearing yourself.
- l11: Rest for ordinary hoarseness; doctor at about 2 weeks with no cold, 4 weeks at most; right away for pain, blood, a lump, trouble breathing.
- l12: Go through the checks in order; the first one the story fails is the cause; do its one fix; if all clear, leave it.

## Defects

### l12 (decides the verdict)
1. Step 2 and Items 3, 7, check 16, worked example. The rule says "stop at the first check the story does not clear", and the note check (3) comes before effort (4). A story that is "a shade over" at the top fails check 3 by that rule, yet the marked answer is effort (4). The l7 tie-break ("fix the pushing first, it made the note go sharp") is not repeated here. A literal reader stalls or picks the note check. Fix: add one line to Step 2 under check 3/4: "Over only when loud and clean when sung lighter is effort, not the note: the note check is for a note that is off at any loudness." Also make the Compare table on Item 3/7 name this.
2. Step 2 "seven checks" leaves out cracking and squeezing (taught in l8). A learner with a flip or a throat squeeze has no check to land on, and "Does it go sharp or strained only where the line climbs or gets loud?" half-fits squeezing. Fix: add them as named outcomes of check 4 with their l8 words, and put one story of each in the practice or check.
3. Each story asks "which check" then "what to try", but the fix is one-to-one with the check, so the second question is a lookup and adds no reasoning. Fix: fold into one question, or make the second question carry a twist (e.g. a story where the obvious fix is wrong).
4. Wrong-option feedback on the check question says what that check is for ("That check fits a story where..."), not what in this story ruled it out. It does not say what decided. Fix: name the deciding fact from the story.
5. Tune tries: five notes played once at 0.8 s each, to be sung back from memory, last held 2 s, two of three to pass. A cold beginner may fail on memory alone, not on the skills taught. No replay is stated. Fix: say whether the notes can be heard again, or reduce to 3 notes before the last long one.

### l7
6. Step 5/6 and the tries: the instruction is "no louder than you talk" but no feedback line or pass rule mentions loudness; only pitch is reported. A learner could shout and pass, or sing softly and be told "over" with no link to the lesson's point. Fix: state whether loudness is measured and shown, or say the tries test only the note.
7. Pattern risk: the right option is signalled by one keyword ("only when loud" gives pushing, "under at any loudness" gives falling short, "shout/will not come" gives out of range). Reasoning is not needed. Polish: add a story where both cues appear (loud and under).

### l8
8. Light, easy top vs cracking: both are "thin"; the table says only one jolts. Fine in the stories, but "thin and airy" appears in the cracking definition and the light-top definition, which could blur on a recording. Polish: give cracking a different word than thin ("a sudden gap").
9. The six-way choice is answered by keywords in the story ("jolt", "louder", "tight not louder", "under"). Same pattern risk as 7.

### l9
10. Gasped breath and pressing on both end with the air running out. They differ only in whether the breath was small and noisy at the last moment; Item 5 and check 15 do not say how the breath was taken, so a learner has only "keeps singing after the air is gone". Check 19 (Oli) fits gasp but its "runs out halfway through the next line" also reads like pressing on. Polish: make each story say the breath size or timing explicitly.
11. Tries hold a note for 6 seconds on "oo" and report the note and steadiness, but nothing reports air left at the end, which is the lesson's test. Fix: add a self-check line ("did you have air left?") or say the tries train steadiness only.

### l10
12. "Find the 'ah' that stays the same with the nose held or not" gives no way to do it. A beginner stalls. Fix: add one concrete step (open the mouth taller, sing "ah" as in "father", repeat the hold-and-let-go test).
13. The nose-held test asks whether the sound changes "a lot" or "little", with no anchor. Everyone's "ah" changes somewhat. Fix: give a reference ("if it goes from open to buzzing or blocked, a lot").
14. Item numbering starts at Item 1 here, while other lessons start at 2 (Item 1 is the warm-up). Cosmetic.

### l11
15. Gap: a hoarse voice 11 to 14 days after a cold is not covered (cold says 7 to 10 days, no-cold rule says two weeks). Item 12 covers only past a month. Fix: one sentence for what to do between 10 days and two weeks.
16. Typed answers: "pain", "blood" or "lump" alone are misses, and "humidifier" is not an accepted answer to Item 6. The "My answer means the same" button covers it, but a beginner will see "Not this one" first. Polish: accept the bare nouns and "humidifier".
17. Items 2 and 3 ask for two numbers (two weeks / four weeks) that differ only by "whatever the cause"; easy to swap on recall. Polish: bold the cold/no-cold condition in the Step 1 text.

### Across the lessons
18. Wording is consistent and plain; no unexplained jargon found beyond "flat/sharp" (explained in l3) and "half-step" (used in feedback; assumed from earlier). Fine.
