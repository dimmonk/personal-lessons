# Stand-in review: Singing, lesson 2 "Match a note" (part 2, the pilot)

Reviewer: stand-in learner (Opus 5.5), 2026-10-11. Read: the design record's opening JSON and the gate 4 row for part 2, the
learner view (`docs/learner-view/singing-l2.md`), the lesson (`public/subjects/singing/l2.lesson.js`) and the code that makes and
scores each try (`public/app/lessons/sing-task.js`, `sing-score.js`, `sing-strip.js`, `sing-run.js`, the redo rule in `queue.js`).
Disclosure: printing the head of `design.md` also showed the first lines of the gate 5 list (lessons 1 to 6, one line each)
before I stopped; I did not read the rest of gate 5, the evidence or any review. The app was not run: a headless phone has no
microphone, so the screens were judged from the learner view and the strip's drawing code. I cannot sing; the judgement below is
what a beginner who does sing would get.

## Before
An adult with no singing background, on a phone. Asked to hear one note and sing it back, I would get roughly near it most of
the time, sometimes a half-step or more off, and I would have no idea whether I was under or over. I could not say whether I
was "in tune" at all, and I could certainly not hit 4 of 5 notes within a quarter of a half-step on demand.

## Notes, in order
1. **The why (3 lines).** Short, plain, and it tells me the plan: first with a line, then without. Fine. Nothing to cut.
2. **Range (only when none is stored).** "Slide up to the highest easy note, and hold it", then down. Clear. The failure line
   ("hold each end for a moment") tells me what to do if it does not work. Necessary: every later note is placed inside it.
3. **Warm-up.** An 8-second hum with the line drawn. Skippable. "Or hum through a straw" assumes a straw I do not have on me; harmless.
   It taught me nothing I used later except that the line moves with my voice, which is useful the first time.
4. **How a try goes.** "The line is your voice. The bar is the note. Aim to put the line on the bar." Clear and enough.
   One confusing phrase: "sing it back on 'oo' and keep going until the line ends". The line is my own voice, so it ends when I
   stop; what actually ends is the bar (the 1.9-second window). In the no-line set there is no line at all. A beginner can work
   it out from "Sing now" disappearing, so it does not block, but it should say "until the bar ends".
5. **8 tries with the line.** Hear a 1.5-second note, then sing for 1.9 seconds while my voice draws a line against the bar. This
   is the real teaching: I can see I am under and push up until I sit on the bar, live. After each try a word ("On the note",
   "A shade under", "Well over") and the strip again. The tip "Hear the note in your head before you sing it" is the one
   strategy taught and it is a good one. Two small snags:
   - The bar is drawn 8 px thick at its minimum, about plus or minus 32 cents on this axis, while "on the note" is plus or minus 25. A
     line sitting just inside the bar's edge can be scored "A shade over". Rare, but it would confuse me the first time it happens.
   - The score takes the middle of the window, so if I start off and slide onto the bar I can still miss. That is honest (the
     real moment needs me to start on the note), but nothing says so; a beginner may feel cheated once.
6. **8 tries without the line.** "No line this time. Listen, sing, and see how you did afterwards." The word and the strip after
   each try tell me which way I was off, so I can correct the next one by ear. This is the step that turns watching into hearing.
   Good.
7. **Misses come back.** Every miss returns three tries later with a new note "until it is on the note", with no ceiling
   (`queue.js` re-inserts on every miss). For a typical beginner hitting half the notes this doubles the sets (about 32 tries,
   still near ten minutes). For a weak pitch-matcher hitting one in five, the first set alone becomes about 40 tries and has no
   end until they hit. Not a screen I could not follow, but it can trap exactly the learner who needs the most help.

Nothing in the lesson could be answered without doing it: every note is new, drawn from my own range, and scored from my voice.
A beginner who already matches well will pass the check without learning much, which is the right outcome for them.

## The end check (as written, no lesson open)
5 new notes from my range, no line, the word after each; pass at 4 on the note (within 25 cents, octave-folded). This is exactly
the part 2 check in gate 4 and a small, honest version of the gate 2 test (hear, sing back, no display). As a beginner who had
just done 16 to 30 tries with feedback, I would expect to land 3 or 4; a pass is reachable, not given.

## After
- I can hear a single note and sing it back close to the target without a display, and I know which way to move when I am off
  (tries 2 to 9, the line against the bar; then tries 10 to 17, the word after).
- I can tell "a shade under" from "on the note" by ear more often than before, because the no-line set made me guess first and
  then showed me (tries 10 to 17).
- I have one deliberate habit: hear the note in my head before I open my mouth (the reason line after each try).
- I know my comfortable low and high notes (the range exercise), which later lessons rely on.

## The five answers
- **Gain: yes.** Hearing one note and singing it back within a quarter of a half-step, without a display, and knowing whether I
  was under or over. That is the base of the gate 2 test and I did not have it before.
- **Doing: yes.** Under a minute of reading; the rest is about 22 to 35 sung tries, each hear-then-sing. Almost all time is the
  real action.
- **Check: yes.** New notes, no line, the part's own threshold, in the form of the real moment (hear it, sing it back).
- **Clear: yes, with three non-blocking snags.** (1) "keep going until the line ends" should be "until the bar ends"; (2) the bar
  is drawn wider than the on-the-note band, so a line inside the bar's edge can score "a shade over"; (3) nobody says the score
  is taken from the middle of the note, so sliding onto it late still misses.
- **Waste:** little. The straw mention can go (most phones-in-hand learners have none). The warm-up is already skippable.

## Fixes recommended (not blocking the pass)
1. Change "keep going until the line ends" to "keep going until the bar ends" (and in the no-line set, "until 'Sing now' goes").
2. Draw the bar at the true tolerance (plus or minus 25 cents), or widen the minimum only visually with a fainter edge, so inside the
   bar always means "on the note".
3. One line in "How a try goes": "Start on the note: the middle of your try is what counts, so sliding onto it late still misses."
4. Put a ceiling on redos in a practice set (for example at most one return per try, or at most 8 extra tries per set), so a weak
   pitch-matcher is not held in the first set indefinitely; their misses are the evidence the next session needs, not more of the same now.
