# singing: design record (build-subject skill)

Written 2026-10-10. Gates 2 and 3 are drafts waiting for the owner's approval; until then V69 keeps every lesson of this
subject as it is. The content that exists predates the gates and is audited against this record (gates 4 and 5, designed
blind) before anything is kept.

Approved by the owner: 2026-10-10, "approve all, keep going" (gates 2, 3 and the pilot, for every subject; the pilot is still to be tried on a phone, and what it shows comes back into this record).

## Gate 1. Kind of learning and evidence
Kind: body (the voice and the ear together). Evidence: `docs/research/learning-to-sing.md` (S1–S6); review: `docs/research/review-after-lessons.md`. The principles that drive the design: copy heard targets in your own range, melodies not only single notes (S1); a live pitch line with a result after each try (S2); the line fades and the end check is without it (S3); instructions about the sound, not the body (S4); a straw or hum warm-up as low-risk, not as a cure (S5); short regular sessions (S6).

## Gate 2. End result (draft)
- After this subject you can: sing a short melody you have just heard, in your own range, in tune and without any display, with long notes held steady and no pushing.
- Real moment: Your own voice: singing along in the car, at karaoke, with friends.
- Real-world test: without the pitch line, the app plays a new five-note melody in your range; you sing it back. At least 4 of 5 notes within a quarter of a half-step of the target, and the long note steady for 2 seconds.

## Gate 3. Practice (draft)
What the app must be able to do first (delivery check): a live pitch line against a target over time; hiding it; playing a short melody and scoring each sung note; finding the learner's comfortable range; timing a steady held note. It already plays notes and detects sung pitch.

Copy heard targets with your voice, in your own range: single notes, then slides, then short melodies. Each try shows your
pitch as a line against the target and a result; then the same target with the line hidden and only the result after
(feedback fades, S3). Instructions name the sound, not the body (S4). Short daily sessions; a minute of reading at most.

## Gate 4. The parts of the end result
Ordered so each builds on the ones before. Every target in every part is placed inside the range found in part 1 (S1).

| # | Part | Its check (a small version of the gate 2 test, always without the line) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Your range**: the low and high notes you sing comfortably | The app finds a comfortable low and high note from two easy slides; every later target sits inside them |
| 2 | **Match a note** you have just heard | 4 of 5 single notes within 25 cents (a quarter of a half-step) |
| 3 | **Hold it steady** | A matched note kept within 25 cents for 2 seconds, twice in a row |
| 4 | **Move between notes**: a smooth slide that lands | 4 of 5 slides land within 25 cents of the second note |
| 5 | **Steps and leaps**: two-note intervals heard and sung back | 4 of 5 intervals with both notes within 25 cents |
| 6 | **Short melodies**: three to five notes, heard then sung back | The gate 2 test |
| 7 | **A lighter top**: the highest notes of your range sung without pushing | 4 of 5 top-of-range notes on pitch at no more than your own talking loudness (measured against a loud and a talking note you sing first) |

Map both ways: lessons 1 to 7 below serve parts 1 to 7 one each; parts 1 to 6 together are the gate 2 test, part 7 its "no
pushing" clause. Nothing else is taught.

## Gate 5. Each lesson
Every lesson has the same shape: one short paragraph of why (a minute at most), the exercise with the line, the same exercise
with the line hidden, then the part's check. About eight to ten minutes. A straw or hum warm-up opens every session (S5).

1. **Find your range** (part 1). Do: hum a slide up as high as is easy, then down as low as is easy; repeat once; the app
   shows the notes reached and sets the range. Feedback: the line follows your voice. Check: the range is set. Why: S1
   (targets out of range make errors worse).
2. **Match a note** (part 2) — the pilot. Do: hear a note, sing it on "oo", see your line against the target and the result;
   eight tries with the line, eight without. Check: part 2. Why: S1, S2, S3.
3. **Hold it steady** (part 3). Do: match a note and keep it on the line for two, then four seconds. Feedback: the line and
   how long you stayed on it. Check: part 3. Why: S2, S3, S4.
4. **Slides that land** (part 4). Do: hear two notes, slide from one to the other, land and hold. Check: part 4. Why: S1.
5. **Steps and leaps** (part 5). Do: hear two notes, sing them back; small steps first, then larger leaps. Check: part 5.
   Why: S1 (intervals get squeezed), S2.
6. **Short tunes** (part 6). Do: hear three, then four, then five notes, sing them back; the line, then without. Check: the
   gate 2 test. Why: S1, S2 (most gain on short melodies), S3.
7. **A lighter top** (part 7). Do: sing a note loud and one at talking loudness to set the scale, then top-of-range notes at
   talking loudness, on "oo" or through a straw first. Check: part 7. Why: S4, S5.

The weekly review for Singing is a short round of the checks of the lessons done, not questions (R8: review in the real form).
