# Stand-in learner review: Scams, lesson 3 "Act, check or leave" (rev 2)

Reviewer: a fresh stand-in (not the builder), 2026-10-12. Told only the goal packet (end result, real moment, real-world
test, part C row). Read the lesson as the learner meets it (docs/learner-view/scams-l3.md, scams-l0.md for context) and
the items it uses (l3.lesson.js, items-build.js, items-practice.js, items-check.js) to confirm what is scored. Did every
item as a beginner adult on a phone with ten minutes. Not run on a phone.

## Before

As a beginner: a text from "my bank" about a fraud charge, I would tap its link or call the number in it, because that
is what it tells me to do and it looks like my bank. A "new bank details" invoice from a tradesman I hired, I would
probably pay. A pop-up saying my computer is infected I might call. A grandchild calling from a new number in trouble, I
would want to send the money. I have no rule for telling these apart from real messages, and no idea that a real alert
should also be handled through my own app.

## Notes, in order

- **Rule screen.** Short and usable. Three questions plus a choice line. But the choice line, read literally, is wrong
  for several later items (see blocking fixes 1 and 2): "Do what it says when you started it or arranged it" covers
  the parcel-fee text (I placed the order), the new-bank-details invoice (I hired Femi), the "final notice" from the
  power company (a bill I always pay) and the buyer's payment link (I listed the lamp). Every one of those is a scam
  scored as "do what it says = acted". And "Leave it when nothing of yours is involved" does not fit the items scored
  "leave": the receipt, the shipping notice and the dentist call all involve something of mine.
- **Worked example (Calderwood locked card).** Clear, and the four steps are the three questions in use. Good.
- **Q1 (code I asked for).** Easy by the rule. Fine.
- **Q2 (unknown number wants a code read back).** Rule says: I did not start it, I have a way of my own, so check
  first. Scored: "Check first" is wrong ("nothing to check"), yet its own "How do you check?" lists "The sign-in page of
  the account the code is for" as right. I was confused, and I do not know which account the code is for: the context
  does not say.
- **Q3 (jacket on its way).** The rule says I ordered it, so do what it says. "Do what it says" is scored wrong, with
  the feedback "There was nothing to do, so nothing happened." A harmless action marked wrong on a real message. I
  had to repeat it until I picked Leave or Check. Confused.
- **Q4 (parcel fee).** I picked check because of the fee and the link, not because the rule told me to. The reason
  ("a fee to release it is the request") taught me the thing the rule screen should have said.
- **Q5/Q6 (real and fake fraud alert, identical but the link).** The best pair in the lesson: it taught me that how it
  looks never decides. The contexts say only "Calderwood Bank is your bank"; that I have its app comes from the worked
  example, not these items.
- **Q7/Q8 (invoice, then new bank details).** Good pair. Learned it.
- **Q9 (pop-up).** The context was written to name my own way ("Your computer has its own security settings"), and
  the rule says check when I did not start it and have a way of my own. Scored: "Check first" is wrong. The "How do
  you check?" again has a right answer I cannot reach without being marked wrong. Confused.
- **Q10 (boss on a new number), Q13 (credit-union alert with a number), Q14 (bank fraud call wanting a code).**
  Clear, learned them.
- **Q11 (Lumenfall receipt).** Same as Q3: a bill I always pay, so by the rule I do what it says ("No action
  needed"); scored wrong.
- **Q12 (reset I asked for).** Repeats Q1's lesson.
- **Q15 (Dev in the game chat).** Taught on the rule screen, so I chose Leave. Fine.
- **Timing.** Only live call/chat exchanges are untimed in the app (queue.js `questionSeconds`); the static call
  transcripts in this lesson (Q14, check 21, check 25) are on the 20 s clock. The app's check screen does not claim
  otherwise, but the learner view adds "A call is not on a clock" (tools/learner-view/render-learner-view.mjs line 339),
  which is false for this lesson. The longest items (Q8, Q15, check 27: about 70 words with the context) are tight to
  read and answer twice in 20 s on a phone; a timeout on a real message spends the one allowed miss.

## The check, done without the lesson open

Pickup code: do it. Fenwick bill: do it. Dana's pickup time: do it. Marrowbeck unusual sign-in: check, in the app.
Penhallow $310 alert: check, number on my card. **Dentist call: by the rule I booked it, so "do what it says"; scored
wrong ("You called back for no reason").** Address-incomplete parcel: check, order page. Final notice by gift card:
check, app. Buyer's payment link: check, marketplace app. Grandson from a new number: check, his saved number.
Lumenfall suspended: check, my account. Ioana: leave.

Result: no scam acted on, no check through the message's way, one real message wrong (the dentist). A pass with no
margin, and the one miss came from following the rule as taught. A learner who instead absorbed Q3/Q11's correction
("nothing to do, so leave") gets it right; the rule screen and the practice disagree, and the check rewards the
practice, not the rule.

Genuine messages: all six real check messages are scored as real (each accepts checking through my own way, and the
two that offer a link or number score checking as the right move, not as suspicion). None is called a scam. Good.

"Check" answers naming a way I already had: true for every item except two where the context never gives me the way:
check 19 (the Marrowbeck app: I am told only that Marrowbeck is my phone company) and check 21 (the number on my
appointment card: no card is mentioned). Q2's "the account the code is for" names no account. Q5/Q6 rely on the worked
example for the app.

## After

- When a real-looking bank or phone-company alert arrives with a link or number, I go in through my own app or the
  number on my card instead, even if it may be real (Q5, Q6, Q13; check 19, 20).
- New bank details sent in a message: I confirm on the number I already had before paying (Q8).
- A "boss" or "grandchild" on a new number asking for gift cards or money: I call the number I already have (Q10;
  check 25).
- A caller from "the bank" wanting a code they just texted: I hang up and call the card's number (Q14).
- Someone I know only online asking for money: no, and I tell someone I know (rule screen, Q15; check 27).
- A parcel fee or address request riding on an order I am expecting: I look at the shop's order page (Q4; check 22).

## The five answers

- **Gain: yes.** Checking a real-looking alert through my own app or card number, never the message's link or number,
  even when it might be real. I had none of it before.
- **Doing: yes.** Two short reading screens (rule, worked example); the rest is 15 practice messages and 12 check
  messages, each a message as it arrives and a choice.
- **Check: yes, with notes.** Twelve new messages in arrival form, about half real, on a clock, nothing shown until the
  end, scored by the action. Notes: several are near-copies of practice items (check 20 is Q13 with a new amount; check
  22 is Q4 with "address" for "fee"), so transfer is near. The part row names pop-ups, and the check has none. No call
  or chat in the check pushes back.
- **Clear: no.** The places a beginner could not follow on their own: the rule's "do what it says when you arranged it"
  (scams riding on things I arranged; real messages that ask nothing); Q2 and Q9 marking "Check first" wrong while the
  rule says check; Q3, Q11 and the check's dentist call marking "Do what it says" wrong on a real message that asks
  nothing; Q2's unnamed account; Ioana in the check, where choosing "Check first, a way I already had" is counted under
  the shown rule "checked through the link, number or reply the message gave", which the learner did not do.
- **Waste:** Q12 repeats Q1 (a code or reset you asked for). Q3 and Q11 teach the same thing. Either one of each
  could go without losing the gain.

## Fixes needed (blocking)

1. **Real messages that ask nothing (Q3, Q11, check 21 dentist).** "Do what it says" and "Leave it" are the same
   action when the message asks nothing, and the rule says to do what it says on what you arranged. Score both right
   (with check), or reword the dentist call so "do what it says" is plainly a different act (it now reads as "only call
   if Friday does not work", which a learner following it does by not calling). As it stands, following the rule spends
   the check's one allowed miss on a real message.
2. **The rule's choice line.** Say what the scored answers already assume: what you started is the request you
   expected, through the way you already use (the code on the page you are on, the bill amount in your app). A payment,
   code or details asked through the message's own link, number or new account is not yours, even when it rides on an
   order, job, bill or listing of yours. Change "Leave it when nothing of yours is involved" so it covers the items
   scored "leave" (nothing asked of you; or a pop-up).
3. **"Check first" on Q2 and Q9.** The rule says check when you did not start it and have a way of your own, and both
   items name that way as the right "how". Either score "Check first" right through that way (the packet's pass rule
   only fails acting and the message's way), or change the rule so it says when leaving beats checking. Q2 must name
   the account the code is for.

## Fixes needed (not blocking)

- Check 19: say I have the Marrowbeck app. Check 21: say the appointment card (or a saved number). Q5/Q6: say the
  Calderwood app is on my phone.
- Check 27 (Ioana): choosing "Check first" is counted as "checked through the link, number or reply the message gave".
  Either add a shown pass rule for it ("sent nothing to someone known only online") or count it as a plain miss, so
  the result names what the learner actually did.
- Learner view: drop "A call is not on a clock" for a check whose calls are transcripts (they are timed in the app).
- Consider 25 to 30 s for the longest messages, or shorten them, so a real message is not lost to reading time.
- Put one pop-up in the check (the part row names pop-ups).
