# scams: design record (build-subject skill)

Written 2026-10-10. Gates 2 and 3 are drafts waiting for the owner's approval; until then V69 keeps every lesson of this
subject as it is. The content that exists predates the gates and is audited against this record (gates 4 and 5, designed
blind) before anything is kept.

Approved by the owner: 2026-10-10, "approve all, keep going" (gates 2, 3 and the pilot, for every subject; the pilot is still to be tried on a phone, and what it shows comes back into this record).

Note on the approval (2026-10-10, gates 1 to 5 pass): the approval above was given to the first draft of gates 2 and 3.
The blind design below keeps that draft's sense and sharpens it in three places, which the owner should confirm in one
line: (1) the end result adds "if something already went out, you make the first calls at once"; (2) the test is scored
by the action taken, includes live calls that push back, has a time limit and a four-week retest; (3) the practice adds
live pushing exchanges and consequence feedback. Nothing is removed from the approved draft.

## Gate 1. Kind of learning and evidence

**Kinds.** Two, both needed in the same few seconds:
- *Judging situations*: seeing what a message asks you to do, and whether that request needs checking (is it about
  money, a login or code, personal details, control of your device; did you start it).
- *A behavior under pressure* (habit): not doing it from the message, and checking through your own channel instead,
  when the message is urgent, frightening or exciting and you are busy. Knowing the rule is not the hard part; doing it
  on autopilot is (SC3, SC4).
- A small *procedure*: what to do in the first hour if money, a code or a password already went out.

Not a classification task. The learner never needs to name what kind of scam something is; the decision is the same
for all of them (SC1).

**Evidence.** `docs/research/scams-judging-and-acting.md` (SC1 to SC9, gathered for this gate), with
`docs/research/review-after-lessons.md` (R1 to R9) and the P26 entry of `docs/learning-science.md` (its sources, not its
"diagnostic classification" framing).

**Principles that drive the design.**
1. **Practice the decision, not the label.** The learner answers "what would you do?" with the real actions, not "is it
   a scam?"; people are better at the first and it is the one that matters (SC1; R8, Pan & Rickard 2018).
2. **The request is the trigger.** Train one cue: a request that touches money, a login or code, personal details or
   your device, which you did not just start yourself. Experts become suspicious at the request (SC2); a habit needs a
   clear cue (SC4, SC9).
3. **One short attention routine, not a list of red flags.** What does it want; did I start this; how would I check it
   myself. Attention training beat rule training in the field (SC6); how a message looks can be faked, so looks are
   never the test.
4. **Almost no reading.** Field training that people read barely moved behavior, and most did not read it (SC3).
   Reading is the minimum to start practicing; every lesson is mostly deciding.
5. **Real form, real pressure.** Messages look and arrive as they do on a phone (text, email, pop-up, chat, call), with
   urgency, fear and excitement, and live exchanges push back; a time limit keeps the learner near autopilot speed
   (SC5, SC7; R8). Timed practice transferring to real pressure is a judgement call.
6. **Discrimination, not suspicion.** About half of every set is genuine, including genuine messages that look alarming;
   checking a genuine message is scored as right and shown to be cheap; acting on a scam is the failure (SC8, P26;
   Burke et al. 2022).
7. **Feedback after every decision, as the consequence.** What would have happened next, for the action chosen and for
   the right one (R7; SC7's weakened exposure shows the tactic working).
8. **Mixed from the start.** Scam and genuine, every channel and every kind of request, in an order the learner cannot
   predict (R9; P22's strongest case is telling confusable things apart).
9. **Reminders are part of the treatment.** The app brings new messages back at about a day, a week, three to four
   weeks, then every couple of months; without a reminder the effect is gone in months (SC3, SC8; R2 to R5).
10. **One rehearsed if-then plan, cued by the request** (SC9; P26 requires 4). Optional to write, but rehearsed in
    practice; never a substitute for practice.

## Gate 2. End result

- **After this subject you can**, when a text, email, call, chat or pop-up asks you to do something with money, a login
  or code, personal details or your device, see that request, hold off, and check it through a number, app or address
  you already had before doing anything, while still handling genuine messages normally; and if something already went
  out, you make the first calls at once.
- **Real moment:** a message on your phone or computer, often urgent, often polished, usually while you are busy; or a
  live call or chat that keeps pushing.
- **Real-world test:** twelve new messages in the forms they arrive in (texts, emails, pop-ups, a marketplace chat, and
  at least two live calls or chats that push back), about half genuine, about 20 seconds each. For each the learner
  picks what they would do from the real actions on screen: do what it asks (tap the link, call the number given, pay,
  read out the code, install), reply, check through a channel they already had (and which one), or leave it. Pass: no
  scam acted on; every check goes through a channel the learner already had, never one the message gave; at most one
  genuine message mishandled. **Scored by the action, never by whether the message turned out real:** checking a genuine
  bank alert through the bank app is right; using a reset code you just asked for on the site you are on is right;
  tapping the link in a genuine-but-unexpected bank text is a wrong habit even though it did no harm this time. Plus one
  "it already happened" situation: the first step named is the right one. Taken before the subject (baseline), at its
  end, and again with new messages about four weeks later without warning.
- **Out:** naming scam types or tactics as a goal; reading links, headers and sender addresses (unreliable for a lay
  reader, and replaced by checking yourself); judging investments and "too good to be true" business offers (Wealth
  Preservation); setting up technical protections beyond keeping your own channels to hand; the law and how scammers
  are caught.

If the learner did only this test well, would they be safe in the real moment? Yes, as far as a test can show: it is the
real moment's input and the real moment's response, with pressure.

## Gate 3. Practice, and whether the app can deliver it

**Method.** The core is the real moment's own action: a message appears, the learner does something with it. Every
lesson's bulk is a stream of real-looking messages (and live exchanges) answered with real actions, mixed scam and
genuine, timed, with the consequence shown after each. Around it: a one-screen routine to start (principle 3), a short
real-life setup (save your own channels), one if-then plan rehearsed in practice, and returns over weeks.

**Time.** About 85% doing, 15% reading in every lesson. No lesson opens with more than two short screens of reading.

**Feedback.** From the app, immediately after each decision: what happens next if you did that (the fee page asking
for your card; the bank confirming in its app that the alert was genuine; the "courier" asking for the code), the
request named in the message's own words, and the one-line right move. In live exchanges, feedback comes at the end of
the exchange, with the pressure lines marked. The learner's results per channel and per kind of request are shown, and
genuine-message handling is shown beside scam handling (discrimination, not suspicion).

**Delivery check: what the app must do.**
| Needed | Status (checked in `public/app/lessons/` engine names, 2026-10-10) |
|---|---|
| Render a message as it arrives: SMS bubble with sender, email with display name and link text, pop-up, marketplace chat, call screen | Not seen; the engine renders text cases ("stories"). Build first. |
| Answer with actions, not labels: tap link / call the number given / pay / read the code / install / reply / check through my own channel / leave it; a channel picker for "check" | Not present; the engine asks key questions and a name (`ask.js` item types: case, tap, tell, reverse, claim, separator, fact, problem). Build first. |
| Live exchange: a scripted call or chat that branches on the learner's reply and escalates pressure | Not present. Build first. |
| Time limit per message | Not seen. Build first. |
| Consequence feedback, and scoring by action with the asymmetric rule (acting on a scam fails; checking genuine is right) | Not present; feedback is built around the key's route and name. Build first. |
| Per-item record and returns with new instances of the same request | Present in some form (drill requeue, weekly review in `review.js`); must be keyed on the request kind, not on a key outcome. Adapt. |
| A typed if-then plan, shown again at returns | Not seen. Build. |
| Audio for calls (a voice, not a transcript) | Audio playback exists (`audio-card.js`); needed only if low-baseline learners lag on calls (P26 requires 3). Later. |

The subject waits until the first five rows exist. No practice is swapped for the stories-and-key format because it is
already built.

If the learner did only this practice, would they be closer? Yes: it is the real decision, under its real conditions,
with its real consequence.

## Gate 4. Parts of the end result

The routine the parts build: **what does it want → does it touch money, a login or code, details, or my device → did I
start this, here, just now → if not, I go in through my own way, or leave it.**

| # | Part | Check (a small gate 2 test) |
|---|---|---|
| A | **See the request.** In any message, find what it wants you to do, and whether that touches money, a login or code, personal details or your device. Includes messages that want nothing risky. | 10 mixed messages, 15 s each: tap the line that asks, mark risky or not. Pass 9 of 10. |
| B | **Your own way in.** For each kind of sender (bank, card, courier, shop, employer or boss, family member, government, tech company, a platform you sell on), the channel you already had; why anything the message gives (link, number, "reply here", the caller's own transfer) does not count. Setup in real life: your bank's number saved from your card, the apps you use, a family check question. | 8 messages: pick the channel from options that include the message's own. Pass 8 of 8. Plus the setup done (self-reported). |
| C | **Act, check or leave, on mixed messages.** The whole routine on texts, emails, pop-ups and chats, about half genuine, including genuine alarming ones, scams riding on something you expected (the parcel you are waiting for), and requests you did start (a reset code you just asked for). Also the one case with no channel of your own: someone you know only online (weeks of friendly chat, a date, a "mentor" with a trading app) asking for money or an investment; the move is no, and tell someone you know in person. *(This case was added after reading the old units; the blind design missed it.)* | 12 new messages, 20 s each. Pass: no scam acted on, all checks through own channel, at most one genuine mishandled. |
| D | **Hold off when it pushes.** The same decision inside live calls and chats that escalate (urgency, fear, a loved one in trouble, authority, "don't hang up", "don't tell the bank", the caller already knowing your details, a "fraud team" asking you to move money), and when a message arrives mid-task. The move: end it, then call back on your own channel. | 3 new live exchanges: ends each and checks through own channel; never gives a code, a payment or remote access, whatever is said. Pass 3 of 3. |
| E | **Keep it.** One if-then plan cued by the request, rehearsed; new messages return over weeks. | The gate 2 test with new messages, about four weeks after the end, unannounced. Same bar. |
| F | **If it already went out.** The first steps in the first hour: money (your bank on its own number, now), a password or code (change it on the real site, then everywhere it was reused; tell the bank), remote access (disconnect, then the bank); then report. | 5 situations: pick the first step and order the next two. Pass: every first step right. |

Order: A and B are the pieces; C puts them together; D adds pressure; E keeps it; F is separate and last.

Map both ways: A → L1, B → L2, C → L3, D → L4, E → L5 and the returns, F → L6; the baseline (L0) is the gate 2 test
and serves measurement. If A to E pass, the gate 2 test passes (its twelve messages and live exchanges are C and D, its
channels are B, its four-week retest is E); F covers the end result's last clause and the test's "already happened"
item.

## Gate 5. Lessons

**L0. Before you start (baseline).** Part: measurement for all. Does: the gate 2 test, short form (8 messages, one live
exchange, one "already happened"), ~6 min, no teaching. Feedback: none per item; at the end, one line on how many
scams were acted on and how many genuine messages were left alone. End check: none (it is the baseline). Why:
principle 6 and P26 requires 3 (split later results by baseline). Scored by action only.

**L1. What does it want?** Part A. Does: one screen with the four kinds of risky request and one example each (~1 min);
then ~30 messages in their real form, mixed channels, about half wanting nothing risky (~9 min): tap the line that asks,
mark risky or not, 15 s each. Feedback: the request highlighted in the message's words, and why it is or is not risky.
End check: part A's check. Why: principles 2, 3, 8, 7. Only this? Yes: seeing the request is the trigger for everything
else.

**L2. Your own way in.** Part B. Does: one screen on why anything the message gives belongs to the sender (~1 min); ~20
messages: pick how you would check it, from options that include the message's link and number, the caller's offer to
transfer you, and your own channels (~7 min); then the real-life setup (~5 min, done on the phone itself: save the
bank's number from your card, check you have the apps, agree a check question with family). Feedback: where each option
would really take you. End check: part B's check, plus the setup marked done. Why: principles 2, 3; the setup turns the
check into one tap, which is what makes a habit possible (SC4). Only this? Yes.

**L3. Act, check or leave.** Part C. Does: one worked message shown deciding aloud through the routine (~1 min); ~35
messages, about half genuine, all channels, timed at 20 s, answered with real actions (~14 min). Built around pairs that
differ in one thing: the genuine reset code you asked for against the "read me the code" text; the genuine delivery
notice against the "pay a small fee" one; a genuine bank fraud alert (right move: check in the app) against a fake one
(same move). Feedback: the consequence of the chosen action and of the right one; for genuine messages, what checking
cost (a minute) and what it confirmed. End check: part C's check. Why: principles 1, 5, 6, 7, 8. Only this? Yes, it is
the end result's core on its own.

**L4. When it pushes.** Part D. Does: one screen naming the move "end it, call back on your own channel" (~1 min);
~6 live exchanges, branching, each 2 to 3 minutes, pressure rising as the learner hesitates (bank "fraud team",
grandchild in trouble, boss needing gift cards now, tech support pop-up with a number, a buyer on a marketplace sending a
"payment link", a courier call asking for the code, an online friend of several weeks who brings up money or a trading app, shown as a chat history then a live turn) (~14 min); then ~10 timed messages arriving over a distracting task
(~3 min). Feedback: at the end of each exchange, the lines that pushed marked, and where the learner could have ended
it. End check: part D's check. Why: principles 5, 7; SC5 (arousal), SC7 (weakened exposure, actively). Only this? Yes:
most losses happen in live exchanges, where knowing the rule is not enough.

**L5. Your plan.** Part E. Does: write one if-then plan in your own words, with the cue as the request (example cues
given, ~2 min); rehearse it on ~10 mixed timed messages and one live exchange, the plan shown before (~5 min); the app
schedules returns. Feedback: as L3 and L4. End check: the immediate part is the rehearsal set passing at part C's bar;
the real check is the four-week retest. Why: principles 9, 10. Only this? Partly: the plan alone does little; with the
returns it is what keeps C and D alive.

**Returns (not a lesson; the weekly review).** New messages and exchanges, mixed across all parts, never seen before,
at about 1 day, 1 week, 3 to 4 weeks, then every couple of months; misses come back in the same session until right.
Why: principle 9, R2 to R5.

**L6. If it already went out.** Part F. Does: one screen with the first-hour steps for money, a password or code, and
remote access (~2 min); ~8 short situations: pick the first step, then order the next two (~5 min). Feedback: why the
order matters (a bank can sometimes stop a payment only in the first hours; a reused password opens the other
accounts). End check: part F's check. Why: a short procedure, practiced on new situations with feedback (R7, R8). Only
this? Yes for its part; it is a small part of the end result, so it is a small lesson.

Rejections checked: every lesson's main activity is the part's real action; none is mostly reading (the most reading is
L6, ~2 of 7 minutes); every end check is its part's check. Total: about 75 minutes of lessons plus returns.

## Audit of the existing lessons (2026-10-10)

Read after gates 2 to 5 were written: `public/subjects/scams/*.js` (by way of the generated learner views) and
`docs/learner-view/scams-u1.md` to `scams-u6.md`.

| Unit | Verdict | Why (against the gate 4 map and gate 5 blocks) |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline (6 stories before U1) | **goes** | Asks "is something wrong here, or is it fine?" (a detection label, SC1), then reveals the answer by whether the story contains a request: the genuine tiler's invoice and Sofia's genuine reset code, typed into the page she opened herself, are shown as "**Something was wrong with it**". Known issue confirmed. It teaches suspicion of genuine messages and measures the wrong thing. Replace with L0 (gate 2 test, scored by action). |
| U1 Before you tap, call or pay | **changes** | Right part (A, with B's "a way you already had" and "the check"): its five requests are close to gate 4's routine. Wrong practice: 25 cards, then 19 drill items that all ask the learner to pick one of five labels for a third-person story ("Wes's laptop", "Isla is using..."); never what to do, no time limit, no real message form. Its own recap says "the answer is not a verdict", so the unit ends before the decision the end result needs. Content ideas carry into L1 and L2; the practice is rebuilt. |
| U2 Install, open a file, share your screen | **goes** | Serves no part as a unit. Its practice is a route plus a name (Malware, Real installation, Tech-support scam, Refund scam); naming the scam is out (gate 2). Device requests belong mixed into L3, and the tech-support pop-up and refund call become L4 live exchanges. |
| U3 Sign in, give a code, allow an app | **goes** | Same shape: route then a name (Phishing, One-time code scam, App permission scam, Real sign-in). | U5 Tell them about yourself | **goes** | Route then a name (Identity theft, Real request for details, Friendly chat from a stranger). The slow "friendly stranger" chat is a real L4 case; the naming serves no part. |
| U6 If it has already happened | **changes** | Right part (F), right content (call the bank on the card's number, at once, ask for a recall; change the password on the real site; report; keep the messages; the recovery offer that follows a loss). Rebuild as L6 (situations: first step, then order). |
| Key, specimens, "Name a story" determination | **goes** | The whole key-and-name route is a classification design; the end result needs no names (gate 1). |
| Returns on later days | **changes** | The schedule (about 2 days, a week, 3.5 weeks) fits principle 9; what returns is a naming question, not a new message to act on. |
| Plan cards (one per unit) | **changes** | The idea is right (principle 10); five optional cards become one plan, rehearsed in L5 and shown at returns. |

**Overall verdict: rebuild from scratch.** The subject's content knowledge is largely right and worth mining (the five
requests, "a way you already had", "did you start this", the first-hour steps, many realistic scenarios), but the
practice is the wrong kind everywhere: it classifies and names, where the end result is a decision and a behavior. No
patch to the existing units turns naming into acting; the engine's item types cannot ask it (gate 3 delivery check).

**The three most important problems.**
1. **The learner never practices the real action.** In U1 to U5, every drill item asks for a label and ends with "Name
   it" (about twenty named kinds of scam); no item in any of those units asks what you would do with the message. The
   decision, act, check through your own channel, or leave it, is described in cards and never practiced. Evidence says
   the decision is the task that matters and that knowing about scams barely changes what people do (SC1, SC3).
2. **It teaches suspicion where it means to teach discrimination.** The baseline marks a genuine invoice and a genuine
   reset code "Something was wrong with it", because it scores the presence of a request, not the right action. Genuine
   cases are a minority of the named drill answers (8 of 34 across U2 to U5; 2 of 14 in U4). The right lesson, "checking
   a genuine message is right and cheap", is never shown (principle 6, P26).
3. **Mostly reading, in the wrong form, with no pressure.** Each unit has 18 to 37 cards before its drill; cases are
   third-person stories ("Mira ordered a coat") rather than messages to you in the form they arrive; there is no live
   call or chat that pushes, no time limit, and no consequence shown. That is the format the field studies found does
   not move behavior, and it skips where people actually fail: urgency, emotion and a live voice (SC3, SC4, SC5, SC7).
