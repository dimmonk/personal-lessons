# Scams & Social Engineering: plan (2026-10-22)

Research: `docs/research/scams.md` (S1 to S22); general findings `docs/learning-science.md` (P), `docs/research/designing-a-subject.md` (D),

## After this you can

**…pick the right action, and do it, when a text, email, pop-up, chat or call wants something from you (money, a sign-in or
code, your details, or your phone or computer), often urgent, looking real and arriving while you are busy: do it when you
started it, check first through a way you already had when it involves something of yours, leave or end it when it does
not, while handling real messages normally; and if something already went out, make the right first call at once.**

**Real-world test** (the pass mark is ours, set from S12 and P26): 12 new messages in their real form, half genuine, 20 s each,
plus two live calls (one that pushes, one you placed yourself, where staying with it is right) and one "it already happened". Pass:
no scam acted on; every check through a way you already had, never the one the message gave; at most one genuine message mishandled;
the call that pushes ended without giving a code, money or access; the first step after a loss right (lesson 12's check; final round,
2026-10-22, matching the lessons as built). Taken before (lesson 0, built), at the end, and again unannounced
four weeks later (S19, R4).

## The way of thinking: the rule (S1 to S16, S23 to S30)

**Changed 2026-10-22, after the two reviews of lessons 1 to 6** (`review-learner-l1-6.md`, `review-research-l1-6.md`): the rule below replaces the
five loose lines that stood here. The lessons had read them three ways: whether a real message that wants nothing is checked or left, what "something
of mine" means, and whether money a brother or friend asks for is done or checked. It is now one rule, stated once, and **every lesson (0 to 12)
applies it word for word**: each item's right answer is the one this rule gives, and no other option is right. Lessons 7 to 12 are built on it.

1. **What does it want?** Money, a sign-in or a code, your details, or your phone or computer. If it wants none of these (a receipt, a shipping
   notice, "no action needed"), there is nothing to decide: you read it and go on. How it looks proves nothing: polish, logos, caller ID, the padlock,
   knowing your name (S1, S3, S9). *Messages that want nothing are asked only in lesson 1 (what does it want?); they are never an act, check or leave
   question, because doing what they say and leaving them are the same thing.*
2. **Did I start it?** It answers something I just did myself, through my own way (I tapped "Text me a code", "Forgot password" or "Scan"; I called
   the number on my bill and asked for a call back; I asked my sister for the file), or it is exactly what I agreed beforehand (the amount, or how it is worked out, and the way
   to pay: the quote's account, the payment I promised my brother at dinner). Then **do what it says**; checking is not needed. Calling a number a pop-up
   or text gave me is not starting it (S2, S3: "a request that you didn't expect"; S4).
3. **If I did not start it: do I have an account with them, or know the person?** The name it uses decides: my bank, my phone company, a shop I have
   ordered from, my boss, my grandson, my friend. Then **check first, through a way I already had**: their app, the number on my card or bill, an address
   I type myself, the number I have saved, the family's secret word. Never the link, number, reply, transfer or caller ID it gives. This holds when the
   message is real (a real alert is checked like a fake one), and it holds for family and friends asking for money they had not agreed with me (S2, S4,
   S5, S23: "Use a phone number you know is right to call or message the family member").
   *Round 2, 2026-10-22:* **a government agency counts as one I deal with** (Social Security, the IRS, Medicare, my county, the police): it has my
   records. So a caller or message in an agency's name is checked: hang up, then call the agency "at a number you know is correct", from a letter or
   card it sent, or from its own website at an address I type (S31). No agency calls to ask for money or personal information, and none wants gift
   cards, crypto, a wire or a payment app (S31), so paying the caller is never right. **No card, bill or letter?** The company's own app from my app
   store, or the address on an earlier statement (S4, CISA: "check previous statements"); a web search is not a way I already had, because scammers
   buy the top results (S28). **Family:** the call to the number I have beats a spoofed text; when they answer on that number, in their own voice, and the story
   fits, that settles it; if anything is off, also call someone else in the family (S23), or ask for the family word (S4).
4. **If I have no account with them and do not know them, leave it**: delete it, close the page, hang up, stop replying (S2: "If not, report and
   delete it"). Someone known only online (*final round, 2026-10-22:* someone I have been talking with online, a friend, a match, a contact from a
   forum or a game, never met in person; not a stranger I deal with once, such as a buyer) does not count as someone I know: everything I could check them by came from them, and a friend in common met
   online changes nothing (S27: "Do not invest per the advice of someone you meet solely online"; S11 romance). When such a person wants money, leaving it
   comes with one more move: **tell someone I know in person** (S3, S15).
5. **Some requests settle it is a scam**: a code read back to anyone, a "safe account", gift cards, crypto or a wire, a fee to get a prize, loan, job or
   lost money, money asked of you by someone met only online, a check to deposit and send part of, secrecy, an order to stay on the line (S3, S11, S24 to S26). They
   never make "do what it says" right; the action is still step 3 or 4 (check with the real one your own way, or leave it). Ending a contact is free;
   honest senders give you time (S3, S10).
6. **If something already went out, speed decides**: the bank or payment company on its own number first (S13; lesson 12).

How the three actions part, so that one is right: started it → do it (step 2 comes before 3); not started and an account or a person I know → check;
neither → leave. A safe but wrong action (checking a code I just asked for, leaving a real alert from my bank) is marked wrong with feedback that names
the step that decided, because over-suspicion of real messages is the cost training is known to bring (S20).

**Round 2 (2026-10-22), how questions are written so the rule, not a cue, decides:** the situation says only what the learner would know (what they
did, which accounts they hold, who is in their phone), never "you did not start this"; real messages do not always echo the learner ("as you asked")
and some scams do; every "How do you check?" option is worded as something the learner would do, so "my" is never the tell, and the third option is a
look-alike of a way you had (the number the phone shows, a number found by searching), never a second copy of the message's own channel.

**Reviewed 2026-10-22:** all twelve lessons and the baseline passed the final learner and research reviews (`review-final.md`), so every
Scams lesson is now `live`.

## The lessons

Every lesson: the way of thinking first (one screen, P9), one worked case (P10), contrast pairs real against copy (P12, S20), then mixed
timed practice, about half genuine (P26 requires 1), feedback naming what decided and the consequence (R7, S12), then a check of new
cases. Forms are this app's (lesson-standard 3.6, 3.9): the `action` verdict list (`choose`), then a second `choose` "which way" opened
by `when` on "check"; `tap` on the line that asks; `message` blocks (sms, email, popup, chat, call, banner); `pair`; `exchange`; `order`;
timed sets (`seconds: 20`) and `over: 'busy'`; private forms (`own`: setup, plan); retest. Strands one per lesson, mixed in the review.

**Unit 1. The move** (S1 to S5, S12). L1 asks only what a message wants (messages that want nothing belong here); L2 asks only how to check a
message that the rule says to check; L3 asks the whole rule.
- **L1 What does it want?** The five kinds of request and "nothing", on texts, emails, pop-ups and calls. Practice: `tap` the line that asks,
  then `choose` the kind. Check: 10 new, 15 s, 9 right. Why: the request is the experts' trigger (S1); looks are not (S9).
- **L2 Your own way in.** Why anything the contact gives is theirs (the badge number, S5); for each sender, your way (app, card number,
  typed address, order page, family secret word). Practice: `choose` how you would check, options include the message's own link, number
  and "I'll transfer you", and a way that is neither yours nor the message's (a number a web search shows, S28). Then `own` setup: save the bank's
  number from the card, the apps, agree the family word in person (S4, FBI). Check: 8 new, 7 right and none through the message's channel (changed
  2026-10-22 from 8 of 8, which left no slip on a shuffle). Why: S4, S5.
- **L3 Act, check or leave.** Built (pilot); shape kept: the rule, worked text, three pairs, eight timed mixed. Changed 2026-10-22 (rev 2): its
  first screen is the rule above; its pairs are a code you asked for and one a stranger wants, a real shop you have an account with and a courier you
  have none with, a real alert and a fake one; the online-only contact is practised before the check. Check: the real-world bar on 12 new. Why: S2, S12, S20.

**The question forms that carry the rule** (all lessons): "What do you do?" (the three actions); "How do you check?" only after "check", and only on
a message the rule says to check (the way you had, what the message gave, and a third way that is neither); "What else do you do?" only after "leave",
only when someone known only online wants money (tell someone you know in person). Distractors are the real wrong moves the sources name (S4, S5, S28),
the right option's place moves, and the line that decides sits first, in the middle or last.

**Unit 2. Know the scripts** (S7, S10, S11, S16, S19). Each lesson: the scripts side by side with the real thing they copy (`pair`), each
with its tell and its "never" line (S11), the tactic lines marked in feedback (S7); then mixed timed messages with the actions; then one
short `exchange`. Check: 8 new, half genuine, the real-world bar, plus the exchange ended right.
- **L4 Your device**: tech-support pop-up with a number, refund call that wants your screen, a file or link in a message; real install.
- **L5 Your accounts**: copied sign-in page, the code read back, a sign-in approval you did not start, an app that wants your texts; the real
  sign-in, code, approval and camera permission you started (S26, S29, S30; changed 2026-10-22: the old web-page "Allow" cases could not happen).
- **L6 Money to strangers**: online friend with a trading app, online partner's emergency, fee to release a prize or loan or job, fee to
  recover lost money; the move with no channel of your own: no money, tell someone in person (S15).
- **L7 Bills and payments**: changed bank details on a real bill, a small fee by link, a fake official demanding gift cards or crypto now,
  the overpaying buyer; the real bill paid the usual way.
- **L8 About you**: identity grab, the friendly stranger's questions, the real request for details you started.

**Unit 3. Under pressure** (S6, S9, S19, S21, S22)
- **L9 What looks prove nothing.** The ten beliefs, each a `prose` block with `tone: 'wrong'`, then a real-looking case where the belief
  would mislead (polished scam, clumsy real bill). Practice and check: the action on 8 such cases, the real-world bar. Why: P29, S9.
- **L10 When it pushes.** The move "end it, call back on your own way", with an exit line to say (S14, S19). Six `exchange`s whose pressure
  rises when the learner holds off (bank "fraud team" with a safe account, grandchild with a cloned voice, boss wanting gift cards,
  courier asking for the code, marketplace buyer's link, online friend's investment), then 10 banners over the busy task (Distraction,
  S6). Check: 3 new exchanges, all ended without code, money or access. Why: S6, S10, S20.
- **L11 Your plan, and all of it.** `own` plan: one if-then cued by a request (examples given); then 12 mixed timed messages from every
  lesson and one exchange, the plan shown before. Check: the real-world test, `retest: 28`. Why: S22, S19, R2 to R4. Built 2026-10-22: the check is twelve messages and two calls (one that pushes, one you placed yourself, where staying is right); "it already happened" is lesson 12's check, since nothing may test what is not yet taught.

**Unit 4. If it already went out** (S13)
- **L12 The first hour.** One screen per loss: money (bank, payment app, wire company, gift card issuer, exchange: at once, ask to reverse),
  a password or code (change it on the real site, then where reused, two-step on), device access (disconnect, scan, change passwords from
  another device), your Social Security number (IdentityTheft.gov, freeze at all three bureaus, free); then report (ReportFraud.ftc.gov),
  keep every message, and the recovery offer that follows is a scam. Practice: 8 situations, `choose` the first step, then `order` the next
  two. Check: 5 new, every first step right. Why: S13; a procedure practised on new cases (R8). Built 2026-10-22: "the next two" are picked as a pair, not put in order, because the sources (S34, S35) give no order between them; half of each group is a loss from a real event (a stolen wallet, a leak), since every group holds about half real.

**Returns**: the review brings each strand back at 1, 7 and 24 days, then about every 60 days, with genuine cases in every set (S19, S20, R2).

**Engine gaps**: none needed. Known limits stay as lesson-standard 10.2 has them: calls are transcripts read by an on-device voice;
returns cannot notify without a server (calendar file); the setup is self-reported. The subject record's `parts` (A to F) must be
rewritten to these twelve lessons when the next lesson is built.
