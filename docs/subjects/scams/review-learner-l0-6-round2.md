# Learner review, round 2: scams l0 to l6

Reviewer: cold beginner (adult project manager). Read only docs/learner-view/scams-l0..l6.md. Answers covered first, then compared.

## L0 Before you start (baseline, not taught): PASS
No way of deciding is taught here, by design. I answered all 10 by instinct; nothing is graded.
1. Q4 (Penhallow "declined at Dunmore Gas") has "Leave it" feedback "Your card stays blocked until you notice", but the text never said the card is blocked. Fix: say "Your card may really have been declined."
2. Q9 and Q10 have no feedback until the end, which is fine, but Q10 "only the first step counts" is never stated before the answer screen. Say it in the prompt.

## L1 What does it want?: PASS
Way of deciding, in my words: read each line, find the one that asks me to do something, and sort it as money, sign-in or code, my details, my phone or computer, or nothing. How it looks proves nothing.
Check: 10/10 by reasoning.
1. Step 1 list is one run-on paragraph in the view ("Money. To pay... A sign-in or a code. To type your password..."). Five categories with a one-line example each need to be visually separate. Fix: five rows.
2. "A code is a kind of sign-in" is first said only in Item 3 feedback. Put it in Step 1, with "reading out a code".
3. Every item asks twice (tap the line, then pick the category). The tap already gives the answer away for "nothing", because the button is the only option. Fix: drop the tap on items where the right answer is "nothing", or ask category first.
4. "Nothing" items (1, 9, 11, 19) are guessable: no verb of request, no link, bland text. Add one "nothing" item that has a link or a deadline (a real "your parcel arrives Friday, track it here" with no ask).
5. Item 2 reason says "Same sender name as the last one", but the learner sees only the answer reason, not the earlier screen if the order is shuffled. Remove.
6. Items 3, 7 and 17 end with "which matters later". It reads as a cliffhanger and teaches nothing yet. Cut or say "next lesson: did you start it?".
7. Check Q16 has two defensible "what it wants" answers: "Download this app so I can see your screen" is phone or computer, but the second line "I will fix your phone" is also read as money by some. Low risk, noted only.

## L2 Your own way in: FAIL
Way of deciding, in my words: anything that comes with the message (link, number, reply, badge, "I'll transfer you") belongs to the sender, so check using something I had before the message: the app, the number on my card or bill, a saved number, an address I type.
Check: 8/8, but 8/8 by elimination: in all 8 items the wrong options are literally "the link", "the number in the text", "reply", "search the web", and the right one is the only option that does not match. I could pass without understanding why.
1. Pattern: Check Q11-18 all have the right answer as the only option not from the message or a search. No item makes a different kind of choice. Fix: add items where the message-given route looks sensible (e.g. the number on your own bill differs from the text's) and one where two own ways exist and one is better (app versus a saved number).
2. Step 2 own-ways list is one run-on paragraph and its last two sentences are garbled: "A secret word you agree with your family, face to face. Someone who calls as family in trouble and cannot say it is not family." Unclear who cannot say what. Fix: "A caller who claims to be family in trouble must give the word; if he cannot, it is not family." Also the secret word is never used in any question.
3. "A web address you type yourself": how do I know the address? Worked example (county site) and Check Q11 ("patient portal, at the address I type myself") assume I know it. Say: from a bill, a card, or a bookmark made earlier; else search is not safe.
4. Step 1 says search results are not yours because of ads, but real searching for a bank's official number is something I'd do; the lesson never says what to do if I have no number on a card or bill (no card, new company). Gap.
5. Items 7 and 15 are "boss/landlord from new number/new address": the own way is the saved number. Item 15 also lists a number in the email. Fine, but Item 7's feedback "A new number can be anyone's" is only half taught.
6. Several wrong-option feedbacks end "This one may well be real, but you cannot tell that from here." That is the right idea but is said 12 times; by the third time it is noise. Say it once in Step 1.
7. Step 6 "Nobody checks" tasks: fine, but "Done now" is not used afterwards; no item uses the saved number, the secret word or the bill number in a way that rewards having done them. Low priority.
8. Item 8 (Dunmore Gas autopay failed): the correct answer is "my account", but a beginner might equally defend "the app" if they have it. Say which own way exists in the stem (as Items 1, 6, 18 do).
9. Pass rule "none checked through the link, number or reply" is not explained anywhere before the check.

## L3 Act, check or leave: PASS with defects
Way of deciding, in my words: ask what it wants; did I start it (then do it); if not, do I have an account with them or know the person (then check my own way); if neither, leave it. If someone online wants money, also tell a person I know face to face.
Check: 12/12, but see defects 1 and 2.
1. The stem tells you the answer. Real items say "you just tapped", "you agreed", "calling back as you asked"; scam items say "you did not start", "you have never used". The learner is told the facts the rule needs, so the item tests lookup, not judgment. Real life never says "you did not start this". Fix: put the facts in the stem only where a real person would know them, and add items where they must be inferred (a delivery text when the stem only says you "ordered something recently").
2. Pattern: every real message has an echo of the learner's own action ("as we said", "as you asked", "you asked for this"). No real message lacks it, no scam has it. Fix: add a scam that fakes the echo ("as you asked, here is your refund link") and a real one without it.
3. Item 5 and Item 6 are the same text with a different link; they are intended as "same words, same move", but the learner sees two nearly identical screens in a row. Reads like a duplicate. State why in the feedback or space them.
4. Item 10 and Check Q23 (gift cards plus manager name / two-hour deadline): "Leave it" is also safe, and its feedback says "You are safe". The marked answer is "check first". Both are defensible. Fix: tell the learner why check beats leave when the name is someone you know (the real person may need to know their account is used), in Step 1 not just in feedback.
5. Check Q21 (Kestrel Dental asks for my member number by reply): the number it asks for is a detail, the dentist is mine, "check by number on appointment card" is right, but a reply to a dentist's text is common. A beginner will doubt. Fix: add a line to the stem showing something off (a new number, a link).
6. Item 15 and Q27 (online-only friends) require "tell someone in person". Good, but the rule says "Someone I know only online does not count". A friend of four months and five weeks is exactly what the learner feels is "someone I know". The lesson asserts it; one worked example of why would help. Step 1 gives it one clause.
7. Item 9 reason "A real security pop-up never tells you to call a number" is a taught-later fact stated as certain. Lesson 4 teaches it; here it appears first without support. Add it to the rule or soften.
8. Item 14's "Get put through to a manager" and Q25's "number that called me": first use of caller-ID spoofing in L3 is only in the wrong-answer feedback (L2 did introduce it, fine).
9. "Half the ones you get are real" (why): no data given; unsupported number in a learner-facing line. Fix: "Many are real."
10. Check Q26: "My Lumenfall account on my TV" is the right route; a beginner may not know a TV is an account route. Minor.

## L4 Your device: PASS with defects
Way of deciding, in my words: a pop-up that tells me to call, a caller who wants to see my screen, or a file or app I did not ask for is a way into my device; open or install only what I requested; unrequested files from someone I know are checked on their saved number; strangers are left.
Check: 9/9.
1. Pattern from L3 repeats: real items say "you asked", "you just tapped"; scams say "never heard of". Same fix as L3 defect 1 and 2.
2. Item 7 (mug shop, "Install our tracking app") and Check Q19 (credit union "new version of our app, download at link"): "Leave it" is also defensible and safer, and the check route "app store opened myself" for Q19 is really "update through the store", not a check. Mark one as the lesson wants and say why check beats leave (the account may show a real delay).
3. Step 2 table: first cell says "Open a website so I can see your screen" but "press Allow" is never explained (what Allow does). Explain once: it hands over your screen.
4. Item 12 and Q23: a link from a friend after I asked is fine, but Step 3 says accounts get taken over. A chat link from Dana "as you asked" with a link is the same risk. The lesson never says why a requested file is safe if the sender's account was taken. Add one line: the thing you asked for, in the form you expected, is safe enough.
5. Item 15 and Q24 teach a good thing (hang up) but the only wrong replies are obvious ("Press Allow"); the "question why" reply is a middle option that always continues. Pattern: the continue option is always the middle one in position 2-3. Randomise.
6. Q24 stem "You called it" with a pop-up number: the lesson's Step 1 line "Calling the number a pop-up gave you is not starting it yourself" is excellent but only appears in the feedback of this last item. Move it to the stem or Step 1 teaching (it is there). Fine.
7. "Real tech companies won't call you first" is stated as absolute (Step 2, Item 9). In practice some do, for accounts you hold. It is protective; tone it ("almost never").

## L5 Your accounts: PASS with defects
Way of deciding, in my words: a page I reach from a message can copy my sign-in; a code is only for me to type on a page I opened; an approval pop-up is fine only right after I typed my password; an app permission is fine only if it answers what I just tapped.
Check: 9/9.
1. Items 5 and 10 and Q19 use "Check first" for what is really a different action ("Deny, then change my password"; "reset my password in the app"). The label says check but the action is to fix. Beginners will pick "Leave it" because deny is a leaving. Fix: add a fourth outcome in the how-to or relabel the second question "What do you do about it?".
2. Item 5 (3 AM approve): "Leave it" feedback says "You are safe tonight", and "Deny" is arguably leaving it. Both defensible. Same fix.
3. "Approve" pop-ups (MFA) are unexplained until Step 3; the first two sentences do not say what "Approve" does. One line: "Approve lets whoever typed your password in."
4. Step 4 and Items 13/14: "Allow when it answers what you just tapped" is a fair rule but camera for "Scan a receipt" is a given. Add a case where the permission does not match (a flashlight app asks for contacts).
5. Item 15 and Q24 teach well. Item 15 reason "A real buyer pays without a code from your phone" is asserted; the Step 5 worked example explains. Fine.
6. Q23 (new phone, old phone shows approve): correct, but the stem says "you just typed", telling you the answer again (see L3 defect 1).
7. Item 11 / Q22 "sign in with your email password" in a video player: good items.

## L6 Money to strangers: PASS with defects
Way of deciding, in my words: money to someone I know only online is never safe, even if they have photos or a mutual online friend; I send nothing and tell a person I know face to face. A fee to get a prize, loan, job or lost money back is the scam. Someone I know who asks for something not agreed gets a call on their saved number.
Check: 9/9.
1. Items 5 and Q18 and Q19: "leave it" is the answer for every offer from a stranger; the only variation is the story. Pattern is complete: if I do not know them and they ask for money, leave. Add one stranger offer that is real but unrequested (a bank's rate letter) so "real or not" has a test.
2. Item 5: "You posted your resume... never heard of Harbor Staffing" says leave, but a recruiter finding your resume is normal. The scam is the check, not the contact. Reason says that; the stem hides it. Make the stem show the odd request earlier.
3. Item 6 (staff app asks bank account number): real, marked "do what it says"; a beginner may fear giving a bank number. The reason ("you opened it yourself") is fine; add that an employer needs it only inside their app.
4. Step 2 mentions ReportFraud.ftc.gov only in Step 3; Step 3's "Neither one charges you" good. "Crypto wallet", "trading app" are unexplained (Q14). One line.
5. "Tell someone I know in person" is correct, but the cost to the learner of the second question (the "What else") is high: the wrong options feel like the good-sounding ones ("keep chatting, send nothing"), and I picked it correctly only because "tell someone" is the only action. Pattern: right answer is always the only option that is a different kind of step. Fix: add a wrong "tell someone" option that is the wrong person (the sender's friend).
6. Q14 shows a mutual forum friend: good, and taught.
7. Item 13 / Q22 chat: "Tell him you want a video call first" is a continue option and always leads on; fine.

## Verdicts
- L0: PASS (2 minor)
- L1: PASS (7)
- L2: FAIL (9; elimination pattern, garbled own-ways list, no way to find an address)
- L3: PASS (10; stem gives the answer, echo pattern)
- L4: PASS (7)
- L5: PASS (7; "check first" mislabels deny/reset)
- L6: PASS (7)

Defects by kind: pattern/answerable-without-reasoning 8, two defensible answers 5, unexplained word or step 10, garbled or missing teaching 5, unsupported claim 3, feedback repetition or clarity 5.
