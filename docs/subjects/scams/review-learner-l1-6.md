## The way of deciding, in my own words (written before the practice)
- L1: Ignore how it looks; find the sentence that asks for something (money, a code/sign-in, details, my device) or none.
- L2: Anything that comes with the message (link, number, reply, "transfer you") belongs to the sender; check through something I had before: the app, the card number, a typed address, a saved number.
- L3: Three questions: what does it want, did I start it just now here, do I have my own way. Started it: do it. Not started and I have a way: check. Nothing of mine involved: leave.
- L4-L6: the same rule applied to devices, accounts, money. I could state it each time.

## Defects

### L1
1. Item 1-10 and check: the wanting line is the last or second line in nearly every scam item, and the "none of them" button is right only on short two-line texts. I could answer by position. Fix: vary line position, add a scam whose ask is in line 1 and a real one with a long second line.
2. Step 2 worked example: "Verify your identity" is answered "a sign-in or a code". A beginner could equally say "your details" (identity). Fix: change the sentence to "Sign in at the link" or accept both and teach it.
3. Step 1 never says why a real bill (Item 5, check 13) "wants money" yet is not a problem; the feedback leans on lessons not yet taught. Fine as a naming exercise, but add one line: naming comes first, judging later.

### L2
4. Items 1-10 and check: the right option is always "the thing I had before"; every option that mentions the message is wrong. Pattern solves it with no reasoning. Fix: add items where an option is a plausible own channel that is not (a number found by searching the web, a number from a past text) and an item where the sender-given channel is fine because nothing is at stake.
5. Item 4 / check 12 (real shipping notice): marked "check your own way". L3 Q3 and Q11 then say "leave it" for the same kind of message. Direct contradiction between lessons (see 7).
6. Step 6 "Done now" tasks: "secret word with family" is never taught how or why. Fix: one worked line, or drop.

### L3
7. Q3, Q11, check 20 (dental), check 15/16: "leave it" is right for real messages that want nothing, but L2 taught "check even a real shipping notice". On Q3 I chose check (consistent with L2) and was told that was wrong. Fix: L2 must say real wants-nothing messages are left alone, or L3 rule 3 must say when checking is skipped.
8. Check 20 (dental call moving my appointment, "call us back if Friday does not work"): I did not start it, I have a way (appointment card), so rule says check. Marked "leave". Two defensible. Fix: make the call ask nothing and need no reply, or mark check.
9. Q9 and every "Leave it" with a second "How do you check?" (antivirus, a shop I know): leave vs check turns on "is there something of mine", but a pop-up claims my computer is involved. Step 1 says "leave when nothing of yours is involved", which does not decide it. Fix: define "of mine" as an account or order I hold.
10. Every item: the "How do you check?" screen has the same two distractors ("reply and check with them", "the number/details in the message") with boilerplate feedback. The right option is the only one that differs in wording. Pattern answer. It is also unclear when this second question appears (after a correct "do what it says" it asks how to check, which is odd). Fix: say on screen when it appears; vary distractors.
11. Q26 romance chat: right answer "tell a friend I know in person" is not taught before L3 (first appears in L6 Step 1). Also "check first" is called wrong though a person I know is a way. Fix: move the stranger-online rule before L3 or take Q26 out.
12. Q7 feedback for "check first" ("You called Femi ... not needed") penalises checking, teaching that checking costs. Fine for balance but with defect 7 leaves the learner unsure.

### L4
13. Steps 1-3: "A real warning never tells you to call a number" and "A real company does not call you first about your computer" are absolute claims. A beginner will take them as the rule. Soften to "almost never" or tie to the pop-up case.
14. Item 1, 9, 13, 18, check 16: "Check first" is wrong ("nothing to check") but the right check answer lists "my phone's own settings" or "my security program" -- that is a way I already had. Self-contradicting feedback. Fix: for leave items, drop the second question or call it "what could you look at".
15. Item 4: caller returns my call and is marked right with no check on identity, after L2 taught caller ID can be faked. Arguable. Fix: say "you called the number on your bill" in the scenario.
16. Items 15 and 24 (call practice): "Say you will call your phone company yourself" and "Say you will check your computer first" are the correct action in words, but the call goes on and the learner is told later to end sooner. Both replies defensible. Fix: treat them as the right ending, or reword.
17. Item 5 / 11 / check 22: "a person I know on the number I have" is not taught for unknown senders in this lesson. Item 5 and 11 feedback blurs "leave it" and "ask a friend".

### L5
18. Check 18 (call, not expecting a parcel): "Leave it" is right, yet the right check answer is "my shop's order page" -- the feedback says there is nothing of mine, then offers a thing of mine. Contradiction. Fix: change to "my phone's own settings" or remove.
19. Item 3 (bank call asking for a code): "check first" right; Step 4 and Item 15 (buyer asking for a code): "leave it" right. The same ask is answered two ways, depending on whether I hold an account. That is the rule but is only implied by Step 4 text "there is nothing to check". Fix: state once, in Step 2.
20. Check 23 (new phone signed in, "no action needed") is marked leave, but is word for word the opening of the L1 worked scam text. A learner who learned "new phone" = scam will hesitate. Fix: say why this one is safe (I just did it); the scenario does, the feedback should lead with it.
21. Same distractor boilerplate as 10. Also check Q24 call: "Say you need to check" continues the call; same as 16.

### L6
22. Item 2, Item 8, check 15, 21 (brother, sister, friend ask for $20-$60): "do what it says" although I did not start it. The L3 rule is "do it only when you started it". Contradiction. Also L3 Q10 and Q24 (boss, grandson) say check. The real difference (known person, usual number, small sum, usual chat) is never named as a rule. Fix: add a fourth line to the L3 rules or add a step here.
23. Item 7, check 20 (fund recovery): "Leave it" right, yet "my bank on the number on my card" is offered as the check. Same as 14 and 18.
24. Step 1: "everything you can reach comes from them" is stated, but Step 4's reasoning says "no friend in common"; a learner may think a shared friend would make it fine. Fix: say a mutual friend does not change it.
25. Item 6 (job offer I interviewed for, reply needed): right is "do what it says", but "reply to confirm" uses the sender's channel, which L2 says to avoid. Two lessons disagree. Fix: add "reply to the address you applied through" or use the app.
26. The subject goal says "if something already went out, you make the first calls at once". Nothing in L1-6 teaches this; "no money" is the only answer. A beginner who has already paid has nothing to do. Likely later lesson; confirm.

### Cross-cutting
27. Pass rule "at least 8 of 8" (L2 check) leaves no slack and on the shuffle one slip fails. Fix: 7 of 8 or add questions.
28. The "A wrong idea, marked wrong" pattern is used in L1 and L2 only; later lessons have no equivalent, so misconceptions from earlier lessons (checking real messages) are not re-corrected.
29. Names (Calderwood, Penhallow, Thistlewick, Quillfeather...) recur so heavily that I started using the name as a cue: Quillfeather and Delivery Notice are always scams. Fix: let a real message come from every sender name at least once.
