## The way of deciding, in my own words (written before the first practice question)

- l1: Read every line, find the one that asks me to do something, and name what it wants: money, a sign-in or code, my details, my
  phone or computer, or nothing. How it looks proves nothing.
- l2: Whatever arrives with the message (link, number, reply, badge, transfer, the caller ID) is the sender's. I check through
  something I had before: the app, the number on my card/bill/bottle, an address I type from a bill or letter, a saved number.
- l3: What does it want? Did I start it (what I remember, not what it claims)? If not, do I deal with them or know them? Started: do
  it. Deal with them: check my own way. Neither: leave it. Someone known only online counts as neither; if they want money, also tell
  someone I know in person.
- l4: A pop-up that tells me to call is never real; calling its number is not starting it. Open a file or install an app only when I
  asked for it; a known sender's unasked file is checked on their saved number.
- l5: A code is typed by me where I opened the page, never read to anyone. An Approve or codes I did not trigger: deny, change the
  password in the app opened myself. Allow a permission only when it answers what I just tapped.
- l6: Known only online = not known: send nothing, tell someone in person. Paying to get a prize, loan, job or lost money back is the
  trick. A known person asking for money I had not agreed: call their saved number.

I could state each one. No defect there.

## Defects that decide the verdict

**D1 (l6 Item 14, Rosa chat, turn 1 "Stop replying").** The feedback reads "Rosa was real and needed the money; the check had already
settled it." At turn 1 no check has happened, so the feedback tells me something false and does not say what decides (the rule: a
person I know, money not agreed, so I check; stopping is the "leave" a known name does not get). It shares one ending with turn 2's
"Stop replying anyway", where the line is true. Fix: give turn 1's "Stop replying" its own ending, e.g. "Rosa was real and needed the
money. She is someone you know, so a call to her saved number settles it; dropping it costs her, not a scammer."

**D2 (l6 Step 4 against Item 14 turn 2).** Step 4 teaches "Phones and chat accounts can be taken over, and voices copied. A call to the
number you have beats a text, but a stolen phone answers too" and "If still unsure, check with someone else in the family." Item 14
turn 2 then marks "Stop replying anyway" wrong once Rosa answers, and offers no "check with someone else in the family" option. A
beginner who applies the screen literally ("a stolen phone answers too", "voices copied") stalls between the two options and can be
marked wrong for following the lesson. Fix: make Step 4 say what the call settles ("When she answers on the number you have, in her own
voice, and the story fits, that settles it; if anything is off, check with someone else in the family"), or add the family-check option
at turn 2 as a second right path.
