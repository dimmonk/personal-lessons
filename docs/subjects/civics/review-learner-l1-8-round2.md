## The way of deciding, in my words (written before the first practice question)

- L1: Congress writes the laws, the president carries them out, the courts say what they mean; three so no part gets too strong.
- L2: each number comes with its reason (two senators per state for small states, two-year House terms to follow opinion, justices for life to be free of politics).
- L3: before blaming anyone, ask which level (country, state, city or county) and which part; money, war, treaties are federal only; schools, police, licences, land use are the states'.
- L4: find who the story says acted; name the level; name whether they wrote, carried out or judged. Ignore the president if he only spoke.
- L5: ask what lets them do it, from the table: Congress its listed powers, a state anything not federal-only, a city only what the state hands down, the president own powers and own offices, outside that what Congress said (allows, silent = not settled, forbids). "In court" only with a filed case.
- L6: of six checks, pick the one under way or about to happen now.
- L7: leave the page; open an outside report about who is behind it, or the original record. Take a story as it stands only with a checked source (or the original) and an ordinary claim.
- L8: the four questions in that order.

All eight could be stated after one read, so no defect on that test.

## Defects that decide the verdict

**D1 (L8, Check 12 "Mayor orders $500 tickets for sidewalk parking, starting tonight"; also practice Item 4 "President orders airlines to pay refunds").**
Two of the five answers can be argued either way from what was taught.
- *Take or check:* L7 taught "alarming or sudden" with a bank freeze and the end of Social Security checks. A mayor ticketing an already-banned
  practice in a paper I checked reads as ordinary; I answered "Take it as it stands" and was marked wrong ("alarming and sudden"). The only
  signal is "starting tonight" in the headline, and nothing taught me that a start date makes a story sudden. Item 4 (refunds "starting at
  midnight tonight") is the same call.
- *Theirs to do:* L5's table says of a city "Only what its state hands down. The story has to show the state law." This story shows no state
  law, only "A city rule already bans parking on sidewalks", and nothing says the city rule sets a $500 fine. By L5's own row I could not
  say "theirs"; the lesson never says what to answer when a local story shows no state law.
Because the check needs each story fully right and 5 of 6, one arguable sub-answer decides the result.
**Fix:** in L7 teach what makes a claim alarming or sudden with one middling case (a big change that takes effect at once against an everyday
one) and make L8's conflict stories plainly alarming; for Check 12 either say the city rule sets the $500 fine and that state law lets cities
ticket parking, or pick an act whose power the table settles. Add to L5's city row what a city's own rule means (carrying out its own rule
is theirs).
