# US Civics & History: plan (2026-10-22)

Evidence: `docs/research/civics.md` (C1 to C22). Learning principles: `docs/learning-science.md` (P ids). Lesson form: `docs/lesson-standard.md`.

## What you'll be able to do, and when
**After this you can** answer the citizenship interview's civics questions aloud from memory, and, reading a news story
about a US law, order or ruling, say who acted (level and branch), whether it was theirs to do, who can stop it, and
whether to take the story as it stands or check who is behind it first.

**The test.** *Interview:* 20 questions drawn from the 128, heard, no options, answered aloud then typed; **16 of 20** on
**two mocks at least a week apart** (the real bar is 12, C1; 16 and the second mock are ours). *News:* 6 new stories,
mixed federal, state and local, each with the four questions; **5 of 6 fully right** (ours).

## The way of thinking
1. **Two axes before anything else**: which level (federal, state, local) and which branch (writes the law, carries it out,
   judges it). The Constitution divides power twice (Madison's "double security", C5); the novice default is "the
   president did it", even for a local tax (C8).
2. **On what power?** Congress: its listed powers. States: what was not given to the federal government (10th Amendment).
   Local: what the state hands down. Clash: federal law wins (C6). The president or an agency: *what did Congress say?* A
   law that allows it is the strongest ground, silence is uncertain, acting against a law is the weakest (Jackson's three
   zones, still the Court's test, C7). "In court" only when the story says so.
3. **Who can stop it?** Veto and override, the courts, Congress changing the law, federal law over a state, the voters,
   an amendment (C5, C6).
4. **Who is telling me?** Judge a source by leaving it and seeing what others say about it, not by its look, its .org or its
   About page (fact checkers, C15, C18).
5. **History in its time**: each event placed in time and in its chain of cause and effect; a historical claim in the news
   asked where it comes from and from when (sourcing, contextualization, corroboration, C11, C12).
Knowing the facts comes before using them (C12, C13), and the interview facts are recalled, not recognised (C19).

## The lessons
Practice forms: **Recall** = a `text` ask (`accept`) under a `spoken` block, said aloud then typed, every accepted answer
shown at once. **Story** = an `article` (news) block, then `choose` asks on subject lists (`level`, `actor`, verdict
`theirs`/`not theirs`/`in court`, `stop`), a `tap` on the deciding line, `fact` naming the interview question it rests on;
slips name the novice errors (`president-default`, `look-not-source`). Facet `level`, `mix` equal.
