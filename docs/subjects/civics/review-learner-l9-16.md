Way of deciding, in my words, before the first practice question:
- L9: each idea explains a part of government I will be asked about; the Declaration broke from Britain, the Constitution built the government.
- L10: rights split into "everyone here" and "citizens only" (vote, run, jury); the four vote amendments run in order, each adding people.
- L11: place every event in time and next to its cause; the chain of taxes, protest, laws, Declaration, war holds the names.
- L12: same chain, plus separate what a paper says from what it did (the Emancipation Proclamation).
- L13: in a news story, find the line naming an older event, put it in its date, and name what led to it.
- L14: keep each war with its reason, each date with its event.
- L15: pair each symbol with what it stands for; Memorial Day (died) against Veterans Day (served).

## Defects that decide the verdict

1. **L13, check questions 5 to 7 and practice items 1 to 4: answerable by pattern.**
   - Part 1 ("tap the line that names something from long ago") is always the one sentence containing a year or a named old law. The other three are mundane (hours, tickets, lemonade). I tapped by looking for a year without reading for reasoning.
   - Part 2: "Louisiana Purchase" is a throwaway wrong option in 5 of the 8 questions (items 1 and 2's options, check 6 and 7, and item 3's neighbours in style). I learned to cross it out on sight.
   - The taught rule ("the cause came before it") only removes options dated later. Options dated earlier but unrelated (Stamp Act for the 1965 act, Britain's taxes for the 1964 amendment) need topic knowledge the lesson never states as a second rule. The feedback does say "nothing to do with it", but the Step 3 table does not tell me to match the topic.
   - Pass is 2 of 3 with four options on part 2, so a pattern-follower passes.
   - Fix: (a) add a second row to the Step 3 table: "Same subject as the old thing, and earlier"; (b) make at least some story lines without a year (a named ruling or law only) and put a year in a today-line, e.g. "the fee was set in 2024"; (c) vary the throwaway distractor and include earlier, same-field wrong causes (e.g. the 13th Amendment as a wrong cause for the 15th is wrong only by being a different step); (d) raise the check to at least 5 questions and require all or all but one.
2. **L11 check, 17 of 18 required.** Naming five original states and the five-person "name one thing" items each depend on exact phrase matching. I lost two on wording (Jefferson "bought Louisiana", Franklin "founded libraries" are accepted only as listed forms), and the "My answer means the same" route is the only rescue. A beginner who understood but phrased differently is sent back for a full retake. Fix: lower the pass to 16 of 18, or make the check a 12-question sample, since 17 of 18 on free typing is stricter than the real interview (12 of 20).

## Stalls (no verdict effect)

3. **L9 Step 1 and Q4/Q10:** "popular sovereignty", "social contract", "natural rights", "limited government" are accepted answers but never explained. A beginner can only say them by rote. Add one plain line each in Step 1, or note "say it as a phrase".
4. **L9 Q13 and Q14:** "Name two important ideas from both documents" and "documents that influenced the Constitution" are lists to memorize with no reasoning; the table gives them but not why. Acceptable for the interview, but the Step 2 table is the only teaching.
5. **L9, L10, L11, L12, L15 result screens:** every result says "Level of government (Federal beside State beside Local)". That facet means nothing for these lessons and confused me. Show it only on lessons where questions have a level.
6. **L10 Step 1 table:** "the right to bear arms" under "everyone living here" is also an accepted answer for the interview, but a newcomer will want to know whether non-citizens can in fact own guns. The lesson does not say; it just lists. Reword as "the interview's list says" or drop the claim of "right of everyone".
7. **L11 Step 3:** "Sugar Act", "Stamp Act", "Townshend Acts", "Boston Massacre", "Intolerable Acts" are named with one clause each, so Q4 (name one reason) is answered by recognising names, not understanding. The chain table helps; the ordering item (item 8) is the strongest question in the set and a good model.
8. **L11 accepted answers not taught:** Jefferson "First Secretary of State, founded University of Virginia, Virginia Statute", Washington "President of the Constitutional Convention", Hamilton "First Bank", "Aide to Washington" and Franklin "first free public libraries" are accepted but not in the Step 5 table. I would not know them, but any one suffices, so it does not matter.
9. **L12 Q2 "Spanish-American War":** accepted, taught only in a Reason line; the Step 1 table ends in 1865. A beginner meeting it first in feedback will be fine, but it is not previewed.
10. **L12 Step 3 versus item 5:** the table says the interview accepts "Lincoln freed the slaves" but the item's accepted list puts "Freed slaves in the Confederate states" alongside it. Item 5 is the best "claim versus its source" teaching in the set. Good.
11. **L14 Step 1:** Vietnam and Gulf War have only "In the 1900s" for a date; the ordering item cannot include them, so the lesson's "put it in its time" move is dropped for two of six wars. Add years (1955 to 1975; 1990 to 1991).
12. **L14 Q8 and Q14:** "Longest economic recession in modern history" and "fought to end racial discrimination" must be typed near-verbatim. A beginner who says "a time of very bad economy" or "gave equal rights to Black Americans" is marked wrong first time. The "My answer means the same" button exists, so this is a polish, but suggest a hint of the key words in the Step 2 table, which already has them.
13. **L14 Q12:** "Russia" is accepted for the Cold War rival, while the lesson says Soviet Union. Fine for the interview, but contradicts the lesson's own correct term; leave it, mention in feedback.
14. **L14 Allied/Axis/Central Powers:** appear in accepted answers for Q2 and Q3 and in the table quotes, never explained. A beginner would not know what "Allied Powers" means. Add a one-line gloss ("Allies: Britain, France, Russia, later the U.S.").
15. **L15 Q2:** "New Jersey" is an accepted answer for where the Statue of Liberty is, with no explanation (Liberty Island is in New Jersey waters). It reads as an error to a beginner. Add one line.
16. **L15 Q8 and Q9:** Memorial Day versus Veterans Day is taught well with a side-by-side; feedback on a wrong answer says the other holiday's meaning, which is the right feedback.
17. **L16 Step 1 and Step 2:** the check cannot start until my own senators and others are typed, which is a good habit, but the page says "Go on" is shut until I save. Beginners who live abroad or have no state yet (prospective applicants) are stuck. Offer "I'll do this later" with the own-answer questions left out, which the draw already supports.
18. **L16 teaching:** there is no teaching, only the draw, and the feedback does not tell me which earlier lesson to review for a miss. Add the lesson title to the result for each miss.

## What worked

- Every question gives the accepted answers in full, so I always saw what counted.
- "Name any one" Reason lines say so, and the "Not yet" button removes the guessing penalty.
- The chain ordering items (L10 item 5, L11 item 8, L12 item 11, L14 item 20) test the reasoning and cannot be guessed by position: the options are shuffled and the feedback gives the date.
- L13 item 4's feedback ("That came in 1865, after 1863, so it could not have led to it") tells me exactly what decided.
