## The way of deciding, in my words
Ask what the argument is about. If it is something checkable (a figure, a date, a notice), it is a fact: give the correction, even if the person is defending a side, because studies found corrections almost never make people believe the claim more. If the person is stating how they feel about a group or policy and waving off evidence, it is a view: tell one short true story about a real person, not another argument. If one sentence has both a checkable number and a feeling, split them and use each move on its own part. One angry reply is not proof of backfire.
