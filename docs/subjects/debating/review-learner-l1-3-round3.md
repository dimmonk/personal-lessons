# Learner review, round 3 (cold read, lessons 1-3)

## Lesson 1: What kind of conversation is this?

Way of deciding, in my words: ask what the talk started from, what each person wants, and what would end it. A doubted claim = persuading. A shared choice weighed for everyone = deciding together. Competing wants traded = bargaining. Nobody knows, all want to learn = finding out. One person lacks it, the other knows = asking. Lines aimed at the person, no point answered = quarrel. Loudness and topic don't count.

My answers before reading: Items 1-10: persuading, deciding, bargaining, quarrel, finding out, asking, persuading, deciding, (tap Hal's question) asking, (tap the Dad line) quarrel. Checks 11-18: persuading, deciding, bargaining, finding out, asking, quarrel, (tap Ana's "medal" line) quarrel, (tap Mina's "parallel park" line) quarrel. All right, none needed a second read of the scenario.

Defects:
1. Giveaway wording. "Nothing has to be decided" (Items 1 and 7) and "nothing is being chosen" (Check 11) hand over "persuading" and remove the work of ruling out deciding together.
2. Item 8 (siblings, house): Ana and Ben each state an option with a reason, so persuading was a live second answer until I read the feedback. Cal's "find out what it would cost" also pulls toward finding out.
3. Item 7 reuses the Step 3 brothers-shouting frame, so it is partly a memory check. "Read the stamp on the shingles" also looks like a plan to find out.
4. Check 13's wrong-answer feedback says "best for the shop", but the scenario never mentions a shop.
5. Unclear words: "bring the other round", "a point and a swipe", "let off steam".
6. Wrong-answer feedback in the view reads "a slip: by-topic" or "a slip: missed-shift". If that reaches the screen it is a placeholder, not feedback.
7. The Result section and "seed of the question" lines are spec text and mean nothing to a learner.
8. Step 3's "A wrong idea, marked wrong" box is slightly preachy. Otherwise the tone is plain.

Verdict: PASS (defects 1, 2 and 6 should be fixed).

## Lesson 2: Why people dig in, and what is a myth

Way of deciding, in my words: after a correction, expect a small move toward the facts, not a backfire and not a win, and expect the gain to fade. Stories moved views a little; arguments alone did not; careful listening added nothing to a story. A person defending a side treats your points as things to rebut, and side-tied topics get the numbers read the side's way. This is facts to remember. It does not give a way to decide a case.

My answers: Items 1-9 and Checks 10-17 were all right. Most were answered from the lesson text or by elimination.

Defects:
1. Guessable without reading. The right option is nearly always the hedged one ("a little", "often did not last", "at least four months"). The wrong ones are extremes ("large, for good", "nearly all", "twice as effective", "every correction"). Check 10 ("None of them" against "one in five / half / nearly all") and Items 6 and 9 are the clearest.
2. Item 6, option 4 ("lowered a little, but not when arguments were left out"). The lesson never says whether a story alone was tested, so I could not rule it out from the text. It is arguably not false.
3. The practice is recall of study results, not applying a way of deciding. Items 2, 4 and 7 and Checks 12, 14 and 17 ask the same fact two or three ways.
4. Step 1: "the trouble is the link between a group and a false claim" is unclear. Step 3: "Using the account on your own reasons is an inference, since it is about judging other people's arguments" needed three reads. "One account of reasoning" names nobody.
5. Jargon in the feedback with no explanation: "d = 0.08", "preregistered", "230 canvassers, 6,869 voters".
6. Textbook-like throughout. Hedge sentences ("Nothing here shows what happens between brothers") are repeated in the lesson, the examples and the feedback.

Verdict: FAIL (defects 1-3).

## Lesson 3: Your goal, and when to leave

Way of deciding, in my words: look at the last lines. If they answer your point, question or offer, keep going, even if blunt. If they dig at you and ignore your point, change the subject once. If they threaten or block you, you already changed the subject and they came back, or several people are going at you, leave (online, stop replying). Aim for a small step, not a win.

My answers: Items 1-9: keep (Ken), change (Rita), leave (second Dave line), keep (Kim), keep (Joyce), change (Vic), leave (tap Carrie and Bill), keep (Tom), leave (Marcus). Checks 10-17: keep, keep, keep, change, change, leave, leave, leave. All right, with the correct taps.

Defects:
1. Tone gives it away. A friendly, cooperative reply means keep going. A dig means change. A second dig means leave. I never had to weigh anything.
2. The line to tap is nearly always the other person's last line, so position alone gives it away (Items 1-6, 8, 10-17).
3. The feedback for Items 6 and 13 uses facts the rule does not use ("you will have lunch with Vic tomorrow", "you will see Gus at the mailbox again"). Step 2 says the move is the same however nasty the dig, so this looks like a second rule.
4. The "why" promises a fair goal and what to say. Practice tests only the move. The goal is never practised, and no item asks for or chooses wording.
5. Item 9 (Marcus blocks the door): "leave at once" is not possible while he is in the way. Step 3 says to move toward other people, but the item feedback doesn't say that, so the answer feels like a contradiction on first read.
6. Item 5 (the smell in the kitchen) is trivial "keep going", and the lesson's point about aiming small does not apply to it.
7. The pigeon-chess joke in Step 3 is padding.

Verdict: PASS, marginal (defects 1 and 3 should be fixed).
