# Learner review, Debating lessons 4 to 9 (cold read, beginner)

Verdicts: L4 PASS, L5 PASS, L6 FAIL, L7 FAIL, L8 FAIL, L9 PASS.

## L4 Character, emotion, argument (PASS)
Way of deciding: read their last reply and ask what it is about: you (trust), their feelings, or your reasons; two at once is allowed. If the argument failed, ask whether it was too long, began from something they reject, or the reply was about something else. Tone does not decide.
Blocking: none. Right answers look balanced across categories and positions. Notes:
- Chain-length is visible in my own message, so "too long" is guessable from the long chain alone. No long chain gets an unrelated reply.
- Check 9 (Reyes, "is the tree company paying you?") is a trust reply, filed as "something else". Step 5 never says that case.
- "levy", "ordinance", "stormwater" may be unfamiliar.

## L5 What a true story gets you (PASS)
Way of deciding: look at their reply. A shift in their own view is a small move. Warmth plus "I still hold my view" is a hearing. Off-topic, or arguing past the story, is neither. Separately, check each line of my story against the listed facts and cut what goes beyond them.
Blocking: none. The decoys work (wary yet moved; cheerful yet ignoring; "Sorry" about a phone). Notes:
- Item 9 line 1 ("Jo's son was bullied at the bus stop") stated flat, when I only heard it, is arguable.
- Every false line is an embellished motive, feeling or presence. Fine, but predictable.

## L6 Restate, then ask for the reason (FAIL)
Way of deciding: ask "would they keep the view if the feared result did not happen?" Yes means a line they will not cross. No means it rests on predicted results. A checkable number or date means give the correction. Restate only what they said.
Blocking:
- Form cue in the restatement items. "Nothing is added" is always a 3-line restatement (Item 2, Check 14). Items with an added line are 4 lines (Items 8, Check 13, 15, 16). Only Item 7 breaks it. Counting lines gets 6 of 7.
Notes:
- "Street epistemology", "canvassers" and "conspiracy thinking" are unexplained.
- No view-rests item mixes cues, such as a prediction that cites a number.

## L7 Which true experience to share (FAIL)
Way of deciding: pick the experience about the issue itself, then the one with harm, then the one that happened to me, over a statistic.
Blocking:
- Item 8 (cap on hospital bills): the "right" story has the hospital explaining the bill and me paying it off in a year. That reads as against my own side, so the study option is arguably better. A beginner will doubt it. Check 12 (dentist bills dropped) is similarly weak.
Notes:
- The right answer is often the longest or most causal ("lost his car and then his job").
- The me / relative / read-about set appears five times, and "you" is always right.

## L8 Replies that work against you (FAIL)
Way of deciding: check a draft for five faults. A line aimed at the person, too many steps, a start they reject, a story stretched past the facts, a restatement with something added.
Blocking:
- Tone cue. Every wrong reply is the rude or dramatic one ("Spare me", "only a fool", "drowning in bills", "lost everything", "you want cyclists off"). Every distractor is calm and cooperative ("I can send you the page", "look at it together", "Let us count"). The picks beat the rule without applying any of the five tests (except Items 6 and Check 16).
- Step 3 says blunt, warm, long or short replies can be fine. No item has a blunt-but-fine reply or a warm-but-bad one.

## L9 Checking your own side (PASS)
Way of deciding: write down the test I used on the source I doubt, then hold my favoured source to the same test. A "but, this one is different" is the change.
Blocking: none. Decoys exist: Item 4 and Check 15 favour the writer and are consistent. Notes:
- A note that ends in the writer's favour is usually the drop. The drop line is usually the third sentence and often carries "but", "just wanted" or "settles it". A learner using that gets 7 of 8 in the check.
- "axe to grind" and "rebut" are idioms.
