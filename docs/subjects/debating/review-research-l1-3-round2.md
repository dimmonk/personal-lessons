## Findings

1. **Check on your own reasons is "another person", but the lesson drops the condition the note sets.** The note (F3 Use) says people
   judge fairly "provided they have no axe to grind". Lesson 2 cq17 (debating-l2.md:281-290) makes the roommate she is about to argue
   with the right check; item 4 (debating-l2.md:99-106) and l2.lesson.js:33 state "people are good at judging other people's
   arguments" with no condition. An opponent has a side, and F4 and the biased-assimilation passage say that is exactly when judging
   goes wrong. The note itself marks this principle an inference; the graded answer treats it as settled.
2. **"Capable people defend it with skill" (l2.lesson.js:30; debating-l2.md:75, 258, 264).** Note F4 says the safe claim is only that
   numeracy did not stop the congruent reading, and that "more skill = more polarization" is contested and not to be said. The
   replication found no moderation by numeracy, so "defends it with all his skill" (cq15) asserts the contested part. Item 2's right
   answer has the same wording.
3. **"Arguments alone" is turned into "facts/numbers", and the stories result is stretched.** In Kalla & Broockman the arguments-only
   arm was canvassers' persuasive arguments on strangers, not numbers. Lesson 2 items 3, 8 and cq16 (debating-l2.md:84-93, 155-164,
   268-277) apply it to a brother on the phone, a friend, a neighbour, and make "stories move views more than arguments" a graded
   right answer from one arm (Exp 1) and d = 0.08. The note itself says it is not shown to work in families. Also "without judging"
   is taught as the active part: a later preregistered study (Santoro, Broockman, Kalla & Porat, PNAS 2025, abstract only) found
   high-quality non-judgmental listening did not add persuasion; the narrative did the work. Not in the note; the lesson's emphasis
   should be checked against it.
4. **A graded answer rests on an unsourced inference.** cq12 (debating-l2.md:216-225) marks "the fact moves her feeling about the
   vote less" as right. The reason line says it is an inference, but the lesson scores it, and no source in the note measures
   attitude versus belief on the same fact.
5. **Walton's table has more types than the lesson uses, and the cases fit the omitted one.** Table 2 includes Deliberation ("Act on a
   thoughtful basis") and Information-Seeking. Most lesson cases are decisions about what to do (speed bump debating-l1.md:68, sell
   the cabin :217, release date :270, chores, the boat, leaf blowers), which is Walton's deliberation, not persuasion about a
   disagreement over what is true. The note (research:198-199) says the other types "are not taught" and gives no reason. Also
   Walton's Debate row is "Air strongest arguments for both sides"; the lesson's "show" (heckling, a hands-up vote) is the
   lesson's own category (research:200-203 admits this for quarrel vs show only). l1.lesson.js:29 and subject.js:34 label it as
   Walton's kind.
6. **Mechanism asserted for single cases (l2.lesson.js:38; debating-l2.md:50, 246).** Nyhan's abstract ties fade to group
   identity in general; "his side pulled him back" and "her mother's group pulled her back" (cq14) are graded as right for one
   person. The note's F2 Use says only that Nyhan "ties that" to identity.
7. **l3.lesson.js:27 "The research points to another one" (stories) for a friendly person who waves away sources.** F5 has no arm
   with that kind of person, and the note says the effect is shown only for trained canvassers on strangers.
8. **Not read, unresolved departure risk:** couples/sibling "change the subject, come back later" (debating-l3.md Rita item,
   cq13). The note admits the couple-conflict literature (repair attempts, breaks) was not read; changing the subject can be
   avoidance there. Marked as a judgement call, not resolved.
9. **Unsourced additions in feedback:** "tell your manager if you need to" (debating-l3.md Item 9 reason); the step-out lines
   (marked at l3.lesson.js:28, acceptable); pass marks (marked in the note).
