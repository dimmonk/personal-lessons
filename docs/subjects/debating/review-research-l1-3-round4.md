# Research check, debating lessons 1-3, round 4

Verdict: **FAIL** (one finding a learner would act on). Spot-checked online: Wood & Porter 2019 (five experiments, 10,100+, 52 issues, no backfire, "precisely the kinds of polarized issues where backfire should be expected"), Kalla & Broockman 2020 (arguments alone no effect in Exp. 1; narrative exchange durable 4 months; Exp. 2-3 transgender replicate, 230 canvassers, 6,869 voters). Both match the note and lessons. Walton 2009, Santoro 2025, Mercier & Sperber rely on the note's quotes; nothing contradicted them.

## Fail

1. **L2 cause-to-move map contradicts the studies it cites** (l2.lesson.js "What the studies say to do"; items a2-ray, a2-church, c2-dan, c2-law, c2-pam; stem "Which move do the studies back?"). For a person defending a group's claim about a fact (Ray's cost estimate, Dan's contract number, Walt's county numbers), the key says "tell a story, not a correction". Wood & Porter tested exactly polarized, group-tied factual claims and found corrections still work "at least somewhat"; Nyhan says the same. Kalla & Broockman's null is on attitudes toward groups, not on beliefs about a fact. The note itself lists "an attitude-versus-belief split" as not claimed (research note section 4), yet the keyed answers rely on that split. The lesson's disclaimer covers only the topic, not this. Fix: restrict the story move to attitude disagreements, or let a correction stay a correct answer where the dispute is a checkable fact.

## Notes (minor, not failing)

- l2 "trained canvassers who only gave arguments changed no one's attitudes": the sources say no average effect ("indistinguishable from zero"), not no one.
- L2 items a2 "worn off" (a2-gas, b2-gwen, c2-ed variants): feedback says "One repeat of the old claim does not show why it happened", yet the key requires "worn off". Self-contradictory; the lesson should call it the best reading. Nyhan gives no time scale; the cases (two days, two weeks, a month) imply one.

- L1 matches Walton 2009 (six types, embedding versus blocking shift, quarrel row) and drops the "show" type. No carried-over structure found; L1 and L3 have a similar "tap the deciding line" form, but it is justified by shifts and last-lines reading.
