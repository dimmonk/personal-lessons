## L2: Why people dig in, and what is a myth

My own words before the first case: a correction usually nudges a belief a little, rarely backfires, and often fades. Hot, identity-linked topics make even capable people defend their side. Do not call them dumb or liars, and do not expect a win. Stories without judging worked a bit better than arguments alone. Other people are the best check on my own reasons.

My answers: all Items 1-9 and Checks 10-17 matched the key, but mostly by tone (see 1).

4. "A well-known account of reasoning says... If so, the best check is another person" is vague, and Item 4 and Check 17 lean on it.

## L3: Your goal, and when to leave

My own words before the first case: aim for a small step, not a win. Keep going while they answer you and it is a real disagreement, a bargain or a joint puzzle. Change the subject when it turns into digs or a performance and you will see them again. Leave if they attack who you are, a change of subject failed, a crowd is piling on, or you are not safe. Step out with no jab and no last word.

My answers: all Items 1-9 and Checks 10-17 matched the key. Hesitations: Item 2 and Check 13 (is "you always count everything" a dig or an attack on who you are?), Item 3 (Dave is a neighbor you keep seeing, and his insult alone should mean leave; the "already tried" reason is a second reason), Check 14 (Aunt Joan asking the table to pile on).

Defects:
1. The dig vs "attack on who you are" boundary is fuzzy. "You never remember what you owe" and "lazy slob" differ only by degree, and "insults" is the only test given.
2. The scripted leave line ("I want to talk about this, but not like this... we can pick it up another time") does not fit Item 9 (Marcus blocks the door, so how do I step out?), Item 17 (a stranger who wants a fight; I do not want to talk), or the online pile-ons (Items 6, 16).
3. The "Which line fits best?" question adds no thinking. Once the move is chosen the line is fixed by form: leave gets the quoted line, change gets a "Did you see...?" question, keep gets the one that engages. The nasty options are obvious by tone.
4. Step 2's "A friendly person who waves away your sources is a different problem" dangles. No item tests it, and it needed a second read.
5. The worked-example answer "Keep going / Fair. I'll send you..." is jumbled. It reads as one line instead of a move and then a line.
6. Item 4: who pays the $50 and who the $40 is unclear, and I read it twice.
7. Padded or preachy: the chess-with-a-pigeon joke, and "It is not a verdict on the person". Result example "4 of 5" mismatches the 8-question check.
8. Changing the subject is taught as "Let me redirect to the topic" in Dave's case ("I'd like to talk about the fence"), but that is not a change of subject. This is odd as the "already tried" evidence.

Verdict: FAIL, but closest to passing. Fixes 2, 3 and 8 would likely clear it.
