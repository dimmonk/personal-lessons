# Learner review, Debating lesson 2 (Why people dig in), round 6

Verdict: FAIL (3 defects that could mislead or let a beginner game it).

## The way of deciding, in my words
Ask what the argument is about. If it is something I can look up (a figure, a date, a notice), it is a fact: give the correction, even to someone defending their side, because backfire is rare. But if they already agreed and then went back to the old claim, don't repeat the correction now; count it as a small win and come back another day. If it is how they feel about a group or policy, facts won't do much; tell a short true story about one real person. One angry reply is not proof the correction backfired.

## Defects (mislead or block)
1. Second question is deducible from the first. Fact-not-accepted always gives correction, view always gives story, dropped always gives wait. "Hold the correction back" is always the backfire decoy, never right. I scored all 17 items by pattern, not by thinking. Tests nothing separate.
2. Dropped-fact case has two defensible answers. Step 1 says a dropped claim is still a fact, and Step 2 says to give the correction; the "wait" answer is admitted as "a judgment made here" with no source. In Items 3, 5, 12 and 15 "give the correction" looked equally right to me on first read.
3. Result section lists facets from a different lesson ("persuading beside deciding together beside bargaining beside finding out together... keep going beside change the subject beside leave the talk"). None of this was taught here. Confusing and misleading.

## Stuck, misread, second reads
- Step 1 says check "did they accept it earlier and then drop it" before the dropped idea is explained; I had to reread after Step 2.
- "Gain" (Step 2) is vague at first: gain in what?
- "Backfire" is explained only by implication in the opening.
- "Axe to grind" is an idiom. Step 4's skin cream and gun table is never used in any question.
- Step 4 (people defend their side and reason badly) seems to clash with Step 2 (give the correction to side-defenders). I wanted one line reconciling them.
- Pass rule says "at least 7 right" of 8 questions, but each question has two parts. Is that 8 or 16 answers?
- Item 14 (Sam and Jo): no sides, no real dispute, just someone told to look at a report. Easy and slightly off-topic.
- Item 4 (Ray, "argued against the bill for years"): tempted to call it a view first; the cost estimate is the fact, but it is the trickiest one.
- Items 8 and 17 (bike lanes, shelter): the lesson says the story evidence is only the nearest, with no test; fine, but the learner is asked to pick "the move the studies back" when no study covers it.

## Guessable from form
- Longest option is the "wait" one, and it is right only in the dropped-fact items.
- The "Hold the correction back" option never wins.
- Items whose second line says "I don't care about the study / what the numbers say" are always views.
- Items with a quoted document (report, notice, register) are always facts.

## Polish notes
- Draft artifacts show in the printout ("Revision 1, draft", "seed of the question", "slip: ...", "(right)"). Presumably not shown to a real learner.
- Step 5 largely repeats Step 6.
- Many Reason paragraphs are identical word for word across items, so they teach nothing new on repeat.
