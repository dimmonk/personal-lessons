# Debating: plan (Field: Communication)

Sources are in `docs/research/debating.md` (F1-F10) and `docs/learning-science.md` (P/D ids).

## What you'll be able to do, and when
**After this you can** tell, in the first minute of a disagreement, what kind of conversation it is and whether it is
worth continuing, and then make the next move that the evidence supports (listen and restate, ask their reason, share
one true experience, offer one fact), **when** someone at work, in your family or with a neighbour disagrees with you.
(F1, F5, F7; P16, D6)

**Real-world test:** 12 new, unseen short exchanges (mixed: quarrel, honest disagreement, a person fused with a group
position, a performance for an audience). For each: name the type, say continue / change topic / leave, and pick the best
next reply from four. Pass: 10 of 12. Later returns repeat it on fresh cases (P24). A self-check in life ("I named the
type before I answered") is welcome but not scored (D4).

## The way of thinking (what experts do; research note section 1)
1. **Name the game first.** Persuasion, quarrel, negotiation, inquiry or a show for an audience each have different
   rules, and unnoticed shifts cause the mess (F1).
2. **Expect a small move, not a win.** Facts usually move people a little, then fade; the blocker is identity, not
   intelligence, and arguments alone did nothing in the best field test (F2, F4, F5).
3. **Lower the stakes before you argue.** Listen without judging, restate their view fairly, ask for their reasons;
   people judge a clear argument fairly when nothing is being attacked (F3, F5, F7, F8).
4. **Use all three means, honestly.** Be credible, mind their state, keep the argument short; a true story of harm earns
   a hearing a statistic does not (F9, F6, F5).
5. **Doubt your own side too.** Your arguments are the biased ones (F3).

## What it teaches (every ask, and what the evidence did to it)
- **"Chess with a pigeon":** kept as the quarrel/closed-exchange case: spot it and stop or change the game. Corrected:
  "pigeon" is a label for the situation, not the person (F1, F4).
- **Bonhoeffer's stupidity:** taught as one real type (a person under a group's spell, F10). Corrected: it is not about
  intelligence (F4), and reasons and stories do move many people a little, so "never reason" is wrong (F2, F5).
- **Logos, ethos, pathos:** taught from Aristotle (F9). Corrected: he calls argument the core and character the
  strongest; "logos loses" is only true of a bare fact against identity (F5, F6).
- **Argumentation theory / dialogue types:** lesson 1 (F1). **Backfire effect:** lesson 2, as a myth (F2). **Identity
  and motivated reasoning:** lessons 2 and 9 (F3, F4). **Deep canvassing:** lesson 7, small effect stated (F5).
- **Street epistemology / Socratic questions:** lesson 6, limits stated; brand dropped (F8). **Competitive debate and
  pragma-dialectics rules:** dropped, reasons in research note section 2 (judgement call).
- Cases are set in everyday US situations; no claim about Canada versus US norms (nothing read supports one).

## Lessons (Unit 1 to 4), each with its check and source
**Unit 1: Decide whether to argue**
1. *What kind of conversation is this?* Five types; goal and who is listening; spotting a shift. Check: classify 8 short
   exchanges, then say what each goal is. Source F1; P12 (contrast), P9.
2. *Why people dig in, and what is a myth.* Facts usually land and then fade; identity makes skill defend a side; the
   backfire story is mostly false. Check: 8 statements, mark the wrong idea and give the right one. Source F2, F3, F4;
   P29.
3. *Your goal, and when to leave.* Aim for a notch; when to continue, change the subject or exit; a polite exit line.
   Check: 8 cases, choose continue / change / leave. Source F1, F2, F5 (small effects); judgement call on the exit
   lines, marked.

**Unit 2: The three means**
4. *Character, emotion, argument.* What each looks like in a sentence; which is missing in a failing reply; short
   arguments. Check: label means in 8 replies, fix one. Source F9; P12.
5. *Why a fact alone fails, and a story helps.* The arguments-only versus story result; respect is not persuasion; no
   invented stories. Check: pick the reply that fits the case. Source F5, F6.

**Unit 3: The moves**
6. *Restate, then ask for the reason.* Fair restatement; "how would that work?" and "how sure are you?"; when they do
   not work (sacred values, deep belief). Check: choose or write the restatement, then the question, in 8 cases. Source
   F7 (marked practice-based), F8, F3; P19.
7. *Share one true experience.* Relevant, harm-focused, short, non-judging; plus one fact. Check: pick or build the
   reply from options in 8 cases. Source F5, F6.
8. *The bad moves.* Look-alikes: mocking, labelling "stupid", fact-dumping, the invented story. Check: spot the move in
   8 replies and replace it. Source F2, F5, F6, F10; P12, P29.
9. *Check your own side.* Where your own reasoning is the biased one; one question to ask yourself first. Check: 6
   cases, find the myside move. Source F3, F4.

**Unit 4: Put it together**
10. *Mixed live exchanges.* Multi-turn cases: type, goal, then each next move, feedback names what decided. Check: the
    real-world test above on new cases. Source all; P22, P24, D6.
