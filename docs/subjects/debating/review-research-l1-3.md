## B. Departures from how the cited experts reason
1. **Own-side checking vs. Mercier & Sperber** (L2 Step 3 "the fix starts with expecting that of yourself"; item 8; check Q17 "check your own reasons as hard as hers"). In M&S the bias is a feature of how reasoning works, and the corrective comes from other people's evaluation in discussion (the note's own sect. 2.3 quote), not from self-checking. Plan principle 5 ("Doubt your own side") cites F3 for something F3 does not say. Mark as an inference, or reword to "let them check it".
2. **"Show" vs. Walton's quarrel** (L1 items 9, check Q16; L3 items 5, 6, check Q16). In Walton's table a quarrel's individual goal is to "hit out at and humiliate" the opponent. Mina publicly humiliating Greg for readers, or the heckler at the town hall, fits that row as well as the "debate / persuade third party" row, which is a regulated contest. Lesson 1 resolves it by an extra audience test not in Walton. Defensible, but it should be stated as the lesson's own rule, and Q17 (Carlos jibe plus appeal to Rose and Paul) is genuinely ambiguous between quarrel and show; the quarrel answer is scored a slip with no explanation.
3. **Change-the-subject for a friendly source-rejecter** (L3 item 2, check Q13) sits against F5, where canvassers kept a non-judgmental exchange going with people who disagreed. The plan's later lessons (6, 7) teach exactly the moves for such a person, so lesson 3 may teach "stop" where the evidence says "try a story". Needs a reconciling line or a research source.
4. **Couples and siblings** (item 8 Rita, Q14 Paul): "change the subject, come back later" is offered with no source. The field that studies couple conflict (Gottman, not read) uses repair attempts and timed breaks. Out of research scope; at least mark as judgement call.
