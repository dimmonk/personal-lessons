## The way of deciding, in my own words (written before the first practice question)

- l1: Say what the headline claims (who, and does it say "makes" or only "goes with"). Find the sentence holding the number it rests on, or say "Not given". Then: holds up if the text shows it, does not show it if the text itself shows it goes too far (smaller numbers, other people, another explanation, or it says so), cannot tell if the thing I need is simply missing.
- l2: Turn a count into "so many in 1,000" by dividing by the total, then compare with the usual. Money: per person, per year. Points are out of 100. Above usual means more than usual; same or below does not show it; no total or no usual means cannot tell (unless the text says in words it is usual).
- l3: Get the starting risk, take the percent off it, say "from X in 1,000 to Y in 1,000 over the time". A "half" or "a third" is a share of the risk; "spares half of patients" is a number of people. Compare the headline's kind of size to that. "Makes the difference" needs a coin toss or lottery. No start or no end number: cannot tell.
- l4: Picture 1,000 people. How many have it; the test finds some; it falsely flags some of the rest; add the positives; how many are real. Judge "positive most likely means you have it" by that count. Missing how common it is or the false alarm rate: cannot tell.
- l5: Find what the number is compared with, then ask if it is fair: same period, same counting, rates not totals, same mix (compare kind by kind), and a measure people are paid to raise. Nothing to compare with: cannot tell.

## Where I got stuck (none blocking)

### l0
1. Baseline screen: "Does not show it" versus "Cannot tell from this" are never defined before the baseline. Ungraded, so I guessed on question 4 and was fine, but it feels like a trick. Fix: one line under the verdict buttons ("cannot tell = something needed is missing").
2. Result example line says "Of 6 claims the text does not show... of 4 that hold up" but the baseline has 3 and 2. It is only an example; make the example use the real counts so it does not look like a miscount.

### l1
3. Check 12 (Hillside Diner): "Cannot tell" is the marked answer, but "Does not show it" is also defensible, since the owner's word is the only evidence and the numbers (14 dishes, 42 seats) measure nothing. Feedback explains well, but it is the one item where two options are close. Fix: add a clause to the text that settles it (for example the owner "has not counted sales").
4. Item 2 (crash lights): the text pre-empts the "bad year" objection ("picked for their traffic, not for a bad year"). I would not have thought of that, so the line reads as a clue planted for the answer. Fine, but it hands me the verdict.
5. Pattern available: "Not given" is right exactly when no sentence has a digit. Real reasoning is still needed for the verdict, so not decisive.

### l2
6. Percentage points (Step 2) appear in one practice item (Item 5) and nowhere in the check or the mixed items, so the idea will not be tested again. Fix: one check or fresh-numbers item on points.
7. Check 19 (pounds per student) drops "in 1,000" and divides directly. The step on money teaches "for each person" but not for a weight. Obvious, but untaught. Fix: add "or any amount" to Step 3.
8. "Far more / soar / boom" versus the rule "above the usual holds up": no item sits between slightly above and far above, so the threshold for "far" is never tested. All items are clearly 2x or equal, so no stall.
9. Feedback for typing 15 on check 16 says "That is out of 100 people", but the subject is flights. Fix: "out of 100 flights" (text should follow the item's noun).

### l3
10. Item 2 (Cardiol): the text never says whether a coin toss was used. A learner who just applies "no coin toss means does not show it" gets the right verdict without doing the 80 to 60 working. The reason shown covers only size. Fix: say "a coin toss decided" in the text so size is the only thing wrong.
11. Item 4 (Lumina "1 in 3" with "40% fewer"): "Cannot tell" is right by the rule, but I was tempted by "does not show it" because 40% is not a third. Feedback handles it; consider naming that mismatch in the reason.
12. Wording pattern: headlines with "spares / keeps X of users / protects" usually fail and headlines with "halves / cuts by" usually pass. Computing is still needed (Item 6 Tolpex and the fresh items break the pattern), so this is not a gap.

### l4
13. Step 1 has to carry four steps and a worked count in one paragraph; I needed a second read before the "5 in 100 of 980" line sank in. The worked example and the first guided item then made it clear. Fix: split Step 1 into two short screens.
14. Typed answers have two blanks ("[ ] of the [ ] who test positive"). The feedback on wrong entries is good, but the first time I did not know that the second blank is the total positives, not the total tested. Fix: label the blanks "have it / all positives".

### l5
15. Step 3 (two hospitals) packs eight percentages into a paragraph. I read it twice. The item that uses the idea (Item 6) hands over the per-test rates, so it is solvable even if the step is skimmed. Fix: show it as a small table.
16. Item 2 (Brookdale vs Fenwick): none of the three claim options is "Brookdale recycles twice as much in total", so I picked the claim by elimination rather than by reading. Fix: add that as the mixed-claim distractor.
17. Check 11 (season tickets): the first sentence (June last year, 950) is also a number to compare with, so "tap the sentence it is compared with" has two defensible answers until you match the headline's 60%. The feedback explains it. Fix: reword the tap prompt to "the comparison the headline uses".
18. Check 14 and Check 13 are both "fair, holds up" with identical shape; a learner who has seen "same system / same routes" cues can pass them by pattern. The cue is the very thing taught (same counting), so acceptable.
