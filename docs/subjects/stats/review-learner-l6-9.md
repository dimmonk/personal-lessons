## L6 Who was counted, and how
Way of deciding, in my words: find the sentence that says who was counted. Ask how many, did they pick themselves, were only survivors counted, are they the people the claim is about, how were they asked, who counted. Then, if people were picked by chance, check the result still beats the margin (for "most", above half at the edge). A missing "how picked" means cannot tell.

Defects:
1. Item 7 (IronWorks), "who was counted" tap. Two sentences are defensible: "Our door scanner logs every visit by every member" and the small print "840 of our 1,200 members". Tapping the scanner sentence gets "That is not the sentence", which is wrong for a reader who sees "every member". Fix: reword the scanner sentence so it does not name who was counted, or give it a note such as "That is how it was counted, the numbers are in the small print".
2. Item 6 (Harlan Bank), tap. "The bank emailed all 2,000" and "150 answered" are both reasonable. The feedback on the first ("how many were asked, not how many answered") is good. Polish only.
3. Check 13 (soccer), tap. The correct option merges the meeting sentence and the "Saturdays it is!" sentence into one tap target. Polish.
4. Item 3 and Check 14 reason about "inside the margin" on a two-way race. The lesson only teaches "stays above half". It works by analogy but is not taught for a gap between two. Fix: add one line to Step 2: "for a lead, the lead must be bigger than the margin".

## L7 Could anything else explain it?
Way of deciding: ask whether anything else could explain it. Check for other differences between groups, the arrow the other way, a bounce back from a worst year, or nothing to compare with. A coin toss or lottery (with a dummy treatment) removes all of these. A claim that things only went together can hold even with other explanations. Cause claims hold only if the split was by chance and the numbers fit the size. Missing "how the groups were formed" means cannot tell.

Defects:
5. Pattern leak on "Could anything else explain it?". In Items 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 10 and Check 13, 14, 16 the right option is the one concrete, text-specific explanation. The distractors are the fixed "Nothing else: split by chance" and "Cannot say: the text does not tell how..." boilerplate. A beginner can pick the odd one out. Fix: make the distractors specific too, or put the boilerplate options on right and wrong items about equally often.
6. Step 2's "does not show it" list omits "the numbers say less than the claim", although Item 8 (Brightmind, a quarter not half) depends on it. The "holds up" sentence has it ("numbers fit the size"). Fix: add it to the "does not show it" list.
7. Check 14 (Hawks), tap. "This season they won 8 of 16" is keyed. "3 wins ... worst season" is also part of the change (3 to 8). The feedback ("the season before, not the change") is arguable. Fix: key the sentence that holds both numbers, or reword the question to "this season's number".

## L8 Read the chart's numbers
Way of deciding: ignore bar height. Check where the scale starts. Work out the real change as change ÷ start × 100. Check the time covered and start from a usual year, not a low year. Then compare with what the claim needs ("doubles" +100%, "half" 50%, "a quarter" 25%). If the numbers fit it holds up, even on a cut scale. If they show less it does not. If earlier years are missing for an "ever" claim, cannot tell.

Defects (D8 to D10 are leaks to fix before ship):
8. Direction label gives away the verdict. The percent boxes are labelled "down [ ]%" or "up [ ]%". In Check 12 (Carver shelter) and Item 8 (Harbor Bakery) the box says "down", while the ad and story say up. The learner sees the contradiction before reasoning. Fix: use a neutral box with a sign, or "change [ ]% (use minus for a fall)".
9. Prompts hand over the fair start year: "From 2018 to 2024" (Item 2), "From last June" (Item 8), "From 2019 to 2025" (Check 12). The reasoning the lesson teaches (pick a usual year) is done for the learner. Item 2 also says "Type 0 if they did not", which signals the answer. Fix: ask "by what percent did the claim's own start year change" and "what is a fair start?" separately, or ask the plain change over the whole chart.
10. Item 6 (Cedar Hill) and Item 8 (Fairfield, L9), tap. The post's sentence "reading scores are up a tenth" gets "That is not the sentence" with no reason. It does contain a size. Fix: note "That repeats the claim; the actual scores are only on the chart".
11. Not taught: "triples" means +200% (Items 3, Check 10, L9 Check 14), and "a fifth", "a tenth" in percent. A beginner can work it out but the lesson only gives double, half, quarter. Fix: add triple, a fifth and a tenth to the Step 1 list.
12. Check 12, "than before" is vague. The 2025 figure beats 2022 to 2024. The feedback depends on 2019. Fix: say "than before 2022" in the claim option.
13. Item 8 (Harbor Bakery). "Up 50% since February" is true, and "best spring ever" is the claim. A beginner could say "holds up". The claim option fixes this, but the worked example never showed a true number under a false claim. Polish.
14. Item 5 feedback uses "bigger" for a fall ("how much bigger it looks"). Polish.

## L9 The whole claim, unprompted
Way of deciding: say the six questions (what it claims and its number, how big, compared with what and fair, who was counted, could anything else explain it, verdict). Put every one to each claim, since any one can break it.

Defects:
15. Item 1 asks me to recall the six questions, but L9 never shows them first. The strict matcher then marks natural paraphrases ("who was asked", "how big is the change") as misses, which I had to override with "My answer means the same". Fix: show the six once before Item 1, or widen the accepted phrases.
16. The size box appears only on some claims (Items 2, 4, 6, 8; Checks 12 to 15). Its presence hints which question might decide, so L9 is not fully unprompted. Fix: ask for the size on every claim, or on none.
17. Items 4 and 13 (the positive-test items) need the "picture 1,000 people" method from an earlier lesson and give no help here. I could do them only because I remembered the method. Fix: add a one-line reminder on the item or in Step 1.
18. Item 5 (Brookline), cannot tell versus does not show it. Both are defensible. A total is not a rate, but the text never says city sizes. Feedback is clear, so this is polish.
