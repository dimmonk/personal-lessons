## L1 What it claims, and the number it rests on: PASS (check 5/5)
Way of deciding, in my words: say what a reader takes away (does it say X makes Y happen, or only that they went together, and about whom),
tap the sentence with the number (or Not given), then: holds up if the text shows it; does not show it if the text itself says it goes too
far; cannot tell if the needed thing is simply missing.
No stall. Polish:
1. Check "k-5k": "They work!" is a general claim; the who-slip "PowerPeak shakes cut a runner's 5K time by 2 minutes" is close to what the
   post says. I chose the marked answer, but only after a second read. Make the slip clearly about other people ("cut 5K times for every
   runner in her club").
2. Check "k-shelter": "Placing more pets makes Harbor Shelter take in fewer animals" is not a reading anyone would make; easy elimination.

## L2 How big it is: out of how many: PASS (check 5/5)
Way of deciding: count ÷ total × 1,000, compare with the usual; a strong word (soar, boom) needs half again the usual or more; equal or a
little above is ordinary; no total or no usual means cannot tell unless the text says in words it is usual.
No stall. Polish: "clearly above the usual" for a plain "more than usual" has no number; no item tests it, so it is harmless now.

## L3 A risk, before and after: PASS (check 5/5)
Way of deciding: find the risk without it, take the percent off, say before and after in 1,000 over the time; a share claim ("halves") is
checked against the share, a people claim ("spares half", "keeps 1 in 5") against how many fewer people; a cause needs a coin toss.
No stall. Polish: k-tutor's feedback says "students chose the program", which the story never says (I had to re-read to look for it).

## L4 A test says yes: PASS (check 4/4)
Way of deciding: picture 1,000, how many have it, how many of them the test finds, how many of the rest it flags, then what share of the
positives are real; most real = holds; missing false-alarm rate or how common = cannot tell.
No stall. Polish: generated items name the outlet after the test ("Orrel swab · Health News"), which reads oddly; the result example says
"4 of 5" on a check of 4.
