# Learner review, Strategy lessons 1 to 14

Role: cold learner (adult project manager, two small businesses run, no business vocabulary). Read only `docs/learner-view/strategy-l1.md` to `strategy-l14.md`. Files only; nothing run. Lessons 1-5 were read fresh (earlier review not opened).

How I scored: the views print the right answer beside each option, so I could not answer fully blind. I formed my answer from the notes first where I could and then compared. Where I name a score it is "agreed with the marked answer by reasoning"; the exceptions are listed as defects.

Defect tags: [P] answerable by pattern (position, length, wording, template) without the reasoning; [A] ambiguous, two answers defensible, or answer rests on something not taught; [E] error or mismatch in the text; [G] gap in what is taught or practised; [F] feedback that does not say what decided; [C] carried over from another lesson.

Verdicts: L1 PASS, L2 PASS, L3 PASS, L4 PASS, L5 PASS, L6 FAIL, L7 PASS, L8 PASS, L9 PASS, L10 PASS, L11 FAIL, L12 PASS, L13 FAIL, L14 FAIL. PASS means a beginner can pass by reasoning; the listed defects should still be fixed. FAIL means the check can be cleared (or is not valid) without the lesson's reasoning.

## L1 Goal, wish, or strategy? PASS (check 6 of 6)

The way of deciding, in my words: find the line that says what is in the way and why; if none, it is a goal (target or wish) or, if steps are listed, a to-do list; if there is one, check that one way past it is chosen and every step serves that way; then it is a strategy.

1. [E] Item 7 reason (and the same text in lesson 14 pool q-print): "in six words" but the line "Online printers undercut us on cards and flyers" has eight. Fix: "in eight words" or drop the count.
2. [P] Tap questions: the right line is the only descriptive sentence among imperatives, and usually the longest (Items 1, 2, 5, 8, 14; check 11, 15). Fix: add plans where the challenge is short ("Rivals undercut us.") and a step is long and has a "because".
3. [G] Step 1 says "every plan here faces a challenge from rivals or the market", but Luis (idle winters) and Mike (rainy weeks) face weather and slack, not rivals. Fix: reword the sentence or the notes.
4. [A] Check 13 "This year we focus on families with young children" is arguably a (thin) choice of customer; I hesitated between to-do list and goal. Fix: make the focus line plainly empty ("grow in every direction") or accept it as an approach to nothing in the teaching.

## L2 Better, or different? PASS (check 6 of 6; check 13 was the close call)

Way of deciding: ask whether doing the same things the rivals do, only better, would fix it. Yes if rivals do the same work better and show it can be done; no if we already run as well, or gains are copied at once, or a rival's price is below what we can reach.

1. [A][F] Item 4 (pizza): help marks two lines, but only one tap is accepted. The "$9 to make, $8 price" line is as decisive as the chain line. Fix: mark one line, or accept either.
2. [P] The right tap is the line that compares us to rivals and is almost always the longest (Items 1, 3, 4, 6, 8; checks 11, 14, 15). Fix: as L1 defect 2.
3. [G] Item 7 first line is "So we fit fewer jobs into the season." with no antecedent; needed a second read. Fix: move it after its cause or drop "So".
4. [F] Check rule "None took running the business better for a strategy" is confusing, since no check question asks "is it a strategy". Fix: "None took a position problem for a running problem".
5. [P] Two options only: guessing clears 5 of 6 about 11% of the time, and the wording cues above lift that. Fix: add a third outcome (e.g. "need more facts") or a written reason.

## L3 Value created, and who keeps it PASS (check 7 of 7)

Way of deciding: value = most customer would pay minus least suppliers and staff would take; customer keeps (most minus price), business keeps (price minus cost), suppliers keep (cost minus least). A move grows value only if what customers would pay rises by more than the work added, or what suppliers would take falls; a price or pay change only moves it.

1. [A] Item 15 (pay cut $1, "all of the cleaners stayed"): staying at lower pay is evidence their least-they-would-take was already lower. Compare Item 6, where staying at the same pay means the least fell. Fix: replace "all of the cleaners stayed" with "the cleaners have no other jobs nearby" or state that what the job is worth to them is unchanged.
2. [G] Items 7 and 11 both show the mill $20 a ton lower; the difference is whether the mill's willingness changed. A beginner needs one sentence in Step 4 contrasting "pushed" with "would have taken it anyway".
3. [G] The lesson never says how an owner finds "the most a customer would pay" or "the least staff would take" in real life; every item hands the numbers over. Fix: one line in Step 1 on where owners can get each number (asking, offers, wages elsewhere).

## L4 Who squeezes the profit PASS (check 6 of 6; check 15 needed care)

Way of deciding: find the line that shows who takes the money (a few big buyers or a middleman, a supplier I cannot replace, easy entry, another way to meet the need, or alike rivals fighting on price); compare when two press; growth tells nothing, easy entry sends the growth to newcomers.

1. [P] The right sentence is the longest and most numeric in 13 of 17 items (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 13, 15, 16, 17), and tapping it then gives away the force question. Fix: shorten key lines, put a numeric red herring in a long line (as Items 7, 16, 17 begin to do).
2. [A] Item 3 (Paccar) asks "which force did Paccar step around", a different question from the one taught, and rivals also press hard. Fix: teach this move in Step 3, or ask the standard question.
3. [A] Item 2 growth question: the report shows suppliers take the money, yet the answer is "growth stays with the firms already there"; "great business" is never reconciled. Fix: ask "who takes the profit as the trade grows" with suppliers offered.
4. [A] Check 15 (car dealers): the report opens "Buyers shop around", and the buyers feedback says "limited hold". Mixed signal. Fix: drop the first clause or make it plainly weak.
5. [F] About 25 wrong-pick notes say only "The report gives no sign that this force takes much of the money here". They do not say what decided. Fix: name the line that settles it.
6. [G] Buyers are only ever tested as one big buyer or middleman; "can switch at will" is taught and never practised.

## L5 Find the core PASS (check 4 of 4; I checked every share: all 11 are arithmetically right)

Way of deciding: subtract each line's direct cost from what it brought in; the core is the line customers come for rather than to a rival that earns the most; its share is its earnings over all lines' earnings, losses taken off; a losing line stays (fix it) only if the core's customers buy it along with the core.

1. [A][G] Items 3, 7 and check 9 key "keep it" to a feeder: library classes brought the families, offices first tried the delivery app, hotels send the couples. Step 1 teaches only "the core's customers buy it along with the core". A literal reader can say let go. Fix: add "or it is how core customers find you" to the rule, or reword those notes to "buy both".
2. [G] Table heads read "product by product" and "way of selling by way of selling"; clumsy. Fix: "by product", "by way of selling".
3. [G] Share above 100% is taught and never practised.
4. [P] The core is always the line named in the bracket note; the trap lines say "every rival does it the same way". Acceptable, but a learner who reads only the brackets scores. Fix: put one non-core fact in a bracket that is tempting.

## L6 What you say no to FAIL (check passable by keywords)

Way of deciding: ask what the move gives up; if it gives up what pulls the other way, it is a real trade-off; if both ways run on the same people and space, it is a straddle.

1. [P] Straddles all carry "like [rival]", "also/adding" and "the same staff/chairs/cook"; trade-offs carry "only", "no longer", "turn down", "end this summer". Items 1-9 and checks 10-15 alternate straddle and trade-off. Two options only. A learner scoring 6 of 6 could have learned nothing. Fix: mixed items (a trade-off made to copy a rival; a straddle that is not copying; a move that adds without cost to the core, with a reason it is or is not a straddle) and make the learner write what is given up.
2. [G] The test "gives up nothing" is circular. The lesson never says how to tell whether two things pull against each other; the notes always say so.
3. [A][F] Option label "copying while keeping the old way" does not fit Item 5 (Carlos serves everyone, copies nobody); the lesson also names "serve every kind of customer" as a straddle.
4. [A] Items 8 and 9 give up half the business or the half-day families. Whether the choice is wise is not asked; a cold learner may mark them wrong because they are unwise.
5. [F] Check rule "None tried to serve everyone, or to copy a rival while keeping your own way" is repeated in L10 (see L10 defect 4).

## L7 Do the pieces fit? PASS (check 5 of 5)

Way of deciding: state the position (who, what they get that others do not); hold every activity against it; a piece that serves another customer or the trade's usual way fails; a costly-looking piece can still fit.

1. [C][P] Taught examples return as items: Step 1 names "a cafe that lets one pass keep a seat all day" (Item 4 is that) and "a gym that pays for coaches all day" (check 14 is that). Both answers are given away. Fix: use different examples in Step 1.
2. [P] The second question always has the same two wrong options (another kind of customer; the usual way done harder). The right option is the one that is neither. Fix: vary the wrong options, make one a plausible fit that fails on the position.
3. [P] Misfit lines carry "lowest, cheapest, discount, like the mall stores, 8 to 5, speed at the till"; "every activity fits" items carry "though", "paid a little more", "at most two". Fix: neutral wording on both.
4. [G] "Does it help the other activities?" is taught and never tested; every item tests only "serves the position".

## L8 Will it last? PASS (check 6 of 6)

Way of deciding: first, what does it give (customers pay more, or it costs less than at rivals); then what stops a copy (only we have it; many activities that fit together; or nothing). Lasts only with both.

1. [P] Barrier keys: "sold to any / can order / available online" gives nothing; "only one / no new / ten-year deal / rules allow none" gives only-we-have-it; "would have to change ... all at once" gives many activities. Fix: items where a purchasable thing is hard to get (a waiting list), and where a fit-together bundle can be copied by a rival that already runs that way.
2. [G] Items 8 and 15 give nothing, yet the screen still asks what stops a copy ("opens only after a certain answer"); it is not clear to the learner when it opens. Fix: skip the second question when there is no benefit.
3. [G] Two sources of lasting cost advantage (scale, accumulated experience) are not mentioned, though Step 1 says cost advantages count.

## L9 Growth that keeps the position PASS (check 6 of 6)

Way of deciding: name the core; ask whether the offer serves the same kind of customer with the same activities; if yes it deepens the core; if it needs new activities or another customer it blurs it; ignore the money.

1. [P] Blur notes include "new crews, night shift, hire, different hours, a chain"; deepen notes literally say "the same mechanics / same hours / same press". Binary.
2. [A] Item 1: park mowing for Luis, whose crews already mow (L1, L5 show mowing). "Different activities" is arguable. Fix: choose an activity he does not do.
3. [E] Item 4 says "more than our wheel sales", but wheels appear only in Item 3's note. In a shuffled or standalone view it points at nothing. Fix: say "more than we make from wheels" with a wheel line in the note, or drop the clause.
4. [A] Check 13: the piano maker is a single business buying all deliveries (like the supermarket in Step 2), not owners who pay double. Same customer? Fix: make the delivery for piano stores and owners.
5. [G] Maytag line "from 8 to 12 cents to under 1 cent" reads as a range; I had to reread. Fix: give one figure.

## L10 Where to play and how to win PASS (check 6 of 6)

Way of deciding: a plan must say which customers and where, and why they would pick us; otherwise it is one of six traps (do it all, head-on, many fronts, something for everyone, a dream, copycat).

1. [P] In question 1, the real plan is always the one in the form "We play for ... We win with ..." (about 11 of 14 items). Fix: write real plans in plain words, and let a trap plan borrow the form.
2. [A] Item 4 (Tom Plan 1) fights the big home store for weekend shoppers, which Item 3 calls head-on; Item 8 and check 14 also name a chain. Two traps defensible. Fix: remove the big-chain target from the many-fronts plans.
3. [A] Item 9 Plan 1 and check 15 Plan 1 (every pet, every length of day): something for everyone or do it all. Fix: keep one trap per plan.
4. [C] The check rule "None tried to serve everyone, or to copy a rival while keeping your own way" is copied from L6; "keeping your own way" is not a trap here. Fix: state L10's own rule.
5. [G] The learner never judges a single plan alone or two weak plans; every item is a good plan beside a bad one. The "fit" between place and win (Step 1) is never tested.
6. [F] Wrong-trap notes read "It does not ...", which teach nothing about the plan chosen.

## L11 What would have to be true FAIL (check clearable by one word)

Way of deciding: list what would have to be true for the move; mark which has proof (said, signed, paid, measured, done) and which does not; test the one with least behind it first, cheaply; choose the move whose conditions held.

1. [P] In every one of the 12 condition items, the right answer is the single sentence containing "nobody has / no one / none has been / not said / never / no driver has come in". Every other condition carries a concrete proof word (signed, quoted, measured, agreed, approved). I could answer without thinking. Fix: make the weak condition sound plausible without a flag ("Most customers will want this"), and make strong-looking distractors that are not evidence (a friend said, the owner's belief, a survey of friends).
2. [G] Never two weak conditions to rank (least backed vs most damaging); never what to do when the test fails or passes. The cheap-test line appears only in two unscored free writes.
3. [P] Both ordering items (5, check 9) have one decoy ("write a full plan", "hire a forecast firm") that stands out as the only step not about conditions or tests. Fix: put several plausible but wrong steps in.
4. [F] Check rule is only "at least 3 of 4", with no rule about the mistake the lesson names (planning before testing).

## L12 How did others like it do? PASS (check 5 of 5; I verified every share and sum)

Way of deciding: start the forecast from the share of similar ventures that worked; move off it only for a real difference; judge a rival's answer by whether the customers are its own and it can follow without changing how it runs; judge a copy-this example by whether its reason holds here.

1. [G] Finding "ventures alike in what makes them work or fail" is the first instruction, and every item hands me the table. Choosing comparables is never practised. Fix: an item with two tables (one similar, one not) or a list of nearby businesses to pick from.
2. [G] "Move from it only for a real difference" is never practised (how far, for what). Fix: one item with deposits paid, asking where to start.
3. [P] In the three-option "where to start" question, the right option is the one with a number; the wrong ones are always "from the plan" and "from the one that did well".
4. [P] Rival items key on "matched every cut / lower rent" (answers) against "second cook / not its customers" (lets go); same-reason items key on line three repeating or negating line two.

## L13 A bet you can afford FAIL

Way of deciding: ask whether there is a record of the same kind of project; if so start from it; if not, start with what I have, a partner, and a loss I can afford.

1. [P] Record items are all "a second X / a branch" with "the state lists"; no-record items all say "Nobody ...". The wrong answers are fixed templates ("Borrow $N", "Pay for a forecast", "Go by own forecast"). 15 of 15 items fall to word-spotting.
2. [A][G] The title is "a bet you can afford", yet record items say "we could lose $10,000" beside a $250,000 gym and the right answer is "size the bet from the record". It never says what sizing means, what to do if the record is poor, or whether an unaffordable bet is acceptable. A careful learner could object to Mark's second gym, Grace's second salon, Dana's second bakery. Fix: teach how to size a bet from the record (compare survival share to the loss I can take) and add an item where the record is poor or the bet is unaffordable.
3. [G] Nothing tests a project that is partly known (a dog-friendly gym).
4. [F] Check rule "None bet more than you could afford to lose" conflicts with the record items, where the right answer states no cap.

## L14 Run the six questions FAIL

Way of deciding: take a decision through six questions in order: running better or position; what is in the way; where the money comes from; what stops a copy; the choice; judge the bet by record or by what I can lose.

1. [G] The check is ten single-topic questions drawn from a pool of about 240 that are the lesson items from L1-L13, word for word, including the worked-example items whose answers the lessons showed (w-bakery, w-cake, w-gyms and the like). It tests recall of what I already saw. Fix: write new items for the pool, and keep the worked examples out of it.
2. [G] The lesson trains the integrated run (Items 1-4) but the pass test never asks for it. Fix: make at least three of the ten questions whole decisions.
3. [G] The six-question list says "who takes the rest" (the five forces), but no item asks it; fit, growth and the cheap test are also missing. The list and the practice differ.
4. [P] In Items 1-4 the note lines come in the order of the six questions (challenge, runs well, core, barrier, record), and the options carry phrases that match ("many towns, county lists" versus "nobody offers").
5. [F] Wrong taps in question 2 all say "That line answers another of the six questions" without saying which. Fix: name the question the line answers.
6. [A] Item 3: the reason says "the plan must lean on more than the pass", but the right Q5 option is the same pass-and-quiet plan and Q4 says nothing stops a copy; a reader wonders whether the plan is sound. Fix: reconcile the reason with Q4.
7. [E] q-print reason keeps the "six words" error (L1 defect 1).
8. [G] The three disqualifier rules (goal for strategy, growing for growth, serving everyone) cannot trigger if the draw has no such question. Fix: always draw one question per rule.
