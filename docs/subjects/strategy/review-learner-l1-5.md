# Learner review, Strategy lessons 1 to 5

Reviewer: cold learner (project manager, two small businesses, no formal business training). Read only docs/learner-view/strategy-l1.md to l5.md. Note: I could read the marked answers at the same time as the questions, so "answered first" means I decided before reading the "(right)" tag; all my answers matched the marks. The value-stick picture (L3) is described in words only, so I could not judge it visually.

Scores on the checks (my answers vs the marks): L1 6/6, L2 6/6, L3 6/6, L4 6/6, L5 4/4.

## Lesson 1: Goal, wish, or strategy? Verdict: PASS

Way of deciding, in my words: find the line that says what is in the way and why. No such line: a target or wish alone is a goal; a list of steps is a to-do list. A named obstacle but steps that do not get past it is also a to-do list. Obstacle + one way past it + steps that serve that way is a strategy.

Defects (minor):
1. Items 3, 6, 9, 12 ("Give every member a great experience", "Every job done on time"): these read as steps to some learners, so "goal or wish" vs "to-do list" is arguable. The page only separates them by "real steps". Fix: add one line in Step 1 saying a step is something you can schedule or buy ("Buy two plows"), not a quality you hope for.
2. Never practised: an obstacle plus an approach but no steps, and an obstacle plus steps that serve it but no stated approach. Learner cannot tell where these land. Fix: add one item of each, or say in Step 1 that both count as unfinished.
3. Words "mission", "vision" (Items 6, 9) are used as wishes without being introduced. Fix: one clause in Step 1.
4. Pattern shortcut: the right "in the way" line is almost always the only one with a number plus "because/since/and" about a problem; strategy items always have a long second line starting "So we". Answerable without the reasoning. Fix: put an approach-like line in some goal/to-do items, and a strategy whose challenge is short.

## Lesson 2: Better, or different? Verdict: FAIL

Way of deciding: ask whether doing the same work as rivals, only better, would fix it. If rivals do the same work better, run it better. If the business already matches rivals, or rivals copy each gain, or a rival's cost cannot be reached, choose a different position.

Defects:
1. Item 4 (pizza) is arguable. The shop's cost is $7 and the chains' price is $8, so the shop can match their price and still make $1. The chains' own cost is never stated. "Competing at their price cannot work" is not supported by the numbers. Fix: make the shop's cost $9 or more, or state the chains' cost.
2. "Choose a different position" is never shown concretely (no example of what the new position could be besides the Southwest line). A learner cannot picture the answer, so it feels like "the other button". Fix: one worked line on what a different position looks like, or say it is the next lessons' job.
3. Dead feedback "That is not the sentence." (Item 2 line 2, Item 5 line 2, Check 13 line 1, Check 14 line 2, Check 15 line 2). It tells the learner nothing about what decided. Fix: give each a reason like the other lines have.
4. Only two answer choices and the right line is in position 2 in about 9 of 15 items, and it is always the line that names rivals. A learner can score by pattern. Fix: shuffle line positions, add a distractor line that mentions rivals but is background.
5. Unexplained words: "squeezed", "best practice", "lean" (Step 1). Fix: plain words.
6. Item 9 (old press): "every shop runs a newer press" is copyable kit, the same kind of thing Item 8 says rivals copy. A learner may wonder why this is not a position problem. Fix: add a clause saying the rivals' newer press shows the gap can be closed, which is what decides.

## Lesson 3: Value created, and who keeps it. Verdict: PASS (with defects)

Way of deciding: value = the most a customer would pay minus the least staff and suppliers would take. Customer keeps that top minus the price; business keeps price minus cost; suppliers keep cost minus the least. A move grows value only if it lifts the top or lowers the bottom; changing only price or cost just moves it.

Defects:
1. Step 1 never says why "the least suppliers and staff would take" differs from "the cost". A beginner can follow the arithmetic but not the idea. Fix: one sentence and a tiny example ("a cleaner paid $20 who would have worked for $17").
2. Item 13 (pay cut, "cleaners less happy, all stayed"): arguable. Unhappy staff could be said to raise the least they would take. The marked answer (only moved) depends on "all stayed". Fix: remove "less happy".
3. Wording "top / bottom of the stick" in feedback, while the Step 1 picture is a bar "from least to most" (reads left to right). Fix: say "upper end/lower end" consistently, or make the bar vertical.
4. Pattern: in the tap-the-line items the right line is the one that states a dollar amount customers or staff "would pay/take", and the "only moves" items say "the same". Learner can answer without the reasoning. Fix: some "grows" items stated without a dollar, some "only moves" items with a dollar.
5. Item 7 (mill forced to cut price): taught only by one rule line in Step 4. The forced-cut-vs-genuine-saving contrast (Item 10) is good but the learner has to reread. Acceptable.
6. Numeric items all use the same three sub-questions seven times: easy to run on autopilot. Minor.

## Lesson 4: Who squeezes the profit. Verdict: FAIL

Way of deciding: find the line that shows who takes the money and why, name the one of five (buyers, suppliers, newcomers, substitutes, rivals), check the others are weak. Growth alone says nothing.

Defects:
1. Item 8 (dog walking): Step 1 says buyers push price down when they "can switch at will". The report says owners "can switch walkers with one tap". That matches the lesson's own buyers definition, so two options are defensible. The feedback ("walkers arriving faster than owners push") is a judgment the lesson never taught. Fix: remove the one-tap line or change it to owners who rarely switch.
2. Item 6 (delivery apps): the apps keep 30% and decide who is seen. Many learners would call them a supplier of customers the restaurants cannot replace. The marked answer "buyers" is arguable. Fix: reword so the apps clearly buy from restaurants, or add this case to Step 1 and say which side it counts as.
3. The "Yes/No, is growth enough" question (Items 2, 8, Check 12) has "No" as the right answer every time, and Step 1 already says so. Answerable by pattern. Fix: add a case where growth plus weak forces make "good to be in" defensible, or reword.
4. Dead feedback "That is not the sentence." (Item 5 line 1, Item 10 line 1, Check 15 line 2). Fix: give a reason.
5. Pattern: the sentence that contains "many suppliers", "easy to find" or "no new ... in years" is always a distractor, and the right sentence is usually line 2 (about 9 of 16). Learner can eliminate without reasoning. Fix: mix in reports where the distractor lines carry real weight and the deciding line is plain.
6. Item 1 feedback brings in "Paccar" and "drivers who own their own truck" with no setup. Fix: drop it or explain in one line.
7. Every report has exactly one force, so the "name the one that takes most" skill is never tested against two real forces. Fix: add at least one report where two forces are present and one clearly bigger.

## Lesson 5: Find the core. Verdict: FAIL

Way of deciding: for each line, subtract its direct cost from what it brought in; the line that earns the most is the core; its share is its earnings over the earnings of the money-making lines; a losing line stays if the core's customers buy it with the core (fix its price or cost), otherwise let it go.

Defects:
1. Step 1 says "compare lines of one kind at a time", but the practice tables mix kinds: Item 1 (tree work, lawn mowing, garden design, snow removal are services) fine, but Item 2 mixes customers (walk-ins, school district, law firms) with a product (restaurant menus); Item 6 mixes a venue, a customer group and a sales channel. Fix: make each table one kind, as the rule says.
2. Item 6 lines overlap: catering trays are mostly ordered "through the delivery app", so the same sales sit in two lines and the core's share is double counted. Fix: choose lines that do not overlap.
3. The share rule leaves a losing line out of the total (Item 1: 61.9% not 72.2%). No reason is given, and the "wrong" 72.2% is what many people would compute. Two defensible answers. Fix: give the reason in Step 1, or ask for the share of the net total.
4. The own-business output ("Fix its price or cost, or think about letting it go") skips the "does it come with the core?" test the lesson just taught. Fix: add the question for each losing line in Step 5.
5. Pattern: "Keep it and grow it" is never right (10 of 10 items), and the decision is always settled by the bracket note. Fix: add a case where a loss-making line is tied to the core but the fix is to drop only part, or where no line loses money.
6. Never practised: lines earning about evenly, or nothing clearly core, though Step 5 prints those messages. Fix: add one item.
7. "Keep and grow the core before anything else" is stated without a reason beyond "pays for everything". A learner may ask what if the core is already at its limit. Fix: one sentence acknowledging the limit.
8. Overheads (rent) are left out with no remark that the core could still lose money overall. Fix: one sentence.
9. "<- marked after the answer" on table rows while help says deciding words are marked: unclear what the learner sees before answering. Fix: state it once.

## Defect count by kind

- Arguable or unsupported answer: 6 (L2 pizza; L3 pay cut; L4 dog walking, delivery apps; L5 share rule, overlapping lines)
- Pattern shortcut: 6 (one per lesson, L4 has two)
- Dead or missing feedback: 2 (L2, L4)
- Undefined or unexplained word/idea: 8
- Contradiction with the lesson's own rule: 3 (L5 mixed kinds, L5 own-business step, L4 buyers definition)
- Missing case: 5
