# Strategy: plan (field Business, subject `strategy`)

Written 2026-10-22 (build-subject step 4). Sources: ST ids in `docs/research/strategy.md`; P, D and R ids in
`docs/learning-science.md`, `docs/research/designing-a-subject.md` and `docs/research/review-after-lessons.md`.

**The owner's asks, in his words.** Steven runs and has run two businesses with no formal training: "I learn on the field
but I still don't think I know what I'm doing at a high level to move a business to the next level", and "what do people
learn in business school". So: (1) what an MBA strategy core teaches, (2) at a high level, (3) usable to move his own
business forward, (4) cases an owner-operator faces plus well-known company cases where they teach the reasoning. Other MBA
core courses are their own subjects; this one teaches only what strategy needs of them (ST18).

## What you'll be able to do, and when

**After this you can look at a business decision (expand, add a product, chase a big customer, answer a rival, invest)
and tell whether it is a strategy, say where the business makes its money and why, and pick the move that keeps it
different in a way rivals cannot copy, when you face that decision in your own business.**

**Real-world test:** ten new one-screen cases an owner meets (an offer, a plan, a rival's move, a page of numbers), never
seen in a lesson, mixed across every lesson, answered with no help. Pass: 8 of 10, and none of the three costly errors
(a goal taken for a strategy, growth for its own sake, a move that tries to serve everyone). The pass mark is **ours**
(judgement call); the three errors are the ones every source names (ST5, ST4, ST6, ST2, ST10).

## The way of thinking (from ST, section F)

The field has no single method; its main schools agree on six questions, asked in this order:
1. **Is this a strategy problem, or a running-the-business problem?** Doing the same things better is not strategy (ST1, ST16).
2. **What is the challenge, said plainly?** A target or a wish is not a diagnosis (ST5).
3. **Where does the money come from, and who takes the rest?** Value created is what customers would pay minus what
   suppliers and staff need; then which force squeezes it, and which customers and products earn most (ST6, ST7, ST8).
4. **What do we do differently, what does it give us, and what stops a rival copying it?** (ST1, ST2, ST3, ST9)
5. **The choice:** where to play and how to win, what we will not do, and actions that fit (ST2, ST3, ST5, ST10).
6. **Before betting:** what would have to be true, how similar bets turned out, how rivals will answer, and can we
   afford to lose it (ST11, ST12, ST13, ST14).

## What it teaches

The six questions, the value arithmetic, and the eight novice errors of section F of the research note, each named and
corrected (P29). Ask by ask: "what business school teaches" is the value-creation, value-capture and lasting-advantage
core (ST18), lessons 3 to 9; "at a high level" is questions 1, 2, 5 and 6, lessons 1, 2, 10 to 14; "move a business to
the next level" is lessons 5, 9 and 13 (find the core before growing, grow without blurring, bet small where the future
is unknown). Not taught: corporate portfolios, execution, finance, marketing (other subjects or outside the core, ST18).

## How every lesson runs

The rule and why (one screen, P9); one worked case through the questions taught so far (P10); a pair of cases with the same
deciding feature on different surfaces, then a look-alike pair (P12, Loewenstein et al. 1999 in P12); the owner's wrong
idea stated, marked wrong, corrected (P29); practice from memory with an explanation after every answer, with help
(`shown`, deciding lines marked) fading to none (P16, P19, 4.2); earlier lessons mixed in unlabelled (P22, `mix`); a check
on new cases. Cases are mostly owner-operator decisions (a facet `scale`: `owner`, `known`; owner cases at least 0.6 of
each set: our call, from Steven's ask), with the known-company cases the sources themselves use (Southwest, Continental
Lite, Maytag, Paccar, Vanguard). US, dollars. Retest at 28 days (R2, P24).

Subject lists (worded once, P5): `verdict` per lesson as below; `slip`: `goal-as-strategy`, `better-not-different`,
`growth-for-growth`, `serve-everyone` (straddling and something-for-everyone, ST2, ST10), `fast-growth-attractive`,
`list-not-choice`, `inside-view`, `rival-ignored`, `surface-analogy`, `bet-too-big`. Added after the round-2 reviews: `usual-way-as-fit` (lesson 7's check rule: the trade's usual way kept where it pulls against
the position, ST1, ST3) and `no-choice` (lesson 10's check rule: a plan that does not choose taken for one that does, ST10). `danger`: the three errors of the test.

## Judgement calls made while building lessons 1 to 5 (2026-10-23, after the two reviews)

- **Lesson 1 before lesson 2**, though question 1 comes first in the way of thinking: a learner has to read a plan for its
  challenge before asking what kind of challenge it is. Lesson 1's rule states the limit (a challenge that is only doing
  the same work worse than rivals is a running problem) and lesson 2 pairs the two (research F, the reconciliation).
- **Lesson 2 keeps a `tap`** of the line that settles it: finding the fact that shows the cause is the skill ST16 names
  ("make sure the problem is caused by ... strategy or competitors"). Two check stories hold facts that pull both ways.
- **Lesson 3 drops the `estimate`** (single subtractions of whole dollars) and the `tap`: a move is judged by two numbers
  weighed (what customers would pay more against what the move adds to the work), so its verdict is grows, shrinks or only
  moves (ST7: value created is WTP less WTS, and a move's own cost raises WTS).
- **Lesson 5's share** is of the total profit of the lines of one kind, losses taken off (ST19); its core is the line that
  is distinctive and earns the most (ST4, ST8). Its rule for a losing line, revised 2026-10-23 after the reviews of all
  fourteen lessons: first ask whether a change of price or of what it costs to serve could make it pay, and if so fix it
  (ST19: the customer-profitability literature makes losing customers pay before dropping them); if not, keep it and cut
  its loss when the core's customers buy it with the core or find the business through it; else let it go. The second
  test is ours (judgement call): it follows from judging a line by what would stop if it stopped. After the round-2 reviews the three outcomes are three
  answers (`fix`, `cut-loss`, `let-go`), so the marked answer always says what the rule says.

## Judgement calls made after the reviews of all fourteen lessons (2026-10-23, fixes-l1-14.md)

- **Lesson 6** decides by Porter's own test (ST2): does the new work pull against what the position needs? A pull with one
  side dropped is a trade-off, a pull with both kept a straddle, and an addition with no pull fits (a third verdict, `fits`),
  so lesson 1's and lesson 9's additions that drop nothing are not straddles.
- **Lesson 8's barriers** are "the common answers", not the whole list: a fourth, customers who would lose by leaving
  (switching costs, trust built over years), comes from ST9's sources; scale and network are named as others.
- **Lesson 10** adds a plan that chooses both parts whose way to win does not fit its customers (`misfit`), and a plan
  judged alone (`chooses`): the two parts are one choice (ST10).
- **Lesson 11** weighs which condition is least likely to hold (a fact against, then talk, then evidence), not which one
  lacks a proof word (ST11: the conditions the team most doubts).
- **Lessons 12 and 13** start from the BLS survival record (ST20): about half of new US establishments are open after five
  years. Lesson 13 asks first whether anyone can see people buying it yet (record, or try it small), and second, for every
  bet, what can be lost: the stake stays within the affordable loss either way (ST14 as a general habit, ST12's wide class).
- **Lesson 14's check** is ten fixed new whole decisions (k14), not a draw from the lessons' items, since every lesson item
  had been answered with feedback before; each costly error can be made in at least two of them; three more (r14) are held
  back for the review.

## Engine gaps (both optional; no lesson is blocked)

1. **The learner's own business** (lesson 5). Private forms are a fixed set (5.3, `OWN_SLOTS`). Smallest real design: a
   form `business: { lines: [{ name, kind: 'product' | 'customer' | 'channel', revenue, directCost }] }` (no account
   numbers, V81), and a work `core` in `own-work.js`: each line's contribution and share of the total, sorted, the lines
   that lose money flagged, and the top lines that make most of it named, ending with `SAY.workNotAdvice`. Why: P25 and D6
   (practice on the real case); Porter's questions are meant for one's own books (ST8).

   shares labelled (shapes in `tests/lessons/shapes.mjs`). Without it, a `document` `table` carries the numbers. Why: P30
   (draw the structure).
