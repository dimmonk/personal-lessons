# Stand-in review: Psychology, lesson 1 "What did they do?" (part A, the pilot), second review
Reviewer: a stand-in learner (Sonnet 5.5), 2026-10-12; opened: docs/learner-view/psychology-l1.md
Reviewed: sha256:cb7185c4ac80381a7614f43afc28c20886632e790462ec0105356831b835b973

I read the learner view only. I did not start the app, so this review judges the words and the order of the items, not how the screens look or feel on a phone.

## Before

The real task: a friend (or my own head) hands me a label about someone, and I say what the person actually did.

Honestly, as a beginner, I can do part of this by common sense. I can usually find the sentence that says "he left at 4:55" and tell it from "he is lazy". What I cannot do reliably: I would let a guess about why ("he wants attention", "she obviously thinks...") slide in as if it were something that happened, and I would not notice when an account contains nothing that was done at all. I have no habit of restating a label as plain events ("Dev ate the leftovers") before judging it.

## Notes

Where I was confused, or where something taught me nothing I used:

- Step 1 names three kinds of "not what they did": a word for the person, a guess about why, a feeling. Then Question 1 feedback uses two more, "background" and (from Question 10) "about the one telling it", which Step 1 never named. I worked them out from the first feedback, so it did not block me, but the list I was taught was shorter than the list I was tested on.
- Step 1 says "Some accounts have no line about what the person did. Then say so." The worked example (Step 2) has a doing line, so this idea is only said, never shown, until Question 4. In Questions 1 to 3 the "Nothing here says what they did" button was just a distractor that I ignored. Question 4 was where it started to mean something.
- Question 2, wrong tap on "That's the third time you've flaked on me": the feedback says "a count of times. It is not what he did this time." I paused here. A count of times does sound like things he did. It is a fair point (it is your tally and your word, not this one event), but the sentence is the one place the explanation made me doubt the rule instead of confirming it.
- Question 5: "She should have called first" is filed as "a word or a verdict about the person". It is a judgement about what she ought to have done. Fine for my answer, but the label does not quite fit it.
- Question 18, first part ("She was being weird at dinner. I just know she talks about me behind my back."). This is the hardest item and I nearly tapped it as the doing line. "Being weird" feels like behaviour. The lesson never taught that a vague description of behaviour ("weird", "off") is not something you could point to. Step 1's test ("something you could have seen or heard") covers it if you apply it carefully, but nothing earlier practised this case. Question 4 (the only earlier "nothing here" item) is much easier, because "toxic" is plainly a word.
- Question 12's feedback is long and ends with a hotline paragraph. It is the right content (it changed how I read the item), but on a phone it is a wall of text after a tap. I read all of it only because the content was serious.
- Question 10: the feedback says "on Monday and again on Wednesday", but the thread shows only "Monday to Wednesday", with no times per message. I could not tell which line was which day. Harmless, but the reason mentions something I could not see.
- Question 4 and Check question 18 both ask "What is the best next step?" with the same four options and the same right answer, "Find out what happened". By Question 18 this is memory, not thought. The four options (watch, set a limit, get outside help) were also never explained before Question 4; I could answer by elimination from the "Nothing here says what they did" wording in the wrong-answer feedback, and the line "First, find out what the person did" in the opening.
- The result line's example reads "4 of 5: passed" or "3 of 5: not yet", but the check is 6 questions with 5 needed. A beginner would see two different totals. Also unclear: Check question 18 has two parts. I cannot tell whether that is one question or two for the score.
- Position pattern (matters for the check, see below): the verdict ("X is so Y") is almost always the first line of a message or note. The doing line is the second line in most items (Questions 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11 and Check questions 13, 14, 15, 16 all have it second). The long line with a time, place or number is nearly always the right one. Someone who learned nothing could score well by spotting that.
- Screens that taught me nothing I used later: the "why" paragraph and the repeated "What counts as what they did" panel on every item (after Question 4, I stopped looking at it). The worked example's three-step working I did use for Question 5's "say it without the word".

Items I could answer without having learned anything from the lesson: Question 1 (both parts), Question 2 first part, Question 6, Question 7 and Question 13 are all answerable from common sense, because the verdict is a plainly "is so X" sentence and the event is the only concrete one. The items that needed the lesson were Question 4, Question 9, Question 10, Question 12, Check question 16, 17 and 18, where there are two answers, or none, or a vague line that looks like a doing.

- Check question 14 (Ben): "I turned the heat down to 60 while you were out." Right.
- Check question 15 (Sofia): "At the party she talked about her new job for the whole hour we were together." Right.
- Check question 16 (Leo's manager, two lines): "On Monday she read out my sales numbers..." and "Two weeks ago she corrected my mistake in front of a customer." Right.
- Check question 17 (Jess, two lines): "Can't come Thursday, sorry." and "Can't come Saturday either. Something came up." Right.
- Check question 18 (Priyanka): "Nothing here says what they did". Right. Next step, "Find out what happened". Right.

Score: 6 of 6 accounts right (pass needs 5). If the check scores Question 18's next-step part as its own item, 7 of 7. I was unsure about Question 18's first part for a moment (the "weird at dinner" line), and settled it by asking "could I have seen or heard this?".

## After

What I can do now that I could not (or would not reliably) do before, each tied to an item:

1. Split a short account into the line that says what was done and the lines that are a word for the person, a guess about why, a feeling, or background. Taught by Step 2's working ("Name what the other lines are") and drilled in Questions 1 to 8.
2. Restate a label without the label ("Rita rang the bell at midnight and woke the baby"). Taught by Step 2's third step and done in Questions 5 and 8 (the free-writing items). This is the closest thing to the real task and it is the one ability not scored by the check.
3. Pick out two separate things done when an account gives two (Cal's two loans, Alex's two shut-downs, Cole's two acts). Taught by Questions 9, 10, 12.
4. Notice when an account has no doing in it at all and say so, rather than treating a guess as a fact. Taught by Question 4 and used in Check question 18.
5. Notice that the person telling me (a friend or my own note) sits on one side, and that a feeling is evidence about the teller, not the other person. Taught in the "What you would need to see" lines of Questions 3, 4, 11.

Modest honesty: I could already do much of (1) by common sense. The new part is naming the kinds of non-doings, doing it reliably when the guess sounds convincing, and restating without the word.

## The five answers

**Gain:** I can take a one-sided account of a person and say what they actually did, apart from the label, the guess about why, and the feeling, including noticing when no doing is stated. (A model's judgement as a stand-in learner, not a real learner's.)

**Doing:** Yes. After a short Step 1 and a worked example, nearly all of the time is tapping or writing on a new account: about 12 practice items and 6 check items, each of them the real action on a message, thread or note. Only the "why" and Step 1 are reading.

**Check:** Yes, with limits. The check uses the same form as the real moment (a friend's message, a thread, my own note after an upset) on accounts I had not seen, and it tests picking the doing line, picking two, and spotting none. I passed 6 of 6. Limits: it tests recognising the line, not writing "what they did" in my own words (the two free-write items are unscored), and the answer position and "long concrete line" shortcuts mean a pass is weaker evidence than it looks.

**Clear:** A beginner could follow every screen on their own. Places that are not fully clear (none stops a beginner):
1. "Background" and "about the one telling it" are used in feedback but never taught in Step 1.
2. "Nothing here says what they did" is only said, not shown, until Question 4.
3. Question 2's "a count of times. It is not what he did this time" feedback can read as contradicting the rule.
4. Check question 18's "was being weird at dinner" is a vague-behaviour case that Step 1 never practised.
5. The result example "4 of 5" does not match the 6-question check, and it is unclear whether Question 18's second part is scored.
6. Question 10's reason names Monday and Wednesday, which are not shown on the lines.
7. Question 12's feedback is a long block on a phone.

**Waste:** Cut or merge without losing the gain: the repeated "What counts as what they did" panel and the full "each need found or missing" list after every item (I stopped reading it); two or three of the near-identical five-line items (Questions 1, 3, 7, 11 are the same shape, and Questions 6 and 13 and 14 are close); the second "best next step" question in Check question 18 (a repeat of Question 4's, and it tests memory); the "why" paragraph, which the first step restates.

**Verdict: passes.** Doing and Check are yes, the check was passed (6 of 6), and nothing under Clear blocks a beginner.

## Fixes (recommended, none needed for the verdict)

1. Show "no doing at all" once before Question 4, with a clear case in Step 2, or add a second example to Step 2. Teach "background" and "about the one telling it" in Step 1 so the list taught matches the list tested.
2. Practise one vague-behaviour line ("was being weird", "acted off") as a non-doing before Check question 18, so the check does not test something not yet taught.
3. Vary where the doing line sits (not mostly second) and let a verdict sometimes come later or inside a longer line, so position and "the line with a time" do not give the answer away.
4. Add one check item that is scored on production: pick the best plain restatement of the doing from four, or score the free-write by a simple rule. Today the ability that is closest to the real task is not in the check.
5. Make the result example match the check (6 questions, 5 needed) and state whether Check question 18's next-step part counts. Drop the repeat of the same next-step question in the check, or change its content.
6. Reword Question 2's feedback for "third time you've flaked" so it does not read as if a count of times were not about what he did (say it is your tally, and the one thing he did this time is in the first line). Move "She should have called first" to its own label ("what she should have done: a verdict").
7. Show the day on Question 10's thread lines, or drop the weekdays from the reason. Split Question 12's feedback so the hotline message is its own short screen.
