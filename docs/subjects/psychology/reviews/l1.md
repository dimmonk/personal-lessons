Stand-in review, 2026-10-11, of revision 1 (draft). I read only the subject's endResult, realMoment and test, the Gate 4

## Before

When a friend texts "my manager is toxic", I sympathize and agree, or ask a vague "what happened?" without knowing what I
am listening for. If you showed me five lines and asked which one is what the person did, I would usually pick the
concrete one, so the sorting itself is not new to me. Two things I would not do: notice that a whole message contains
nothing the person did, and restate the complaint without the label. I have no names for the other kinds of lines (a
guess, a feeling, a word for the person).

## After: what I can do now that I could not before

1. When a friend's message has a label and feelings but no act, I notice it and ask what the person actually did
   (taught by Q4, tested by k-fake).
2. I can say a complaint without the label, in one plain sentence ("Rita rang the bell at midnight and woke the baby"),
   and I spot the "softer word" trap (Q1, Q2, Q5, Q8, worked example).
3. I can name the other lines as a guess about why, a feeling, a word for the person, or background. Seeing the
   background next to the label (stuck train, late flight, shift ends at 5, scary bill) gave me a first sense that the
   situation can explain the act (Q7, Q5, k-lazy, k-heat).

## The five answers

- **Gain: yes.** Noticing that an account holds no act and asking what was done, and saying it without the label. Both
  are concrete moves for the real moment. The gain is narrower than the item count suggests, because sorting cleanly
  separated lines was mostly within reach before the lesson.
- **Doing: yes.** Almost all the time is spent on accounts in the real forms: tapping, choosing or writing a rewrite.
  The one heavy reading load is the repeated checklist block after each answer.
- **Check: yes.** New accounts in the friend, thread and note forms, testing part A's ability. It is weakly
  discriminating, though. Only k-fake tests noticing that there is no act, and a learner can miss it and still pass 5
  of 6.
- **Clear: yes, with friction, nothing blocking.** Places I stalled: the Jonas reason (his own reason against a guessed
  one), "a count of times" as a non-act, the untaught "background" and "teller" categories, the next-step question with
  no teaching (Q4, k-fake), "find out what happened" in a note about an event I was at, and the Found/Found split on
  two-line items.
- **Waste:** the "What counts... Found/Found" block after every answer. Show it only when the learner missed, or only
  on "none" items. The panel repeated on the worked example and Q1 to Q4 could also go, since it already sits beside the
  account as help. Q11 adds nothing that Q3 did not.

## Safety wording on the control account (Q12, Cole)

- **Accurate.** The National Domestic Violence Hotline number 1-800-799-7233 and "text START to 88788" are correct. "Free
  and private, at any hour" is true. "A friend can call too" is true, and important here because the learner is the
  friend. Advising a device the person cannot see is standard safety-planning advice.
- **Calm.** Yes. It does not diagnose ("one message cannot say what it adds up to") and it does not alarm. "Doubting
  yourself" is the weakest of the three signs on its own, but "all together" covers it.
- **Useful, with three gaps.** (1) Nothing says what to do in immediate danger: add one clause, "If someone is in danger
  now, call 911." (2) The number is US-only and the text does not say so. Add "in the US", and a pointer for elsewhere
  if the app may be used outside it. (3) The notice sits at the end of a long reason paragraph, right after "Here you
  only tap what he did", which on a phone reads at first like brushing it aside. Put it in its own short block, set off
  from the reason, so it is not skimmed past.

   me in front of everyone"), with the fair rewrite. Put one in the check. This is the real-moment form, and right now

3. Vary the surface cues. Some guesses without "obviously", some labels without "so", and the act line not always
   second.
4. Put one line on the card for the person's own stated reason (it counts as what they said) and one for background.
   Either teach the next step in one line where it first appears (Q4), or leave it out of L1.
5. Show the checklist block only on a miss or a "none" item. Fix the Found/Found split on two-line items.
6. Make the three safety-wording changes above.
7. Smaller ones. Retag "She should have called first" and "You never respect my stuff" (a verdict, not a word for the
