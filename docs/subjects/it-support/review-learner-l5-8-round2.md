## Way of deciding, written before the first practice question

- L5: List every cause, including the favorite. Pick the test whose two results point at different causes and which is cheap (a minute, changes nothing): another person, the same person on another computer, a known-good part, before or after sign-in, can it reach a web page. A restart, a reinstall, a repeat or a test of something not on the list is never the test. Cut again until one cause is left.
- L6: Work in rounds: list the causes, run a test, cross off only the causes that result rules out (a cause no test touched stays), change one thing for the next cause, look, write down what changed and what happened. A clean test means move on, never try another of the same. If two things changed at once, the note says the cause is not known.
- L7: Close only when the user, on their own work, says it works; silence is not yes, so follow up another way. Hand on when the desk rule says so, with their words, what you tried in order, what each test showed, and the setup, and tell the user who has it and when they hear. The note holds their words, the setup, what fixed it, what caused it (say "likely" if not proved). The same fault enough times (the desk's number) is raised as a problem.
- L8: Do not argue, dismiss or judge. Say back their facts and what has stopped them, say sorry for the trouble, confirm who they are before any reset or unlock, say your next step and a time, keep the time even with no news, promise steps and never a result, use plain words.

## Numbered defects and fixes

### L5

1. Item 14 (Nina, three swaps): the text never says Dee's monitor and Dee's computer work. Test 3 only proves Nina's cable is bad if Dee's setup is good. Fix: add "Dee's monitor and computer work" to the table title or stem, as check 16 already does.
2. L5 item 9 (turn 1) and check 19 (turn 1): "How old is the computer" and "When did it last work" are marked as slips ("ran a test that does not split"). The subject's own goal is to get the user's story, so a beginner is told a good move is wrong. Fix: change the slip text to "a question for later; first split the causes" or accept story questions and only fail restarts and orders.
3. "Port" is used in the Step 2 table and Step 3 swap text but only explained in Item 6. Fix: define "port" in Step 2 where "a port" first appears.
4. "Memory" ("Add memory", items 10, 17), "Wi-Fi card" (item 8), "factory settings" (item 12) are never explained. Fix: a short gloss on first use.
5. Step 2 says a good test "changes nothing", but a swap moves a cable or part, which is a change. Item 2 says moving the monitor "changes nothing". Fix: say "changes nothing in the user's setup that you cannot put back".
6. Item 4 marks "check Caps Lock and retype" as no test. A real desk does this first. Fix: say it is a quick known fix to do before the list, and rewrite the distractor so it does not look like standard practice.

Patterns that give answers without reasoning (L5): options with "reinstall", "restart", "once more", "order a new" are always wrong. The right answer is nearly always the one that moves the problem to another person, computer or part. The Item 12 table gives the answer away (one minute, changes nothing). Item 8's right answer is the only one with the word "list". Fix: put at least two items where the right test is not "another person or computer", and where a repeat-looking option is right.

### L6

7. The lesson says "take the next cause on the list" but never says how to pick which. Items 3, 10, 14 and check 14 only work by using the story clue ("began after the password change"). Fix: add a line in Step 2: take next the cause the user's story or the cheapest test points to.
8. Step 1 says a test "changes nothing", but "a part known to work, put where the doubtful one was" is called the clearest test, and Item 11 then counts "change the cable and move to another port" as changes. A beginner cannot tell when a swap is a test and when it is a change. Fix: say a known-good swap counts as the test, and only a swap that is meant to fix counts as a change.
9. Step 1 says several trivial changes together are allowed ("not a law"), yet item 8 marks "updates and cleanup together" as a slip. Fix: either drop the caveat or add to item 8 that these changes are not trivial because the cause matters.
10. "Router" (items 1, worked example), "forget the saved network", "toner" (check 15) are not explained. Fix: gloss each on first use.
11. Items 7, 11 and check 13 are the same item (tech says "I was sure it was X, I will try another"). The answer is "cross off ... and test the next". All "two changes together" options contain "and"/"together". Fix: vary the shape. Include one where a combined change is harmless and one where crossing off is wrong because of a second fault.

### L7

12. Step 1 says have the user try it first, then tell them what happens next. Item 9 and Item 1 put "tell the user what you did, what to try" before "the user tries". Fix: reword Step 1 to tell first, then try, then yes.
13. A silent user: Items 10 and check 20 keep the ticket open with no end. Real desks close after a set number of follow-ups. Fix: say the desk's rule sets how many follow-ups and days, and until then the ticket stays open and is not closed as fixed.
14. Notes in items 5 to 7 and in the Priya sample are labelled "Your own note, written after an argument", also for colleagues' drafts, hand-on notes and for a user's chat reply (L8 items 3, 8, 12). Fix: label by author ("Draft from a colleague", "A user's message").
15. Item 5's note says "Caused by: a damaged Office file after an update" as fact, and the item says the line is fine. Step 3 says to mark a likely cause as likely. Fix: change to "likely".
16. "ITIL 4", "knowledge-centered service" and "A+" are named with no gloss. "Moves 7 to 9" and "move 8 stands in for move 7" read as unexplained numbering. Fix: one-line gloss, and name the move instead of the number.
17. Item 14: the "right" quote reworded his words ("big sales spreadsheet" for "the big spreadsheet from the sales folder"), against "not relabelled". Fix: use his exact words shortened.

Patterns (L7): every right answer says "tell her what you changed", is the longest, and keeps the ticket open until the user says yes. Answers starting "Close" are wrong in nearly every item. Fix: add items where closing now is right (the user has already confirmed) so the word alone does not decide.

### L8

18. Check 18: the "right" reply says "give me her number and I call her". The number comes from the unverified caller. Fix: "I call her on the number we have on file".
19. Item 9: "give me their name and I call now" has the same hole. Fix: look the manager up in the company directory, do not take the caller's name or number.
20. "Escalate" means hand on to a senior technician in L7 but "going over your head to your manager" in L8 (check 15, check 14, glossed in each stem). Fix: use "complain to your manager" in L8, keep "escalate" for hand-on.
21. Step 3 says "an explanation from the employee lowered anger". Items 6 and 8 and check 16 reward giving an explanation. Fix: say what the finding means: a defending explanation lowers satisfaction, a plain-words cause on request raises it.
22. The "right" replies state facts the scenario never gave: check 16 ("I have checked that the folder exists and that your account can reach it"), item 11 turn 2 ("it is in the stock room"), item 6 ("some computers are still looking in the old place", a diagnosis nobody made). Fix: put the facts in the stem, or make the reply say only what you will do and when.
23. Worked example step 4 and item 4 raise "three lockouts in a week" as a problem, but L7 says the count is the desk's rule, and no rule is given here. Fix: add the rule to the stem.
24. Item 13: the stem quote lacks "locked out of my laptop" and "client", but the right answer contains both. Fix: put the full quote in the stem.
25. Item 4 turn 2 right reply promises "the unlock takes two minutes", which is a result time. Item 10 turn 1 right reply says "this afternoon", not a clock time. Fix: give a time you control ("I stay on the line until you are in") and use clock times everywhere.

Patterns (L8): "I am sorry, but ..." is always wrong, "I understand" or "let me look into it" or "I will pass it on" always goes on to a second turn, and the right reply is almost always the longest and has a clock time. Fix: make one right reply short, and one wrong reply long with a time.
