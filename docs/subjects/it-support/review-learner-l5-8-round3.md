## Way of deciding, per lesson

**L5.** List every cause that fits the story, favorite included. Then pick the cheapest test whose two results point at different causes: another person on the same computer, the same person on another computer, a known-good part swapped in, before or after sign-in, can it reach the internet. Reject restarts, reinstalls, repeats of a try already made, and tests of something not on the list. Cut again until one cause is left. If results contradict each other, suspect a second fault.

**L6.** Work in rounds: write the causes, run a test that changes nothing, cross off only what the result truly rules out, change ONE thing for the cheapest cause left, look, write down what changed and what happened. A clean test crosses the idea off for good, even a favorite. A cause no test touched stays on the list. If a known-good swap fixes it, keep it as the one change.

**L7.** Close only after the user tries it on their own machine and says it works. Silence is not a yes, so follow up on the desk's schedule. Hand on when the desk's rule says so (time box, team, access), with their words, what you tried in order, what each test showed, and the setup, and tell the user who has it and when they will hear. The note holds their words, the setup, what fixed it, and the cause (marked "likely" if not proved). The same fault on enough tickets under the desk's rule becomes a problem.

**L8.** First reply: say back what they told me, including the work they cannot do. Say sorry for the trouble, with no blame and no defense. Then give my own next step and a time I will write or call, in plain words, and keep that time even with no news. Never promise a result. Check identity with the desk's own check before any reset or unlock, however hurried. If I need more, an open question can be the whole first move. A one-minute polite check by the user before closing is not arguing.

## Defects

1. **BLOCKER (L7, Step 5 vs Step 7 Item 9).** Item 9 marks the order "tell the user, user says it works, write the note, close". The worked example teaches a different order: it says "Now the ticket can close" and only then "Write what the next tech needs", and the answer says "the note then gives...". No step before Item 9 says the note comes before the close, so a beginner following the example puts close before note and is marked wrong. Fix: reorder the worked example so the note is step 3 before the close, change its final sentence to say the note is written and then the ticket closes, and state the order once in Step 1 or Step 3 ("write the note, then close").

2. MINOR (L5, Item 10). The right answer has Sia restart and watch where the wait comes. Step 3 teaches "a restart is a fix or a repeat, not a test". The restart here is only a way to watch the start-up, but a beginner may reject it. Fix: reword the option as "watch the next start-up, or a restart Sia was going to do anyway, and note whether the wait is before or after sign-in", or add one sentence to Step 3 that a restart can be where you watch, as long as you do not count its result as the fix.

3. MINOR (L5, Q16). The right test, "open a web page on her phone on the same Wi-Fi", is a second device, which is not in the Five cheap tests table. Fix: add "another device on the same network" as a row or fold it into row 5.

4. MINOR (L5, Item 11). The reason says "Ben's own mouse works on his computer" but the ticket never states this; the table only gives two swaps. Fix: add a line "Ben's mouse and computer work normally" to the ticket.

5. MINOR (L5 Items 9 and Q20, vs L7/L8). The "right" reply on the calls gives no sorry, no next step for the user and no time ("I will check the shared folder itself"). Later lessons teach exactly those. A learner could think the L5 style is complete. Fix: have the right replies end with a step and a time, or add a line to the reason that the call manner is taught in lesson 8.

6. MINOR (L6, Step 4). The worked example tests the favorite (phone join, one minute) before the ten-second look at the switch, while Step 2 says take the cheapest test. A beginner sees two rules. Fix: say in step 2 of the working that the favorite goes first by L5's rule, or reorder so the switch comes first and the favorite stays on the list.

7. MINOR (L6, Item 7). The right answer, "plug the laptop into its own charger", needs a fact the ticket does not give (that Mo has a separate laptop charger), and its tail "that tests the next cause" gives the answer away. Fix: add "Mo's laptop also came with its own charger" to the ticket and drop the tail.

8. MINOR (L7, Item 2). The right answer offers "a loaner dock if there is one", but the ticket never says whether one exists. Fix: state "the desk has one loaner dock free" in the setup.

9. MINOR (L7, Item 11). Hand-on A holds one clue B lacks ("started after she changed her password"), yet B is marked right and A is called incomplete. Arguable. Fix: give B the same password line, or remove that line from A.

10. MINOR (L7, Item 12 right answer). It promises "you will check in at the end of the day", which is a new commitment not in the desk's table. Fix: use "I will write you at 5:00 with where it stands", as lesson 8 Item 10 does, and state the time in the setup.

11. MINOR (L8, Check Q15 and Item 15 marked answers). The ticket says the arrival of the part is unknown, yet the marked-right replies promise to tell her "the delivery time" by 4. That is a result outside the technician's hands, which is what the pass rule forbids. Fix: "I will write you by 4 with whatever the supplier has told me, even if it is no news, and I will check for a loaner for tomorrow."

12. MINOR (L8, Check Q17). The right reply says "if not, I send the approval request now", but no approval request process is given. Fix: state in the ticket that the desk can send an approval request for programs not on the list.

13. MINOR (L8, Check Q14 vs the rest). The right first reply is an open question with no step or time, and it ends the call. Step 2 does allow this ("the question can be your whole first move"), but it is the opposite of the other items, where a missing time is a wrong answer. Fix: add to the item's setup that you know almost nothing yet, or add a second item that tests this exception.

14. MINOR (L8, Step 3). It says an explanation lowered anger, but Step 1 says explaining is arguing. The line "give the cause in plain words; do not use an explanation to defend the desk" resolves it, but only in a sentence at the end. Fix: put an example pair in Step 3 (explaining why the printer failed vs explaining why the earlier tickets were closed).

15. MINOR (pattern answers, all four lessons). Answers can be found without reasoning. L5: choose whichever option brings in another person, computer or device. L6: choose the option that crosses off the tested cause and changes exactly one thing. L7: choose the longest note, or the one with Their words/Setup/Fixed by/Cause fields; "Not given" is the only option with no pattern. L8: choose the reply with sorry plus a concrete step plus a time; wrong replies are the ones that say "I understand", "feel that way", "soon", "do my best" or "definitely". Fix: in a few L7 items make the wrong note the long structured one with a wrong fact (a relabelled cause stated as proved), and in L8 add wrong replies that have sorry, a step and a time but break another rule (defend, promise, skip the check).

16. MINOR (IT words used before they are explained). "Browser" (L5 Q16, L6 Item 6/Q12), "configured/setup" (L5 Items 7, 19, L6 Q12), "router" (explained inline only in L5 Q16 and L6's why), "Office/Office 365/Teams/Settings app" (L7), "update logs" (L8 Item 2, explained only in Q14), "print spooler" (L8 Item 1, explained only after the answer). Fix: one inline plain gloss at first use in each lesson.

17. MINOR (facts the ticket does not give). L6 Item 6 relies on Ken's laptop using the same office Wi-Fi as the co-worker's phone (not stated). L7 Item 3's rule is in the log header but the 20 minutes are only implied by the time stamps. Fix: add the one line to each ticket.
