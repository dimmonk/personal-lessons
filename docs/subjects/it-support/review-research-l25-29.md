# IT Help Desk lessons 25 to 29: research and fact check (2026-10-23)

Read: plan, both research notes, both rulings files, l25..l29 lesson, items, check and reserve files, subject.js strands, lessons 15, 21, 22 where they overlap. No node scripts run. Two small read-only Python scans were run (right-option length, name roles); counts below come from them and are approximate (a regex over the option rows).

Sources read for uncertain facts:
- CompTIA Core 2 2.6 ten-step list: the A+ map (copy of CompTIA's objectives hosted by onlc, "version 2.0") gives the ten steps the lesson uses. I could NOT reach CompTIA's own text. A CompTIA community post (cin.comptia.org/posts/23799) says 220-1202 has 10 removal steps against 7 in 220-1102 (a learner's post, no list). Professor Messer's 1202 page and Infosec Learning's 1202 lab still teach the 7-step grouping (quarantine includes disabling System Restore; remediate includes update, scan, safe mode; reimage is a branch of remediate). The lesson's order is the A+ map's order and is consistent with both groupings. State in the lesson file that the exact wording was not seen on CompTIA's own page.
- Windows format: Microsoft KB 941961 ("Change in the behavior of the format command in Windows Vista and later"): a full format writes zeros to the whole disk; quick format (/q) does not. (learn.microsoft.com/en-us/troubleshoot/windows-server/backup-and-storage/format-command-not-write-zeros-to-disk)
- Degaussing and SSDs: NIST SP 800-88 Rev 1 as summarised by bitraser.com and erecycler.com (secondary): degaussing does not work on flash; flash needs ATA Secure Erase / crypto erase / sanitize, or destruction; plain overwrite can miss over-provisioned areas.
- BitLocker editions: Microsoft Support (Device Encryption): BitLocker Drive Encryption is Pro, Enterprise, Education; Home has device encryption only, turned on automatically only with a Microsoft or work account. Lesson 25 ("Pro and above") and lesson 22 agree.
- Known, not re-fetched: WinRM ports 5985/5986; SSH 22; RDP 3389; NTFS-versus-share "most restrictive wins" over the network and NTFS only at the console; NTFS Write cannot delete, Modify can; MSDS now SDS; 3-2-1 and GFS; Type 1 / Type 2 hypervisors; UAC behavior; Windows Home cannot join a domain; SPICE is the KVM/QEMU remote display with audio and USB redirection; df columns (the l29 df table is internally consistent: 16/(16+22)=42%).

## BLOCKERS

**B1. Right option is the longest too often (ruling 8). Lesson 25, 28 (also 27).** Share of questions whose right option is the longest of four (chance is about 25%): items-l25-reserve.js 12 of 15; items-l25.js 10 of 19; items-l28-reserve.js 8 of 16; items-l27-reserve.js 6 of 10; items-l25-check.js 1 of 7, items-l28.js 2 of 16 (fine). The l25 reserve and group 1/2 right options carry a definition clause ("An access control vestibule, with a second door that opens only after the first has shut") that the wrong options do not. Both exchanges (l25-q-call "check" and l26-g-call "safe") are also the longest and the only one with a stated plan. Fix: shorten l25 and l28 reserve rights, lengthen wrong options to the same shape, rewrite so the right option has no extra clause the wrong ones lack. Re-measure.

**B2. l25-k-bitlocker (items-l25-check.js).** The right option, "the recovery code saved when BitLocker was turned on", needs a fact the ticket never gives: that a key was saved and where (desk records, directory, Microsoft account). Without it the correct move is not that. Ruling 13. Fix: add a row to the ticket ("From the desk's records: recovery key ID 4F2A-9C10 is stored for this laptop") and make the right option "Look the key up by the ID shown and enter it".

**B3. Lesson 26 "standard format" and "low-level format" contradict lesson 21 (ruling 21).** l26.lesson.js table: "Standard format: clears the list of files; data often recoverable; never alone", "Low-level format: writes over the whole drive". Items l26-p-wipe, l26-q-vendor, l26-g-ssd, l26-r-dead, l26-r-format ("standard format only clears the list"). Lesson 21 (f1, g3, x-items) and Microsoft KB 941961 teach: `format` without /q writes zeros over the whole drive, `format /q` and diskpart `clean` do not. A learner who just learned lesson 21 reads "standard format, data recoverable" as wrong, or learns that a full format is not a wipe. Also no real user can do a true low-level format of a modern drive; A+ uses the term for a write-over. Fix: define "standard format" as the quick format (A+ wording) and say in one line that a Windows full format (no /q) writes zeros and counts as a wipe for a drive that is reused (not for an SSD, see M4); say "low-level format" in the A+ sense as a write-over done with a tool; make every item say "quick (standard) format". Also teach the SSD rule (wipe by the maker's secure-erase or destroy).

**B4. Odd-one-out "No" answers (rulings 8 and 20). l28-q-ai and l28-k-policy.** Each has one option starting "No:" (right) and three starting "Yes". The learner needs no knowledge. Fix: vary the openings (right option "Yes, but only the order number", wrong ones "No, ..." too, or recast as "What do you do?"). Also l28-q-ai's right text is garbled: "may keep what is typed in, even data" (missing a word, probably "even card data"), and a good answer also reports the leak to management (move 8, lesson 27) because card numbers already went out.

**B5. Lesson 29 part one has no gate and does not test what the plan says (plan "Real-world test").** Plan: "(1) the job: 10 new tickets in their real form... pass when the first move is right on 8 and no ticket loses a user's data or is closed without the user's confirmation (lesson 29's check, part one)". Reality: the ten tickets are a plain `set` with no pass rule (l29.lesson.js); the only check is the 40-question mock. Each ticket is a single multiple-choice first move; whether data is lost or a ticket closed early is not measured end to end (only two decoys carry those labels). Six of the ten are plain chats; one email, two with tables, no phone exchange; "real form" is only partly true. Fix: either put the ten tickets in a graded part with a pass of 8 and no data-loss or early-close pick (the engine may need a second check), or reword the plan to say what lesson 29 does (an ungraded rehearsal of ten first moves plus the mock). Do not call it the "real-world test" of job readiness; it is a self-test on our own items.

**B6. Lesson 29 mock is neither proportional to the domain weights nor from every exam topic (plan: "in proportion to the domain weights... from every exam topic").** The l29 code comment already says "not weighted" but the plan still claims it. The draw lists 76 strands, one pool each. By strand count: Core 1 about 41 of 76 (hardware 17, networks 17, mobile 7), Core 2 about 35 (operating systems 17, security 10, operations 8, Windows troubleshooting 2). Against the weights (A+ map section 1): Core 2 software troubleshooting 23% gets about 3% of the draw (win-boot-fix, win-run-fix); Core 2 security 28% about 13%; Core 1 virtualization and cloud 11% about 3% (virtual, cloud); Core 1 hardware troubleshooting is over-represented. The eight strands of lessons 1 to 8 are not in the draw, so Core 2 4.1 (ticketing, asset management, SOPs, SLAs) and 4.7 (communication) are never on the mock. And the pool is the same questions the learner already practised, so 80% measures memory of our items, not the real exam (plan already calls the margin a judgement call; say the pool is the same). Fix: either weight (draw a stated number per domain: engine permitting) or edit the plan and the learner text to "drawn from lessons 9 to 28, one pool per topic, not weighted"; add the lesson 1 to 8 exam strands (4.1, 4.7) or say they are not on it; tell the learner the mock reuses practised questions.

**B7. l29-t-phish: resets with no identity check, as the first move (rulings 6 and 11).** Right option "Reset her password, sign out her sessions, and report it". Ruling 6: before any reset the technician confirms who is asking by the desk's own check; l25-q-call and l26-g-call both teach it. The lesson 29 recap also says "Never start with a part, a reset or a reinstall", which this right option breaks. Fix: "Confirm it is Cleo by the desk's own check, then reset her password, end her sessions and report it, keeping the email".

**B8. l29-r-ads (items-l29-reserve.js): skips step 1.** Right option "Take it off the network, then follow the malware removal steps", feedback "Quarantine comes before scanning". Lesson 26 (l26-p-first, l26-r-quar) teaches verify first, quarantine second, and marks quarantine-first as wrong ("ORD"). The ticket has clear signs, but the exact order is the point of the objective. Fix: right option "Verify it is malware (home page, pop-ups, Task Manager), then take it off the network", with quarantine as the next step in the feedback.

**B9. Names (ruling 22: each name has one job across the subject).** Violated throughout. Lessons 25 to 29 add to the clash; the earlier lessons already had it. Fix: one rename pass: a name table (name, one job, lesson), regenerate speakers from it; Priya's worked tickets in l25 and l26 should be two different people.
