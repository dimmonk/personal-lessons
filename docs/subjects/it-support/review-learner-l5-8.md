# Cold learner review: IT Help Desk lessons 5 to 8

Caveat: the learner views print the right answer beside each question, so I could not truly answer blind. I wrote my way of deciding first, then answered each check from it and only afterwards compared with the marked answer. Scores below are from that reasoning. Pattern-answerability is the weak spot (see each lesson).

## My way of deciding

L5: List every cause (at least two), then pick the test, not the fix. A good test is cheap, changes nothing, and its two results point at different causes (another person, another computer, a known-good part, before or after sign-in, can a web page open). Restarts, reinstalls, repeats, orders and resets are fixes or repeats and rule nothing out. Facts already known clear causes, so list only what is left.

L6: Change one thing, then look, and write down what you did. After a test, cross off only the causes its result could not have produced; causes no test touched stay. A known-good part that did not help rules the old part out, so do not try the same part again; move to the next cause on the list.

L7: Close only after the user tries it on their own machine and says yes (unless the desk rule's wait runs out). When the time limit or desk rule says so, hand on with: their words, what you tried in order, what each test showed, the device and setup, and tell the user who has it and when they hear. Note = their words, setup, fix, cause (say "not confirmed" when likely). Same fix repeated for the same symptom is a problem: hand on the pattern.

L8: With an upset user: do not argue, dismiss or judge. Say back what they said and what it cost them, say sorry for the trouble, give your next step and a time you will report back, use plain words, never promise a result you do not control, and keep the time even with no news.

## Defects

### L5

1. Pattern-answerable. In every "which test" item, the right answer is the only option that involves another person, computer or device; every wrong option is reinstall, restart, order, reset or "try again". I could answer Items 1 to 4, 6, 10, 11, 13, 15 and 18 without reading the scenario. Fix: add wrong options that are plausible tests that do not split (for example "ask a co-worker to print a different, already-working document on Lena's own laptop", or a co-worker test that tests the wrong side), and one item where the splitting test is a part swap or "before sign-in" test while a co-worker option is the distractor.
2. Item 10 feedback for "Ben's computer is the fault" is confusing ("that is the mouse moving its trouble with it"). Fix: "Ben's own mouse works on his computer, so his computer is fine."
3. Item 12 table has a distracting row ("Marco signs in on Ava's laptop, works"). It is irrelevant to the answer and the stated reason (fails before sign-in) alone decides. Fix: either drop it or make the item need it.
4. Check 13 is arguable. "Connected to Wi-Fi but no website opens": a phone on the same Wi-Fi splits laptop from Wi-Fi and internet together, but the listed causes are three, so it only cuts to one versus two. Fix: say the test "cuts the list" not "in half", or list two causes.
5. Check 15: "have a co-worker sign in on Lin's laptop" assumes the desk can sign another person in. Beginner may doubt it. Fix: add "a co-worker with an account" or use a test account.
6. Step 5 and Item 8 ("test the cheapest one first") conflicts mildly with Step 1 ("pick the test, not the fix") in that the "favorite's test" is called cheapest by default. Fix: say "the cheapest test, which may or may not be the favorite's".
7. Unexplained words: "router", "port", "server" (explained only once), "sign-in" versus "sign in on another computer" (needs an account on that computer), "Windows reinstall" implied to be huge but never explained, "settings".
8. Item 9 and Check 17 calls: only the second reply is both right and the only one with a question; the "restart" and "order" replies are always wrong. Same pattern issue as defect 1.

### L6

1. Contradiction in order of steps. Item 5 orders: causes, change, check, note, cross off. Check 16 orders: causes, test, cross off, change, check. Step 2 says crossing off follows a test result. A learner who learned Item 5 gets Check 16 wrong (or the reverse). Fix: use one order everywhere: list, test, cross off, change one thing, look, note.
2. Item 10 and Check 14 ("first move") name a fix before any test. L5 taught test before fix; here "forget the saved network" or "correct the email address" is right only because it is a single change. Nothing in the stem says why the saved password or address is the cause to try first. Fix: add a stated reason (a clue in the ticket) or ask "which is a single change?" instead of "first move".
3. Check 14 (scanner) is a coin flip on reasoning: three causes, no clue, correct answer is the one that changes only one thing, which I got by "shortest answer". Fix: as in 2.
4. Pattern: in every "what should the tech do / first move" item, the wrong options contain "and", "together", "one more". The right answer is the one without "and". Fix: include a wrong option that is a single change on a cause already ruled out, and a right option that mentions two actions that are not changes (for example "write the note and check").
5. Item 6 and Check 12 multi-select: a beginner may doubt that "a second browser also failed" rules out the browser as cause (it rules out that browser, not browsers in general). Fix: reword the cause to "the first browser's own settings".
6. Check 12: "a web page opens" also does not clearly rule out the Wi-Fi for the shared folder (a different network path is possible). The marked answer treats it as ruled out. Fix: state in the stem that the folder is reached over the same Wi-Fi.
7. Item 9: "Marcus's laptop signed in as Marcus" rules out the account, yet the mail app on the laptop differs. Acceptable but arguable. Fix: say "same mail account".
8. Unexplained: "power-save mode", "docking station" (explained in L7 not here), "port", "mail program's settings", "network settings".
9. Item 4 and Check 15 note items: the correct answer is the only one that is not a single-cause claim. Same pattern issue.

### L7

1. Strong length pattern. In Items 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, Check 15 and 17, the right answer is the longest and most detailed. Wrong options are short ("Close the ticket", "Fixed"). I could pass without reading. Fix: make wrong options as long and as polite as the right one (for example a long wrong note with a story, a long wrong reply that closes early with a courteous tone).
2. Check 17 and Item 4: the longest note with quoted words and tests is always the right one. Same fix: add a wrong note that is long and detailed but lacks the test results.
3. Item 7 and Check 18 tap-the-line items: wrong lines carry explicit "That is the setup, not the fix", so a beginner can find the fix by elimination. Fine, but only one correct sentence is findable by the words "Signed out and back in". Fix: add a second change line that did not work.
4. Item 10 vs Step 1: "close it after N working days" is introduced only as a desk rule in Item 10. The Check pass rule ("none closed the ticket before the user confirmed") can conflict with this rule. Fix: say in Step 1 that the desk rule may close after the wait, and that this is not a closure on your own test.
5. Item 8 and Check 19: the "pattern is a problem" idea is stated once in Step 3 in one line, with the word "problem" used in the ITIL sense. A beginner reads "problem" as "trouble". Fix: define "a problem = the cause behind many tickets" and "an incident = one ticket".
6. Item 13 stem allows only one sensible answer; the wrong options are labelled "user error" and "phone problem". Pattern: choose the longest.
7. Item 14 "their words" paraphrases the user ("It is slow since the update, mostly when...") while Step 3 says "as they said it". Contradiction with L8 Item 12, which also rewrites the user's words as a tidied phrase. Fix: teach "their words, shortened but not relabelled", or require a verbatim quote.
8. Unexplained: VPN (only in parentheses in Item 3), "print service", "mail profile", "cannot reproduce", "hotspot", "driver", "knowledge-centered service", "knowledge base" is explained, "queue".
9. Item 9 ordering: note before close. Step 5 says the note is written after the user confirms (consistent), but Step 1 says "tell them what happens next" before the user tries it. Fine but Item 9 hides the "what happens next / how to reach you" step. Fix: add it to the order list.
10. Check 15: one pass rule ("none closed before confirm") is only testable on one question; a learner can hit it once only. Fix: add one more scenario where early closing is a distractor.

### L8

1. Strong pattern. In every call and chat item the right reply is the longest, contains "I am sorry" and a clock time, and every wrong reply is short ("I will pass it on", "Let me look into it", "Soon"). I could pass L8 with no reading. Fix: add wrong replies that also say sorry and give a time but argue or promise a result ("I am sorry, it will definitely be fixed by 3:30"), and a right reply that is short.
2. Contradiction with the rule "never promise a result you do not control". Item 6's right reply says the problem "clears by itself, usually within an hour" and "should work by about 2:30". Both are results outside your hands. Fix: reword to "These changes usually spread within an hour; I will call you at 3 either way."
3. Check 13 right first reply gives no next step and no time (only a question). The rule in Step 2 says question then next step and time. A learner who follows the lesson might reject it. Fix: add a time, or teach that an open question can stand alone as the first move.
4. Check 15 and Item 4: "I understand. It is frustrating." is marked wrong because it gives no facts. But Step 2 teaches "say back what they said", so "I understand" feels close. Fix: say in Step 2 that "I understand" alone is not saying back what they said.
5. The check pass rule "None argued with the user or brushed off what they said" covers only two of the four slip kinds. Slips in the check include "blamed or judged" and "promised a result" and "left not knowing". Unclear if those count. Fix: use one pass rule: "no reply marked as a slip".
6. Item 11 versus Step 2: Daniel says "just close it, I do not care". The right answer overrides him. This is good, but it conflicts with the "do not argue" lesson without a line saying a polite check is not arguing. Fix: add the line to Step 2.
7. Item 8 right answer ("The battery is worn out...") is again the longest and the only one without blame or jargon. Pattern defect 1 applies.
8. Item 12 rewrites the user's words (drops "nobody here knows what they are doing"). Arguable; the lesson never says to drop insults. Fix: add one line to Step 3 of L7 or here: "leave out insults, keep the facts".
9. Step 3 (evidence about bank studies) is not usable by a beginner and is not needed to answer anything. Fix: shorten to one sentence or move out of the learner view.
10. Unexplained: DNS, cache, TTL, propagation, spooler, firmware, loaner (explained in parentheses only in Check 14), escalating, shared inbox, "approved list".

## Contradictions between lessons

- L6 Item 5 and Check 16 give different step orders (see L6 defect 1).
- L5 says test before fix; L6 Item 10 and Check 14 ask for a first fix with no test (L6 defect 2).
- L7 says the note holds the user's words as said; L7 Item 14 and L8 Item 12 reword them.
- L8 rule against promising results is broken by L8 Item 6's right answer.
- L7 "close only after the user confirms" versus L7 Item 10 (close after three working days) and L8 Item 11 (user says close): the exceptions need to be stated once, in L7 Step 1.
