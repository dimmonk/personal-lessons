# How experts read a person's behavior: research for the Psychology subject (build-subject step 2, 2026-10-18)

The real task: hear a story about someone (or catch a thought of your own) and judge what is going on without over-reading
it. The people who do this for a living are clinicians making a judgment about behavior; the research on how they do it
well, and how laypeople and novices do it badly, is the source of the subject's way of thinking. Strength: **strong** =
replicated or a field standard; **moderate** = well-cited single line of work; **guidance** = a professional rule.

Passages (added 2026-10-18): each "Passage" line below is a quote of under 40 words, or a close paraphrase, from the abstract
or page named, with its URL. Abstract-level only: publisher full texts and books were not reachable. DSM-5 wording is from a
secondary reproduction (PsychDB), not the APA text. "Judgement call: passage not verified" means I could not read the passage.

## 1. How skilled clinicians decide

- **Describe before you label; rule out the situation and other causes first.** DSM-5's general criteria for a personality
  disorder (APA 2013, Section II, "General Personality Disorder") require an enduring pattern that deviates from cultural
  expectation, is inflexible and pervasive across personal and social situations, starts by adolescence or early adulthood,
  is stable over time, causes distress or impairment, and is *not better explained* by another condition, a substance or a
  medical cause. Strong (field standard). → Question 2 (one moment or a lasting pattern) and the situation check.
  - Passage (DSM-5 general criteria A-F, as reproduced by PsychDB, a secondary reproduction, not the APA text): "An
    enduring pattern of inner experience and behavior" that "deviates markedly from the expectations of the individual's
    culture"; "inflexible and pervasive across a broad range of personal and social situations"; "stable and of long
    duration, and its onset can be traced back at least to adolescence or early adulthood"; "not better explained as a
    manifestation or consequence of another mental disorder."
    https://www.psychdb.com/_export/xhtml/personality/introduction (the APA/DSM-5 p. 646-7 wording itself not read).
- **Kind of problem first.** Clinical assessment separates a symptom or state, an interpersonal behavior, and a trait or
  pattern before choosing a diagnostic frame; case formulation models (Persons 2008; Eells 2007) start from "what kind of
  problem is this" and its precipitating vs perpetuating factors. Moderate. → Question 1 (what is going on).
  - Judgement call: passage not verified (Persons 2008; Eells 2007 not read).
- **Function, not surface.** Functional analysis in behavior therapy asks what a behavior does for the person
  (antecedent–behavior–consequence; Haynes & O'Brien 2000); DSM differentials between Cluster B patterns turn on the
  motive and what threatens it (fear of abandonment in borderline vs need for admiration in narcissistic, criteria sets
  301.83 and 301.81). Strong. → Question 3 (what it does for them).
  - Haynes & O'Brien 2000: Judgement call: passage not verified.
  - Passage (DSM-5 301.83 BPD, PsychDB reproduction): "Frantic efforts to avoid real or imagined abandonment";
    "alternating between extremes of idealization and devaluation." NPD stem: "A pervasive pattern of grandiosity (in
    fantasy or behaviour), need for admiration, and lack of empathy." https://www.psychdb.com/personality/borderline ,
    https://www.psychdb.com/personality/narcissistic . The note's contrast (abandonment fear vs need for admiration) is
    our reading of the two criteria sets, not a quoted DSM statement.
- **Differential diagnosis.** Experts list the nearest alternatives and the one finding that separates them (DSM-5's
  "Differential Diagnosis" sections for every disorder; First 2014, *DSM-5 Handbook of Differential Diagnosis*). Strong.
  → Question 4 (the nearest look-alike and the detail that decides).
  - Judgement call: passage not verified (DSM-5 "Differential Diagnosis" sections; First 2014 not read).
- **Guard against your own bias: seek what would disconfirm.** Clinicians' main judgment errors are premature closure,
  confirmation bias and ignoring base rates; accuracy improves when they actively consider alternatives and disconfirming
  evidence (Garb 1998, *Studying the Clinician*; Croskerry 2003 on cognitive errors in diagnosis and debiasing; the specific list is Garb's, not verified). Strong. → Question 5 (what would
  prove the read wrong).
  - Passage (Croskerry 2003, Academic Medicine 78(8), abstract, PMID 12915363): "A critical subset of diagnostic errors
    arises through cognitive errors"; the paper proposes "cognitive debiasing", mainly metacognition (stepping back to
    examine one's own reasoning). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12915363/
  - CORRECTION: the abstract does not name premature closure, confirmation bias or base rates, and does not say
    considering disconfirming evidence improves accuracy; it says debiasing strategies are proposed and that there is
    skepticism they work. Those named errors are Judgement call: passage not verified (full text not read). Garb 1998:
    Judgement call: passage not verified.
- **Withhold the label when the evidence is thin.** DSM-5 provides "unspecified" and allows deferral when information is
  insufficient; the APA's Goldwater rule (1973, reaffirmed 2017) bars diagnosing anyone not examined. Over-labeling is the
  documented lay and novice failure (Garb 1998; Lilienfeld et al. 2015 on misused psychological terms). Guidance + moderate.
  → the naming rule and "too little to tell".
  - Passage (APA, Goldwater rule, APA blog 2016): "it is unethical for a psychiatrist to offer a professional opinion
    unless he or she has conducted an examination"; the APA says it has abided by the principle since 1973.
    https://www.psychiatry.org/news-room/apa-blogs/the-goldwater-rule . The "reaffirmed 2017" date and DSM-5 "unspecified"
    deferral: Judgement call: passage not verified.
  - Passage (Lilienfeld et al. 2015, Frontiers in Psychology 6:1100, abstract, PMID 26284019): "we present a provisional
    list of 50 commonly used terms in psychology, psychiatry, and allied fields that should be avoided". The abstract does
    not itself claim over-labeling by novices; that link is our reading. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4522609/

The order of the five questions is the order of that assessment: what kind of problem, how lasting, its function, what
else it could be, what would rule it out.

## 2. What novices get wrong

- **The fundamental attribution error**: explaining others' behavior by character and our own by circumstance (Ross 1977;
  Gilbert & Malone 1995). Strong. → "situation before character".
  - Ross 1977 (Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 10, 173-220): Judgement call: passage not verified (only
    secondary summaries seen; primary text not read).
  - Passage (Gilbert & Malone 1995, Psychological Bulletin 117, abstract, PMID 7870861): "The correspondence bias is the
    tendency to draw inferences about a person's unique and enduring dispositions" from behavior that situational factors
    explain. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7870861/ . Note: this supports the "character over situation" half; the "our
    own behavior to circumstance" half (actor-observer) was not found in what I read.
- **One episode taken for a trait**: laypeople infer traits from single acts (spontaneous trait inference, Uleman et al.
  1996). Strong. → a moment is not a pattern.
  - Uleman et al. 1996: Judgement call: passage not verified. (Gilbert & Malone 1995, above, supports the general move
    from one act to an enduring disposition.)
- **Popular labels used as insults** ("narcissist", "gaslighting", "toxic"): Lilienfeld et al. 2015 (*Frontiers*, "50
  psychological and psychiatric terms to avoid"). Moderate. → the words that are not names.
  - Passage: see Lilienfeld et al. 2015 abstract above (list of 50 terms to avoid, grouped as inaccurate, misused,
    ambiguous, oxymorons, pleonasms). Whether "narcissist", "gaslighting" and "toxic" are on the list: Judgement call:
    passage not verified (list body not read; do not state it as fact).

## 3b. Thinking traps: the choices in lesson 3 and what each rests on

- **Catch the thought, then test it** against the evidence for and against: the "automatic thought" and its examination in
  cognitive therapy (Beck, Rush, Shaw & Emery 1979, *Cognitive Therapy of Depression*, the chapters on eliciting and testing
  automatic thoughts); the daily thought record (Beck 1979; Greenberger & Padesky 1995, *Mind Over Mood*, the thought-record
  chapters). Strong.
  - Beck et al. 1979; Greenberger & Padesky 1995: Judgement call: passage not verified (books not read).
- **The likeliest outcome, not the worst**: "decatastrophizing", asking what is most likely and how one would cope
  (Beck et al. 1979; Burns 1980, *Feeling Good*, chapter on the ten distortions and their answers). Strong as practice; the
  wording is ours.
  - Judgement call: passage not verified (Beck 1979; Burns 1980 not read; the note already says the wording is ours).
- **What you would tell a friend**: Burns's "double-standard technique" (Burns 1980; *The Feeling Good Handbook* 1989).
  Moderate (a technique, widely used).
  - Judgement call: passage not verified (Burns 1980/1989 not read).
- **Often the best test is to ask or look**: the behavioral experiment (Bennett-Levy et al. 2004, *Oxford Guide to Behavioural
  Experiments in Cognitive Therapy*). Strong.
  - Judgement call: passage not verified (Bennett-Levy et al. 2004 not read).
- **A trap fails the test and is let go; a fair worry passes and becomes a plan**: cognitive therapy distinguishes distorted
  thoughts from realistic concerns, and pairs realistic ones with problem solving (D'Zurilla & Nezu 2007, *Problem-Solving
  Therapy*; Leahy 2005, *The Worry Cure*, productive vs unproductive worry). Moderate; the one-line rule is our summary.
  - Judgement call: passage not verified (D'Zurilla & Nezu 2007; Leahy 2005 not read).
- **Judge the thought, never yourself**: labeling is itself one of Burns's distortions (Burns 1980). Moderate; our wording.
  - Judgement call: passage not verified (Burns 1980 not read).
- **A trap often feels useful because it braces you for the worst**: the contrast-avoidance model of worry (Newman & Llera 2011)
  and defensive pessimism (Norem & Cantor 1986). Moderate; stated with "often".
  - Passage (Newman & Llera 2011, Clinical Psychology Review 31, abstract, PMID 21334285): proposes the Contrast Avoidance
    Model, in which worry prolongs a negative mood to avoid a sudden negative emotional shift.
    https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21334285/
  - Passage (Norem & Cantor 1986, JPSP 51, abstract, PMID 3806357): "individuals may sometimes use low expectations to
    cope with their anxiety so that it does not become debilitating". https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3806357/
- **Worry that is constant, overwhelming or hopeless is a reason to see a professional**: generalized anxiety disorder is
  excessive worry most days for six months that is hard to control (DSM-5, 300.02); hopelessness is a recognized risk sign
  (Beck, Steer, Kovacs & Garrison 1985). Strong (guidance).
  - Passage (Beck, Steer, Kovacs & Garrison 1985, Am J Psychiatry 142, abstract, PMID 3985195): in 207 patients followed 5
    to 10 years, a Hopelessness Scale score of 10 or higher identified 91% of those who later died by suicide;
    hopelessness is "an indicator of long-term suicidal risk in hospitalized depressed patients" (a hospitalized,
    suicidal-ideation sample). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3985195/ . DSM-5 300.02 GAD wording ("six months"):
    Judgement call: passage not verified.
- Overgeneralizing and labeling are folded into all-or-nothing for a beginner: a teaching simplification of Burns's ten, said
  in the lesson. Judgment call.
  - Our own teaching simplification; no source claimed.

## 3c. Moves made on someone (lesson 6): each choice and its source

- **A move is named by its full sequence and its function, not one uncomfortable moment**: DARVO is defined by Freyd (1997,
  "Violations of power, adaptive blindness, and betrayal trauma theory", *Feminism & Psychology*) as Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim
  and Offender, a response of a person held responsible; Harsey & Freyd (2020) measured it as all three parts. The course's
  "in that order" is a teaching simplification. Moderate.
  - See the DARVO abstract in section 3 (2023 JIV paper). Freyd 1997 and Harsey & Freyd 2020: Judgement call: passage not
    verified. The "in that order" claim is already marked a simplification.
- **The story must show the act happened** before a denial counts as DARVO: follows from Freyd's definition (a perpetrator's
  response); a person who accurately defends themselves is not doing DARVO. Judgment call drawn from the definition.
  - Already marked as drawn from the definition; the 2023 abstract describes DARVO as a response "exhibited by
    perpetrators", which is consistent. Judgement call: passage not verified for the "accurate self-defence is not DARVO"
    step.
- **Gaslighting is a pattern that makes the target doubt their own perception, and runs on a power gap**: Sweet (2019, "The
  sociology of gaslighting", *American Sociological Review*). Moderate.
  - Sweet 2019: Judgement call: passage not verified. Secondary summaries seen in search (not the article) describe it as
    arising in "power-laden intimate relationships", consistent with the claim but not checked against the text.
- **Love-bombing**: excessive early attention and affection used to gain influence, linked to later devaluation
  (Strutzenberg, Wiersma-Mosley, Jozkowski & Becnel 2017, *Sexuality & Culture*). Moderate.
  - Judgement call: passage not verified (Strutzenberg et al. 2017 not read).
- **Defense mechanisms are universal; the question is how rigid and constant** (Vaillant 1992, *Ego Mechanisms of Defense*).
  Strong.
  - Judgement call: passage not verified (Vaillant 1992 not read).
- **The responses**: keep a record and check with someone outside for gaslighting (the target's doubt is the mechanism, Sweet
  2019; domestic-violence safety planning keeps records, National Domestic Violence Hotline guidance); bring it back to the one
  fact for DARVO and deflection (Freyd's work on naming DARVO reduces its effect, Harsey & Freyd 2020); slow down and keep your
  own friends for love-bombing (isolation is the risk, Strutzenberg et al. 2017); outside help when fear or control shows
  (hotline guidance). Moderate; the one-line wording is ours.
  - Judgement call: passage not verified (hotline guidance, Sweet, Harsey & Freyd, Strutzenberg not read; the note already
    says the wording is ours).

## 3d. Narcissism and its look-alikes (lessons 4 and 5)

- **Confidence vs narcissism is decided by what happens to regard for others when confidence is tested**: narcissistic
  self-esteem is contingent and defended against threat, with aggression toward whoever delivered the threat (Kernis 2003; Bushman & Baumeister 1998, ego threat
  and aggression). Strong.
  - Passage (Bushman & Baumeister 1998, JPSP 75, abstract, PMID 9686460): "The combination of narcissism and insult led to
    exceptionally high levels of aggression toward the source of the insult"; self-esteem alone did not predict
    aggression. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9686460/ . This supports ego threat producing aggression toward the
    source; "derogating others" and "contingent self-esteem" (Kernis 2003) are Judgement call: passage not verified.
- **DSM-5 NPD**: nine criteria, five needed, pervasive since early adulthood, in many settings (APA 2013, 301.81). The "main
  signs" (grandiosity, need for admiration, low empathy) are the criterion stem. Strong.
  - Passage (PsychDB reproduction of DSM-5 301.81): "A pervasive pattern of grandiosity (in fantasy or behaviour), need
    for admiration, and lack of empathy", "as indicated by 5 (or more)" of nine criteria.
    https://www.psychdb.com/personality/narcissistic . "Beginning by early adulthood, in many contexts" is in the general
    criteria above, not read in the NPD text.
- **Grandiose and vulnerable**: two expressions, often in one person (Pincus & Lukowitsky 2010; Miller et al. 2011). Strong.
  - See Pincus & Lukowitsky 2010 quote in section 3. Miller et al. 2011: Judgement call: passage not verified.
- **Borderline**: fear of abandonment, unstable self-image, idealize then devalue; improves with treatment (DSM-5 301.83;
  Linehan et al. 1991; Zanarini et al. 2006 on remission). Strong.
  - See DSM-5 BPD quotes and Linehan 1991 in section 3. Remission: Zanarini et al. 2006 (Am J Psychiatry 163, PMID
    16648323; the note says 2010): "Eighty-eight percent of the patients with borderline personality disorder studied

  - Antisocial quotes: see section 3. Histrionic 301.50 and Hare 2003: Judgement call: passage not verified.

## 3e. Biases, dissonance, anchors and hindsight (lessons 2, 2a, 2b)

- **What came first decides**: dissonance reduction follows an act (Festinger 1957); motivated reasoning starts from a desired
  conclusion that biases search and weighing (Kunda 1990); confirmation bias is one-sided seeking, reading and remembering of
  evidence for a held belief (Nickerson 1998); sunk cost points back at what is spent (Arkes & Blumer 1985). The "timing" test is
  a working rule for telling them apart, said as such. Strong for the effects; the rule is ours.
  - See Kunda 1990 passage in section 3. Festinger 1957, Nickerson 1998, Arkes & Blumer 1985: Judgement call: passage not
    verified. The timing test is ours, as stated.
- **Dissonance in depth**: insufficient justification (Festinger & Carlsmith 1959), effort justification (Aronson & Mills 1959),
  post-decision spreading (Brehm 1956), the self-concept (Aronson 1969), trivialization (Simon, Greenberg & Brehm 1995), failed
  prophecy (Festinger, Riecken & Schachter 1956), the pyramid of choice and talking to someone without attacking the self
  (Tavris & Aronson 2007). Strong.
  - Judgement call: passage not verified for every item listed (see section 3 note).
- **Anchors and hindsight**: Tversky & Kahneman 1974; Strack & Mussweiler 1997; Northcraft & Neale 1987; Fischhoff 1975;
  outcome bias as a close relative (Baron & Hershey 1988). Strong.
  - See Tversky & Kahneman 1974 and Baron & Hershey 1988 passages in section 3. Strack & Mussweiler 1997, Northcraft &
    Neale 1987, Fischhoff 1975: Judgement call: passage not verified.

## 4. What the learning research says about this kind of skill

It is classification of cases by a decision procedure, learned best by explicit instruction of the procedure, worked
examples, contrasting look-alike cases, and mixed, spaced practice on new cases with feedback (build-subject step 3:
Alfieri et al. 2011; Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006; interleaving, Rohrer 2012; clinical-reasoning training by contrasting
cases, Norman 2005). Strong.
